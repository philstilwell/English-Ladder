📅 November 03, 2026 - Mogging Becomes Collins' Word of the Year

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Mogging has been named word of the year by the Collins English Dictionary. The informal term describes outshining someone, especially by looking more attractive than they do. It is popular with Generations Z and Alpha, both online and in everyday conversation. Its usage increased by 210% as it became mainstream in 2026. Depending on the context, people can use it as either a compliment or a playful put-down. Collins' managing director Alex Beecroft explained that language experts study how often words appear and examine patterns in their use. He said the selection shows how each generation influences language. Collins uses a range of sources, including social media, to follow changes in English in the UK. The shortlist also included ghost job, an advertised position that an employer has no intention of filling. In 2025, Collins selected vibe coding, which means creating an app or website by describing it to artificial intelligence.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. outshining (verb, -ing form): appearing better or more impressive than someone else
2. usage (uncountable noun): how much a word is used, or the ways people use it
3. mainstream (adjective): widely known or accepted beyond a particular group
4. put-down (countable noun): a comment that makes someone seem less important or impressive
5. patterns (plural noun): ways something happens repeatedly that help people understand it
6. selection (noun): the act of choosing someone or something
7. sources (plural noun): places, people, or materials from which information is collected
8. shortlist (countable noun): a small group of choices being considered for a final decision

Grammar Focus: Present perfect passive
Use “has” or “have” + “been” + a past participle to describe a completed action with a result that matters now. The subject receives the action. In “has been named”, the focus is on the word receiving the title; “by” introduces who made the decision.
Example from the text: "Mogging has been named word of the year by the Collins English Dictionary."
