Could changing weather mean more problems for travelers?

Intermediate ESL: Weather and travel

Before you read

Read the title above. What do you think the story is about?

Read the story

Air travel may face more disruption as climate change brings more severe weather. During a busy summer travel period, storms near London caused long delays. Air traffic teams guided aircraft around the storms, reducing the space available for other flights. Some departures had to wait on the ground because the skies were congested. Some planes might need to change their routes. When aircraft land at a different airport, there must be space and facilities for the extra arrivals. Pilots use radar and forecasts to help them avoid dangerous weather. However, fast-moving storms can force changes late in a flight.

Words and grammar

1. disruption (noun): a problem that interrupts normal activity
2. delays (plural noun): times when something happens later than planned
3. routes (plural noun): the ways used to travel from one place to another

Grammar Focus: Might for possibility
Might need to means a change is possible. It does not say every plane will change its route.
Example from the text: "Some planes might need to change their routes."

Intermediate ESL: Courts · mentions deaths of children

Before you read

Read the title above. What do you think the story is about?

Read the story

A judge in Massachusetts has declared a mistrial in the Lindsay Clancy murder case. The jury could not agree after a week of discussions following weeks of testimony. The case concerns the deaths of her three children. Her lawyer tried unsuccessfully to prevent the mistrial. Another hearing is scheduled for later in the month to consider the next steps. The prosecutor said the legal team would discuss whether to seek another trial. Clancy remains held on the existing charges. The legal outcome remains uncertain.

Words and grammar

1. mistrial (noun): a trial that ends without a valid final decision
2. jury (noun): a group of people who decide facts in a trial
3. uncertain (adjective): not known or not decided

Grammar Focus: Remain + adjective
Remains uncertain means continues to be uncertain. It describes the current state, not a future verdict.
Example from the text: "The legal outcome remains uncertain."

Read carefully when responsibility is uncertain.

The report describes a growing series of suspected attacks on defence sites in Europe. Germany has blamed Russia for an attack involving drones at Leipzig airport. The airport is used to transport military supplies for Ukraine. Russian officials reject Germany’s accusation. Other countries have reported suspicious fires at defence facilities. Some governments have blamed Russia, while investigations elsewhere are still continuing. Some of the fires may eventually be found to have been accidental. Russia is the main suspect in the report.

Words and grammar

1. blamed (verb): said that someone was responsible
2. suspicious (adjective): making people think something may be wrong
3. suspect (noun): a person or party thought to be responsible

Grammar Focus: A claim with a source
In the report limits the statement to what this source says. Suspect does not mean proven responsible.
Example from the text: "Russia is the main suspect in the report."
