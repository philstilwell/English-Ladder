Trump Criticizes Press at White House Correspondents' Dinner

📅 July 25, 2026 - Trump Criticizes Press at White House Correspondents' Dinner

Read the story

The annual White House Correspondents' Dinner recently took place, an event traditionally celebrating press freedom and journalism. This year, however, President Trump used the occasion to take swipes at various news organizations. His remarks generated significant discussion regarding the relationship between the presidency and the media. Many attendees felt his comments overshadowed the true purpose of the evening. The dinner also served as a moment to commemorate the first such event that was sadly cancelled due to a shooting in April. The cancellation highlighted the serious challenges journalists sometimes face, even within secure environments. Despite the controversies, the event continued its tradition of recognizing outstanding reporting. Journalists and public figures gathered, though the atmosphere was notably different compared to previous years. The President's criticisms were delivered during a speech, drawing both cheers and criticism from the audience. Ultimately, the dinner aimed to uphold the importance of a free press, even with the ongoing tensions.

Words and grammar

1. take swipes at (verb phrase): To criticize someone or something quickly and sharply, often without much warning.
2. overshadowed (verb): To make something else seem less important, successful, or noticeable by comparison.
3. commemorate (verb): To officially remember and show respect for a great person or event, often with a ceremony or monument.
4. uphold (verb): To support something that you believe is right, and to make sure that it continues to exist or be respected.
5. highlighted (verb): Drew special attention to something, making it more noticeable or important.
6. outstanding (adjective): especially good compared with others of the same kind
7. recognizing (verb): publicly acknowledging the value or quality of something
8. attendees (plural noun): people who are present at an organised event

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action or the recipient of the action rather than the doer of the action. It is formed using a form of the verb 'to be' followed by the past participle of the main verb. We often use it when the agent (the person or thing performing the action) is unknown, unimportant, or obvious from the context, or when we want to emphasize the object of the action. For example, 'The event was cancelled' highlights the event itself.
Example from the text: "The dinner also served as a moment to commemorate the first such event that was sadly cancelled due to a shooting in April."
