Lesson 01: Clarifying an institutional investment mandate
Original fictional case: Foundation trustee Nora describes a five-year growth objective for a portfolio. The foundation also needs USD 2 million for a committed payment in six months. Trustee willingness to accept market volatility does not remove that cash requirement. Portfolio manager David is clarifying the mandate; no asset allocation, product selection, or trading authorization has been approved.
Language workshop: A five-year objective includes a six-month cash need
Communication goal: Separate investment objectives, liquidity requirements, and authority before discussing an allocation.
Language guidance: A five-year objective does not give every dollar a five-year horizon. The committed six-month payment must remain visible in the mandate discussion.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): The growth objective and the committed payment have different time horizons. / How much must be available, in which currency, and by what date? / Willingness to accept volatility is not the same as capacity to miss the payment. / Which body can approve the mandate and any changes? / That is an objective, not an assured return. / The six-month payment needs to remain explicit in the instructions. / We need the full cash-flow schedule before assessing the proposal. / We have clarified requirements, not approved an allocation or trade. / Are you asking about the finding or the next step? / Please correct me if I have misunderstood that detail. / That is what the current record says; it has not been independently confirmed. / That is a possibility, not an agreed outcome. / That decision sits outside my authority. / Who is responsible for that specific next step? / Let me restate the distinction more clearly. / We agree on the action, the owner, and the point for review.
Vocabulary: investment mandate: Agreed objectives, constraints, and authority for investment management. / investment policy statement: Document setting out investment objectives, constraints, and governance. / investment objective: Financial aim the investment strategy is intended to pursue. / time horizon: Period relevant to an investment goal or obligation. / liquidity requirement: Need for funds to be available at specified times. / risk tolerance: Willingness and ability to bear investment risk in the relevant context. / risk capacity: Financial ability to withstand loss or illiquidity. / return objective: Target outcome subject to risk and assumptions, not a guarantee. / capital preservation: Objective of protecting capital under the stated strategy and risks. / spending policy: Rules governing distributions or expenditure from assets. / investment constraint: Limit affecting permissible investment decisions. / restricted asset: Holding subject to a relevant legal, contractual, or policy limitation. / discretionary authority: Authorized power to make specified decisions without separate approval each time. / client governing body: Body authorized to make decisions for the institutional client. / mandate amendment: Authorized change to the agreed investment instructions. / cash-flow schedule: Timetable of expected or committed cash movements. / liability matching: Relating assets or cash flows to specified obligations. / suitability assessment: Evaluation against the relevant client circumstances and applicable requirements. / clarification request: Question that resolves an unclear meaning or instruction. / source attribution: Identification of where information came from. / working assumption: Provisional premise used while information is incomplete. / scope boundary: Limit of the work or authority being discussed. / action owner: Named person responsible for a specified next step. / check-back: Restatement used to verify that a message was understood.
Speaking task: Read the corrected intake and distinguish willingness, capacity, liquidity, and authority. Switch roles and state the two supplied horizons and the exact payment amount without selecting an investment. Complete the four-line follow-up with the supplied choices. Check the explanations, then read the corrected exchange twice, changing roles.
Partner: Nora: Foundation trustee / David: Portfolio manager
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A trustee suggests a revised return target in an email. The governing board has not approved a mandate amendment.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Nora: The policy document should bring the objective, cash need, restrictions, and governance together in one place.
David: That is the role of the investment policy statement, using the actual agreed instructions and keeping unresolved items visible.
Nora: I will return with the complete schedule and board requirements before we review any proposed allocation.
David: Then we can complete the appropriate suitability assessment and mandate process. We have clarified the questions, not selected a product, guaranteed a return, or authorized a trade.

Lesson 02: Explaining portfolio positioning and concentration
Original fictional case: Analyst Imani reviews a fictional portfolio with a technology weight of 28 percent against a benchmark weight of 20 percent. The difference is eight percentage points. The portfolio holds 40 securities, but several share exposure to the same technology demand driver. Portfolio manager Owen requests a risk review; no supplied rule establishes that the portfolio is compliant or that a trade must be made.
Language workshop: Eight percentage points is not eight percent more

David: That is a distinct liquidity requirement. We should not give that obligation the same horizon as the five-year objective.

David: Yes. Discretionary authority must be explicit; this conversation is not permission for me to trade immediately.
Nora: The policy document should bring the objective, cash need, restrictions, and governance together in one place.
David: That is the role of the investment policy statement, using the actual agreed instructions and keeping unresolved items visible.
Nora: I will return with the complete schedule and board requirements before we review any proposed allocation.
David: Then we can complete the appropriate suitability assessment and mandate process. We have clarified the questions, not selected a product, guaranteed a return, or authorized a trade.

Dialogue 08: A public rumor does not release a private fact
Original fictional setting: Banking analyst Dev works on confidential transaction material. A colleague in an investment team asks whether a public acquisition rumor is true, hoping to inform a portfolio decision. Dev has not been authorized to share the material or confirm the transaction. Compliance officer Rosa explains the need to preserve information barriers and use the actual escalation process. The case supplies no legal determination that information is public or permission to trade.
Professional roles: Dev / Rosa
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Dev: A colleague on the investment team asked whether an acquisition rumor is true. They know I work on confidential transaction material.
Rosa: Do not confirm or hint at the answer. Your client confidentiality obligations remain relevant even when the question starts with public speculation.
Dev: I was going to say I could not send the documents but could give a quick verbal sense of whether the story was accurate.
Rosa: That could still cross the information barrier. Protecting the attachment while revealing its substance does not preserve the boundary.
Dev: The colleague argues that we work for the same organization and that the information could improve a portfolio decision.
Rosa: Shared employment does not establish need-to-know access for that activity. Banking access and portfolio-research access serve different authorized purposes.
Dev: I should decline without saying anything that confirms whether the rumored transaction is one of our confidential matters.
Rosa: Exactly. Use compliance escalation for the request rather than decide on your own that an informal answer is harmless.
Dev: Does a newspaper rumor mean the information is now public enough for the team to use without further checking?
Rosa: A rumor is not proof of public dissemination of the verified facts. The actual information and legal or policy position need review.
Dev: We should treat the material as potentially sensitive rather than make our own quick conclusion that it could not matter to investors.
Rosa: Correct. Potential material nonpublic information must be handled through the actual controls, not through an improvised materiality judgment.
Dev: If there were a legitimate reason to involve someone from another team, the access process would need to be followed first.
Rosa: Yes. Any wall-crossing must follow the firm's requirements and applicable law, including the consequences for the person's activities.
Dev: I also should not reveal whether the company appears on a confidential monitoring or restriction list.
Rosa: Correct. A restricted list or monitoring status can carry sensitive information; follow the actual handling rules.
Dev: I will tell the colleague I cannot confirm confidential matters and that their question needs the designated compliance route.
Rosa: Good. Their public-side research should remain based on information permitted for that role and its applicable restrictions.
Dev: If material is accidentally sent across the boundary, I will report it promptly rather than forward it or erase the history.
Rosa: Follow the actual record preservation and access instructions. A clear boundary and timely escalation protect the client and both teams without requiring you to make an unauthorized legal decision.
Vocabulary in this exchange: material nonpublic information: Nonpublic information meeting the relevant materiality standard. / information barrier: Controls separating access and communication between activities. / need-to-know access: Access limited to a legitimate authorized purpose. / wall-crossing: Controlled process for bringing someone inside an information boundary under actual rules. / restricted list: Controlled list used for specified compliance restrictions. / client confidentiality: Duty or obligation to protect relevant client information.
