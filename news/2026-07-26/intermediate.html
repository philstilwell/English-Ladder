Read the story

A shocking incident unfolded at a Pride event in the German capital on Saturday night. A suspect deliberately rammed a car into a large crowd celebrating in the streets. This act of violence immediately led to a significant police response in the area. Authorities have launched a manhunt for the individual responsible for the attack. Eyewitnesses recounted scenes of chaos and fear as the vehicle struck attendees. Emergency services quickly arrived at the scene to provide medical assistance to those affected. Several people sustained injuries, though the full extent of the casualties is still being assessed. The police are now conducting an investigation to uncover the motive behind the incident. This event has raised concerns about public safety at large gatherings. The community is seeking answers and hopes for justice to be served quickly.

Words and grammar

1. rammed (verb (past tense)): forcefully hit something with a vehicle or other object, often causing damage.
2. manhunt (noun): an organized search for a person, especially a criminal, by a large number of people.
3. assistance (noun): the provision of money, resources, or help to support someone or something.
4. casualties (noun (plural)): people killed or injured in a war, accident, or other disaster or incident.
5. investigation (noun): a formal inquiry or systematic examination to discover and examine the facts of an incident, crime, or situation.
6. crowd (noun): a large group of people together in one place
7. suspect (noun): a person who police think did a crime
8. capital (noun): the city where a country’s government is based

Grammar Focus: The Passive Voice (Present Continuous)
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action and the object receiving the action, rather than the subject performing it. The present continuous passive is formed with "is/are being" + past participle, indicating an ongoing action. It is often used when the doer of the action is unknown, unimportant, or obvious from the context.
Example from the text: "the full extent of the casualties is still being assessed."
