Lesson 01: Mission, Theory of Change, and Program Design
Original fictional case: The fictional Pathway Project runs employment workshops. Its checked registration records show 200 distinct participants this quarter. The team has not measured employment after the workshops or whether any employment lasts. A donor update calls the attendance figure proof of lasting employment improvement. Program lead Nina and learning officer Joel must correct the claim while retaining the verified participation achievement. Their proposed pathway links workshops to stronger application skills, better job applications, and sustained employment, but those links and the necessary local job opportunities have not been established by the attendance records.
Language workshop: Attendance is an achievement, not a lasting outcome
Communication goal: Explain the proposed pathway from a program activity to its intended result while distinguishing measured facts from assumptions.
Language guidance: Two hundred distinct participants is a verified reach figure in this case. It is not an employment count. Keep the achievement, the measurement period, and the unit intact instead of upgrading participation into a result the records do not contain.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): Two hundred distinct participants attended this quarter. / That figure comes from checked registration records. / It measures participation, not lasting employment. / We expect workshops to support stronger application skills. / That is the proposed pathway, not a demonstrated result. / We need evidence of skill use and later employment. / What duration will we mean by sustained employment? / The pathway assumes relevant job opportunities are available. / Participants can help identify barriers that the workshop design misses. / Attendance alone does not establish the longer-term effect. / We can report the reach confidently without overstating the outcome. / Remove proof of lasting employment improvement from this update. / Which indicator would show the next step in the pathway? / The mission describes what we seek, not what this quarter's records prove. / We should define the purpose and appropriate process before collecting more information. / State what we measured, what we hope to change, and what remains to be tested.
Vocabulary: mission: The organization's central purpose and intended contribution. / program design: The planned structure and delivery of an intervention. / theory of change: An explanation of how actions are expected to produce change in a context. / results chain: A sequence linking resources, actions, and intended results. / input: A resource used to deliver program work. / activity: Work performed as part of a program. / output: A direct product or service delivered by program activity. / outcome: A change experienced by the people or systems a program seeks to support. / impact: A broader or longer-term effect, with its meaning defined for the evaluation. / assumption: A condition or explanation taken as true for planning until tested. / causal link: A proposed or established relationship in which one factor affects another. / pathway: The proposed sequence through which change is expected to occur. / target group: The defined people a program intends to reach. / participant: A person taking part in a program activity. / reach: The extent of contact or participation within a defined population. / needs assessment: A structured examination of priorities, gaps, and existing resources. / community input: Perspectives and knowledge supplied by the affected community. / barrier: A factor that obstructs access, participation, or progress. / enabling condition: A circumstance that helps a proposed pathway work. / indicator: A defined measure used to track a relevant condition or result. / baseline: A reference measurement taken before or at the start of a comparison. / follow-up: Later contact or measurement after an activity. / sustained employment: Employment continuing for a defined period under a specified measure. / learning question: A specific question guiding evidence collection and improvement.
Speaking task: Read turns 1-10, giving the verified participation count clear emphasis. Swap roles for turns 11-20 and separate the mission from the result already measured. Complete the mentoring exchange below and read it aloud without turning participation into qualification attainment.
Partner: Nina: Program lead / Joel: Learning officer
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A mentoring project records 90 distinct participants in April. It has not measured later qualifications. Its proposed pathway links mentoring to study habits and then course completion.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Nina: I will keep the two-hundred-person figure and explain the proposed skills-to-employment pathway separately, with the assumptions visible.
Joel: Include a learning question about whether participants use the application skills. That gives the team a practical next evidence task instead of treating the diagram as proof.
Nina: The donor can then see both the achievement and the remaining work, without assuming the program has already demonstrated its long-term effect.
Joel: Exactly. Our mission remains ambitious. The reporting becomes more credible when it states what we measured, what we seek to change, and which links still need to be tested.

Evaluation officer Priya and program manager Dan must report the improvement among paired completers without presenting it as a proven program effect, a result for all 75 enrollees, or evidence that household finances have improved.
Language workshop: The scores improved, but the cause is not isolated
Communication goal: Report a before-and-after result with the correct population, measurement limits, and a proportionate learning conclusion.
