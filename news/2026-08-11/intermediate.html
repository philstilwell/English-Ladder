📅 August 11, 2026 - Trump's Secret Departure: A Ruse Over Iran Threat

Read the story

Former US President Donald Trump reportedly used an unusual method to depart a NATO summit in Turkey secretly. He was involved in a ruse, a deceptive plan, to mislead reporters about his travel arrangements. Journalists were falsely told he would be on Air Force One with them as they left the summit. However, a secret plane swap was carried out, allowing for his discreet departure. According to reports, Trump hid inside a catering truck during this complex operation. This particular maneuver was reportedly prompted by concerns about a potential threat from Iran. The extraordinary lengths taken highlight the intense security considerations for world leaders. Reporters, who were led to believe he was on their plane, did not discover the deception until later. The main goal was to ensure the president's safety, especially when leaving a sensitive area. Such an incident illustrates the elaborate strategies sometimes necessary for presidential security protocols.

Words and grammar

1. discreet (adjective): Careful in what you say or do, so that you do not embarrass or offend people or tell secrets; not drawing attention to itself.
2. maneuver (noun): A movement or series of moves requiring skill and care, often used to achieve a particular goal or avoid something.
3. prompted (verb (past participle)): Caused someone to do something or caused something to happen; to provoke or inspire an action.
4. deception (noun): The act of hiding the truth, especially to get an advantage or to make someone believe something that is not true.
5. journalists (noun): Journalists are people who write news stories for newspapers or report for TV and radio.
6. operation (noun): an organised activity carried out for a particular purpose
7. potential (adjective): possible, but not yet certain or actual
8. protocols (plural noun): agreed procedures for dealing with a situation

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the action of the verb is more important than who or what is performing the action, or when the agent is unknown or less important. The subject of the sentence receives the action rather than performing it. It's often formed with a form of 'to be' (e.g., “is”, “are”, “was”, “were”) followed by the past participle of the main verb.
Example from the text: "Reporters, who were led to believe he was on their plane, did not discover the deception until later."
