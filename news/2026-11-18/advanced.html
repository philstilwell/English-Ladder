Waymo Pauses US Freeway Services After California Ban

📅 November 18, 2026 - Waymo Pauses US Freeway Services After California Ban

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Waymo has announced a temporary withdrawal of its driverless taxis from freeways throughout the United States. The nationwide decision followed California's prohibition on freeway operations after several traffic incidents. In imposing the ban, California's Department of Motor Vehicles described an “imminent hazard to public safety” and pointed to several Waymo vehicles entering an active construction area on US Route 101. The regulator identified recurring incursions into active construction areas as a safety risk, noting the presence of workers, vehicles and equipment. Waymo said that no injuries or collisions had been reported following the Route 101 incident south of San Francisco. Separate footage circulating widely on social media appeared to show one of its cars attempting to overtake patrol vehicles while they intentionally slowed highway traffic in San Francisco. California's ban took effect on 11 November, with its removal contingent on Waymo demonstrating effective corrective action to address the deficiencies that precipitated it. Waymo said its broader freeway pause was in keeping with its safety principles and was intended to allow it to assess performance in its other operating markets. The company also said it was working diligently with state officials towards restarting freeway services. Data on Waymo's website suggest that its vehicles are involved in 95% fewer crashes causing serious injury than human drivers. Although Waymo operates driverless taxis in 15 US cities, freeway journeys had been available in only a few locations, including San Francisco, Los Angeles and Miami. Its taxis remain permitted to operate on ordinary city streets. In May, the company recalled thousands of vehicles over a software problem that could allow them to enter flooded roads.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. withdrawal (noun): The removal of a service or operation from a particular setting, without necessarily ending it permanently.
2. imminent hazard (noun phrase): A dangerous situation considered capable of causing harm very soon.
3. recurring incursions (plural noun phrase): Repeated entries into an area where those entering should not be, suggesting a pattern rather than an isolated event.
4. overtake (verb): To move past another vehicle travelling in the same direction; an attempt to do this does not establish that the manoeuvre was completed.
5. contingent on (adjective phrase): Dependent on a particular condition being fulfilled, rather than certain to happen.
6. corrective action (noun phrase): Measures taken to remedy an identified problem or failure.
7. deficiencies (plural noun): Weaknesses or shortcomings that prevent something from meeting the required standard.
8. precipitated (verb, past simple): Brought about or triggered an event or decision, often with a suggestion of suddenness.
9. in keeping with (prepositional phrase): Consistent with a stated principle, policy or expected standard of behaviour.
10. diligently (adverb): With sustained care and effort; in a corporate statement, it emphasises a claimed commitment to handling a matter responsibly.

Advanced Grammar: An “-ing” clause after “contingent on”
After “contingent on”, use a noun or an “-ing” clause to express a necessary condition. In “Waymo demonstrating”, “Waymo” identifies the subject of that clause. The construction presents effective corrective action as a condition for lifting the ban, without asserting that it has been demonstrated.
Example from the text: "California's ban took effect on 11 November, with its removal contingent on Waymo demonstrating effective corrective action to address the deficiencies that precipitated it."
