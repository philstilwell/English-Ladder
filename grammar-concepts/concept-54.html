Japan or the US? Articles with country names

Use country names naturally in full sentences by choosing the or no article.

Level ranges are study guidance. Start with the examples and choose your own pace.

NO ARTICLE: JAPAN, CANADA, BRAZIL

She lives in Japan. He works in Canada.

Use these country names directly after in, to, or from. The preposition does not create a need for the.

KEEP THE: THE US AND THE UK

We moved to the US. Our cousins live in the UK.

The short forms follow the country-name pattern: the United States becomes the US, and the United Kingdom becomes the UK.

Understand

The useful distinction

Most ordinary country names take no article: “We live in Japan”; “Our flight goes to Canada”; “She is from Brazil.” No article means that you put nothing before the name. Do not add the just because you mean one particular country.

Some country names normally take the. Names containing words such as Kingdom, States, or Republic usually follow this pattern: the United Kingdom and the United States. Keep the with their common abbreviations when they name the country in a sentence: “He works in the UK”; “They live in the US.”

Learn familiar plural names with the, including the Netherlands and the Philippines: “We visited the Netherlands.” Use the established name rather than guessing from the country's size, location, or islands. These patterns describe ordinary country names used as nouns in full sentences. A country label on a map or an abbreviation before another noun, such as “US dollars”, can follow a different pattern.
