Women Speak Up About Problems in Television

📅 November 14, 2026 - Women Speak Up About Problems in Television

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Recent news stories raise questions about how powerful people in television treat others. These stories follow changes that began years ago. Reporters started looking into these stories years ago. Their work can take a long time. Kirsty Wark says more women speak up because they see other women doing this. Freeman says women now feel that people will listen to them. A workers' group says some television workers are still afraid to report problems. Lucy Beaumont says some well-known people stay quiet because they fear losing work. A meeting with television leaders is planned for next week to discuss keeping people safe. People who run television companies also need to help stop people from hurting others.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. Reporters (plural noun): People who find information and write or tell news stories.
2. speak up (verb phrase): Tell other people about a problem instead of staying quiet.
3. listen (verb): Pay attention to what someone says.
4. afraid (adjective): Worried that something bad may happen.
5. losing work (verb phrase): No longer having a job or paid work.
6. safe (adjective): Not in danger of being hurt.

Grammar Focus: Giving a reason with “because”
Use “because” to explain why something happens or why someone does something. Put the reason after “because”. Both parts have a subject and a verb, as in “She speaks because she needs help”.
Example from the text: "Kirsty Wark says more women speak up because they see other women doing this."
