📅 October 05, 2026 - Data Centres in Scotland: Plans and Worries
Scotland plans to build many large data centres, which causes different opinions.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Many huge data centres are planned for Scotland. These centres help with online tasks like bank payments or using AI. From the outside, data centres look like a factory. Inside, thousands of computers work and make noise. Supporters say data centres can bring jobs and investment. But opponents worry about the energy they use and their size. Scotland has strong renewable energy, which data centres need. However, some centres might use fossil fuels if there is not enough wind power.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. planned (verb (past participle)): decided for the future; arranged beforehand
2. centres (noun): main places for a specific activity or business
3. online (adjective): connected to the internet
4. factory (noun): a large building where machines make things
5. noise (noun): a loud or unwanted sound
6. energy (noun): power from fuel or the sun to make things work
7. renewable (adjective): energy that comes from natural sources and can be used again

Grammar Focus: Using "can" for possibility
We use the word "can" to talk about what is possible or what people are able to do. It shows that something might happen or is allowed.
Example from the text: "Supporters say data centres can bring jobs and investment."

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. hit (verb): to strike something with force
2. drone (noun): a small aircraft that flies without a person inside
3. vehicles (noun): machines like cars or trucks that carry people or things
4. fireball (noun): a large, bright ball of fire
5. injured (adjective): hurt or harmed physically
6. support (noun): help or agreement for someone or something

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense
We use the simple past tense to talk about actions that finished at a specific time in the past. For regular verbs, we often add '-ed'. For irregular verbs, the form changes.
Example from the text: "The drone attack created a large fireball and black smoke."

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. spoil (verb): to ruin something good, often by telling a secret or making it less fun
2. concert (noun): a live music show, usually with one or more singers or bands
3. online (adverb): on the internet, using a computer or phone to see information
4. moments (noun): special, short times or parts of an event
5. avoid (verb): to keep away from something or someone; to prevent something from happening
6. surprises (noun): things that happen or are found unexpectedly, often making you feel amazed

Grammar Focus: Using 'can' for possibility
We use 'can' to talk about things that are possible or things that are able to happen. It shows that something might happen or is allowed to happen.
Example from the text: "Social media can sometimes spoil big concert surprises for fans."
