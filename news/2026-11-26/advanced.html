UK to Publish EU Treaties With Potential Exit Costs

Advanced · C1+

📅 November 26, 2026 - UK to Publish EU Treaties With Potential Exit Costs

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The UK government and the EU are expected to publish two treaty texts on Thursday, following agreements reached the previous week as part of a broader deal. UK legislation intended to enact most of the deal is due to appear alongside the treaty texts. According to the BBC, were either party to terminate the agreement within the first ten years, it would have to pay to re-establish border infrastructure that is to be substantially reduced under the deal. Opposition leader Kemi Badenoch and Reform UK's Nigel Farage have indicated that they would refuse to make such payments. The broader deal covers British food exports, emissions trading and a youth experience scheme. Britain's aviation and maritime industries would also be affected by alignment with EU emissions schemes. Food businesses exporting to the EU are expected to be major beneficiaries of fewer administrative requirements and the dismantling of border facilities. An internal analysis projects a 16% increase in agricultural exports, a forecast rather than an established gain. Industry and government hope that savings for food producers and sellers will reach consumers, although shoppers may see no discernible reduction at the checkout. Industry insiders privately told the BBC that the global economy has too many moving parts to make the outcome clear, particularly amid the energy price shock resulting from war in the Middle East. The treaty text is also expected to specify how the UK will observe and help shape relevant EU rules without having a vote on them. The accompanying legislation is expected to detail plans to align UK and EU law through secondary legislation, meaning rules made under powers granted by an existing Act.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. enact (verb): To give a policy or agreement legal effect by passing legislation.
2. terminate (verb): To formally bring an agreement to an end, with any consequences its terms require.
3. re-establish (verb): To restore something that previously existed after it has been removed or reduced.
4. emissions trading (noun phrase): A system in which participants buy and sell allowances permitting specified quantities of greenhouse gas emissions.
5. alignment (noun): The process or state of bringing rules or systems into agreement so that they operate consistently.
6. beneficiaries (plural noun): People or organisations that gain an advantage from a particular arrangement or change.
7. dismantling (noun): The process of taking facilities apart and removing them from use.
8. moving parts (plural noun phrase): Interacting factors that can change independently, making an overall outcome difficult to predict.
9. observe (verb): To comply with a rule or requirement, rather than merely watch what happens.
10. secondary legislation (noun phrase): Rules made using authority granted by an existing Act, rather than by passing a new Act.

Advanced Grammar: Conditional inversion with “were” and a to-infinitive
Use “were + subject + to-infinitive” instead of “if + subject + past simple” for a formal hypothetical future condition. The main clause uses “would + infinitive” for its consequence; the structure does not establish that withdrawal is likely.
Example from the text: "According to the BBC, were either party to terminate the agreement within the first ten years, it would have to pay to re-establish border infrastructure that is to be substantially reduced under the deal."
