Read the story

French President Emmanuel Macron has warned that Russia's hybrid attacks against Europe have grown more intense. He stated that France has been affected by these attacks in recent weeks. Macron explained that he had ordered a plan to secure essential infrastructure and defense sites. A growing number of European leaders believe Russia may be testing their commitment to supporting Ukraine. The French president recently discussed the security impact of conflicts in the Middle East and Ukraine with party leaders. He mentioned remarks by Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk suggesting Russia was planning drone and missile strikes against countries on Nato's eastern flank. Macron stated that the intent behind these actions is to intimidate Europeans and break their efforts to support Ukraine. He stressed that France would not be intimidated because European security is at stake in Ukraine. Prime Minister Tusk said there was a "real risk" of such strikes on Nato territory, which Moscow might claim were "accidental" to split the alliance. Last Sunday, a Russian drone hit a passenger train near the Ukraine-Poland border, shortly after European security officials had passed through. Hybrid warfare is a strategy combining military force with non-military methods like misinformation and economic pressure. Macron also addressed the energy situation, announcing a G7 meeting to discuss energy issues and raise the question of gas stocks.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. affected (adjective): influenced or changed by something.
2. ordered (verb): commanded or instructed someone to do something.
3. commitment (noun): a promise or firm decision to do something.
4. impact (noun): the strong effect or influence that something has.
5. remarks (noun): comments or statements about something.
6. intimidate (verb): to make someone feel frightened or nervous so that they will do what you want.
7. at stake (idiom): at risk; in danger of being lost.
8. alliance (noun): a group of countries or organizations that have agreed to work together.
9. strategy (noun): a careful plan or method for achieving a particular goal.
10. misinformation (noun): false or inaccurate information that is spread, often unknowingly.

Grammar Focus: Reported Speech
Reported speech is used to tell someone what another person said without using their exact words. We often change the tense of the verb, pronouns, and time expressions in reported speech. For example, 'I am busy' becomes 'He said he was busy.' Reporting verbs like 'said,' 'stated,' 'explained,' 'warned,' or 'stressed' introduce the reported statement.
Example from the text: "Macron explained that he had ordered a plan to secure essential infrastructure and defense sites."

📅 September 17, 2026 - Bank of England Holds Interest Charges, Signals Future Rise
The Bank of England maintained its main interest charges for the sixth consecutive time, but warned they could increase if high energy costs continue due to global conflicts.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The Bank of England has held interest rates for the sixth time in a row. However, it said they are likely to rise if high energy prices caused by the conflict in the Middle East continue. The US-Israel war with Iran has disrupted global energy supplies, which led to a sharp increase in petrol and diesel prices. Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey said that if volatility in energy prices persists, it will greatly impact inflation. He stated that the Bank rate might need to rise to ensure inflation falls back to its 2% target. The UK's central bank aims to keep inflation at a target of 2%, but it has been above that for nearly two years. Official figures showed inflation rose to 3.1% in August from 2.9% in July. The Bank now forecasts that inflation will rise more than it previously thought. It also warned that the price cap on household gas and electricity bills for January is "now expected to rise substantially further." Households feel the impact of a rising Bank rate through higher borrowing costs, but can also benefit from more generous savings rates. The UK economy has been "more resilient" than expected, and the Bank improved its forecast for economic growth between July and September.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. persists (verb): to continue to happen or exist, especially for a long time or in a way that is difficult to stop
2. disrupted (verb): to interrupt an event, activity, or process by causing a problem
3. governor (noun): the official head of a public institution or organization, such as a central bank
4. ensure (verb): to make something certain to happen
5. Official figures (noun phrase): statistics or data formally released by a government or authoritative organization
6. forecasts (verb): to predict or estimate a future event or trend based on available information
7. resilient (adjective): able to quickly return to a previous good condition after being shocked or damaged
8. substantially (adverb): by a large amount or to a great extent
9. benefit (verb): to receive an advantage or something good from a situation

Grammar Focus: First Conditional (If + Present Simple, will/might + Base Verb)
The First Conditional describes real or very possible situations in the future and their likely results. It uses 'if' followed by a verb in the present simple tense, and then a future tense with 'will' or 'might' (or other modals like 'may' or 'can') and the base form of the verb for the result clause.
Example from the text: "However, it said they are likely to rise if high energy prices caused by the conflict in the Middle East continue."

African wild dogs recently recorded a 2,500-mile trek across the continent in search of a mate. This remarkable achievement by three brothers provides optimism that animals can travel vast distances despite human pressures. The brothers navigated Zambia's landscape, which is increasingly shaped by people, successfully moving past obstacles like major cities. However, other wild dogs in the study, tracked with GPS collars, died in illegal snares, showing the serious dangers they encounter. African wild dogs are among Africa's rarest predators, with only about 6,000 remaining in nature. They cannot breed within the group they are born into, so they either wait to inherit their group or leave at age two or three to find a mate. These long, one-way movements in search of a mate are known as dispersals, different from seasonal migrations for food. The findings suggest wild dog populations across eastern and southern Africa may be more connected than conservationists previously believed. Professor Scott Creel, a lead author, said the study offers cautious optimism. He explained, "It's plausible to believe that every major wild dog population in eastern and southern Africa, other than fenced ones, are potentially connected." Bruce Ellender, a co-author, emphasized that species roaming large areas, like wild dogs and elephants, need connected landscapes to survive. He stated, "We can't see these protected areas as islands and expect them to be functional in the long term, we need to think much broader than that."

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. trek (noun): a long and difficult walk or journey, often over mountains, deserts, or through forests
2. optimism (noun): a feeling of being hopeful about the future or about the success of something
3. navigated (verb): found and followed a path or direction, especially with difficulty or care
4. obstacles (noun): things that make it difficult to achieve something or move forward
5. snares (noun): traps made with a loop of wire or rope, used to catch small animals
6. predators (noun): animals that naturally hunt and kill other animals for food
7. dispersals (noun): the movements of animals from their original home to new areas, especially to find mates
8. conservationists (noun): people who work to protect animals, plants, and natural resources from damage or destruction
9. plausible (adjective): seeming reasonable or probable, and therefore easy to believe

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action and the object rather than the subject performing the action. It's often formed with a form of "to be" and the past participle of the main verb. We use it when the doer of the action is unknown, unimportant, or obvious, or when we want to shift emphasis.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. identified (verb): recognized or found something that exists.
2. disrupted (verb): interrupted something or caused it to stop working normally.
3. malicious (adjective): intending or intended to cause harm or trouble.
4. revelations (noun): surprising or important facts that are revealed.
5. potential (adjective): possible in the future, but not yet real.
6. dissidents (noun): people who disagree strongly with official policies, especially in a country with strict control.
7. safeguards (noun): measures taken to protect something or someone from harm or danger.
8. catastrophic (adjective): causing sudden, great damage or suffering.
9. incorporating (verb): including something as part of a larger whole.

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action itself and the object of the action, rather than the subject performing the action. We often use it when we don't know who performed the action, or when the actor is less important than the action or the receiver of the action. It is formed with a form of "to be" + the past participle. For example, in the sentence "The ball was kicked by the boy," the action "was kicked" is more important, or the receiver "the ball" is the focus, not "the boy" (the actor).
