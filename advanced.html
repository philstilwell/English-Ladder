📅 September 29, 2026 - Celine Dion and High Fashion Kick Off Paris Week
Celine Dion made a surprise appearance at L'Oreal's show, marking the beginning of Paris Fashion Week, an event closely watched for the strategies of major design houses and the latest styles from various celebrities.

Read the story

Celine Dion kicks off Paris Fashion Week with surprise performance. The singer appeared at the end of L'Oreal's annual show as the French fashion bonanza kicked off. Celine Dion has opened Paris Fashion Week with a surprise performance of her 2002 hit I'm Alive, underneath the city's famous Eiffel Tower. The Canadian star, who was diagnosed with a rare neurological disease called stiff person syndrome in 2022, took to the runway at the end of L'Oreal's annual show. The singer, who wore a ruched full-length velvet black gown, was later joined by famous ambassadors for the French brand, including Viola Davis, Simone Ashley, Aja Naomi King and Eva Longoria. Dion recently performed a comeback show in the city earlier this month, her first since her 2020 concert in New Jersey - the last before the Covid pandemic cut her tour short. Kendall Jenner also modelled at the show, wearing a sheer black halterneck dress with thigh split and an ivory corset underneath. Pop star Rihanna brought her usual fashion credentials to Paris on day one, with a shimmering, glamorous copper two-piece worn over sheer black lingerie. The fashion world is keeping a particularly close eye on Dior and Chanel, after both signed up new artistic directors last year. This week should see the pair continue to solidify their strategies for the two brands. Expect some serious aesthetic rivalry on the runways.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. kicks off (verb phrase): To start or begin an event, period, or activity, often with significant energy or fanfare.
2. bonanza (noun): A situation or event that creates a sudden increase in wealth, good fortune, or profits, often referring to a spectacular or profitable event.
3. full-length (adjective): Reaching to the floor or extending for the entire usual length, typically describing clothing or mirrors.
4. sheer (adjective): Referring to a fabric or garment that is very thin, light, and transparent, allowing much to be seen through it.
5. halterneck (noun): A style of woman's top or dress in which a strap or straps pass around the back of the neck, leaving the shoulders and often the back bare.
6. thigh split (noun): A design feature in a skirt or dress where a cut or opening extends upwards along the side or front of the thigh.
7. credentials (noun): Qualifications, achievements, or experiences that establish someone's authority, expertise, or suitability in a particular field.
8. shimmering (adjective): Shining with a soft, slightly wavering or trembling light, often giving an impression of subtle movement or sparkle.
9. keeping a particularly close eye on (idiom): To monitor something or someone very carefully and attentively, often because it is important, interesting, or potentially problematic.
10. solidify (verb): To make something stronger, more definite, or more certain; to establish firmly or permanently.

Advanced Grammar: Non-restrictive Relative Clauses
Non-restrictive relative clauses provide additional, non-essential information about a noun or noun phrase in the main clause. They are typically set off by commas and use relative pronouns such as 'who,' 'which,' or 'whose.' The sentence's core meaning remains intact even if the non-restrictive clause is removed, distinguishing it from restrictive clauses that are crucial for defining the noun.
Example from the text: "The singer, who wore a ruched full-length velvet black gown, was later joined by famous ambassadors for the French brand, including Viola Davis, Simone Ashley, Aja Naomi King and Eva Longoria."

📅 September 28, 2026 - Trump-Xi Summit: What Was Left Unsaid
The recent summit between US President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping revealed more through its omissions and the leaders' cautious separate statements than through any explicit, major advancements.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The most revealing aspect of the recent summit in Washington may be found in what the two leaders chose not to discuss, and what remains unknown. However, the talks produced no joint statements and few significant advancements. Instead, both governments issued separate accounts of the meeting, perhaps indicating that despite initial gestures, Washington and Beijing maintain a degree of caution towards each other. Beijing announced an agreement with Washington to initiate a new artificial intelligence discussion forum, with further consultations scheduled for late November. This move suggests both nations recognize the necessity of establishing certain safeguards around AI technology, which is increasingly integral to sectors from commerce to military planning. They also committed to creating a communication channel for AI-related incidents, intending to prevent misunderstandings or unforeseen events from escalating into a broader conflict. Chinese state media further confirmed that a temporary cessation of new tariffs has been extended until January 10. Beijing, however, had sought a more substantial extension to the pause in trade disputes, rendering the two-month reprieve a limited concession rather than a significant achievement. China successfully secured reduced import duties on $30bn (£23bn) of goods entering the US, with reciprocal reductions anticipated from Beijing. Strikingly, crucial subjects were entirely omitted from public statements. There was no mention of boosting China's rare earth mineral exports to the US, nor any sign that restrictions on advanced US semiconductors would be relaxed. The White House factsheet omitted any reference to Taiwan, yet the issue clearly held significant importance for Xi Jinping. According to Beijing's reports of the discussions, Xi urged Donald Trump to formally oppose Taiwan independence and manage the situation with prudence. This summit generated extensive discussions, yet yielded minimal shifts in policy for the time being. The most contentious issues, ranging from advanced technology to Taiwan, persist without full resolution. While both leaders affirm their commitment to stable relations, ongoing US-China communication appears significantly more straightforward than demanding either superpower to make substantial compromises.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. revealing (adjective): Providing insights or information that was previously hidden or unknown.
2. advancements (plural noun): Progress or developments in a particular field, often significant.
3. indicating (verb): Suggesting or showing something, often indirectly.
4. maintain (verb): To continue to possess or keep in existence a state or condition.
5. initiate (verb): To cause a process or action to begin; to start.
6. integral (adjective): Essential or fundamental to the completeness of something; forming a necessary part.
7. escalating (verb): Increasing rapidly in intensity or seriousness; worsening.
8. cessation (noun): The formal or complete stopping of an activity; an end.
9. reprieve (noun): A temporary escape from an undesirable situation or event; a postponement.
10. reciprocal (adjective): Given, felt, or done in return by each of two people or groups; mutual.
11. omitted (verb): Left out or excluded, either intentionally or by oversight.
12. prudence (noun): The quality of acting with careful good judgment, especially concerning practical matters.
13. contentious (adjective): Causing or likely to cause disagreement or argument; controversial.
14. affirm (verb): To state or assert positively; to declare something to be true.
15. substantial (adjective): Considerable in extent, amount, or importance; significant.

Advanced Grammar: Modals of Deduction and Speculation
C1-Higher learners often need to express degrees of certainty or possibility in formal contexts. Modals like "may," "might," "could," "must," "should," along with phrases like "perhaps indicating" or "suggests," are crucial for expressing deduction or speculation about events or situations when direct evidence is incomplete or ambiguous. These forms allow for nuanced communication, distinguishing between strong likelihood and mere possibility, which is vital in reporting and analysis.
Example from the text: "The most revealing aspect of the recent summit in Washington may be found in what the two leaders chose not to discuss, and what remains unknown."

13. borne the brunt (idiom/verb phrase): Suffered the worst or greatest part of an attack, difficulty, or burden; experienced the most severe impact.

The Bank of England has maintained interest rates for the sixth consecutive time, yet indicated a probable increase if high energy prices persist. This decision comes despite an uptick in the rate of inflation, with the main Bank rate remaining at 3.75%. Global energy supplies have been disrupted by the US-Israel war with Iran, leading to a sharp increase in petrol and diesel costs. Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey emphasized that prolonged volatility in energy prices would magnify its impact on inflation. He stated that higher rates would then be necessary to ensure inflation returns to the 2% target. The Bank now anticipates inflation will rise more than previously expected and forecasts a substantial increase in the January price cap for household gas and electricity. Bailey noted the direct effect of elevated energy prices was evident, but officials are still evaluating their broader economic implications. The Bank's main interest rate serves as a critical benchmark for lenders when determining borrowing and saving rates for individuals and businesses. While financial markets have factored in potential rate hikes, Bailey cautioned that the global economic outlook remains "hugely unpredictable." He specified that interest rates would only decrease with "an end of conflict in the Middle East... and energy prices coming really back to where they were before this conflict began." On a positive note, the Bank reported that the UK economy proved "more resilient" than anticipated, revising its economic growth forecast for July to September to 0.4%. Furthermore, food price inflation is projected to rise by 4% by year-end, a reduction from the earlier 6-7% forecast, as higher energy costs have not yet spilled over into other sectors. Households typically face higher borrowing costs when the Bank rate rises, but can also benefit from more generous savings rates. Consequently, numerous major lenders have already raised the cost of new fixed-rate mortgages in anticipation of a higher Bank rate. The Bank also announced a halt to its "quantitative tightening" (QT) program, pausing the annual sale of government bonds. Instead, it will divest smaller portions of its bond stockpile over eight years, a shift from its earlier practice of offloading bonds, which contributed to higher yields. This change in strategy prompted an immediate market reaction, with yields on 30-year UK government bonds falling from 5.86% to 5.75% after the announcement.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. maintained (verb): To keep something at the same level or standard, preventing it from decreasing or changing significantly.
2. consecutive (adjective): Following one after another without interruption; in an unbroken sequence.
3. uptick (noun): A small increase or slight improvement, often temporary, in a trend or measurement.
4. magnify (verb): To make something appear larger, more intense, or more significant than it actually is; to amplify an effect.
5. anticipates (verb): Regards as probable or expects; to look forward to or prepare for something to happen.
6. elevated (adjective): Higher than the usual, normal, or expected level; increased in amount or degree.
7. implications (noun): The possible results or effects of an action or decision, which may not be immediately obvious.
8. benchmark (noun): A standard or point of reference against which things can be measured, compared, or assessed.
9. factored in (phrasal verb): Included or considered something as a relevant element when making a calculation, plan, or assessment.
10. volatility (noun): The tendency of something, especially prices or markets, to change rapidly and unpredictably, often for the worse.

Advanced Grammar: Conditional clauses without "if"
Advanced English often employs alternative structures to express conditions, rather than explicitly using "if." Prepositions like "with" or phrases such as "on condition that" can introduce conditions, implying a cause-and-effect relationship or a necessary prerequisite for a certain outcome to occur. This adds nuance and conciseness to complex arguments, allowing for a more sophisticated expression of dependency.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

2. sleepwalk our way into (idiomatic phrase): To gradually, unconsciously, or carelessly enter an undesirable or dangerous situation without fully appreciating the potential negative consequences until it is too late.
3. bitterly regret (verb phrase): To experience a deep, intense, and often painful sorrow or remorse over a past decision, action, or missed opportunity.
4. stark warnings (noun phrase): Clear, severe, and unequivocal admonitions about potential dangers or negative consequences, leaving no room for doubt about the seriousness of the situation.
5. innate preferences (noun phrase): Natural, inborn inclinations or predispositions towards certain choices or things that are inherent and not learned or acquired through experience.

7. internally hollow (adjective phrase): Lacking genuine inner substance, subjective experience, or inherent consciousness, implying an absence of true feelings or self-awareness.
8. principled (adjective): Acting consistently in accordance with a strong set of moral beliefs, ethical standards, or a clear code of conduct, demonstrating integrity.
