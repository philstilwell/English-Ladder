📅 August 30, 2026 - Iceland Votes Against Restarting EU Membership Talks

Read the story

Iceland recently held a significant national vote to decide its future relationship with the European Union. The central question put to the electorate was whether the government should restart talks concerning potential EU membership. Broadcaster RUV reports that the national vote's results have now been revealed to the public. A considerable majority of Icelandic voters rejected the government's proposal to resume talks. Specifically, 52.8% of citizens who participated cast their ballots against continuing the discussions. This outcome clearly shows a strong public sentiment favouring Iceland's ongoing independent path outside the European Union. The government had initiated the idea of re-engaging with the EU to understand where the public stood on the matter. However, the electorate's decision has made the nation's position unequivocally clear for now. This vote marks a pivotal moment for Iceland's foreign policy and its long-term economic direction. Consequently, the government will now need to carefully consider these definitive results when planning its future diplomatic strategies.

Words and grammar

1. proposal (noun): A suggestion or plan that is formally presented for consideration or discussion.
2. resume talks (verb phrase): To continue discussions, conversations, or negotiations after a temporary interruption.
3. planning (verb): thinking about what you will do in the future and making arrangements for it.
4. reports (verb): to give information about something, often in the news.
5. considerable (adjective): large enough in amount or degree to be important
6. participated (verb): took part in an activity
7. independent (adjective): not controlled by another country, organisation or person
8. electorate (noun): all the people who are entitled to vote in an election

Grammar Focus: Reporting Verbs
Reporting verbs are used to tell others what someone else has said or written. They allow us to convey information from a source without always using direct quotations. Common reporting verbs include 'report,' 'say,' 'state,' 'announce,' and 'claim.' They are often followed by a 'that' clause, although 'that' can sometimes be omitted.
Example from the text: "Broadcaster RUV reports that the national vote's results have now been revealed to the public."
