📅 October 19, 2026 - Mars Mission Aims to Bring Moon Dust Back to Earth

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

A new mission will attempt to grab a piece of a Martian moon and bring it back to Earth. The project plans to set down a small rover to explore the terrain, though this will be no easy feat. The strong gravity of nearby Mars makes it much harder to get a rover onto the moon's surface. Once on the surface, the vehicle will deploy a robotic arm and two samplers to grab some dust. This material will be packed into a small capsule that will return to Earth in 2031. Scientists can conduct several studies even on individual grains, so they do not need to bring back kilograms of rock. Researchers believe the sample will finally shed light on the puzzle of where Mars's moons came from. The results will quickly show whether the moon is the product of a dramatic impact or a captured asteroid. Because the moon is so close to Mars, the returned sample may actually contain grains from the planet itself.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. attempt (verb): To try to do something, especially something difficult.
2. terrain (noun): The physical features of a piece of land or ground.
3. feat (noun): An achievement that requires great courage, skill, or strength.
4. deploy (verb): To bring into action or put something into a position ready for use.
5. capsule (noun): A small, closed container used in space travel to carry things safely.
6. individual (adjective): Considered separately from other things in a group; single.
7. shed light on (phrase): To help to explain a situation or make it easier to understand.
8. impact (noun): The powerful crash or force of one object hitting another.

Grammar Focus: Future Passive Voice
We use the future passive voice to talk about an action that will happen to a subject in the future, especially when the action is more important than who or what does it. It is formed using "will be" followed by the past participle of the main verb.
Example from the text: "This material will be packed into a small capsule that will return to Earth in 2031."
