Anthropic Thwarts AI Misuse, Including Biological Weapon Attempts

📅 September 11, 2026 - Anthropic Thwarts AI Misuse, Including Biological Weapon Attempts

Read the story

Anthropic says it has identified and disrupted attempts to use its AI model for "malicious activity" which could support the development of biological weapons. The revelations in Anthropic's threat intelligence report come after a former top researcher at the company warned of the risks of AI to humanity. The findings, made in the firm's recent threat intelligence report, are the latest in a growing series of warnings from AI researchers and industry insiders about the technology's potential risks to humanity. The cases of concern detected over the past eight months ranged from fake dating apps and hotel Wifi scams to surveillance built to identify dissidents. Claude, Anthropic's AI model, was also used by actors linked to a Russia-based cyber espionage campaign and by an Iranian propaganda institution, according to its report. This includes Google, which wrote on Tuesday that a person had attempted to use its AI tool, Gemini, to obtain a "complete, step-by-step technical guide for synthesizing weaponised biological agents". The lengthy report lists cases in which suspected state-sponsored groups, criminals, spyware vendors, state propaganda institutions, and politically motivated individuals misused its technology. Jacob Klein, the head of threat intelligence at Anthropic, told the New York Times it was "an incredibly nuanced situation". Anthropic stated, "The same information that can be used to develop a biological weapon could also be used to develop, for example, a vaccine or a cure for a disease." The report comes after a top safety researcher at Anthropic warned AI is advancing so quickly he believes there is a greater than 10% chance it "could kill all humans" within the next decade. In an article published on 6 September, OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki called for the industry to implement "voluntary slowdowns" until safeguards are set. In the US, Democratic lawmaker Bernie Sanders has introduced legislation to ban AI superintelligence and temporarily pause advanced AI development. Sanders emphasized, "When scientists tell you there is a chance, a chance that it could have a cataclysmic impact on humanity, you've got be a moron not to say, slow it down." The California-based company said it had incorporated its findings into its processes "to better prevent, detect, and disrupt these activities in the future". Anthropic also said it had shared intelligence with authorities and industry partners where appropriate.

Words and grammar

1. insiders (noun): People within an organization or group who have special knowledge about it that is not available to the public.
2. surveillance (noun): Close observation, especially of a suspected spy or criminal, for monitoring purposes.
3. espionage (noun): The practice of secretly gathering information about an enemy or rival country or company.
4. propaganda (noun): Information, especially of a biased or misleading nature, used to promote a political cause or point of view.
5. synthesizing (verb (gerund/present participle)): Combining a number of things to create a coherent whole, or creating a substance by chemical reaction.
6. weaponised (adjective): Adapted for use as a weapon, or made more potent or effective as a weapon.
7. vendors (noun): Companies or individuals that sell goods or services.
8. nuanced (adjective): Characterized by subtle shades of meaning or expression, making it complex and difficult to understand fully.
9. cataclysmic (adjective): Relating to a sudden and violent change, often with disastrous consequences.
10. superintelligence (noun): A hypothetical intellect that surpasses the cognitive abilities of the smartest human brains across virtually all domains.

Advanced Grammar: Past Participial Phrases
A past participial phrase, often beginning with a past participle (a verb form typically ending in -ed or -en), modifies a noun or pronoun by providing additional descriptive information. It functions similarly to a reduced relative clause, adding detail about the noun it follows or precedes, often indicating a passive action performed on the noun. For example, in "The findings, made in the firm's recent threat intelligence report...", the phrase "made in the firm's recent threat intelligence report" describes the 'findings'.
Example from the text: "The findings, made in the firm's recent threat intelligence report, are the latest in a growing series of warnings from AI researchers and industry insiders about the technology's potential risks to humanity."
