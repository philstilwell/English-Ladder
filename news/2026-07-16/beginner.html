Read the story

There are protests in Ukraine's cities. People are showing they are unhappy. They are protesting against President Zelensky. He removed the defence minister. The minister's name is Mykhailo Fedorov. The president has not explained this. His dismissal is causing great upset. Many people are sad and angry. This includes civil society and the military. People want to know why the minister left.

Words and grammar

1. protests (noun): When people gather to show they are unhappy about something.
2. removed (verb (past simple)): Took someone out of their job or position.
3. defence minister (noun phrase): An important person in the government who manages the country's army.
4. dismissal (noun): The act of firing someone from a job.
5. upset (adjective): Feeling unhappy or angry about something.

Grammar Focus: Present Continuous Tense
We use the present continuous tense to talk about actions that are happening now, at the moment of speaking. We form it with 'to be' (am, is, are) + verb-ing.
Example from the text: "His dismissal is causing great upset."
