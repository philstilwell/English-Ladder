📅 October 13, 2026 - Breaking Bad Actor RJ Mitte Dies Aged 34
US actor and disability advocate RJ Mitte, renowned for his compelling performance in the acclaimed drama series Breaking Bad, has passed away at the age of 34.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

US actor RJ Mitte, best known for his compelling portrayal of Walter White Jr in the drama series Breaking Bad, has died aged 34. His representative expressed profound sadness over the tragic passing of the beloved performer and disability rights campaigner. A local justice of the peace stated that a post-mortem examination would be performed to establish the exact details of his death. As his on-screen father descended deeper into the criminal underbelly, Mitte's character frequently expressed his displeasure with memorable levity. His character's condition was based on a more serious case experienced by a late friend of the show's creator. Colleagues noted that Mitte brought essential authenticity to the role before building a public platform focused on inclusion. He also supported various philanthropic initiatives designed to challenge prevailing societal biases against people with disabilities. Bryan Cranston recalled meeting the young teenager, noting that Mitte approached his acting part with immense joy and enthusiasm. Cranston reflected on their friendship, which remained strong even after the intense media spotlight on the hit series dimmed. Co-star Aaron Paul shared that he was completely heartbroken by the news, describing himself as being at a loss.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. portrayal (noun): The way a character or person is represented or depicted by an actor in a film or television production.
2. profound (adjective): Felt or experienced very strongly and deeply; intense.
3. post-mortem (adjective): Relating to a medical examination of a dead body conducted to determine the specific cause of death.
4. underbelly (noun): The hidden, unpleasant, or criminal part of society.
5. levity (noun): The treatment of a serious matter with humour, especially when contrasting with a tense or dark situation.
6. authenticity (noun): The quality of being genuine, believable, and true to life.
7. philanthropic (adjective): Involving the giving of money, time, or resources to help people and promote their welfare.
8. initiatives (noun): New plans or processes established to achieve a specific aim or resolve a societal issue.
9. dimmed (verb): Became less intense, prominent, or brightly focused over time.
10. heartbroken (adjective): Suffering from overwhelming emotional distress, sorrow, or grief.

Advanced Grammar: Non-defining relative clauses
A non-defining relative clause adds extra, non-essential information about a noun and is separated from the main clause by commas. If the clause is removed, the sentence remains grammatically complete and its core meaning is unaffected. In this example, 'which remained strong...' provides supplementary context about the nature of Cranston and Mitte's friendship without defining exactly which friendship is being discussed.
Example from the text: "Cranston reflected on their friendship, which remained strong even after the intense media spotlight on the hit series dimmed."

📅 October 12, 2026 - Zelensky Conditionally Backs 'Energy Ceasefire' Amid Russian Strikes
Ukrainian President Zelensky has conditionally supported a proposed energy ceasefire with Russia, as claimed by Donald Trump, even while Russia continues its strikes on Ukrainian power infrastructure and a controversial US-Russia fuel deal draws international criticism.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Volodymyr Zelensky has expressed conditional support for a proposed 'energy ceasefire' between Ukraine and Russia, which Donald Trump claimed was already in effect. Trump had insisted that the truce was 'effective immediately' but did not provide any specific details. Zelensky initially expressed surprise at the announcement, then confirmed Kyiv would back the measure if Moscow also agreed. However, Ukraine's President also accused Russia of striking Ukrainian energy facilities after Trump's public post. Trump has been advocating for Ukraine to halt its strikes on Russian oil infrastructure, arguing this would allow Russia to release diesel to the US and global market. This controversial diesel deal, which temporarily lifts sanctions on Russian imports, has drawn increasing condemnation. Zelensky explicitly called the agreement a 'gift' to Russian President Vladimir Putin, a sentiment echoed by European officials and some US politicians. Trump's latest initiative aims to lower domestic fuel prices before the midterm elections, following months of political backlash from a separate conflict. The higher pump prices and subsequent knock-on effects have soured Trump's standing with the American public, contributing to widespread inflation. Ukraine and Russia have engaged in months of retaliatory strikes targeting each other's energy infrastructure. Moscow asserts these installations are legitimate targets supporting Ukraine's military operations, while Kyiv views its attacks as essential to curtailing Russia's war finance capabilities. These sustained attacks have consequently disrupted energy supplies across both nations. Seven senators from both major US parties have issued a statement declaring Trump's deal 'runs directly counter' to a law prohibiting Russian energy imports. They urged the Trump administration to 'reverse course', emphasizing that Congress's intention was clear: no Russian oil imports until aggression against Ukraine ceases. Republican Senator Thom Tillis stated that the deal 'absolutely will' endanger American lives.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. conditional (adjective): Dependent on certain terms or circumstances; not absolute.
2. insisted (verb): Demanded something forcefully, not accepting any refusal.
3. advocating (verb): Publicly supporting or recommending a particular cause or policy.
4. condemnation (noun): The expression of very strong disapproval; censure.
5. explicitly (adverb): In a clear and detailed manner, leaving no room for confusion or doubt.
6. sentiment echoed (phrase): An opinion or feeling that was repeated or shared by others.
7. backlash (noun): A strong, negative reaction by a large number of people, especially to a political or social development.
8. soured (verb): Caused to become unpleasant, difficult, or problematic; spoiled.
9. retaliatory (adjective): Characterized by an action that harms someone in return for an injury or offense.
10. curtailing (verb): Reducing in extent or quantity; imposing a restriction on.

Advanced Grammar: Participle Clauses
Participle clauses, often introduced by present participles (-ing forms) or past participles (-ed forms), provide additional information about the main clause. They can concisely express cause, result, or simultaneous actions. In the example sentence, 'emphasizing' introduces a clause that clarifies the core message accompanying the senators' urging, adding context without a separate conjunction and full clause.
Example from the text: "They urged the Trump administration to 'reverse course', emphasizing that Congress's intention was clear: no Russian oil imports until aggression against Ukraine ceases."

A BBC News investigation has revealed that Bassam al-Hassan, a former high-ranking Syrian officer, is being bankrolled and safeguarded by US and Lebanese authorities. Sources confirm Hassan receives funds from US authorities via an intermediary, making the payments harder to track despite his US sanctions. However, individuals involved in the Tice investigation express concerns that Hassan's deal may continue for an unspecified duration with little discernible progress. He is considered valuable for his alleged knowledge of Tice's fate, Syria's chemical weapons program, and its military ties with Iran. Though Hassan told investigators Tice was executed and suggested a burial site, searches found nothing, casting doubt on his account's validity. Questions remain over the thoroughness of the search, with sources suggesting US authorities halted digging prematurely. The investigation also found that other Syrian officials connected to the Tice case had apparently found refuge abroad.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. bankrolled (verb): To provide financial support for someone or something, often in a significant way.
2. supervised (verb): To observe and direct the execution of a task, activity, or group, ensuring it is done correctly.
3. entities (noun): Organizations, institutions, or groups with a distinct and independent existence.
4. seizure (noun): The action of forcibly taking something or someone, often by legal authority or by force.
5. underpins (verb): To form the basis, support, or foundation for something, strengthening its existence or validity.
6. leadership structure (noun phrase): The hierarchical arrangement of individuals in positions of authority and decision-making within an organization.
7. limited recourse (noun phrase): Having few available options or inadequate means to obtain remedy, assistance, or legal redress.
8. asserted (verb): To state a fact or belief confidently and forcefully, often in the face of contradiction.
9. constitutes (verb): To be or be considered as; to form or make up a particular part of something.
10. intermediary (noun): A person who acts as a link between two parties to facilitate an agreement, communication, or transaction.

Advanced Grammar: Participial Phrases of Consequence
An “-ing” clause can express the result of the preceding situation. Here, “leaving Damascus with limited recourse” describes the effect of the US position on Syria’s options. Replacing it with “because” would make the limited options a reason instead of a result; an “-ing” form by itself does not always express a result.

📅 October 07, 2026 - French School Demonstrations Intensify: Demands for Reform and Police Concerns
Across France, a wave of high school demonstrations has broadened to include university students and unions, driven by demands for better educational funding and mounting concerns over police conduct.

Read the story

Tear gas was used in Paris and Marseille as school protests grew across France. Initially, crowds consisting mainly of teenagers called for more resources for schools and an end to what they characterized as police repression. Hundreds of thousands of people have taken to the streets across France, as a wave of protests by high-school pupils spread to their parents, university students and unions. The government reported that over 250,000 demonstrators participated on Tuesday, with disruptions in almost a quarter of all lycées. Students' unions, however, claimed significantly higher numbers, putting total demonstrators at 450,000 and blockaded high schools at 1,800 on what the protesters dubbed "Act Three" of their movement. Police clashed with demonstrators in Lyon, using tear gas in Paris and Marseille and water cannon in Rennes, though serious trouble was less severe than in previous protests. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu condemned earlier violence, reporting 215 students, 85 staff, and 715 police officers had been hurt since the protests began. Education Minister Édouard Geffray appealed for dialogue over school blockades and warned against "ultra-violent elements" infiltrating student movements. Alissa described schools as “falling apart”, conditions she saw as necessitating protests. Her friend, Sacha, further detailed poor conditions, including leaking ceilings in Marseille's schools, hindering effective study. Widespread concern exists over allegations of "disproportionate force" by police, with France's IGPN police inspectorate investigating multiple cases. One police officer has been charged by prosecutors with "violence... resulting in mutilation or permanent disability" after firing a tear-gas grenade at a high school student. Another alarming incident involved a 15-year-old's hand being torn off after a so-called anti-encirclement grenade was fired in unclear circumstances near a school in Lens. Government efforts, including a website for grievances and internal school discussions, have made little impression on the students so far. With further union-backed mobilisations anticipated, the current wave of discontent appears unlikely to abate in the near future. Alain and Michèle, former teachers in their seventies, joined the Paris march, explaining that they understood the education system’s problems.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. characterized (verb, past tense): Described the distinctive nature or features of something or someone.
2. repression (noun): The act of suppressing something or someone by force, often with a sense of injustice or oppression.
3. dubbed (verb, past tense): Given a particular name or nickname to something or someone.
4. appealed for dialogue (verb phrase): Made a serious and urgent request for discussion and negotiation to resolve a situation.
5. infiltrating (verb, present participle): Entering or gaining access to an organization or place surreptitiously and often with hostile intent.
6. necessitating (verb, present participle): Making something necessary as a result or consequence of a particular situation.
7. allegations (noun): Claims that someone has done something wrong; the word itself does not establish whether the claims are supported or proved.
8. charged by prosecutors (verb phrase): Formally accused by legal officials of committing a crime, leading to legal proceedings.
9. unclear circumstances (noun phrase): Conditions or details surrounding an event that are not fully known, understood, or transparent.
10. impression (noun): An effect or feeling produced on someone's mind, often initially or subtly, by an experience or event.
11. unlikely to abate (idiomatic phrase): Not likely to become less intense, widespread, or severe in the near future.

Advanced Grammar: Present Participle Clauses (Adverbial)
The “-ing” clause “explaining that…” adds what Alain and Michèle said alongside the main report that they joined the march. Its understood subject is the couple. Such clauses can express accompanying actions, reasons or results; “-ing” alone does not establish exact timing.

📅 October 03, 2026 - Concert Spoilers: Ruining Surprises or Building Excitement?
Social media profoundly impacts the live concert experience by disseminating "big moments from headline acts" and revealing show details long before many fans attend, leading to divided opinions and artist responses.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Social media is significantly altering the concert experience, with "big moments from headline acts" often shared online long before many fans attend the actual shows. Months before you have a chance to see your favourite artist, chances are you already know some of the big moments they have planned. You'll also probably know the set list, the stage design, and - if you've really been paying attention - where to stand to get the best view. For many major gigs, there's no escaping a stream of fan videos if you're online, whether you like it or not. For instance, surprises from Olivia Rodrigo's Unraveled tour, which scores of fans paid a lot for, have already been "dissected online". While some fans "lap up" the opportunity to see what's coming, others are taking "extreme measures" to preserve the element of surprise. Aurélia Collins, a dedicated fan, spends weeks trying to "trick her algorithm" into not serving her content about favourite artists. She finds this effort "worth it for the surprise," even if it means her social media feed is a bit bland. Collins was "gutted" when a spoiler about Niall Horan slipped through, just before her first live show after 16 years of fandom. Having missed live music when she was younger because of the cost, Collins now wants to take it in without advance spoilers. She wants the "full experience," emphasizing the element of surprise that people attending the opening night can get. Not all fans view spoilers negatively; Georgia McAdam claims she loves them. McAdam sees online spoilers more as "teasers" and believes no amount of videos can diminish the excitement of the real thing. She acknowledges that sometimes artists offer different "surprise songs" or unexpected actions at shows. Some artists react by "switching up" their sets in small ways each night to ensure fans coming to later dates still see something unexpected. For example, Taylor Swift featured "bespoke outfits" and surprise songs, while Sabrina Carpenter and Jay-Z also varied their shows. Laura Snapes, deputy music editor for The Guardian, notes that artists can ensure "they're staying in the press" by having something different happen nightly. Snapes advises fans seeking surprise to avoid absorbing online content about the shows. Phoebe Bridgers is taking things further by banning phones altogether at her shows. Attendees must lock their phones and recording devices in a special pouch when they enter the venue to see the US singer. Anyone found breaking the rules will be escorted out. McAdam would not support a ban on phones, arguing that recording at gigs is "part of the experience" for many fans. Conversely, Collins states she is "not totally against it" regarding phone bans. What Collins would prefer is to see more spoiler warnings on videos to give fans the option for surprise. Ultimately, Snapes suggests that even with set list knowledge, fans may still witness "moments of humanity and stuff that's unplanned." She cites Taylor Swift blowing her nose during the Edinburgh Eras show as an example of an unscripted moment.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. headline acts (noun phrase): The most important or famous performers at a concert or festival.
2. dissected (verb, past participle): Analyzed or examined in detail, often critically, by a large audience online.
3. lap up (phrasal verb): To eagerly accept or enjoy something, especially information or attention, with enthusiasm.
4. extreme measures (noun phrase): Difficult or severe actions taken to achieve a particular aim, often because normal methods are insufficient.
5. trick her algorithm (verb phrase): To manipulate the personalized content delivery system of a social media platform to avoid specific types of content.
6. gutted (adjective): Extremely disappointed or upset; feeling a deep sense of loss or frustration (informal).
7. take it in (phrasal verb): To fully comprehend, absorb, or appreciate something, often an experience or a view.
8. bespoke outfits (noun phrase): Clothing that is custom-made to a client's specifications or for a particular occasion.
9. switching up (phrasal verb): To change or vary something, often to introduce new elements or to keep it interesting.
10. escorted out (verb phrase, passive): To be formally led away from a place, typically by security personnel, often due to a violation of rules.

Advanced Grammar: Reduced Relative Clauses
Reduced relative clauses provide a more concise way to give extra information about a noun. They often omit the relative pronoun (e.g., 'who', 'which', 'that') and a form of 'be' (e.g., 'is', 'are', 'was', 'were'). For example, 'Anyone [who is] found breaking the rules will be escorted out' becomes 'Anyone found breaking the rules will be escorted out.' The participle ('found' or '-ing' form) directly modifies the noun, making the sentence more efficient.
Example from the text: "Anyone found breaking the rules will be escorted out."

He acknowledged that a more formal regulatory framework might emerge over time, yet maintained that immediate regulation "is not, in my view, the right place to start."

Advanced Grammar: Expressing Obligation and Recommendation with Modals In English, modal verbs like "must" and "should" are used to express different degrees of obligation, necessity, or recommendation. "Must" conveys a strong obligation or something considered essential and unavoidable. "Should" suggests a strong recommendation, advice, or what is generally considered right, but implies less forceful compulsion than "must." The choice of modal can subtly shift the speaker's emphasis on certainty or the imperative nature of an action.
