📅 September 09, 2026 - AI Safety Concerns: Anthropic Researcher Warns of Existential Risk

Read the story

A top safety researcher at Anthropic has warned AI is advancing so quickly he believes there is a greater than 10% chance it 'could kill all humans' within the next decade. Evan Hubinger said the risk from current models was 'low' but he was 'worried' the technology might improve itself to pose an existential risk to humanity. His comments are the latest in a series of increasingly stark warnings about AI, with the debate shifting from whether it truly poses a risk to how big that risk is. Separately, the Financial Times reported Anthropic withheld its latest model from the UK's AI Safety Institute (AISI), a leading body for assessing AI risk. Anthropic has declined to comment on the posts by its employees or the situation with the AISI. Hubinger added: 'I believe Anthropic is trying its best, but we do not yet have a plan to solve alignment for superintelligence and are not clearly on track to.' Hubinger works in AI alignment, which aims to build human ethical ideas and principles into the technology. OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta all disclosed hacks carried out by their AI tools.

Words and grammar

1. advancing (verb): making progress or moving forward in development; becoming more developed.
2. decade (noun): a period of ten years.
3. existential (adjective): relating to existence, especially human existence, or questioning the fundamental nature of something.
4. stark (adjective): very clear, severe, or obvious, often in an unpleasant way.
5. shifting (verb): changing from one position, direction, or opinion to another.
6. withheld (verb): refused to give something or kept something back; past tense of 'withhold'.
7. declined (verb): politely refused to do something or to comment; past tense of 'decline'.
8. alignment (noun): the state of being in agreement or in a correct or desired position. In AI, it means making systems act in accordance with human values.

Grammar Focus: Reporting Verbs
Reporting verbs are used to tell someone what another person said, thought, or felt, without necessarily using their exact words. They allow us to summarize or convey the message. Common reporting verbs include "say," "tell," "ask," "report," "warn," and "believe." They are often followed by "that" (which can be omitted) and a clause.
Example from the text: "Separately, the Financial Times reported Anthropic withheld its latest model from the UK's AI Safety Institute (AISI), a leading body for assessing AI risk."
