📅 September 29, 2026 - Celine Dion Starts Paris Fashion Week with Surprise Performance
Celine Dion opened Paris Fashion Week with a musical appearance at L'Oreal's annual show, marking her return to the stage after a health diagnosis.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Celine Dion has opened Paris Fashion Week with a performance of her 2002 hit I'm Alive. This event took place underneath the city's famous Eiffel Tower. The singer appeared at the end of L'Oreal's annual show. The Canadian star, who was diagnosed with a rare neurological disease called stiff person syndrome in 2022, took to the runway. She wore a ruched full-length velvet black gown for the occasion. Famous ambassadors for the French brand, including Viola Davis and Eva Longoria, later joined her. Dion recently performed a comeback show in the city earlier this month. This was her first since her 2020 concert, which was cut short by the Covid pandemic. Paris Fashion Week is currently showcasing women's spring/summer collections. The event runs until 6 October. The fashion world is keeping a close eye on Dior and Chanel. Both brands signed up new artistic directors last year.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. annual (adjective): happening once every year
2. diagnosed (verb): identified a medical condition or problem
3. syndrome (noun): a group of symptoms that together show a particular medical condition
4. took to (phrasal verb): began to do something in a public or confident way
5. ruched (adjective): gathered into folds or pleats, usually referring to fabric
6. ambassadors (noun): well-known people chosen to represent a company or product
7. comeback (noun): a return to success, fame, or good health after a period of absence
8. cut short (phrasal verb): ended something earlier than planned
9. showcasing (verb): displaying or presenting something in its best light
10. artistic directors (noun phrase): individuals responsible for the creative vision and artistic decisions of a brand

Grammar Focus: Non-defining relative clauses
Non-defining relative clauses give extra information about a noun. They are placed after the noun they describe and are separated by commas. They use relative pronouns like "who" for people, "which" for things, or "whose" for possession. The sentence would still make sense without this extra information.
Example from the text: "The Canadian star, who was diagnosed with a rare neurological disease called stiff person syndrome in 2022, took to the runway."

📅 September 28, 2026 - Trump-Xi Summit: What Was Unsaid Matters Most
The recent summit between US President Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping produced some concrete agreements, but many significant, contentious issues remain unresolved.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The recent summit between Trump and Xi in Washington was notable for what was not explicitly said. On Monday, China released details of some concrete outcomes from the talks. However, the summit produced no joint statements and few major breakthroughs. Instead, both governments issued separate accounts, suggesting they remain wary of each other. Beijing and Washington agreed to launch a new dialogue on artificial intelligence. They also established a communication channel for AI-related incidents to prevent broader confrontation. Chinese state media confirmed the trade truce has been extended until January 10. However, Beijing had hoped for a much longer extension to this truce, making it a modest gain. China secured lower tariffs on $30bn of goods entering the US, with reciprocal reductions expected. Strikingly, there was no mention of increasing China's rare earth mineral exports to the US. The White House factsheet made no mention of Taiwan, although it was clearly a priority for Xi Jinping. According to Beijing, Xi urged Trump to formally oppose Taiwan independence. The US State Department continues to classify travel to China as a Level 2 advisory, urging increased caution. Ultimately, contentious issues from advanced technology to Taiwan remain unresolved after the summit.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. notable (adjective): Important or interesting; deserving attention.
2. concrete outcomes (noun phrase): Results or achievements that are definite and clearly visible, not just ideas or plans.
3. breakthroughs (noun): Important discoveries or developments that help solve a problem.
4. wary (adjective): Feeling or showing caution about possible dangers or problems.
5. dialogue (noun): A formal discussion between two groups or countries, especially to resolve problems.
6. confrontation (noun): A hostile or argumentative meeting or situation between opposing parties.
7. trade truce (noun phrase): A temporary stop or pause in a trade war or disputes between countries.
8. tariffs (noun): Taxes imposed by a government on imported or exported goods.
9. priority (noun): A thing that is regarded as more important than others.
10. unresolved (adjective): Not solved or settled; still needing an answer or a decision.

Grammar Focus: Present Perfect Passive
The present perfect passive is used to describe an action that happened at an unspecified time in the past and has a connection to the present, focusing on the action itself rather than who performed it. It is formed with "has/have been" + the past participle of the main verb. For example, in the sentence "The trade truce has been extended," we know the truce is now longer, but the focus is on the extension, not specifically on who extended it.
Example from the text: "Chinese state media confirmed the trade truce has been extended until January 10."

11. widespread sadness (noun phrase): a feeling of unhappiness or gloom that is affecting many people or a large area.

The Bank now forecasts that inflation will rise more than it previously thought. It also warned that the price cap on household gas and electricity bills for January is "now expected to rise substantially further." Households feel the impact of a rising Bank rate through higher borrowing costs, but can also benefit from more generous savings rates. The UK economy has been "more resilient" than expected, and the Bank improved its forecast for economic growth between July and September.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. persists (verb): to continue to happen or exist, especially for a long time or in a way that is difficult to stop
2. disrupted (verb): to interrupt an event, activity, or process by causing a problem
3. governor (noun): the official head of a public institution or organization, such as a central bank
4. ensure (verb): to make something certain to happen
5. Official figures (noun phrase): statistics or data formally released by a government or authoritative organization
6. forecasts (verb): to predict or estimate a future event or trend based on available information
7. resilient (adjective): able to quickly return to a previous good condition after being shocked or damaged
8. substantially (adverb): by a large amount or to a great extent
9. benefit (verb): to receive an advantage or something good from a situation

Grammar Focus: First Conditional (If + Present Simple, will/might + Base Verb)
The First Conditional describes real or very possible situations in the future and their likely results. It uses 'if' followed by a verb in the present simple tense, and then a future tense with 'will' or 'might' (or other modals like 'may' or 'can') and the base form of the verb for the result clause.
Example from the text: "However, it said they are likely to rise if high energy prices caused by the conflict in the Middle East continue."
