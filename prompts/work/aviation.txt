Lesson 01: Safety Culture and Stop-Work Authority
Original fictional case: At 08:40 local time, ramp agent Dana observes an unidentified wet patch beside ground equipment at Stand 6. Dana invokes the fictional site's local stop-work process for the affected task and informs supervisor Malik. The equipment team has not assessed the patch or its source. Under this local process, the affected task stays stopped until the designated supervisor completes the required assessment and authorizes restart. Dana is not authorized to handle the substance or repair equipment. A colleague suggests continuing because the turnaround is behind schedule. No injury or equipment fault has been established.
Language workshop: A concern must remain visible under schedule pressure
Communication goal: Use a direct stop-work message, describe an observation, and preserve the authorized restart boundary.
Language guidance: Dana can report a wet patch without naming the substance or declaring an equipment failure. The observation warrants the local process, but it does not establish cause, injury, or the correct technical remedy.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): The affected task is stopped under our local process. / I saw a wet patch beside the equipment at Stand 6. / I observed it at 08:40 local time. / I have not identified the substance or its source. / The equipment team needs to assess the condition. / We cannot treat it as harmless because the schedule is tight. / Restart requires the designated supervisor's authorization. / Please confirm that the task remains stopped. / I am not authorized to handle the substance or repair the equipment. / No injury or equipment fault has been established. / The delay does not remove the assessment requirement. / Thank you for raising the concern promptly. / I will coordinate the required assessment. / I have received the report; that is not restart approval. / Record the time, location, observation, and action taken. / We will communicate any authorized change in status explicitly.
Vocabulary: ramp: The airport area used for aircraft servicing and related ground activity. / stand: A designated aircraft parking position. / ground support equipment (GSE): Equipment used to support aircraft ground operations. / hazard: A condition or object with potential to cause harm. / safety risk: The assessed likelihood and severity of harm from a hazard. / stop-work authority: Authority under the relevant process to halt an unsafe or uncertain task. / affected task: The specific activity subject to the concern or restriction. / escalation route: The defined path for raising a concern to the responsible people. / restart authorization: Permission to resume after the required conditions are satisfied. / safety management system (SMS): An organized approach to managing safety risks and controls. / hazard report: A record communicating a potentially harmful condition. / risk control: A measure intended to reduce or manage risk. / safety assurance: Checking whether safety arrangements remain effective. / safety promotion: Training and communication that support safe working practices. / just culture: An approach balancing fair reporting, learning, and accountability. / authority gradient: The influence of rank differences on communication. / schedule pressure: Pressure to meet a planned operating time. / situational awareness: Understanding the relevant conditions and their implications. / unidentified substance: Material whose identity has not been established. / direct observation: Something personally seen or otherwise perceived. / qualified assessment: Evaluation by personnel with the required competence and authority. / hold point: A stage beyond which activity cannot proceed without a required condition. / closed-loop communication: Communication in which receipt and understanding are confirmed. / residual risk: Risk remaining after the relevant controls are applied.
Speaking task: Read turns 1-10 with the corrected words; emphasize the task, stand, time, and unidentified condition. Switch roles for turns 11-20; keep the stop statement direct and the assessment separate from restart authorization. Read the checked Stand 9 exchange twice, switching roles. Retain 07:20 local and the pending authorization.
Partner: Dana: Ramp agent / Malik: Ground-operations supervisor
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: At Stand 9, a worker observes an unidentified patch at 07:20 local. The affected task is stopped under the local process. Assessment and restart authorization are pending.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Dana: Once the equipment team has assessed the patch, I will wait for the designated supervisor's decision rather than infer permission from people returning to the area.
Malik: Correct. The qualified assessment informs the decision, but it is not an unspoken release. Any authorized change must be communicated explicitly through the local process.
Dana: For now, the affected task remains stopped. The substance and source are unconfirmed, assessment is pending, and nobody has communicated restart authorization.
Malik: That is the correct hold point. I will coordinate the assessment and keep the relevant teams informed. Do not substitute the turnaround target for the required decision.
