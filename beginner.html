📅 September 05, 2026 [0 days, 11 hours old] - Mistrial Declared in Important Court Case
A judge stopped a court case about a woman because the jury could not decide if she was guilty.

Beginner ESL: Legal Cases and Court Decisions

Before you read

Look at the title. What do you think you will learn?

Read the story

A mistrial happened in a court case recently. This case is about Lindsay Clancy. It is a murder case. The jury could not agree on a decision for her. The jury deadlocks, which means they had no agreement. So, the judge declared a mistrial. Now, the case is in limbo. This means the future of the case is not clear. People do not know if she will be held criminally liable. This is for the deaths of her three children.

Words and grammar

1. mistrial (noun): A court trial that is stopped or canceled before a final decision because of a major problem.
2. murder case (noun phrase): A legal process in court about a person accused of killing another person.
3. deadlocks (verb): When a group, like a jury, cannot agree on a decision, and no one wins.
4. declared (verb): To officially announce something, usually in a formal way.
5. limbo (noun): A state where something is not decided, and its future is uncertain or unknown.

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense for Completed Actions
We use the simple past tense to talk about actions that finished at a specific time in the past. We often add '-ed' to regular verbs or use a different form for irregular verbs. It tells us something happened and is now over.
Example from the text: "So, the judge declared a mistrial."

📅 September 04, 2026 [1 day, 10 hours old] - Europe Faces Sabotage: Russia is the Main Suspect
Germany blames Russia for attacks in Europe, causing many suspicious incidents.

Beginner ESL: International Relations and Sabotage

Before you read

Look at the title. What do you think you will learn?

Read the story

Europe is targeted by many bad things happening. People call these bad things 'sabotage'. Sabotage means secretly damaging things. Russia is the chief suspect for this sabotage campaign. Germany said Russia attacked Leipzig airport. This was one of many suspicious incidents. A campaign of these incidents is happening in Europe. These events are making countries worried. Police are investigating all the incidents. They want to find out who is doing this.

Words and grammar

1. targeted (verb (past participle)): To be the object of an attack or criticism; something that is aimed at or affected.
2. sabotage (noun): The act of secretly damaging or destroying equipment, property, or plans.
3. chief suspect (noun phrase): The most important person who police think might have committed a crime.
4. campaign (noun): A series of planned actions done over a period of time to achieve a special goal.
5. incidents (noun): Events or occurrences, especially ones that are unusual, unpleasant, or cause problems.

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense (Regular Verbs)
We use the Simple Past tense to talk about actions that started and finished in the past. For many verbs, we add '-ed' to the end to make them past tense. For example, 'blame' becomes 'blamed'.
Example from the text: "Germany said Russia attacked Leipzig airport."

📅 September 03, 2026 [2 days, 10 hours old] - El Niño: A Big Weather Event
The UN and experts say a big natural weather event called El Niño might cause problems around the world.

Beginner ESL: Global weather changes and economic impact

Before you read

Look at the title. What do you think you will learn?

Read the story

The UN warns about El Niño. El Niño is a natural weather phenomenon. It means big changes in weather on Earth. Experts say this El Niño is 'supersized', so it will be very strong. Many countries are getting ready for it. The World Meteorological Organization (WMO) also gave a warning. El Niño could bring big problems. These problems are called disruption. The disruption might affect global economies. Countries need to prepare for its impact, meaning its effects.

Words and grammar

1. natural weather phenomenon (noun phrase): A weather event that happens in nature, not caused by people.
2. warns (verb): To tell someone about a possible danger or problem so they can be careful.
3. disruption (noun): A problem that stops something from continuing as usual; a big disturbance.
4. global economies (noun phrase): How money and trade work in all countries around the world.
5. impact (noun): The strong effect or influence that something has on another thing.

Grammar Focus: Using 'could' for possibility
We use the word 'could' to talk about things that are possible in the future, but not certain. It means something might happen. For example, 'It could rain tomorrow' means rain is possible, but not 100% sure.
Example from the text: "El Niño could bring big problems."

📅 September 01, 2026 [4 days, 9 hours old] - People Forced to Return to Afghanistan: A Difficult New Start
Many people are forced to leave Pakistan and Iran and return to Afghanistan, facing challenges to start their lives again.

Beginner ESL: Forced returns to Afghanistan under the Taliban

Before you read

Look at the title. What do you think you will learn?

Read the story

Many people are leaving Pakistan and Iran. They are forced to return to Afghanistan. These people are called "deportees." Six million people have been forced to go back. This year, one million more people returned. They must start over in Afghanistan. The Taliban group controls Afghanistan now. Some deportees say, "I've never been to Afghanistan." They feel like strangers in their own country. This situation is very difficult for them.

Words and grammar

1. forced (verb): To make someone do something they do not want to do.
2. deportees (noun): People who are made to leave a country by the government.
3. return (verb): To go back to a place where you were before.
4. start over (verb phrase): To begin something again from the beginning, usually after a problem or change.
5. controls (verb): To have power over a place, a group of people, or a situation.

Grammar Focus: Simple Present Tense
We use the simple present tense to talk about things that are always true, facts, or habits. We also use it to talk about situations happening now or regularly.
Example from the text: "The Taliban group controls Afghanistan now."

📅 August 30, 2026 [6 days, 9 hours old] - Iceland Votes Against EU Talks
Iceland decided not to restart discussions about joining the European Union.

Beginner ESL: Iceland and the European Union

Before you read

Look at the title. What do you think you will learn?

Read the story

Iceland had a special event. People voted about joining the EU. The EU is the European Union. Many countries are members of the EU. The government wanted to restart talks. But most people said "no" to this. About 52.8% of voters rejected the idea. They voted against resuming discussions. A broadcaster called RUV reported this news. So, Iceland will not talk about joining the EU now.

Words and grammar

1. voted (verb): To choose something in an election or meeting by casting a ballot.
2. joining (verb): Becoming a member of a group or organization, like a club or a union.
3. EU (noun): The European Union, a group of many European countries that work together.
4. rejected (verb): To refuse to accept something; to say 'no' to an idea or a proposal.
5. restart (verb): To begin something again after it has stopped, like starting a game over.

Grammar Focus: The Simple Past Tense
We use the simple past tense to talk about actions that finished in the past. For regular verbs, we often add '-ed' to the base form. For irregular verbs, the form changes completely. It tells us something happened at a specific time before now.
Example from the text: "About 52.8% of voters rejected the idea."
