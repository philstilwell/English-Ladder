01Clarifying comfort complaints and thermostat readingsAsk which number is being reported · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Separate a control target, a temperature observation, and a comfort complaint.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

An office manager says the meeting room is 22 degrees Celsius. The number is the thermostat setpoint, not a confirmed ambient-temperature reading. Staff report feeling warm there after 14:00. No cause or equipment fault has been established.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

thermostat
A device that senses temperature and requests heating or cooling according to its configured controls.
setpoint
The target value requested by a control, distinct from a measured condition.
ambient temperature
The temperature of the surrounding air at an identified place and time.
thermal zone
An area treated as a unit for heating or cooling control or assessment.

Useful word combinations

  • identify the occupied zone: name the area where people report discomfort
  • distinguish target from reading: separate a requested value from a measured one
  • record the complaint pattern: note when and where the concern occurs
  • clarify the display label: establish what a displayed number represents

C Notice the language

Ask which number is being reported

A thermostat display may show different values. Ask what the existing label says rather than directing control changes. Comfort descriptions are useful information, but they do not by themselves identify a system fault.

  • Does that display show ___ or ___?
  • The complaint concerns ___ after ___ .
  • The target is ___; the measured condition remains ___.

Improve this: "The room is twenty-two because that's the setting."

See a clearer version

Twenty-two is the setpoint; the actual room temperature has not been confirmed.

The revision avoids treating a requested value as a measurement.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Manager: The label says target, not room temperature.
Technician: Then 22 degrees is the ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. setpoint

  1. A. supply-air reading A supply-air reading concerns delivered air rather than the control target.
  2. B. setpoint Correct. Setpoint identifies the target requested by the control.
  3. C. ambient temperature Ambient temperature would describe surrounding air measured at a location.
  4. D. temperature difference A temperature difference compares two values, not one target.
2. Dispatcher: When do staff report discomfort?
Technician: Their report concerns the meeting room ____ two in the afternoon.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. after

  1. A. after Correct. The report begins after the stated time.
  2. B. by 'By' would establish a latest time, not the reported period.
  3. C. until 'Until' would reverse the reported timing relationship.
  4. D. throughout 'Throughout' needs a period, not this single time expression.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Twenty-two is the target

A technician clarifies the fictional meeting-room complaint with the office manager.

  1. Technician: Your request says the room is twenty-two degrees. Which display value is that?
  2. Office manager: The thermostat says target twenty-two Celsius. Staff still feel warm after two.
  3. Technician: Then twenty-two is the setpoint, not a confirmed ambient-temperature reading in the room.
  4. Office manager: I hadn't distinguished those. Does that make the staff complaint less useful?
  5. Technician: No, its location and timing are useful. We'll record the meeting-room thermal zone.
  6. Office manager: Should I report a failed cooling system because the target isn't what people feel?
  7. Technician: The complaint doesn't establish that cause. We need the relevant assessment and measurements.
  8. Office manager: I'll describe feeling warm after two, with the target clearly labeled as a setpoint.
  9. Technician: Please include whether that pattern comes from your observation or reports from colleagues.
  10. Office manager: It's from the afternoon meeting team. I'll attribute it to them in the request.
2. Warm at one desk, not the whole floor

A technician checks a comfort report with a facilities contact.

  1. Technician: The request says the floor is too warm. Which occupied area prompted it?
  2. Facilities contact: Three staff near the west windows reported discomfort during yesterday's afternoon meeting period.
  3. Technician: Have other areas reported the same concern, or is that not yet known?
  4. Facilities contact: We haven't gathered other reports. I shouldn't describe the whole floor as affected.
  5. Technician: Let's identify those desks and the time without assuming the same thermal zone everywhere.
  6. Facilities contact: I can provide the seating plan, but I don't know the actual zone boundaries.
  7. Technician: That's useful background. The system records can establish how the areas are grouped.
  8. Facilities contact: Should I move people around to test whether the complaint follows their seats?
  9. Technician: No experiment is needed for this conversation. Please provide their existing accounts only.
  10. Facilities contact: I'll send those accounts and the plan, keeping the wider-floor condition unconfirmed.
3. The guest says stuffy

A technician clarifies a hotel's comfort wording with its duty manager.

  1. Technician: The guest described the room as stuffy. What did they mean by that?
  2. Duty manager: They mentioned feeling warm and a noticeable odor, not a specific temperature reading.
  3. Technician: Those are distinct reports. Let's retain both without translating stuffy into one technical diagnosis.
  4. Duty manager: The booking form only has a cooling-problem category. Is that too narrow?
  5. Technician: It can route the request, but the description should preserve the guest's actual concerns.
  6. Duty manager: I'll include the room, evening timing, warmth, and odor, with no confirmed cause.
  7. Technician: Please note whether the guest reported this once or on several evenings.
  8. Duty manager: They mentioned two evenings, but I don't have dates for both yet.
  9. Technician: Record that uncertainty rather than inventing dates; the history can be clarified at the visit.
  10. Duty manager: I'll arrange room access and keep the report separate from any proposed remedy.

Scenario 2 · Celsius or Fahrenheit?

A hotel report for Room 215 records a displayed target of 72 without units. The guest's existing photo shows degrees Fahrenheit and labels the value target, with no actual room-temperature reading. The service record otherwise uses Celsius.

Role A: Technician: clarify units and target versus measurement without adjusting controls.

Role B: Duty manager: provide the existing photo and the room number.

Possible opening: “Does the photo identify the units and whether this is the target or room reading?”

Success checks

  • Preserve target status.
  • Keep units explicit when relaying the number.

The manager wants the unit label omitted because everyone will know.

02Identifying equipment and linked componentsVerify the asset rather than trusting a similar label · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Resolve equipment identity using existing records and clear references.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A school request names AC-4, but the roof plan labels outdoor unit CU-4 and the indoor equipment register labels air handler AH-4. Their relationship is not confirmed. The facilities manager has the equipment register; learners must not access a roof or inspect equipment to obtain information.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

air handler
Equipment that conditions or circulates air through a building system using components such as fans and coils.
condensing unit
An assembly containing components that reject heat as refrigerant condenses, commonly located separately from the indoor equipment.
evaporator
A component where refrigerant absorbs heat as it evaporates.
asset tag
A site-assigned identifier linking a particular equipment item to its location and records.

Useful word combinations

  • read back the asset tag: repeat the site's equipment identifier
  • match the indoor and outdoor records: verify the documented component relationship
  • distinguish model from serial number: separate a product type from an individual unit identifier
  • locate the service history: find records for the identified equipment

C Notice the language

Verify the asset rather than trusting a similar label

Site asset tags, manufacturer model numbers, and serial numbers identify different things. Similar suffixes do not prove components are paired. Work from supplied records; do not ask learners to access plant, roofs, or enclosed machinery.

  • The request says ___; the register identifies ___ .
  • Is that an asset tag or a ___?
  • The component relationship remains ___.

Improve this: "Four goes with four."

See a clearer version

Matching tag numbers suggest a possible link but do not verify the component relationship.

The revision keeps a convenient naming pattern from becoming a technical finding.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Manager: AH-4 is our site's label, not the manufacturer's model.
Technician: Then AH-4 is the ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. asset tag

  1. A. asset tag Correct. An asset tag is the site's equipment identifier.
  2. B. model number A model number identifies a manufacturer product type.
  3. C. serial number A serial number identifies an individual manufactured unit.
  4. D. refrigerant designation A refrigerant designation identifies the working fluid type, not this site label.
2. Planner: Do CU-4 and AH-4 definitely form a pair?
Technician: Their matching numbers ____ that relationship.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. do not establish

  1. A. have already verified No verification is supplied in the case.
  2. B. are proof of The naming similarity is not proof of the relationship.
  3. C. have certified Certification is not established by matching labels.
  4. D. do not establish Correct. Similar identifiers do not confirm the actual pairing.

1. Which number belongs to which unit?

A technician reviews the school's AC-4 request with the facilities manager.

  1. Technician: Your request names AC-4, but the register uses AH-4 and CU-4 separately.
  2. Facilities manager: AH-4 is the air handler. CU-4 is listed as the outdoor condensing unit.
  3. Technician: Does the register explicitly link them, or are you matching their final numbers?
  4. Facilities manager: I'm matching the numbers. I don't see a documented relationship on this page.
  5. Technician: Then let's keep the pairing unverified and request the relevant system record.
  6. Facilities manager: I can obtain that from the building archive without anyone visiting the roof.

03Explaining airflow and distribution reportsUse direction and measurement context · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Explain air-distribution terms without inferring performance from a label.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A client calls both ceiling openings air-conditioning outlets. The current drawing identifies S3 as supply air and R2 as return air. The client reports discomfort below S3 but provides no airflow measurements. The technician must explain the labels without diagnosing poor performance or prescribing adjustments.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

supply air
Air delivered by a system into the space it serves.
return air
Air drawn from a served space back toward the system for recirculation or treatment.
airflow
The quantity of air moving through a point or system over time, stated with its measurement basis.
static pressure
Pressure exerted by air in a duct apart from its velocity pressure, stated relative to a reference and interpreted with the measurement location and conditions.

Useful word combinations

  • distinguish supply from return: identify air delivered to and drawn from a space
  • compare reported readings: use matching units, locations, and conditions
  • describe uneven distribution: state where the comfort difference is reported
  • request the balancing report: obtain the record of specified air-distribution assessment

C Notice the language

Use direction and measurement context

Supply and return describe direction and function. Airflow and static pressure are different quantities requiring stated measurement context. Do not teach damper adjustment, testing steps, or conclusions from invented limits.

  • ___ delivers air; ___ draws air back.
  • The report gives ___, not ___ .
  • A valid comparison needs the same ___.

Improve this: "Both openings blow cold air."

See a clearer version

The drawing identifies S3 as supply and R2 as return; their functions differ.

The correction uses the documented functions rather than treating every opening alike.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Client: Which label means air drawn back from the room?
Technician: That is ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. return air

  1. A. supply air Supply air is delivered into the room.
  2. B. outdoor air intake Outdoor air intake draws air from outside rather than identifying return from this room.
  3. C. discharge air to outside Discharge to outside is a different direction and destination.
  4. D. return air Correct. Return air is drawn back from the served room.
2. Reviewer: Can we compare these values directly?
Technician: Only if the units and measurement conditions are ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. comparable

  1. A. comparison The noun does not describe the conditions grammatically here.
  2. B. compare The base verb does not form the required adjective phrase.
  3. C. comparable Correct. 'Comparable' describes conditions suitable for a fair comparison.
  4. D. interchangeably The adverb does not fit after 'are' as the required description.

Your goal

Explain why matching numbers or component labels do not establish equivalent systems.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Two fictional proposals both say 5 kilowatts, but proposal A labels that cooling capacity and proposal B labels it electrical input. The client calls them equal-size systems. Full data sheets and equipment pairings are not supplied. No selection is approved.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

nameplate
The manufacturer's equipment label showing identification and specified ratings or other product information.
cooling capacity
The rate at which equipment is rated to remove heat under stated conditions.
refrigerant designation
The standardized identifier for the working fluid specified for a refrigeration system or component.
matched system
A combination of equipment components identified as compatible for the specified application and rating basis.

Useful word combinations

  • check the complete rating: include the stated conditions and units
  • verify the equipment pairing: confirm the specified indoor and outdoor combination
  • compare capacity on the same basis: avoid unlike rating conditions
  • confirm the refrigerant reference: identify the documented fluid designation without handling it

C Notice the language

Compare like quantities and complete combinations

Capacity and electrical input can use the same unit while describing different quantities. A refrigerant designation is an identifier, not a handling instruction or proof of interchangeability. Use complete product information for specialist review.

  • Both numbers use ___, but one describes ___ .
  • The rating applies under ___ .
  • Compatibility of the proposed combination remains ___.

Improve this: "They are both five kilowatts, so they're equivalent."

See a clearer version

One figure is cooling capacity and the other electrical input; equivalent capacity is not established.

The correction distinguishes quantities before comparing values.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Client: Which figure describes the rate of heat removal?
Technician: That is the stated ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. cooling capacity

  1. A. refrigerant charge quantity Charge quantity is an amount of refrigerant, not a heat-removal rate.
  2. B. airflow rate Airflow rate describes moving air quantity rather than thermal capacity directly.
  3. C. cooling capacity Correct. Cooling capacity describes the rate of heat removal under stated conditions.
  4. D. electrical input Electrical input describes consumed electrical power rather than heat removal capacity.
2. Buyer: Which term names the relationship between the proposed indoor and outdoor components?
Technician: That is their ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. equipment pairing

  1. A. electrical-input rating Electrical input describes a power quantity rather than the paired component relationship.
  2. B. equipment pairing Correct. Equipment pairing identifies the component combination requiring compatibility verification.
  3. C. cooling-capacity rating Cooling capacity describes a performance quantity, not the component relationship.
  4. D. refrigerant designation The refrigerant designation identifies a fluid, not the complete equipment combination.

Your goal

Distinguish a new report, an earlier finding, and a tentative diagnosis.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A client reports water near air handler AH-6 at 15:00 yesterday. Today's supplied visit note says the nearby floor is dry and the source was not identified. An earlier log mentions a drain-pan concern but does not establish a link. No learner is asked to inspect equipment.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

condensate
Liquid water formed when moisture in air condenses, commonly associated with cooling equipment.
drain pan
A component intended to collect condensate or other specified drainage beneath equipment.
service log
A dated record of equipment visits, reported symptoms, observations, and work performed.
provisional diagnosis
A tentative explanation of a heating, cooling, or refrigeration issue that remains subject to confirmation.

Useful word combinations

  • describe the observed moisture: state its location and time without naming a source
  • preserve the visit limits: retain what the technician did and did not establish
  • link the service entry: connect a report to the correct equipment and date
  • qualify the suspected cause: label a possible explanation as unconfirmed

C Notice the language

Use the service log without assuming recurrence

A previous drain-pan concern does not prove a new wet-floor report shares its cause. Condensate is a type of moisture, not a diagnosis of every puddle. Discuss supplied observations without teaching investigation or repair.

  • The earlier entry records ___, while today's note says ___ .
  • That may be relevant, but it doesn't establish ___ .
  • The diagnosis remains provisional because ___.

Improve this: "It's the same drain-pan fault again."

See a clearer version

The earlier log mentions the pan; the current moisture source is not confirmed.

The revision links the history without inventing a repeated cause.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Client: Has the cause been confirmed?
Technician: No, the explanation remains a ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. provisional diagnosis

  1. A. verified recurrence The history does not verify that the same cause recurred.
  2. B. provisional diagnosis Correct. Provisional identifies an explanation still awaiting confirmation.
  3. C. confirmed diagnosis A confirmed diagnosis would overstate the supplied evidence.
  4. D. completed repair finding No completed repair finding is provided here.
2. Reviewer: What does today's note establish?
Technician: The nearby floor was ____ during today's visit.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. dry

  1. A. dry Correct. Dry is the observed condition recorded in the note.
  2. B. leaking continuously Continuous leakage contradicts the supplied observation.
  3. C. repaired A dry observation does not establish a repair.
  4. D. the confirmed source The source was expressly not identified.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Yesterday's water and today's dry floor

A technician explains the supplied AH-6 notes to the client.

  1. Technician: Today's note records a dry floor near AH-6, not a confirmed resolution of yesterday's report.
  2. Client: I noticed water there at three. The old service log mentions the drain pan.

  4. Client: Could it have been condensate from the equipment, rather than another source of water?

A Read the situation

A moment at work

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

commissioning report
A record of specified verification activities, results, and limitations associated with confirming intended system performance.
maintenance interval
The stated period or condition between maintenance activities for particular equipment or tasks.
warranty exclusion
A condition or circumstance expressly outside the coverage described in a warranty document.
outstanding item
A specific unresolved work, record, or review requirement retained for follow-up.

Useful word combinations

  • match the manual to the equipment: verify the document reference against the supplied unit
  • separate completed from deferred: identify work done and work still outstanding
  • confirm the maintenance basis: identify the source of the stated interval
  • transfer the follow-up: agree who owns the unresolved item and next update

C Notice the language

Explain the remaining obligations precisely

A report may contain completed activities and explicit limitations. Match maintenance information to the actual equipment and its stated source. Do not invent intervals, interpret warranty rights, or turn a status call into a service booking.

  • The report is received, but ___ is deferred.
  • The maintenance information applies to ___, not ___ .
  • ___ owns ___; the separate visit remains ___.
