📅 November 17, 2026 - Robert Pattinson confirmed as the next James Bond
Robert Pattinson will become the next James Bond, with a new film expected in 2028 and some details still unconfirmed.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Robert Pattinson, known for Twilight and The Batman, has been confirmed as the next actor to play James Bond. Amazon MGM Studios shared the news on social media on Monday, alongside a picture of Pattinson as 007. At 40, Pattinson becomes the seventh actor to take the role of the famous fictional spy. The 26th Bond film is expected to be released in 2028. Denis Villeneuve will direct the film, while Steven Knight will write it; its title and exact release date remain unknown. The choice follows years of speculation about who would take over from Daniel Craig in this closely watched role. Amazon MGM chose a household name, selecting a star widely seen as a reliable choice who could bring financial success. Pattinson became famous as Cedric Diggory in Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire in 2005. Together, his five Twilight films earned more than $3 billion worldwide at the box office, meaning income from cinema ticket sales. He is also respected in the film industry for taking roles in smaller, independent and experimental films. Reports suggested he had agreed to make three Bond films, but this deal has not been officially confirmed. The film will also be the first Bond release since Amazon took creative control of the series.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. confirmed (verb, past participle): Officially stated that something is true or decided.
2. speculation (noun, uncountable): Discussion or guesses about something when the facts are not yet known.
3. take over (phrasal verb): To begin doing a job or holding a position that another person had.
4. household name (noun phrase): A person or thing that is familiar to many people.
5. box office (noun phrase): The money a film earns from cinema ticket sales.
6. industry (noun): The businesses and people involved in a particular type of work, such as making films.
7. independent (adjective): Made outside the control of the main large film companies.
8. deal (noun): An agreement about work or business.

Grammar Focus: Present perfect passive
Use “has” or “have” + “been” + a past participle for the present perfect passive. It reports a completed action with a present result and focuses on who or what receives the action. The person or group doing it can be left out.
Example from the text: "Robert Pattinson, known for Twilight and The Batman, has been confirmed as the next actor to play James Bond."

📅 November 16, 2026 - Four Planets Line Up Before Sunrise in November
Learn how to find November’s four morning planets and prepare for a better view.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Mercury, Venus, Mars and Jupiter will form a line in the sky before sunrise throughout November. All four planets will be visible to the naked eye, so viewers will not need a telescope to see them. Mercury will be the hardest of the four planets to spot. It will look dim and remain near the horizon, the line where the sky appears to meet the ground. Venus will appear above Mercury and to its right. Venus, which reflects a high proportion of sunlight, is the brightest object in the night sky after the Moon. On 15 and 16 November, Mars and Jupiter will appear very near each other in the sky. According to astronomer Gideon Bendavid-Walker, the gap between them will be roughly the width of a finger held at arm’s length. The pair will appear near the star Regulus, and the three objects may look like a group of three stars. Viewers should choose a place away from light pollution, meaning artificial light that makes the night sky harder to see. They should find an open view of the eastern horizon and allow around twenty minutes for their eyes to adjust to the dark. The best viewing time is approximately 45 to 60 minutes before sunrise.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. throughout (preposition): During the whole period of time mentioned.
2. visible to the naked eye (adjective phrase): Possible to see without using a telescope or similar equipment.
3. reflects (verb, present simple): Sends light back from a surface.
4. proportion (noun): A part of a total amount, considered in relation to the whole.
5. gap (noun): The space between two things.
6. roughly (adverb): Close to a particular amount or size, but not exactly.
7. light pollution (noun phrase): Artificial light that makes objects in the night sky harder to see.
8. adjust to (verb phrase): Become used to a different condition or situation.

Grammar Focus: Relative clauses that add extra information
Use “which” to add extra information about a thing already identified, such as a planet. Put commas around this relative clause. The main sentence still makes sense if you remove the extra information.
Example from the text: "Venus, which reflects a high proportion of sunlight, is the brightest object in the night sky after the Moon."

This apparent rise came during a period when springs and summers were warmer and drier than average. In cold, wet weather or when food is scarce, dormice can enter torpor, a short period of deep sleep that saves energy. Researchers think warmer summers may mean less time in torpor and leave dormice with more energy to feed and breed. The animals may also have a better chance to build up fat reserves before winter. One ecologist who worked on the survey says it is still unclear how dormice will respond to warmer, wetter winters. People working to save wildlife say looking after the woodlands and hedgerows where dormice live is essential to their long-term survival.

4. scarce (adjective): Hard to find or available only in small amounts.

7. respond to (verb phrase): To react or change when something happens.

Near the peak, the Moon will be below the horizon around midnight, so its light is less likely to spoil the view.
