📅 November 14, 2026 - Entertainment's Reckoning and the Limits of Cultural Change
Recent entertainment scandals reflect years of investigative work and changing attitudes, while exposing persistent barriers to reporting abuse and securing institutional change.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Recent entertainment scandals may look like a sudden reckoning, but the article argues that they reflect changes set in motion years earlier. The revelations emerged from journalistic inquiries that had been under way for years. Such inquiries typically proceed through an initial tip-off, evidence-gathering, corroboration and requests for responses before publication. The article reports that entertainer Russell Brand was jailed for rape and sexual assault. It also reports that Married at First Sight UK was cancelled following rape allegations involving the programme. Walliams and the men accused on Married at First Sight UK deny the allegations against them. The article traces much of the impetus for women to disclose their experiences to the revelations about Harvey Weinstein and the #MeToo movement. Seeing action taken can encourage further disclosures by challenging the assumption that reporting abuse achieves nothing. Unions say that precarious incomes can nevertheless make it difficult for junior freelance workers to raise concerns. Harriet Wistrich of the Centre for Women's Justice says wealthy or famous people often use legal measures to deter alleged victims from speaking out. A High Court judge rejected Walliams's request for an injunction to prevent him from being named. According to the article, ministers have criticised non-disclosure agreements and called for tighter restrictions on their use. The article reports that a review found no breach of Ofcom's broadcasting code in Channel 4 and production company CPL's handling of the issues raised. What is increasingly recognised is that senior media executives must help root out abuse, rather than leaving the burden entirely with victims. Philippa Childs argues that existing internal reporting mechanisms do not adequately serve their purpose. Wark warns that abuse cannot be dismissed as a legacy of an earlier era and calls for changes within the industry today. The article ends by asking whether these events could mark a watershed moment for the industry.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. reckoning (countable noun): A period when people or institutions face serious examination of wrongdoing and pressure to answer for it.
2. corroboration (uncountable noun): The process of checking whether additional information independently supports an account or claim.
3. impetus (uncountable noun): An influence that encourages action or helps a development gather strength.
4. precarious (adjective): Uncertain and insufficiently secure, leaving someone vulnerable to an unwelcome change.
5. deter (verb): To discourage someone from acting by making possible difficulties or consequences seem threatening.
6. injunction (countable noun): A court order requiring someone to do or stop doing something, here sought to prevent public identification.
7. non-disclosure agreements (plural noun phrase): Legal agreements requiring people to keep specified information confidential.
8. root out (phrasal verb): To find and eliminate something harmful that has become established within an organisation or system.
9. legacy (countable noun): A situation or influence that continues because of events or practices from an earlier period.
10. watershed moment (countable noun phrase): An event or period marking a decisive change in how a situation develops.

Advanced Grammar: What-clefts for emphasis
A “what”-cleft divides a message into a “what”-clause followed by “be” and the information being emphasised. Here, “What is increasingly recognised” introduces growing awareness, while the following “that”-clause foregrounds the duty assigned to senior executives.
Example from the text: "What is increasingly recognised is that senior media executives must help root out abuse, rather than leaving the burden entirely with victims."

📅 November 13, 2026 - Nvidia Leadership Turmoil in an Unverified Account
Examine an unverified supplied account of a reported Nvidia leadership crisis, distinguishing institutional concerns, provisional assessments and established conclusions.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The supplied account reports the abrupt departure of Nvidia’s chief executive, portraying it as a severe reversal for the AI chipmaking giant. It links the reported crisis to concerns about professional boundaries and possible national security risks. According to the account, Nvidia has come under scrutiny from US lawmakers seeking answers about a possible connection to Chinese intelligence. The text reports that US Attorney General Todd Blanche said the Department of Justice had not yet drawn any conclusions about whether Alice Huang had engaged in espionage for Beijing. It also says several lawmakers demanded that the departing executive brief Congress on the matter. Should the executive refuse to appear voluntarily, he could be subpoenaed, meaning formally ordered to attend, according to a Senate staff member cited in the account. The account says Nvidia saw no reason to believe national security or company secrets had been compromised, while pledging that an independent investigation would consider all available information. It reports that the executive had initially been expected to weather the storm while the company investigated whether he had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort. An employee cited in the text says initial reports about the executive’s private conduct were largely brushed off by rank-and-file workers. The account notes that many employees had earned millions through their ability to cash out company stock as its value soared. It describes a succession process in which Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress took temporary charge while Nvidia sought a permanent replacement. According to the supplied figures, Nvidia’s value had risen by more than 470% over five years and was now down by 31% against a backdrop of wider uncertainty in the technology sector. The account says Nvidia represents about 7% of the S&P 500 index, which is available through a popular index fund used in US retirement savings accounts. It explains that those retirement account balances fluctuate with stock market movements.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. professional boundaries (plural noun phrase): Limits on workplace conduct intended to prevent personal relationships from interfering with professional responsibilities.
2. under scrutiny (prepositional phrase): Being examined closely, especially because conduct or decisions have raised concerns.
3. brief Congress (verb phrase): Give members of Congress a focused account of relevant information about a particular matter.
4. subpoenaed (verb, past participle): Formally required by a legal order to appear or provide evidence; here, required to attend.
5. weather the storm (idiomatic verb phrase): Survive a period of serious difficulty without losing one’s position or ability to continue.
6. foreign intelligence effort (noun phrase): An organised attempt by another country’s intelligence services to obtain information or exert influence.
7. brushed off (phrasal verb, past participle): Dismissed as insufficiently important to deserve serious attention, without necessarily being disproved.
8. rank-and-file (adjective): Describing ordinary members of an organisation, as distinct from those in senior positions.
9. cash out (phrasal verb): Convert an investment into money by selling it, thereby realising its current value.
10. succession (noun): The process through which one person replaces another in a position of authority.
11. against a backdrop of (prepositional phrase): Within a wider situation that provides relevant context without necessarily establishing a direct cause.

Advanced Grammar: Inverted future conditional with “should”
In formal future conditionals, place “should” before the subject and use the base verb instead of an “if”-clause. This structure presents a possible condition; “could” in the main clause makes the consequence possible rather than inevitable.
Example from the text: "Should the executive refuse to appear voluntarily, he could be subpoenaed, meaning formally ordered to attend, according to a Senate staff member cited in the account."

Read the story

A review commissioned by the government is expected to recommend a radical overhaul that could reduce some young people's welfare entitlements. According to the Times, Alan Milburn's report will recommend moving under-25s off the main sickness and disability benefits. The government has tasked Milburn, a former Labour health secretary, with helping reduce the proportion of 18- to 24-year-olds outside education, employment or training, reported as one in eight. The report is expected to propose ending under-25s' eligibility for the health element of universal credit and for personal independence payment, both forms of state support. A replacement allowance would match the basic universal credit rate. Some people could receive additional help with everyday expenses associated with their conditions. Those with more serious disabilities who are unable to work would receive a new payment at a higher rate. Milburn is also expected to recommend billions in employment support, including work placements, job subsidies and individual advisers. Milburn frames the central choice as whether to prioritise young people's longer-term life chances or their immediate financial circumstances. He has warned that the changes would involve difficult consequences for both existing and new recipients. The proposed approach would combine incentives with sanctions, since young people could lose some support if they did not engage with the additional employment services. Milburn says substantial spending would be needed to rebuild the system from scratch. Were Milburn's recommendations to be approved, monetary savings for the British state could still take almost a decade to emerge. A government spokesperson has promised a safety net for the most vulnerable and those unable to work. The government is expected to pursue legislation implementing the plans in the new year.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. commissioned (verb, past participle): Formally requested by an organisation or authority to investigate an issue or produce a report.
2. radical overhaul (noun phrase): A thorough redesign that changes how a system operates, rather than adjusting individual details.
3. entitlements (plural noun): Forms of support that people have a right to receive under the applicable rules.
4. subsidies (plural noun): Public funds used to cover part of a cost, here to encourage employment opportunities.
5. frames (verb, third-person singular present): Presents an issue in a particular way that directs attention towards certain choices or considerations.
6. incentives (plural noun): Opportunities or advantages intended to encourage a particular action.
7. sanctions (plural noun): Penalties for failing to meet requirements, here the possible loss of some welfare support.
8. from scratch (adverbial phrase): Starting again with the fundamental design rather than simply modifying existing arrangements.
9. monetary savings (plural noun phrase): Reductions in the amount of money spent, distinguished from other possible improvements.
10. safety net (noun phrase): Support intended to prevent vulnerable people from being left without essential help.

Advanced Grammar: Conditional inversion with “were” and a to-infinitive
Use “were + subject + to-infinitive” in place of “if + subject + were to + infinitive” for a formal hypothetical future condition. Here, approval remains uncertain, while “could” also qualifies the projected timescale for savings.

Read the story

Hazel dormouse numbers show tentative signs of recovery after decades of decline. The National Dormouse Monitoring Programme, led by the People’s Trust for Endangered Species, has monitored sites in England and Wales since 1998. Its data indicate that dormouse numbers have fallen by almost 60 per cent since 2000, equivalent to an average annual decline of 3.5 per cent. Although the monitoring data point to a possible increase of nearly 10 per cent between 2020 and 2025, researchers stress that there is considerable uncertainty around that estimate. The apparent upturn coincided with springs and summers that were warmer and drier than average. Hazel dormice typically hibernate from around October to May, spending the winter in nests on the ground. In spring and summer, cold, wet weather or a shortage of food can prompt dormice to enter torpor, a short spell of deep sleep that conserves energy. Warmer summers may reduce the need for torpor and leave dormice with more energy for feeding and reproduction, researchers suggest. Ecologist Charlotte Armitage cautioned that it remains unclear how warmer, wetter winters might affect dormice. The researchers say the potential longer-term effects of climate change on the species warrant further investigation. Once widespread, the hazel dormouse is now largely confined to southern England and parts of Wales. Hazel dormice have become much rarer amid widespread woodland loss, and native populations have disappeared from around 20 English counties. Conservationists stress that safeguarding the woodlands and hedgerows where dormice live remains essential to their long-term survival.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

2. equivalent to (adjective followed by a preposition): Corresponding in value when expressed on another scale or in another form.
3. point to (prepositional verb): Suggest a conclusion on the basis of available evidence, leaving room for uncertainty.
4. upturn (noun): A shift towards improvement, especially a rise after a period of falling numbers or weaker results.
5. coincided with (prepositional verb in the past tense): Occurred during the same period as something else, with any causal relationship left open.
6. torpor (uncountable noun): A temporary state like deep sleep in which an animal becomes much less active and uses less energy.
7. conserves (verb, third-person singular present simple): Keeps more of a resource available by reducing how much is used.
8. warrant (verb): Provide sufficient reason to take an action or examine something more closely.
9. confined to (past participle followed by the preposition ‘to’): Restricted in range to a particular area or set of places.
10. safeguarding (verb in the “-ing” form): Taking steps to prevent damage or loss.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

2. circuit breaker (noun phrase): A market safeguard that temporarily halts trading when prices move sharply.
3. curb (verb): To limit the intensity or spread of something seen as harmful or excessive.
4. ballot measures (plural noun phrase): Specific policy proposals put directly to voters for a decision.
5. moratorium (noun): An official pause in an activity, intended to be temporary.
6. prospect (noun): The possibility of a future event that people are taking into account now.
7. yield (verb): To produce a result or benefit, such as a financial gain from an investment.
8. counted on (phrasal verb, past participle form): Expected something to happen or be available and relied on that expectation.
9. scrutiny (noun): Close examination to assess whether something is justified or what effects it might have.
10. backlash (noun): A strong negative reaction to a decision, change or development.

📅 November 05, 2026 - Bank of England rate rise expected amid inflation concerns
An anticipated Bank of England rate increase exposes a difficult balance between inflation and weakening growth, with differing consequences for mortgage borrowers and savers.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The Bank of England is expected to increase its benchmark rate on Thursday, potentially marking its first rise in more than three years. Most analysts anticipate a move from 3.75% to 4%, which would make borrowing more expensive while potentially benefiting savers. The rate has declined from its 2023–24 peak of 5.25%, but policymakers have held it steady for six consecutive meetings. Analysts suggest that inflationary pressure, stemming primarily from petrol and diesel prices, will prompt the increase. One assessment quoted in the report highlights a trade-off between persistently high inflation and sharply weaker growth prospects. The Consumer Prices Index measure of inflation remains well below its earlier peak, although it has begun rising again and is expected to climb further. The reported figure for the year to September 2026 was 3.5%, compared with 3.1% a month earlier and the central bank's 2% target. The effects of the Iran war are filtering through to UK inflation, principally via energy and fuel costs. Were the Bank rate to increase, the 510,000 tracker mortgage-holders would be affected immediately. UK Finance calculates that a quarter-percentage-point increase would add £31 to monthly payments on a typical tracker mortgage. For the 470,000 borrowers on variable mortgage deals, any change in payments would be contingent on their lender's response. Some borrowers are insulated from an immediate change in monthly payments, although lenders have already priced in the expected increase when offering new deals. Rachel Springall of Moneyfacts cautions that borrowers' choice ultimately depends on whether they can stomach further rate increases. The committee's indications of the future course of interest rates will also be closely scrutinised.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. benchmark rate (noun phrase): A central interest rate that serves as a reference for other lending and savings rates.
2. inflationary pressure (noun phrase): Conditions or forces that contribute to increases in the general level of prices.
3. trade-off (countable noun): A situation in which pursuing one desirable outcome involves accepting a disadvantage elsewhere.
4. growth prospects (plural noun phrase): The expected chances and extent of future economic expansion.
5. filtering through (phrasal verb, present participle): Gradually reaching another part of a system and producing effects there.
6. contingent on (adjective phrase): Dependent on a particular condition, event or decision.
7. insulated (past participle used as an adjective): Protected from a particular effect, without necessarily being protected from every related consequence.
8. priced in (phrasal verb, past participle): Already taken into account when setting a price or interest rate, before the anticipated event occurs.
9. stomach (verb): To tolerate something difficult or unpleasant; here, to accept the financial burden of further increases.
10. scrutinised (verb, past participle): Examined closely and critically, especially to identify details with wider significance.

Advanced Grammar: Conditional inversion with “were”
Use “were + subject + to-infinitive” to introduce a hypothetical future condition without “if”. This formal structure presents a possibility rather than a confirmed event; the result clause commonly uses “would + base verb”.
Example from the text: "Were the Bank rate to increase, the 510,000 tracker mortgage-holders would be affected immediately."

📅 November 02, 2026 - Oil Tank Vandalism Kills Birds at Broughshane Waterfowl Park
Examine how an oil spill at Broughshane Waterfowl Park connects vandalism with wildlife losses, an ongoing rescue effort and uncertainty about recovery.

Read the story

Vandalism of a nearby home heating oil tank caused a spill that killed 13 birds from Broughshane Waterfowl Park. More than 20 birds were taken to the USPCA's headquarters for emergency treatment, yet the death toll continued to rise. The attraction in County Antrim remains closed while work to remove oil from its pond continues. A volunteer arriving to feed the birds noticed a kerosene smell and found the black swans coated in oil. The park houses 120 birds in its collection as well as wild birds. Gary Hayward, chairman of Broughshane Environment Wildfowl and Wildlife Trust, described the incident as a nightmare. Hayward described the USPCA and the Northern Ireland Environment Agency as instrumental in treating birds and clearing the spill. Inspectors deployed oil-absorbing barriers and other countermeasures as mitigation, seeking to limit the spill's impact. The agency identified the substance as kerosene, a home heating oil, and traced its source to a nearby tank. Although the waterway has improved, further work will continue for as long as it is deemed necessary. Hayward hopes the incident will act as a deterrent to vandalism. Having suffered extensive contamination, the birds were bathed and treated as quickly as possible. The team administered charcoal to help counter some of the toxins the birds may have ingested. Hopes that the birds were past the worst were tempered by uncertainty over how much they may have ingested. The incident has also placed rescue staff under substantial emotional and mental pressure. Birds can conceal illness effectively, meaning that outward signs may become apparent only when it is already too late. Most rescued birds are non-native species, and their return is contingent on the pond being completely cleaned.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. death toll (noun phrase): The number of deaths resulting from a particular incident, often reported as that number changes.
2. instrumental (adjective): Making an important contribution to achieving something, without necessarily being solely responsible for it.
3. countermeasures (plural noun): Actions or equipment used to oppose a specific threat or reduce its harmful effects.
4. mitigation (uncountable noun): The reduction of harm or severity, without necessarily removing the problem entirely.
5. traced (verb, past tense): Established where something originated by investigating its source or route.
6. deterrent (countable noun): Something that discourages an action by making its possible consequences seem undesirable.
7. contamination (uncountable noun): The presence of an unwanted, potentially harmful substance on an animal or in an environment.
8. ingested (verb, past participle): Taken into the body by swallowing; a formal term that does not imply deliberate consumption.
9. tempered (verb, past participle): Made less strong or confident by a competing consideration, without being completely removed.
10. conceal (verb, base form): Keep something from being apparent or easily detected, without necessarily doing so consciously.
11. non-native species (plural noun phrase): Types of animals or plants that do not naturally occur in the region concerned.
12. contingent on (adjective phrase): Dependent on a condition being fulfilled; meeting that condition does not necessarily guarantee the outcome.

Advanced Grammar: Perfect participle clauses with a shared subject
A perfect participle clause uses “having” plus a past participle to present an event or experience as preceding the main event. Both clauses share a subject: here, the birds experienced contamination before they were bathed and treated.
Example from the text: "Having suffered extensive contamination, the birds were bathed and treated as quickly as possible."

📅 November 01, 2026 - Rediscovered Beetle Shows Signs of Reproducing in Wales
The discovery of beetle larvae in Pembrokeshire suggests renewed reproduction in Wales, while highlighting the species' demanding habitat requirements and the importance of continued conservation.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The short-necked oil beetle has been rediscovered in Pembrokeshire after an apparent absence of 60 years from Wales. Recent surveys had prompted fears of local extinction, meaning that the beetle might no longer exist in Wales. Across the UK, the species went unreported for almost 60 years after 1948, as habitat loss brought it to the brink of extinction. Last year's survey at Castlemartin recorded only dead adults, prompting concern about the species' persistence at the site. After three years of searching, experts discovered larvae, or immature beetles, in a small area of the site, suggesting that they had located part of a larger batch of young beetles. According to Natural Resources Wales, the number and distribution of the larvae corroborated this interpretation. Anne Hobbs, from the Bumblebee Conservation Trust, welcomed indications that the species was reproducing in Wales again. The beetle relies on solitary bees for its life cycle and requires both thick vegetation for cover and abundant flowers. Natural Resources Wales said these exacting habitat requirements leave only a few suitable sites in the UK. The species is classified as nationally rare in Great Britain and is listed as vulnerable on the IUCN Red List. The beetles are typically encountered between March and June in coastal grassland rich in flowers and in sand dunes, with activity greatest on warm, sunny days. Rediscoveries elsewhere in the UK included Devon in 2006 and the Isle of Coll in Scotland in 2009. Castlemartin Range remains the species' only known Welsh location and is subject to sustained monitoring by conservationists. The work forms part of Wales's flagship species recovery programme, which also seeks to conserve other species sharing similar habitats. Hobbs stressed that effective conservation must accommodate the needs of several species.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. apparent absence (noun phrase): A period when something seems to be missing, although its actual absence has not been established.
2. local extinction (noun phrase): The disappearance of a species from a particular area, even though it may continue to exist elsewhere.
3. brink of extinction (noun phrase): A situation in which a species is close to disappearing completely.
4. persistence (noun): The continued existence of a species in a location despite conditions that threaten it.
5. corroborated (verb, past tense): Provided additional support for an interpretation, making it more credible without necessarily proving it.
6. exacting (adjective): Demanding precise conditions that can be difficult to satisfy.
7. vulnerable (adjective): Assigned a conservation category indicating a significant risk of extinction.
8. sustained monitoring (noun phrase): Repeated observation maintained over time to track a situation or detect changes.
9. flagship (noun used attributively): Used to identify a particularly prominent programme that represents an organisation's wider work or priorities.
10. accommodate (verb): Take different needs into account and make provision for them.

Advanced Grammar: Supplementary participle clauses expressing inference
A supplementary “-ing” clause can express an inference from the preceding main clause. Here, “suggesting” presents an interpretation of the discovery without treating it as established fact; it comments on the finding as a whole.
Example from the text: "After three years of searching, experts discovered larvae, or immature beetles, in a small area of the site, suggesting that they had located part of a larger batch of young beetles."
