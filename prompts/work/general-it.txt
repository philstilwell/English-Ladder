Lesson 01: IT Service Language, Tickets, and Triage
Original fictional case: Ren reports that the finance system is down. Ren can sign in and view invoices, but exporting the current month produces an error. Two colleagues report the same export problem; other users and functions have not been checked. The billing team needs its file by 3 p.m. Asha owns service-desk triage and can escalate the ticket, but cannot promise a repair time.
Language workshop: "The system is down" means the export fails
Communication goal: Narrow an ambiguous incident report and agree on an evidence-based update.
Language guidance: Urgency concerns time pressure; impact concerns consequences and scope. Priority normally reflects an organization's agreed assessment, not just the strongest wording in a ticket. A response commitment is not a repair guarantee.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): I understand the export is blocking your billing file. / Which action fails after you sign in? / Can you still view the invoices on screen? / Who else has tried the same export and seen the error? / Please send the exact error text through the approved support channel. / Your billing deadline is 3 p.m.; the repair time is not yet known. / I will escalate the ticket and update you by noon. / Sign-in and viewing work; the current-month export fails for three reported users. / Has anyone verified that the alternative produces the required file? / I cannot confirm a restoration time before the technical assessment. / I am asking the application team to investigate this function. / Other functions and users have not yet been checked. / I will link the reports so the investigation has one shared record. / Which team is accepting the next investigation step? / We will confirm that the required export works before closing the incident. / The next message will include findings, outstanding questions, and the next owner.
Vocabulary: service desk: The contact point coordinating user support. / ticket: A recorded request or issue for tracking. / triage: Initial assessment of impact, priority, and ownership. / incident: An unplanned disruption or reduction in service. / service request: A request for a routine agreed service. / impact: The effect on users or business work. / urgency: How quickly action is needed in the circumstances. / priority: The relative order or importance assigned to work. / affected user: A person experiencing the reported problem. / scope: The functions, users, or locations included. / symptom: An observed sign of a problem. / error message: Text identifying or describing an error condition. / reproduce: Make the reported behavior occur again. / workaround: A temporary way to reduce impact without fixing the cause. / resolution: Restoration or completion according to the agreed criteria. / response time: Time until support responds under a defined measure. / restoration time: Time until a disrupted service is restored. / escalation: Referral for higher authority or specialist attention. / assignment group: The team designated to handle a ticket. / service owner: The role accountable for the service. / knowledge article: A maintained support reference for a known task or issue. / duplicate ticket: Another ticket reporting the same underlying incident. / status update: A communication of progress and remaining uncertainty. / closure criterion: A condition required before work is marked closed.
Speaking task: Check the dialogue. Read the confirmed boundary: Ren can sign in and view invoices, but current-month export fails for three reported users. Switch roles. Keep the 3 p.m. billing deadline separate from the unknown repair time; repeat the noon progress-update commitment. Complete the printer exchange. Preserve the untested alternative and do not promise restoration before the 2 p.m. meeting.
Partner: Asha: Service-desk analyst / Ren: Billing coordinator
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A user can edit and save documents but cannot print to one office printer. Another printer has not been tested. The user needs printed copies for a 2 p.m. meeting.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Ren: Could you read that back before you send it? I don't want the team checking invoice viewing when it's the export I need.
Asha: Certainly. Sign-in and viewing work for you; current-month export fails for three reported users. Other functions remain unchecked. Billing needs the file by three, and the assignment group is the application support team.
Ren: That captures it. Please include me on the noon update. Once they say it is fixed, I can verify the export and compare the file totals.
Asha: I will record that as the closure criterion. We will distinguish a reported fix from your confirmation that the required task works again.

Lesson 02: Networks, Connectivity, and Root-Cause Hypotheses
Original fictional case: The staff portal works from the office network but fails for four people connected through the virtual private network (VPN). The same portal address is used. Nobody has checked name resolution, routing, or access-policy logs for those requests. Eli says DNS must be the cause. Nia coordinates network investigation. The team has an approved diagnostic process and must not change production settings merely to test a guess.
Language workshop: The VPN pattern is a clue, not a DNS diagnosis
