Interested or interesting?

Describe a feeling and what causes it.

Level ranges are study guidance. Start with the examples and choose your own pace.

Try the practice →Learner workbook PDFTeaching guide PDF

FEELING

I am interested in photography.

This describes my feeling.

CAUSE

The photographer is interesting.

This person attracts my interest.

ACTIVITY

I am interested in learning more.

Use learning after in.

Understand

The useful distinction

For many pairs, the -ed adjective describes a feeling: bored, excited, interested. The -ing adjective describes what causes that feeling: boring, exciting, interesting.

People can cause feelings too. A person can be interesting, and a story can be exciting. Choose by meaning, not by whether the noun is a person or a thing. After interested in, use a noun or an -ing verb.

Try it

Check your understanding

Write an answer, then compare it with the explanation. Other wording may also be correct. These responses are for self-checking and are not automatically graded.

1. The long wait made me feel ___ . (bored / boring)

Compare your answer

bored

Describe the feeling of the person waiting.

2. Can a person be interesting?

Compare your answer

Yes. A person can cause interest.

The distinction is feeling versus cause, not person versus thing.

3. Correct this: I am interested in learn Spanish.

Compare your answer

I am interested in learning Spanish.

A verb after this preposition uses the -ing form.

Use it

Share your ideas

Describe an activity and how you feel about it.

See one possible response

The class is challenging, but I feel excited about learning.

Self-check: Does your sentence express your intended meaning? Does its form follow the relevant pattern? Can a reader understand the context?
