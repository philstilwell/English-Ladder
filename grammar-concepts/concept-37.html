Most and almost

Distinguish a majority from something nearly true.

Level ranges are study guidance. Start with the examples and choose your own pace.

Try the practice →Learner workbook PDFTeaching guide PDF

MAJORITY

Most students came.

More than half came; the precise number is unstated.

NEARLY EVERYONE

Almost all the students came.

Very few were absent.

NEARLY A STATE

The room is almost ready.

Some preparation remains.

Understand

The useful distinction

Most before a plural or uncountable noun means the majority or greatest part: most students, most water. Use most of before a determiner or pronoun: most of the students; most of them.

Almost means nearly: almost ready, almost all, almost everyone. It does not normally directly modify a plural noun to mean the majority: say most people or almost all people, depending on your meaning. Almost all is stronger than most.
