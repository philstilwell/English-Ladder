01Clarifying the service and the intended lengthCheck length before interpreting a service label · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Distinguish a client's visual preference from a booking name.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A client booked an overlay but calls it a full set and points to a long reference image. They actually want to keep their current short length. No work has begun. Clarify length and explain why the image does not automatically mean extensions.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

manicure
A cosmetic service for fingernails and hands, with the included elements set by the salon menu.
pedicure
A cosmetic service for toenails and feet, not medical treatment of foot conditions.
overlay
An enhancement product applied over the natural nail without deliberately adding length.
extension
An artificial addition that lengthens the nail beyond its natural length.

Useful word combinations

  • retain the natural length: keep the client's current nail length
  • clarify the booked service: check which service the appointment reserves
  • add extra length: make the finished nail longer than the natural nail
  • describe the desired result: explain the appearance wanted before choosing a service

C Notice the language

Check length before interpreting a service label

Terms such as full set can be used loosely. Ask about added length explicitly rather than treating a photograph as a complete instruction.

  • Do you want added length or ___?
  • Which part of the photo do you mean?
  • Your booking says ___; let's confirm ___.

Improve this: A full set means those long nails.

See a clearer version

Are you choosing the color, the added length, or both?

The question lets the client separate independent choices.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Client: What is an enhancement covering my existing natural nails without adding length called?
Technician: An ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. overlay

  1. A. embellishment An embellishment adds decoration rather than naming the overall covering.
  2. B. overlay Correct. An overlay covers the natural nail without deliberately adding length.
  3. C. extension An extension deliberately adds length beyond the natural nail.
  4. D. infill An infill maintains a grown-out enhancement rather than naming the initial full covering.
2. Client: I like the picture, but only its color.
Technician: Could you confirm ____?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. whether you want to keep your length

  1. A. whether you want to keep your length Correct. The indirect question uses normal statement order.
  2. B. whether do you want to keep your length Do not invert subject and verb after 'whether' here.
  3. C. whether you wants to keep your length 'You' takes 'want,' not 'wants.'
  4. D. whether want you to keep your length Place 'you' before 'want' in this embedded question.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Long reference, short request

A nail technician clarifies an overlay booking.

  1. Technician: Your booking says overlay, but your photograph shows added length. Which part do you want?
  2. Client: Only the pink color. I need to keep my nails short for work.
  3. Technician: Then let's separate the color from the extra length shown in the reference picture.
  4. Client: I called it a full set on the phone. Was that the wrong expression?
  5. Technician: People use that phrase differently. Asking about actual length gives us a clearer shared request.
  6. Client: So an overlay doesn't mean you automatically make the nails as long as the photograph?
  7. Technician: That's right: it goes over the natural nail without deliberately adding length to it.
  8. Client: That's what I meant. I don't want extensions, even if they appear in the photograph.
  9. Technician: I'll note pink color requested, existing short length retained, and no extensions in today's request.
  10. Client: Good. Please show me the color sample separately so the long image doesn't confuse us.
2. Hands or feet on the booking?

Reception checks an unclear online service selection.

  1. Receptionist: Your booking lists a manicure, but your message mentions choosing a color for your toes.
  2. Client: I may have selected the wrong service. I wanted a pedicure for my feet.
  3. Receptionist: Thank you for checking. A manicure is for hands; a pedicure concerns feet and toenails.
  4. Client: Could you simply change the word, or does the appointment need a different time?
  5. Receptionist: I need to check the appropriate appointment space before confirming a different service.
  6. Client: I can come later if necessary. I don't need my hands done this week.
  7. Receptionist: There is a pedicure slot at three. That replaces the manicure rather than adding both.
  8. Client: Three works. Could the confirmation say pedicure so I can check the change?
  9. Receptionist: Yes, I've changed the booking to a pedicure at three and removed the manicure.
  10. Client: The new message matches what I wanted. I'll choose the toe color when I arrive.
3. One longer nail does not define the whole request

A client brings a reference showing mixed nail lengths.

  1. Technician: Tell me which length you want overall, rather than relying only on this reference photograph.
  2. Client: Most of the nails look short, but the middle one looks longer than mine.
  3. Technician: Are you requesting that extra length too, or just asking about the difference in the picture?
  4. Client: I'm asking about the difference. I want all of mine at my present short length.
  5. Technician: Then we won't treat the longer pictured nail as a request for an extension.
  6. Client: Could I still discuss the same color and simple pattern on my shorter nails?
  7. Technician: Yes, we can discuss their appearance separately from length, then assess the actual service request.
  8. Client: I don't want the booking changed to extensions just because I showed this image.
  9. Technician: Understood. Your request stays short length, with the color and pattern still to be chosen.
  10. Client: That's accurate. Let's look at shorter samples before I decide which pattern I prefer.

F Say it

Two scenarios to practice

Scenario 1 · Clarifying the service and the intended length

A client booked an overlay but calls it a full set and points to a long reference image. They actually want to keep their current short length. No work has begun. Clarify length and explain why the image does not automatically mean extensions.

Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.

Partner's role and follow-up

Client: want the pictured pink color but no additional length; used full set without a precise meaning.

Try a harder second round

The client says overlay and extension sound interchangeable. Explain the length distinction plainly.

Scenario 2 · A gift booking names the wrong service

A friend booked a pedicure for a recipient who wants a manicure. A hand-service appointment is available tomorrow at eleven; today's pedicure slot cannot be converted.

Role A: Receptionist: clarify hands versus feet and offer the stated alternative.

Role B: Client: prefer tomorrow's manicure and want the friend informed without discussing personal details.

Possible opening: “The gift says pedicure, but I meant my hands.”

Success checks
  • Identify the requested service plainly.
  • Do not silently add another service.
  • Confirm tomorrow's manicure and removal of today's booking.
Add a complication

The client asks whether both services can fit into the original slot.

Studying alone? Speak both roles aloud. For each scenario, prepare for two minutes, speak for one minute, then answer a follow-up. Switch roles and repeat using fewer notes. Use only the supplied facts; identify missing information instead of inventing it.

Compare scenario 1 with a model response
You've booked an overlay, and you said you want to keep your current short length. The photograph shows added length, so let's separate its color from its shape. Are you asking for the pictured color on your existing length, or do you want extensions?

This is one possible spoken response, not the only acceptable wording. Preserve the case facts when you try your own version.

Notice: Distinguish a client's visual preference from a booking name. Identify the wording that does this, then name one detail from the case that the response preserves.

Apply it to this case

In “Clarifying the service and the intended length”, choose one confirmed detail from the situation and one item that still needs clarification. Say a two-sentence response using “Do you want added length or ___?”. Keep the known detail accurate and ask about the missing one.

Check your reasoning

Compare with the case above and the model response. Can you point to the words that support your factual statement? Is your question about something the case leaves open? If you introduce a possible outcome, clearly label it as a possibility.

Review, then try again

  • Keeps the case facts accurate; clearly separates confirmed and unknown information.
  • Uses understandable sentences and explains specialist terms when the listener needs it.
  • Responds to the other person's question, checks understanding, and uses an appropriate tone.
  • Makes the requested action or unresolved question clear without inventing authority or facts.

Revise one sentence and repeat the response. A useful response can be clear even with a few grammar errors; judge meaning and task completion, not accent.

Vocabulary for this lesson

The hard, visible part of the natural nail.
cuticle

A moment at work

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

reusable implement
A nail-service tool intended for repeated use subject to required cleaning and disinfection.
single-use item
A service item intended for one use rather than reuse on another client.
disinfection
A specified process to reduce or eliminate relevant microorganisms on suitable objects; not the same as tidying.
contact time
The period a disinfectant must remain in contact as specified by its label and applicable procedure.

Useful word combinations

  • confirm station readiness: check whether the required preparation is complete
  • separate used items: distinguish used items from those prepared for a service
  • check the readiness record: consult the salon's recorded preparation status
  • give a waiting-time update: tell a client what is complete and what remains uncertain

Two short language checks

1. Client: Is station three ready now?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. incomplete

  1. A. incomplete Correct. The required checks have not been finished.
  2. B. completed Completed would contradict the supplied status of the checks.
  3. C. repeated Repeated says they happened again, which has not been stated.
  4. D. waived Waived means set aside; these checks still remain required.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. disinfectant's specified contact period

  1. A. appointment reminder time A reminder time is a scheduling detail.
  2. B. client's arrival time Arrival is unrelated to the disinfectant term.

Vocabulary for this lesson

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. orientation

  2. B. symmetry Symmetry concerns corresponding shapes, not which way lettering faces.
  3. C. color intensity Color intensity concerns visual strength, not the direction of letters.
  4. D. orientation Correct. Orientation describes the direction in which the letters face.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. fee

  1. A. record A record can still be kept for a free review.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

removal appointment
A visit booked to assess and remove an existing nail product using the appropriate professional service.
infill
A maintenance service addressing the grown-out area of an existing enhancement when suitable.
booking deposit
Money paid in advance toward an appointment under the salon's stated terms.
consultation
A discussion to clarify preferences, relevant history, and the proposed nail service before agreement.

Useful word combinations

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Receptionist: Is Friday at ten confirmed?
Technician: No, that's the client's ____ time.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. requested

  1. A. missed No confirmed visit has been missed.
  2. B. extended No appointment has been lengthened.
  3. C. requested Correct. The client has asked for it, but availability is unconfirmed.
  4. D. completed A future time cannot be completed.
2. Client: Does a deposit tell you which product is on my nails?
Receptionist: No, it concerns the ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. advance payment

  1. A. optional gratuity A gratuity is an optional tip, not an appointment deposit.
  2. B. advance payment Correct. A booking deposit is payment made in advance under stated terms.
  3. C. remaining balance The remaining balance is what is still owed after any credited deposit.
  4. D. cancellation charge A cancellation charge concerns changing or missing a booking, not the deposit's definition.
