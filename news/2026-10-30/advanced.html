Israeli Opposition Challenges Netanyahu's Claims of Electoral Interference

📅 October 30, 2026 - Israeli Opposition Challenges Netanyahu's Claims of Electoral Interference

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Israeli opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot and Arab-led party leaders denounced Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu over claims of foreign interference after his coalition lost its majority. Netanyahu's Likud party accused Arab-led parties of stealing the election, while the Shin Bet security agency was examining whether they had received illegal financial assistance from Qatar and Turkey. Qatar's foreign ministry dismissed the accusations and charged Israeli politicians with conducting a disinformation campaign against Doha. Neither Netanyahu's bloc nor the Jewish opposition secured a parliamentary majority. The Joint List and Ra'am defied expectations by securing a record 19 of the Knesset's 120 seats after a surge in Arab voter participation. With Netanyahu's bloc on 53 seats and Eisenkot's on 48, the Arab parties emerged as potential kingmakers, whose backing could determine who forms a government. Likud alleged that voters had been bribed to choose Arab parties, following a request by the head of the Central Elections Committee for a security inquiry into suspected foreign interference. Both Arab parties rejected the claim, and the Joint List described it as baseless. What Netanyahu's critics see in the dispute is an attempt to delegitimise the Arab parties and make Eisenkot's only current route to power, an agreement with them, politically impossible. Shin Bet clarified that its inquiry did not implicate an entire community whose members had voted legally. The acting director general of the Central Elections Committee said there was no basis for claims of systematic breaches of election integrity. Eisenkot cautioned that it remained too early to establish whether anything had happened and accused Netanyahu of orchestrating a campaign against the legitimacy of the results. Many Arab Israelis had previously boycotted elections, but their participation was now motivated by a desire to oust Netanyahu's governing coalition. The reported options for forming a government were a Netanyahu–Eisenkot unity administration, a Netanyahu coalition involving defectors from Eisenkot's bloc, or an Eisenkot-led administration backed by Arab parties. Although Eisenkot and all but one member of his bloc had previously ruled out governing with Arab parties, speculation persisted that an agreement might still be reached. A Hebrew University snap poll found that, among voters from each party in the opposition bloc, the most popular option was an Eisenkot minority government involving Ra'am, with the Joint List providing backing from outside the government.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. disinformation campaign (noun phrase): A coordinated effort to spread deliberately false or misleading information in order to influence public understanding.
2. defied expectations (verb phrase, past tense): Produced an outcome substantially different from what people had anticipated.
3. kingmakers (plural noun): Political participants whose backing can determine who gains power, even though they do not lead the government themselves.
4. baseless (adjective): Lacking adequate evidence or justification; here, this expresses the Joint List's assessment of the claim.
5. delegitimise (verb): Undermine the recognition of someone or something as valid, rightful or entitled to participate.
6. implicate (verb): Connect someone with suspected wrongdoing, without necessarily establishing their guilt.
7. systematic (adjective): Following an organised or consistent pattern rather than occurring as isolated incidents.
8. orchestrating (verb, present participle): Deliberately coordinating several actions towards a particular outcome; here, it suggests a planned political campaign.
9. oust (verb): Remove someone from a position of power, including through electoral or parliamentary action.
10. defectors (plural noun): People who abandon their political group to align themselves with another side.
11. ruled out (phrasal verb, past participle): Declared that a particular course of action would not be considered or accepted.
12. snap poll (noun phrase): A public-opinion survey conducted quickly to capture views on an immediate issue.

Advanced Grammar: What-clefts for focusing an interpretation
A what-cleft divides a message into a clause beginning with “What” and a focal statement introduced by “is” or “was”. Here, “What Netanyahu's critics see in the dispute” introduces their perspective, while the material after “is” foregrounds their interpretation of the dispute's purpose. The attribution remains essential: the sentence presents the critics' assessment rather than an established motive.
Example from the text: "What Netanyahu's critics see in the dispute is an attempt to delegitimise the Arab parties and make Eisenkot's only current route to power, an agreement with them, politically impossible."
