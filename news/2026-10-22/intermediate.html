The Trillion-Pound Oil Fund the UK Could Have Built

📅 October 22, 2026 - The Trillion-Pound Oil Fund the UK Could Have Built

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The UK government recently announced a new energy wealth fund with an initial investment of £2bn. Following the announcement, the Treasury calculated how much the UK could have raised by investing early North Sea proceeds. According to internal modelling, the nation could have built a massive financial asset worth a trillion pounds. Over the years, several energy exporting countries have used their oil and gas revenues to create epic national savings accounts. For example, neighbouring Norway has amassed a huge financial reserve from the exact same North Sea resources. Critics argue that early revenues were squandered on wasteful spending rather than being saved for the future. While the new British fund plans to invest domestically, Norway is obliged to invest its money abroad. Furthermore, tax collections from UK energy are forecast to dwindle as North Sea production gradually slows down. Although nobody expects the new £2bn fund to reach a trillion pounds soon, experts still wonder what might have been.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. calculated (verb): Discovered a number or amount using mathematical methods.
2. asset (noun): Something valuable that is owned by a person, company, or country.
3. revenues (noun): Amounts of money that a government or company receives regularly from its business activities.
4. amassed (verb): Gathered a large amount of something, such as money, over a long period of time.
5. squandered (verb): Carelessly wasted money, time, or valuable opportunities.
6. domestically (adverb): Within a particular country rather than internationally.
7. obliged (adjective): Required or forced to do something by a rule, law, or necessity.
8. dwindle (verb): To gradually become smaller in size, amount, or strength.

Grammar Focus: Past Modals for Imagined Situations
We use 'could have' followed by a past participle (like 'raised' or 'built') to talk about things that were possible in the past but did not actually happen. In this story, it describes the theoretical money the UK missed out on saving.
Example from the text: "Following the announcement, the Treasury calculated how much the UK could have raised by investing early North Sea proceeds."
