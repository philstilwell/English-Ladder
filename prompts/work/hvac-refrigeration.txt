Lesson 01: Clarifying comfort complaints and thermostat readings
Original fictional case: Office manager Asha tells service coordinator Ben that the meeting room is 22 degrees Celsius. The number is actually the thermostat setpoint, not a confirmed ambient-temperature reading. Staff report feeling warm there after 14:00. No cause or equipment fault has been established. Ben needs to clarify the value, keep the staff report attributed, and record the afternoon pattern. The conversation uses existing information and gives no instruction to adjust or test the equipment.
Language workshop: Twenty-two is the target, not the finding
Communication goal: Separate a thermostat setpoint from a measured room temperature while preserving the time and location of a comfort complaint.
Language guidance: Twenty-two degrees Celsius is the setpoint in this case. It must not be relabeled as ambient temperature. The complaint concerns staff feeling warm in the meeting room after 14:00, not a verified temperature or fault.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): Is 22 the setpoint or a measured room temperature? / The thermostat is set to 22 degrees Celsius. / No confirmed ambient reading has been supplied. / Staff report feeling warm in the meeting room. / The reports concern the period after 14:00. / A target setting does not disprove a comfort complaint. / We have a setpoint and an occupant report, not a diagnosis. / No sensor fault has been established. / What does the current record identify that value as? / Please keep meeting room in the service description. / The value is stated in degrees Celsius. / This clarification does not specify a new setting. / I will record what the staff have reported. / We do not have a measured temperature above the target. / Setpoint 22; staff feel warm after 14:00; ambient reading unavailable. / I will pass on that description for the relevant assessment.
Vocabulary: HVAC: Heating, ventilation, and air conditioning. / thermostat: Control associated with maintaining a selected temperature. / setpoint: Target value used by a control system. / ambient temperature: Temperature of the surrounding air at the relevant location. / temperature reading: Value reported by a temperature-measuring device. / degrees Celsius: Temperature unit used in the supplied report. / comfort complaint: Occupant report that environmental conditions feel uncomfortable. / occupant feedback: Account supplied by people using the space. / sensible cooling: Heat removal associated with lowering air temperature without counting moisture condensation. / afternoon pattern: Reported tendency occurring later in the day. / sensor: Device responding to a physical condition for measurement or control. / measured value: Quantity established by a measurement. / displayed value: Number shown by a device or interface. / target temperature: Temperature the control is intended to seek. / latent cooling: Heat removal associated here with condensing water vapor from air. / relative humidity: Water-vapor level relative to saturation at the same temperature. / occupancy: Use of a space by people. / control schedule: Timing arrangement for control settings or operation. / zone: Defined area treated as a control or service region. / heat gain: Heat added to a space from its surroundings or internal sources. / equipment fault: Established malfunction of a system component. / reported period: Time span described in an account. / service description: Concise account used to request relevant attention. / attributed report: Statement linked to the person or group who supplied it.
Speaking task: Read turns 1-10. Stress setpoint, no ambient reading, meeting room, and after two to preserve the complaint's meaning. Switch roles for turns 11-20. Keep Celsius with the target and distinguish missing information from no measurement ever taken. Complete and check the service exchange. Read the actual report without inventing a sensor fault or setting change.
Partner: Asha: Office manager / Ben: Service coordinator
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: Complete the service report using the target, time pattern, and missing measurement. Do not add a diagnosis.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Asha: Could you read it back with the target and missing measurement separate? I need a short version for the facilities manager.
Ben: Here is the attributed report: staff feel warm in the meeting room after 14:00; thermostat setpoint 22 degrees Celsius; no confirmed ambient reading supplied; cause unestablished.
Asha: That preserves the staff account without inventing a measured temperature above target. I'll stop describing twenty-two as the actual room condition.
Ben: I will pass on that record. It distinguishes the target temperature from the actual condition that still needs assessment, while keeping the staff's reported experience visible.

Language workshop: Two ceiling openings, two functions
Communication goal: Translate supply and return labels into plain language while preserving the difference between a comfort report and an airflow measurement.
Language guidance: S3 is labeled supply and R2 return on the current drawing. This tells the reader their stated functions. It does not establish actual flow rate, velocity, balance, or the cause of the discomfort.

Dialogue 06: A refrigeration report needs two separate handoffs
Original fictional setting: A technician and food-business manager coordinate an equipment concern and a stock review.
Professional roles: Technician / Food-business manager
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Technician: We have the cabinet alarm history, but no product records.
Food-business manager: Our stock lead has those. I assumed your report would decide whether the stock could remain for sale.
Technician: My report addresses equipment findings; it doesn't replace your stock lead's product-safety decision.
Food-business manager: I'll contact the stock lead immediately and make sure they receive the existing product information through our normal arrangements.
Technician: Thank you. For the equipment enquiry, please retain the cabinet reference, alarm identifiers, timestamps, and any recorded limitations.
Food-business manager: The log mentions defrost around the event. Should I tell the stock lead that the alarm was therefore normal?
Technician: No. That entry alone doesn't establish why the alarm occurred or whether the products met their required conditions.
Food-business manager: I'll report the coinciding entry as context, not as a confirmed explanation or an assurance about the stock.
Technician: The technical reviewer will also need to clarify which sensor produced the displayed cabinet-temperature value in this report.
Food-business manager: I'll retrieve the record without asking staff to alter settings or run experiments.
Technician: That gives each responsible person the relevant information while keeping the equipment question distinct from product disposition.
Food-business manager: I'll track both follow-ups separately so completion of one doesn't silently close the other concern.
Vocabulary in this exchange: alarm history: A record of equipment alarm events, including their identifiers, times, and available status information.

Interior designer: I'll arrange the joint review and keep the alternative unapproved until the actual requirements are reconciled.
