📅 September 08, 2026 - Nigella Lawson Joins Great British Bake Off as New Judge
Celebrated TV chef Nigella Lawson has joined The Great British Bake Off as a new judge, replacing Dame Prue Leith, while a fresh group of diverse contestants prepares for the 17th series.

Read the story

TV chef Nigella Lawson has been praised by critics for her new role as a judge on The Great British Bake Off, which returns to Channel 4 later this month. Lawson is taking over from Dame Prue Leith, who said she was leaving the series in order to "work less and play more." The Times said that "lashings of Nigella Lawson help everyone rise to the occasion", while the Telegraph declared "she is more delectable than anything the contestants could whip up in a showstopper challenge". Contestants for the 17th series have also been confirmed, including a paediatric doctor, a competitive sprinter, a hairdresser and a drag artist. Ben Dowell of the Times picked out some of Lawson's lines from the first episode, including "I want it really sumptuous", which he wrote was delivered "in that - there is no other word for it - highly seductive voice of hers". "You can tell the production team are pleased to have her - at times... it feels as if the programme is entertaining royalty," Dowell continued, adding: "Bake Off has found a new star." The Telegraph's Anita Singh concurred, writing: "Nigella is heaven for social media. The woman is a walking meme factory." A competitive sprinter, Moyin, 22, from Essex, balances his studies with athletics and enjoys using flavours inspired by his Nigerian heritage. Yannis, 28, a paediatric doctor from London, wants to challenge the stigma that Bangladeshi men don't belong in the kitchen.

Words and grammar

1. critics (noun): People who express their opinions and judgments about art, books, films, and other cultural products, often professionally.
2. lashings (noun): A very large quantity of something, often used informally when referring to food or drink.
3. delectable (adjective): Extremely pleasing and delicious, especially in taste or smell.
4. showstopper (noun): An outstanding part of a performance or display that makes people stop and admire it, often used in competitive baking for an impressive creation.
5. sumptuous (adjective): Of a rich and impressive character, often referring to food, surroundings, or clothing that looks expensive and luxurious.
6. seductive (adjective): Very attractive and charming in a way that makes you want to have, use, or know more about something.
7. entertaining royalty (phrase): Treating someone with great respect and special care, as if they were a king, queen, or a very important person.
8. concurred (verb): Agreed with someone else's opinion or statement.
9. meme factory (noun phrase): A person or thing that frequently produces or inspires ideas, images, or behaviors that spread rapidly and widely on the internet, often for humorous purposes.
10. stigma (noun): A strong feeling of disapproval that most people in a society have about something, especially when it is unfair, or a mark of disgrace.

Grammar Focus: The Passive Voice
The passive voice is a grammatical structure used when the focus is on the action itself or the recipient of the action, rather than the performer of the action. It is formed using a form of the verb 'to be' followed by the past participle of the main verb. We often use it when the agent (the person or thing doing the action) is unknown, unimportant, or obvious from the context, or when we want to make a statement more formal.
Example from the text: "TV chef Nigella Lawson has been praised by critics for her new role as a judge on The Great British Bake Off, which returns to Channel 4 later this month."

📅 September 07, 2026 - Welsh Government Seeks Public Opinion on New Energy Company
The Welsh government is asking the public for their views on a new state-owned company designed to build wind and solar plants, aiming to reduce energy costs in the long-term without setting consumer bills directly.

Read the story

People across the country are being asked to give their opinions on proposals for a new government-owned renewable energy company in Wales. This proposed company's primary purpose would be to build and own wind and solar power plants. Its long-term plans aim to reduce energy costs for people. However, the new company would not directly set household electricity charges or replace existing suppliers. It also intends to support communities in taking a part-ownership in these energy projects. The Welsh government currently has an onshore wind and solar project creator called Trydan Gwyrdd Cymru (TGC). Ministers are suggesting combining TGC with other current Welsh energy initiatives to create a stronger new enterprise. Enterprise Minister Adam Price expressed a desire for a Welsh energy system that is 'clean, cheap and owned by us'. The company would also seek to attract investment into Welsh energy infrastructure. Additionally, a Welsh Energy Wealth Fund is being considered to support vulnerable people in Wales.

Words and grammar

1. proposed (adjective): Suggested or put forward for consideration.
2. primary purpose (noun phrase): The main reason or goal for which something exists.
3. long-term (adjective): Continuing for, or concerned with, a long period in the future.
4. household (adjective): Relating to a house and its occupants.
5. intends (verb): Has a plan or purpose in mind.
6. part-ownership (noun): The state of owning only a portion or share of something.
7. current (adjective): Happening or existing at the present time.
8. enterprise (noun): A business or a project, especially one that is difficult or important.

Grammar Focus: The Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action itself or the recipient of the action, rather than who performed it. It is often formed with a form of 'to be' (e.g., is, are, was, were) followed by the past participle of the main verb. This construction is common in news reporting, especially when the person or group doing the action is less important or obvious.
Example from the text: "People across the country are being asked to give their opinions on proposals for a new government-owned renewable energy company in Wales."

It's frequently used in news reporting when the agent (who or what performed the action) is unknown, unimportant, or obvious, or when we want to emphasize the outcome. It is formed with a form of 'to be' followed by the past participle of the main verb.
