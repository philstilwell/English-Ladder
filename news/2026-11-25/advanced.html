US Attorney General Sidesteps Questions About Possible Nvidia Investigation

Advanced · C1+

News archive →
📅 November 25, 2026 - US Attorney General Sidesteps Questions About Possible Nvidia Investigation

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

While describing Nvidia as a “good corporate citizen”, US Attorney General Todd Blanche declined to clarify whether the chipmaker was under federal investigation. Blanche said he had no information about newly reinstated chief executive Jensen Huang’s security clearance. Huang regained his position less than two weeks after being forced to resign following revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy. Huang is due to face congressional scrutiny next week over national security concerns about his relationship with an alleged Chinese spy who led Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer. The commerce department announced that it was preparing to blacklist Megaspeed, which was suspected of helping to divert advanced chips to China. In a joint statement, the commerce and justice departments said they had begun investigating Megaspeed in 2024 over potential breaches of export controls, rules governing which goods may be sent abroad. Blanche emphasised that the justice department had hundreds of export investigations under way at any given time. Nvidia’s board defended its abrupt reversal on Huang’s leadership as serving the interests of shareholders, employees, partners and customers. The board said it had received input from senior government officials and was confident that Huang’s reappointment posed no national security risk. Trump said he had spoken to Nvidia’s directors and persuaded them to bring Huang back. Senator Pete Ricketts argued that Huang’s conduct did not absolve Nvidia of its responsibility to answer concerns about national security and unaccounted-for chips. Congressman Robert Garcia said Trump’s direct involvement in the decision raised serious questions about national security and corruption. Senator Ruben Gallego called for an investigation into stock positions held before the reappointment announcement. Although Nvidia shares closed nearly 6% lower, they remained above their level at the end of the previous week. Monday’s increase had exceeded 31% following news of Huang’s return. Trump said he would consider pardoning Huang for any potential criminal activity, while indicating that he did not expect a pardon to be needed.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. corporate citizen (countable noun phrase): A company viewed in terms of its responsibilities towards society and its conduct under the law.
2. declined to clarify (verb phrase, past tense): Chose not to provide an explanation that would make an uncertain matter clearer; a restrained expression often used in formal reporting.
3. security clearance (noun phrase): Official authorisation allowing someone access to particular sensitive information.
4. scrutiny (uncountable noun): Close, critical examination intended to assess conduct, decisions or possible problems.
5. divert (verb, base form): To redirect goods away from their intended destination or route.
6. export controls (plural noun phrase): Government rules governing which goods or technologies may be sent abroad and under what conditions.
7. reversal (countable noun): A change that replaces an earlier decision or position with its opposite.
8. absolve (verb, base form): To release someone from blame or responsibility; here, from an obligation to explain the company’s conduct.
9. unaccounted-for (adjective): Not satisfactorily explained in terms of location, use or what happened.
10. stock positions (plural noun phrase): Investors’ holdings in company shares at a particular time.

Advanced Grammar: Reduced concessive clauses with “while”
A reduced clause beginning with “while” and an “-ing” form can express concession when its understood subject matches the main-clause subject. Here, “while describing” contrasts Blanche’s endorsement with his refusal to clarify the investigation’s status.
Example from the text: "While describing Nvidia as a “good corporate citizen”, US Attorney General Todd Blanche declined to clarify whether the chipmaker was under federal investigation."
