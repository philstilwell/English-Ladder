Lesson 01: Clarifying a prep list
Original fictional case: The prep list says "soup: 24" without a unit. Yesterday's sheet used portions, while a storeroom label uses containers. Prep cook Asha has not started a batch. Lead Mateo intended twenty-four portions from the current recipe, not twenty-four containers. Asha needs to ask about the unit, confirm the current recipe, and make the corrected instruction visible. The conversation supplies no container capacity, portion volume, ingredient weights, or recipe conversion factor. Those details must come from the actual approved recipe and production arrangements, not from guessing what the number means.
Language workshop: Twenty-four of what?
Communication goal: Clarify an ambiguous prep quantity, confirm the recipe basis, and read back the instruction before production starts.
Language guidance: A portion is not a container unless the actual arrangement explicitly makes them equivalent. The number twenty-four cannot be converted into liters, containers, or ingredient weights without the relevant recipe and capacity information.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): The prep list says soup, twenty-four, but the unit is missing. / Do you mean twenty-four portions or twenty-four containers? / I mean twenty-four portions. / Use the current recipe. / Not twenty-four containers. / I have not started the batch yet. / What portion size and finished yield does this recipe use? / I will use the yield stated in the approved recipe. / Container size does not define the portion count by itself. / Let us add portions to the prep list. / Soup: twenty-four portions, using the current recipe. / Which approved recipe version applies to this task? / Yesterday's sheet does not settle today's missing unit. / Ingredient quantities must come from the actual recipe calculation. / I will check the corrected instruction before production starts. / We now agree on the item, quantity, unit, and recipe basis.
Vocabulary: prep list: Record of food-preparation tasks and required quantities. / mise en place: Ingredients, equipment, and organization prepared for the work ahead. / portion: Specified amount intended for one serving or defined use. / batch: Quantity produced together in one preparation cycle. / batch size: Amount to be produced in a particular batch. / recipe yield: Quantity or number of portions an approved recipe produces. / portion size: Specified amount allocated to each serving. / unit of measure: Stated basis such as grams, liters, containers, or portions. / container capacity: Amount a particular container can hold. / production quantity: Amount planned for preparation. / standardized recipe: Defined recipe specifying ingredients, method, and yield for consistent production. / current recipe: Recipe version established for the present task. / recipe version: Identified edition or revision of a recipe. / scaling factor: Ratio used to adjust a recipe from its original yield to a target yield. / ingredient weight: Mass of a recipe component. / volume: Space occupied by a substance, measured in a stated unit. / tare: Container weight excluded to determine the contents' net weight. / net weight: Contents' weight excluding packaging or container weight. / gross weight: Combined weight of contents and relevant container or packaging. / production sheet: Record of planned and actual kitchen output. / prep target: Required output for a preparation task. / read-back: Repetition of an instruction to confirm the details understood. / clarification: Question or explanation that resolves an ambiguity. / task start: Point at which the assigned preparation begins.
Speaking task: Check the answers. Read the prep-list conversation, stressing portions rather than containers. Switch roles. Repeat the twenty-four-portion instruction, current recipe, and no-batch-started status without inventing a volume. Complete and check the sauce transfer. Read the eighteen-portion instruction and keep the tub interpretation rejected.
Partner: Asha: Prep cook / Mateo: Kitchen lead
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A prep line says sauce: 18. Lead Noor clarifies eighteen portions from the current recipe, not eighteen tubs. No batch has started, and no portion volume is supplied.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Asha: I will add portions to the line now. Whoever takes over this prep should not have to ask the same question again.
Mateo: Agreed. Make the production quantity explicit. The corrected entry should preserve soup, twenty-four portions, and the current recipe basis without implying the task is complete.
Asha: My repeat is soup, twenty-four portions, current recipe. No batch started. I will check the recipe details before beginning the actual preparation.
Mateo: That read-back is correct. We have resolved the missing unit, and the next step is to prepare against the approved recipe and actual kitchen process.

Language workshop: Three total, not five
Communication goal: Distinguish a replacement ticket from an added order and keep a one-item substitution linked to the current quantity.
Language guidance: Revised 41 refers to the same ticket, but the meaning of the new quantity still needs confirmation. Here, Cole explicitly says replaces. Three total is not two plus three, and one salad substitution does not apply to all three tarts.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): I have the original two-tart ticket and the revised slip for three. / Does the revised quantity replace the old one or add to it? / It replaces the old quantity. / Ticket 41 is three tarts total. / Not two plus three. / One of the three has salad instead of potatoes. / The other two keep potatoes. / The salad substitution applies to one item only. / Which guest gets the salad substitution? / Both slips refer to ticket 41. / Do not treat the earlier slip as another order to produce. / Keep the revision trail according to our system. / Heard: three total, replacing the earlier two. / This correction does not mean the tarts are already ready. / I am confirming this ticket, not every tart outstanding across the kitchen. / Ticket 41: three total, one salad instead of potatoes, earlier quantity replaced.
Vocabulary: ticket number: Identifier linking an order and its updates. / revised ticket: Updated order record changing a previous instruction. / replacement instruction: Direction that supersedes an earlier direction rather than adding to it. / additional order: New quantity added beyond the existing confirmed order. / current total: Complete quantity now required after applying the confirmed change. / superseded ticket: Earlier record no longer governing the current instruction. / amendment: Change to an existing order or document. / modifier: Instruction changing a particular item's standard preparation or accompaniment. / substitution: Replacement of one component with another. / instead of: Phrase indicating replacement rather than addition. / extra: Additional quantity beyond what is already included. / all day: Kitchen shorthand for the total quantity outstanding across the stated order scope. / expediter: Person coordinating order information and dish completion at the pass. / ticket rail: Place where order slips are held and organized for service. / duplicate production: Making an order twice because its records or instructions were misread. / active instruction: Direction currently governing the work. / change acknowledgment: Explicit confirmation that a revision has been received and understood. / item identification: Details distinguishing the particular dish affected by a request. / seat mapping: Link between an ordered item and the guest's place at a table. / side order: Accompanying item ordered alongside a main or other dish. / standard accompaniment: Side normally supplied with an item unless modified. / one-item change: Modification affecting one item rather than every item in the order. / order scope: Set of tickets or items to which a quantity or instruction applies. / revision trail: Record showing how an instruction changed over time.

Server: They said they avoid mustard and asked whether this sauce contains it. They haven't explained why yet.
Cook: Please ask whether they're reporting an allergy or a preference, and keep their food order pending meanwhile.
Server: I'll ask directly. I won't assume the answer just because their first request sounded casual at the table.

Cook: Thank you. I'll identify the dressing and the unchanged main separately when I pass the question to the lead.
Server: I'll tell the diner which component we're checking and ask for their stated concern without suggesting an answer.
Cook: Then we can review the current dressing information before agreeing that a substitution addresses what they need.
Server: Yes. I'll get their answer before we suggest a replacement.

Cook: I'll leave their setup unchanged and give our expediter the specific reason our station isn't fully ready.
Lead cook: Good. The other station confirms its extra ladle is allocated, so we'll need a different replacement option.
Cook: I'll keep our utensil request open. The completed ingredient count doesn't need repeating when the replacement arrives.
Lead cook: Once we have a replacement, just update that outstanding item.

Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):

Outgoing cook: Fourteen was my physical count just before handover. The sheet may still show the earlier count.
Incoming cook: Do you know why two portions differ, or are you only reporting the numbers you can see?
Outgoing cook: I only know the figures differ. I haven't matched the change to orders or a waste entry.
Incoming cook: Then we should leave the reason unresolved. Who will reconcile the physical count with the records?
Outgoing cook: I'll bring the discrepancy to the lead. Could you use fourteen as the observed count in our discussion?
Incoming cook: Yes, as an observed count, not a final reconciled figure or confirmation that all portions are available.
Outgoing cook: That's an important distinction. The lead also needs to confirm any allocations before updating the service availability.
Incoming cook: I'll keep the next prep decision pending until that availability figure is clear. Can you stay for the review?
Outgoing cook: I can stay for the handover discussion and show the lead where the fourteen portions were counted.
Incoming cook: Then I'll tell the next station that the count is under review, with the difference and owner identified.
Outgoing cook: I'll retain this follow-up until the lead explicitly accepts responsibility.
