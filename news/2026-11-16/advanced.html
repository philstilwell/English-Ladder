Four Planets Form November’s Pre-dawn Parade

📅 November 16, 2026 - Four Planets Form November’s Pre-dawn Parade

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

November’s pre-dawn sky offers a diagonal configuration of four planets. Mercury, Venus, Mars and Jupiter will be observable without optical equipment in the eastern to south-eastern sky. Of these four planets, Mercury will be the hardest to discern. As the smallest planet and the one nearest the Sun, Mercury is persistently caught in solar glare. Its visibility is confined to twilight, and it remains faint and low on the horizon. An unobstructed south-eastern view across a flat horizon offers the recommended setting for spotting Mercury. What makes Venus especially conspicuous is that only the Moon appears brighter in the night sky. Among the major planets, Venus has the greatest reflectivity, the capacity to send incoming sunlight back into space. Observers can distinguish Venus from nearby Spica by looking for a steady glow rather than the star’s twinkling. Mars and Jupiter will be in particularly striking apparent proximity on 15 and 16 November. According to astronomer Gideon Bendavid-Walker at the Royal Observatory Greenwich, their angular separation, the angle between their positions in the sky, will be just 1.2 degrees. He compares that interval with the width of a finger held at arm’s length. Near Regulus, the two planets may resemble a group of three stars. While Uranus will be setting in the west before sunrise, Saturn and Neptune will be visible for a time in the evening. Observers should choose a location away from artificial skyglow with a good view of the eastern horizon, allowing around twenty minutes for their eyes to acclimatise to darkness. The recommended viewing period for the planetary parade is approximately 45–60 minutes before sunrise.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. configuration (noun): The arrangement of several objects in relation to one another, considered as a whole.
2. discern (verb): To manage to see or recognise something that is difficult to distinguish from its surroundings.
3. solar glare (noun phrase): Intense light from the Sun that makes nearby objects difficult to see.
4. confined to (adjective phrase): Restricted to a particular situation, place or period; here, a limited lighting condition.
5. unobstructed (adjective): Having nothing in the way that blocks the view.
6. conspicuous (adjective): Readily noticeable because something stands out strongly from its surroundings.
7. reflectivity (noun): The capacity of a surface to send incoming light back rather than absorb it.
8. apparent proximity (noun phrase): Closeness as it appears to an observer, without establishing the actual distance between the objects.
9. angular separation (noun phrase): The angle between the directions in which two objects are seen, rather than the distance between them in space.
10. acclimatise (verb): To become accustomed to different surrounding conditions; here, for the eyes to become accustomed to darkness.

Advanced Grammar: Pseudo-cleft sentences for explanatory emphasis
A pseudo-cleft uses a “what” clause followed by “be” to emphasise the information that follows. Here, the opening clause establishes what needs explaining, while the clause after “is” foregrounds the reason Venus stands out.
Example from the text: "What makes Venus especially conspicuous is that only the Moon appears brighter in the night sky."
