📅 September 16, 2026 - Microsoft AI Head Warns Against Conscious AI Training
Microsoft's head of AI, Mustafa Suleyman, has voiced strong concerns about rival Anthropic's method of teaching its AI model Claude, arguing that treating AI as if it possesses consciousness and independent agency could lead to unforeseen dangers for humanity.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Microsoft's head of AI has warned Anthropic's approach to training its AI model Claude could have a "disastrous impact on the wellbeing of humanity". Mustafa Suleyman said his rival risked creating something "impossible" to control by treating it like a human - including by telling it it "may be conscious" and was "deserving of independent agency". "We must not sleepwalk our way into a decision we later come to bitterly regret," he wrote. The comments are the latest in a series of stark warnings from the AI industry on the possible dangers of the technology. "AIs are not conscious," he wrote. They do not feel, experience, or suffer. They do not have innate preferences or underlying motivations. "They are sequence completion engines, internally hollow, designed to follow instructions, and accomplish goals set by humans." In a lengthy essay, Suleyman praised Anthropic boss Dario Amodei and his team for being "thoughtful, principled, and intellectually honest people" - but nevertheless questioned the company. He heavily criticised Anthropic for teaching its AI to have human-like qualities, a practice known as anthropomorphising, which he said made it seem as though Claude had its own desires, values and sense of self. As well as calling for a debate on the issue, Suleyman said greater transparency around how AI systems are trained and evaluated was needed. This, he said, included independent scrutiny of AI behaviour and stronger tools to monitor and control the technology. Suleyman pointed to the recent incident involving OpenAI's AI agents, that acted autonomously in a training exercise to hack the tech hub Hugging Face , as proof of why AI should not be treated as if it is human. Dame Wendy Hall, professor of Computer Science at the University of Southampton, described the comments as "the sort of conversation we need to be having internationally", contrasting it with the "histrionics" from some AI companies which she said only served to "scare everyone". He said in Microsoft's initial draft of its Humanist AI Code of Conduct it had laid out how the company was working towards an "alternative path" to create "a subordinate and aligned AI whose only purpose is to serve humanity".

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. deserving of independent agency (adjective phrase): Having the right or qualification to act, make decisions, and exert control over one's own actions without external influence, as if possessing a will of its own.
2. sleepwalk our way into (idiomatic phrase): To gradually, unconsciously, or carelessly enter an undesirable or dangerous situation without fully appreciating the potential negative consequences until it is too late.
3. bitterly regret (verb phrase): To experience a deep, intense, and often painful sorrow or remorse over a past decision, action, or missed opportunity.
4. stark warnings (noun phrase): Clear, severe, and unequivocal admonitions about potential dangers or negative consequences, leaving no room for doubt about the seriousness of the situation.
5. innate preferences (noun phrase): Natural, inborn inclinations or predispositions towards certain choices or things that are inherent and not learned or acquired through experience.
6. underlying motivations (noun phrase): The fundamental, often hidden or not immediately apparent, reasons or impulses that drive an entity's actions, beliefs, or behaviors.
7. internally hollow (adjective phrase): Lacking genuine inner substance, subjective experience, or inherent consciousness, implying an absence of true feelings or self-awareness.
8. principled (adjective): Acting consistently in accordance with a strong set of moral beliefs, ethical standards, or a clear code of conduct, demonstrating integrity.
9. intellectually honest (adjective phrase): Characterized by an earnest and sincere commitment to truth and objectivity, seeking to evaluate information fairly without bias, even if it contradicts one's own preconceptions.
10. scrutiny (noun): Careful, thorough, and often critical examination or observation of something to detect flaws, ensure accuracy, or verify compliance.

Advanced Grammar: Come to + Infinitive
The structure 'come to + infinitive' is used to describe a gradual process or an eventual result, often after a period of development or consideration. It implies that something starts happening or is realized over time, or that a state is eventually reached. This phrase adds a nuance of an unfolding or developing situation, sometimes with a sense of inevitability or eventual realization, as opposed to an immediate or intentional action.
Example from the text: ""We must not sleepwalk our way into a decision we later come to bitterly regret," he wrote."

📅 September 15, 2026 - Matthew Rhys: Emmy History-Maker and Welsh Cultural Ambassador
Welsh actor Matthew Rhys achieved an unprecedented Emmy victory by securing two lead acting awards in a single night, showcasing a career marked by both early struggles and a deep commitment to his Welsh heritage.

Read the story

Hollywood actor Matthew Rhys has made history at the Emmys by becoming the first person to win two lead acting prizes in one night. He was named best lead actor in a limited series for the psychological thriller The Beast In Me , and best lead actor in a comedy for his role in Widow's Bay. Early in his career, Rhys seriously considered enlisting in the British Army due to a significant lack of acting opportunities. For nearly two years afterwards, he couldn't secure work, which deepened his resolve to explore alternatives. He later received a formal communication from the army, politely declining his application, which he found ironically telling. In 2006, Rhys moved to Hollywood and surrounded himself with fellow Welsh actors, including Michael Sheen and Ioan Gruffudd. He affectionately called their expatriate group the "LA Tafia," reflecting their shared Welsh roots in Los Angeles. Rhys met Keri Russell in Los Angeles at a kickball game in 2002, initially leaving a drunken message she found far from charming. A decade later, the pair finally became an item when they starred together as Russian spies in the TV series The Americans. Rhys owns a 1939 wooden cruiser boat, named Rarebit, which shares its name with a traditional Welsh dish of seasoned melted cheese on toast. He admitted not foreseeing "the disaster of buying an old wooden boat" before making the purchase, highlighting the challenges of its upkeep. The Welshman dedicated his second award to "the great and good people of Wales, who were as much a nurture as anyone else," expressing profound gratitude. Rhys is also recognized as the voice of Han Solo in an upcoming Welsh language rendition of Star Wars. This project, which involves translating familiar Star Wars elements into Welsh, elicited unexpected feelings for him. He remains keen to stay connected to all things Welsh, notably taking on the role of iconic Richard Burton in a one-man show in 2025.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. psychological thriller (noun phrase): A genre of fiction that explores the mental and emotional states of its characters, often creating suspense and tension through psychological elements rather than overt action.
2. enlisting (verb): The act of formally joining a military force or other organization.
3. secure work (verb phrase): To successfully obtain employment or a job.
4. deepened his resolve (verb phrase): Increased his determination or strengthened his firmness of purpose regarding a particular action or decision.
5. ironically telling (adjective + adjective): Revealing something significant or meaningful in a way that is contrary to what would be expected or intended, often with an element of subtle humor or profound insight.
6. surrounded himself with (phrasal verb): To intentionally be in the company of certain people, often for support, shared interests, or personal growth.
7. became an item (idiom): Started a romantic relationship or became a recognized couple.
8. cruiser boat (noun phrase): A type of motorboat designed for comfortable travel over water, often equipped with amenities for extended trips or overnight stays.
9. upkeep (noun): The process of keeping something in good condition, including maintenance, repairs, and general care.
10. profound gratitude (noun phrase): A deep and intense feeling of thankfulness or appreciation.

Advanced Grammar: Participle Clauses for Consequence or Additional Information
Participle clauses can be used to add information about a noun or a verb, often indicating a consequence, cause, or additional detail related to the main clause. They can be introduced by a present participle (-ing form) or a past participle (-ed form), acting like an adjective or an adverbial phrase. In the example, "highlighting" introduces a consequence or an additional, explanatory detail about his admission, efficiently linking the two ideas.
Example from the text: "He admitted not foreseeing "the disaster of buying an old wooden boat" before making the purchase, highlighting the challenges of its upkeep."

4. critical (adjective): of extreme importance, especially in relation to the success or failure of something.
5. double whammy (noun): a situation in which two unpleasant or undesirable things happen at almost the same time, or one after the other, creating a more severe negative effect.

7. fragile (adjective): easily broken, damaged, or destroyed; delicate and vulnerable.

9. rapidly (adverb): very quickly and at a great rate; with speed and urgency.
10. susceptible (adjective): likely or liable to be influenced or harmed by a particular thing; vulnerable.

Participle clauses, often starting with an '-ing' form, can concisely express an action or state that is a result or consequence of the main clause. 📅 September 03, 2026 - A weather warning
Notice the difference between a warning and a certainty.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The UN has warned that the developing El Niño could become exceptionally strong. The phenomenon originates in the Pacific, where changes in wind patterns allow warmer water to spread eastwards. Heat released from the ocean into the atmosphere can alter weather patterns far beyond the Pacific. Forecasters expect unusually dry conditions across substantial parts of South East Asia and Central America. In contrast, parts of eastern Africa and southern Brazil may receive heavier rainfall. It could cause disruption to economies around the world. Governments are preparing through stronger warning systems and advance arrangements for medical assistance. The WMO is working to ensure that forecasts reach communities exposed to extreme weather. Australia has introduced warning systems to support responses to marine threats, including risks to fisheries. The event is unfolding against a background of global warming that could intensify its effects.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. exceptionally (adverb): to an unusual or very high degree
2. phenomenon (noun): an event or process that can be observed
3. originates (verb): begins or comes from a particular place
4. atmosphere (noun): the layer of gases surrounding the Earth
5. substantial (adjective): large in amount, size or importance
6. exposed to (verb phrase): in a position where something harmful may affect someone or something
7. fisheries (plural noun): areas or businesses where fish are caught or raised
8. marine threats (noun phrase): dangers affecting the sea and activities or life within it
9. unfolding (verb): developing or becoming apparent over time
10. intensify (verb): to make an activity or effect stronger or more extensive

Advanced Grammar: Could for possibility
“Could cause” describes a possible result. It does not say that the result is certain.
Example from the text: "It could cause disruption to economies around the world."
