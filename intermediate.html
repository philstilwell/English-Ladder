📅 November 28, 2026 - Huw Edwards Interviews Raise Concerns About Media Responsibility
Explore why new interviews with Huw Edwards have caused criticism and raised concerns about broadcasters' responsibilities.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

New interviews with former BBC News presenter Huw Edwards have caused a backlash, meaning a strong negative public reaction. In 2024, Edwards was convicted of offences involving sexual images of children. He has been interviewed for a Channel 4 documentary and a podcast by the Observer and Tortoise Media. The prime minister's spokesperson said broadcasters should act responsibly and consider their audiences when deciding what to show. The spokesperson said victims of child sexual abuse should come first and expressed no interest in hearing Edwards' account. However, the spokesperson said Channel 4 was independent and could make its own decisions about individual programmes. Channel 4 defended the documentary, saying its director challenged Edwards about his actions and placed no limits on the topics discussed. A comment in the report said any programme featuring Edwards needed to challenge his past actions and raise unanswered questions. A statement in the report said people convicted of these serious crimes must face the consequences of their actions. Duncan Craig, who leads an organisation supporting male survivors of sexual abuse, said media companies seemed to be ignoring the victims. Craig said he believed in rehabilitation, or helping people change after committing crimes, but did not think these programmes offered it. The documentary and podcast are due to be released on Tuesday, with some material expected from the Observer on Sunday.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. backlash (noun): A strong negative reaction from a group of people.
2. convicted (verb, past participle): Officially found by a court to have committed a crime.
3. audiences (plural noun): The people who watch or listen to programmes.
4. independent (adjective): Able to make its own decisions about its programmes.
5. defended (verb, past simple): Gave reasons to support something after other people criticised it.
6. challenge (verb, base form): Ask difficult questions about someone's actions instead of simply accepting their explanation.
7. consequences (plural noun): The results of someone's actions, especially unpleasant ones.
8. rehabilitation (noun): The process of helping someone change their behaviour after committing a crime.

Grammar Focus: Past simple passive
Use "was" or "were" plus a past participle to form the past simple passive. This structure focuses on the person or thing affected by a completed past action. Add "by" when you need to name who performed the action.
Example from the text: "In 2024, Edwards was convicted of offences involving sexual images of children."

📅 November 27, 2026 - Zelensky Calls for More US Pressure on Russia
Zelensky criticises US support for Ukraine as missile deliveries face delays and negotiations show little progress.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Ukraine's president, Volodymyr Zelensky, expressed frustration with the US, saying it was not putting enough pressure on Russia during negotiations. He said delays in receiving missiles used to stop other missiles were costing Ukrainian lives. He also complained that the US was focusing on the Middle East and providing more help there. Zelensky said it was strange that Donald Trump had posted a picture showing himself embracing Vladimir Putin and describing him as a friend. The latest negotiations, held with help from a third party in Abu Dhabi, made no clear progress. The talks were expected to resume in December, according to Zelensky. He called for support from allies, countries that help Ukraine, and urged the US to put pressure on Russia. The US says it is neutral, meaning it does not take either side, while seeking a peace agreement that can last. Zelensky reported a serious shortage of defensive missiles and said the US was delaying deliveries of Patriot air defence systems. Russian attacks have become more frequent and deadly, and the number of civilians killed has reached its highest level. Power cuts and attacks on transport networks have raised operating costs so much that some businesses have shut. Zelensky acknowledged that Ukraine was preparing for a difficult and dangerous winter.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. frustration (uncountable noun): The feeling of being upset because a problem continues or something prevents progress.
2. delays (plural noun): Situations in which something happens later than expected or needed.
3. negotiations (plural noun): Discussions in which people try to reach an agreement.
4. resume (verb): To continue an activity after a pause.
5. allies (plural noun): Countries or groups that support and help one another.
6. neutral (adjective): Not supporting either side in a disagreement or conflict.
7. shortage (countable noun): A situation in which there is less of something than people need.
8. operating costs (plural noun phrase): The money needed to keep a business running.

Grammar Focus: Passive reporting with “was/were expected to”
Use “was/were expected to” followed by the base form of a verb to report what people thought would happen later. This passive structure leaves out who held the expectation. It reports an expectation, so it does not confirm that the event happened.
Example from the text: "The talks were expected to resume in December, according to Zelensky."

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. confirmed (verb, past participle): Officially stated that something is true or decided.
2. speculation (noun, uncountable): Discussion or guesses about something when the facts are not yet known.
3. take over (phrasal verb): To begin doing a job or holding a position that another person had.
4. household name (noun phrase): A person or thing that is familiar to many people.
5. box office (noun phrase): The money a film earns from cinema ticket sales.
6. industry (noun): The businesses and people involved in a particular type of work, such as making films.
7. independent (adjective): Made outside the control of the main large film companies.
8. deal (noun): An agreement about work or business.

Grammar Focus: Present perfect passive
Use “has” or “have” + “been” + a past participle for the present perfect passive. It reports a completed action with a present result and focuses on who or what receives the action. The person or group doing it can be left out.
