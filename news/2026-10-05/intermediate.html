Scotland's Data Centre Boom: Investment vs. Environmental Concerns

Intermediate · B1–B2

📅 October 05, 2026 - Scotland's Data Centre Boom: Investment vs. Environmental Concerns

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Every time you ask ChatGPT a question, read a Google summary or make an online bank payment, a data centre is working behind the scenes. Now Scotland is part of a global race to build many more of them - on a massive scale. The explosion in artificial intelligence (AI) over the past few years has seen tech firms scrambling to build new 'hyperscale' data centres. Supporters say they are vital for the future and could bring billions of pounds in investment, as well as thousands of jobs. Opponents fear the impact of their size on local communities, the vast amounts of energy they use and even the noise they will make. If one planned for Chapelcross in Dumfries and Galloway goes ahead, it will have a capacity of 1,000MW, making it one of the largest data centres in the world. The Scottish Greens and campaign groups have called for a moratorium on large data centres, arguing they could undermine climate targets and place additional pressure on renewable energy supplies. The Scottish government has so far refused to impose a moratorium. As well as using huge amounts of electricity, the giant data centres can require large quantities of water to prevent them from overheating. Those behind AI data centres say they will create jobs and offer a stake in a key future technology.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. behind the scenes (adverbial phrase): working secretly or without being seen by the public, often to make something else possible.
2. massive scale (noun phrase): on a very large size or extent, much larger than usual.
3. explosion (noun): a sudden and rapid increase in something, like a quick growth.
4. scrambling (verb): moving or trying to do something quickly and with difficulty, often in a hurry.
5. hyperscale (adjective): describing something, especially a data centre, that is extremely large in size and capacity.
6. vital (adjective): extremely important or necessary for something to exist or be successful.
7. moratorium (noun): a temporary ban or stopping of an activity, usually by official order.
8. undermine (verb): to make something weaker or less effective, often gradually or secretly.

Grammar Focus: First Conditional
The first conditional is used to talk about possible future situations and their likely results. We form it with "If + present simple, will + base form of verb." It suggests a real possibility that can happen in the future.
Example from the text: "If one planned for Chapelcross in Dumfries and Galloway goes ahead, it will have a capacity of 1,000MW, making it one of the largest data centres in the world."
