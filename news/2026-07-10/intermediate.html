📅 July 10, 2026 - Wildfire Tragedy in Southern Spain: Victims and Missing Persons Reported

Read the story

A large wildfire recently broke out near Los Gallardos in Almería, a region in southern Spain. This tragic incident has led to a significant loss of life and a high number of missing persons. Authorities have confirmed that at least twelve people are dead as a direct consequence of the fire. Furthermore, a considerable number, specifically twenty-three individuals, have been reported missing since the blaze began. A local official indicated that four of the victims who perished might be British nationals. The fire broke out unexpectedly, causing widespread concern among residents and holidaymakers. Emergency services are working tirelessly to locate the missing and to manage the ongoing crisis. Investigations will likely determine the exact cause of the fire and assess the full extent of the damage. The community is deeply affected by the tragic events, hoping for news about the missing individuals. This disaster underscores the dangers of wildfires, especially in dry, warm climates like southern Spain.

Words and grammar

1. tragic incident (noun phrase): A very sad, unfortunate, or terrible event that causes suffering or distress.
2. perished (verb): Died, especially in a sudden, violent, or unexpected way, often referring to a disaster.
3. underscores (verb): Emphasizes or highlights the importance or truth of something, drawing attention to it.
4. ongoing crisis (noun phrase): A serious and difficult situation or period of danger that is currently happening and continuing.
5. emergency services (noun phrase): organisations such as firefighters and medical teams that respond to urgent danger
6. holidaymakers (plural noun): people who are away from home on holiday
7. considerable (adjective): large enough in amount or degree to be important
8. unexpectedly (adverb): in a way that people did not expect

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action or the receiver of the action, rather than the performer of the action. It's often used when the agent is unknown, unimportant, or obvious, or when describing unfortunate events. We form it with a form of 'to be' followed by a past participle verb.
Example from the text: "specifically twenty-three individuals, have been reported missing since the blaze began."
