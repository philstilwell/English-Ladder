Lesson 01: Defining the client and the cross-border mandate
Original fictional case: Orion Group contacts coordinating lawyer Leila about a supply dispute. The contract names Orion Components Ltd as signatory, while the email comes from the parent company, Orion Holdings. Contact Theo has not yet confirmed which entity will be the client or who can authorize the engagement. Documents mention operations in two jurisdictions. No representation, conflict clearance, or merits opinion has been established.
Language workshop: The parent is not the signatory
Communication goal: Clarify legal entities, instructions, and jurisdictional scope before summarizing a cross-border matter.
Language guidance: The parent's email does not establish that the parent signed the contract or is the client. Cross-border facts also do not automatically make the dispute one between states.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): Which legal entity signed this agreement? / Is the proposed client the parent or the contracting subsidiary? / Who is authorized to give instructions for that entity? / We need to define which jurisdictions and questions the engagement covers. / That local-law point needs the appropriate local counsel. / We have not yet confirmed the engagement or completed the required checks. / A private cross-border contract dispute is not automatically a dispute between states. / The signatory is identified; client identity and authority remain to be confirmed. / Are you asking about the finding or the next step? / Please correct me if I have misunderstood that detail. / That is what the current record says; it has not been independently confirmed. / That is a possibility, not an agreed outcome. / That decision sits outside my authority. / Who is responsible for that specific next step? / Let me restate the distinction more clearly. / We agree on the action, the owner, and the point for review.
Vocabulary: legal entity: Organization or person recognized as having a distinct legal identity. / parent company: Company controlling another company under the relevant relationship. / subsidiary: Company controlled by another company. / affiliate: Entity connected through the relevant ownership or control relationship. / contracting party: Person or entity that is a party to the contract. / signatory: Person or entity identified as signing a document in the relevant capacity. / client identity: Identification of the person or entity for whom counsel acts. / engagement letter: Document defining the agreed professional engagement. / scope of engagement: Boundaries of the work counsel is engaged to perform. / conflicts check: Review for relevant conflicts before or during representation. / authority to instruct: Power to give instructions for the relevant client or matter. / local counsel: Lawyer advising on the relevant local jurisdiction. / coordinating counsel: Lawyer organizing advice or work across the matter. / jurisdictional nexus: Connection linking a matter to a particular legal jurisdiction. / private international law: Rules addressing cross-border private-law questions, including applicable law and jurisdiction. / public international law: Law concerning states and other subjects of the international legal order. / corporate capacity: Legal ability of an entity to act in the relevant context. / instruction record: Traceable account of instructions received and their source. / clarification request: Question that resolves an unclear meaning or instruction. / source attribution: Identification of where information came from. / working assumption: Provisional premise used while information is incomplete. / scope boundary: Limit of the work or authority being discussed. / action owner: Named person responsible for a specified next step. / check-back: Restatement used to verify that a message was understood.
Speaking task: Read the corrected intake and distinguish the group, parent, subsidiary, signatory, and proposed client. Swap roles and give the final intake summary without claiming that representation has been accepted. Complete the four-line follow-up with the supplied choices. Check the explanations, then read the corrected exchange twice, changing roles.
Partner: Theo: Group legal contact / Leila: Coordinating lawyer
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: After intake, another affiliate joins the email chain and asks for advice. The engagement for that affiliate has not been confirmed, and no conflicts outcome is supplied.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Theo: One executive called this public international law because the companies operate in different countries. Is that a useful description?
Leila: Not automatically. Private international law questions can arise in a private contract dispute; the international element alone does not make it a state dispute.
Theo: I will return with the exact entity details, the authorized contact, and the questions we need answered in each jurisdiction.
Leila: That will give us an accurate instruction record and a sound basis for deciding the engagement, without assuming liability or offering a merits opinion prematurely.

Lesson 02: Separating governing law from the dispute forum

Marcus: R is listed as having signed, while S is listed as a party for which the treaty is already in force.
Lina: Then we must verify the relevant consent to be bound and timing for R rather than treating two different table entries as equivalent.
Marcus: I should not replace that with the opposite claim that signatures never have any legal effect, should I?
Lina: Correct. The instrument and circumstances matter. Ratification may be relevant, but we should not impose one universal procedure on every treaty.
Marcus: We also need a date for the briefing, not just a statement that the instrument is in force somewhere.

Lina: No. We need to assess whether a reservation or another relevant statement affects the analysis, including its actual legal character and effect.
Marcus: Where should I verify the status rather than rely on an undated summary copied into our internal notes?
Lina: Use the relevant depositary information and other appropriate primary materials, with the date and source clearly recorded.
Marcus: The provision is available in several languages, but the working English translation in our file is not labeled authoritative.
Lina: Then identify the authentic text arrangements before treating that translation as controlling wording.
Marcus: I also quoted one sentence without the preceding definition, which may have made the obligation sound broader.
Lina: That is why contextual interpretation matters. We should read the term within the actual instrument and applicable interpretative framework.
Marcus: Could we state the treaty's purpose without using that purpose to erase limits that appear in the actual wording?
Lina: Yes. Object and purpose belongs in the analysis, not as a slogan replacing the text or the status questions.
Marcus: My revised note will distinguish S's listed position from R's unresolved status and identify the date and source for each.
Lina: Good. Keep temporal scope explicit too, so a careful status statement does not quietly become an answer about every past or future event.

Dialogue 08: A commercial proposal is not a signed settlement
Original fictional setting: Company representative Nadiya and counsel Felix discuss a proposed settlement of a cross-border commercial dispute. The business teams have discussed a payment figure, but release scope, governing documents, approval authority, and execution remain unresolved. No final legal-effect opinion or binding settlement is established. An email uses without prejudice and subject to contract; counsel explains that their effect must be assessed in the actual jurisdiction and context.
Professional roles: Nadiya / Felix
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Nadiya: The business teams discussed a payment figure, and I would like to tell the board that the dispute is settled.
Felix: We can report progress, but the settlement proposal still leaves the release, approvals, documents, and execution unresolved.
Nadiya: They called it an agreement in principle. I thought that phrase supplied a clear legal answer about the whole negotiation.
Felix: Agreement in principle needs its actual terms and context examined. It should not become a shortcut around the unresolved legal questions.
Nadiya: The parent approved the discussion, but I do not know who can bind the subsidiary involved in the claim.
Felix: Then verify settlement authority for the actual entity. Group-level support and power to bind the relevant company may be different.
Nadiya: The proposed release says all claims, but nobody has clarified which parties or periods that phrase is intended to cover.
Felix: The release needs a defined scope. A broad phrase can conceal a major disagreement rather than resolve it.
Nadiya: One email says without prejudice. Can I assume that makes every statement protected in every country involved?
Felix: No. Without prejudice needs assessment under the applicable law and context. The label is not a universal protection switch.
Nadiya: Another draft says subject to contract. We should check its actual effect rather than rely on familiar wording alone.
Felix: Exactly. Subject to contract is part of the material counsel reviews, not a substitute for that review.
Nadiya: We also want the proposal to avoid suggesting that payment is an admission of the allegations.
Felix: Then the intended no-admission clause needs careful drafting and review alongside the rest of the agreement, rather than being assumed from the figure.
Nadiya: Even if signatures are arranged, we still need to know when payment becomes due and what approvals come first.
Felix: Yes. The payment condition and any relevant prerequisites must be explicit in the actual document.
Nadiya: I have been using executed to mean both signed and completely carried out, which could confuse the status report.
Felix: Clarify execution as the signing stage here, and report performance separately when the actual obligations are carried out.
Nadiya: My revised update will identify the commercial discussion and the open terms, without declaring the legal result settled.
Felix: That gives the board an accurate picture and leaves settlement implementation to be planned against the verified final position, not an optimistic summary.
