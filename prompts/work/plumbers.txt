Lesson 01: Identifying fixtures and service history
Original fictional case: A tenant reports a problem with the bathroom sink in Unit 3B. The building has both a basin in the bathroom and a utility sink beside it. The tenant means the bathroom basin and noticed water on its cabinet base at 07:00. No source is established.
Language workshop: Locate the fixture before naming a cause
Communication goal: Clarify everyday fixture names and record the source of an account.
Language guidance: Clients may use sink for different fixtures. Ask for room, unit, and fixture references rather than inferring a pipe route. This is an information-gathering exercise, not a request to test, dismantle, or operate plumbing.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): Do you mean the ___ or the ___? / Where did you notice ___? / I will record ___ as your observation, not ___.
Vocabulary: fixture: A plumbing-connected item such as a sink, basin, toilet, or shower that supplies or receives water. / branch line: A smaller piping route that connects part of a system to a larger main or distribution line. / riser: A vertical pipe or group of pipes serving different levels of a building. / service history: The record of earlier reported plumbing issues, visits, findings, and work for an identified location or asset.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: You are the tenant. Clarify the fixture and when you noticed the water.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: The manager calls it a riser leak. Preserve the tenant's observation without accepting the unverified pipe diagnosis.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Could you confirm whether you mean the bathroom basin or the utility sink beside it? I will record water on the bathroom cabinet base at 07:00 as your observation. That identifies the affected location, not the source. We can link this visit to the correct fixture's service history without assuming which pipe is involved.
