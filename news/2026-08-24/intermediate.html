Read the story

The BBC recently produced an in-depth report documenting the Taliban's approach to governance in Afghanistan. This comprehensive investigation takes place five years after the group’s return to power, marking a period of profound national change. During their visit, BBC journalists observed senior figures within the Taliban administration, gaining direct insights into their methods. The report highlights how the Taliban are attempting to reshape the country, influencing areas such as divorce laws and the pervasive use of smartphones. Leaders are actively implementing their interpretations of Islamic law, which profoundly impacts the daily lives of Afghan citizens. For example, new legal frameworks are being established concerning family matters, showcasing their strict adherence to religious doctrines. Interestingly, despite their often conservative stance, the Taliban also utilize modern technology like smartphones for administrative purposes and public outreach. This duality indicates a complex strategy, blending traditional values with contemporary tools to maintain and exert control. The international community continues to monitor these developments closely, expressing ongoing concerns about human rights and social freedoms. Ultimately, the BBC's findings offer a clear picture of how the Taliban are systematically reshaping Afghanistan according to their distinct ideology.

Words and grammar

1. in-depth report (noun phrase): A detailed and thorough study or analysis of a particular topic.
2. governance (noun): The way that an organization, country, or system is controlled and managed.
3. senior figures (noun phrase): Important and powerful people in an organization or government.
4. conservative stance (noun phrase): A position or attitude that favors traditional values and is resistant to change.
5. duality (noun): The state of having two parts, often opposite or contrasting, existing at the same time.

Grammar Focus: The Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action itself and what is affected by it, rather than on who or what is performing the action. It's often formed with a form of 'to be' + past participle. This structure is common when the agent is unknown, unimportant, or obvious, or when we want to emphasize the object of the action.
Example from the text: "new legal frameworks are being established concerning family matters"
