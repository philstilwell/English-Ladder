📅 August 29, 2026 - China's Hidden Flood Footage: Lack of Information on Tibet Victims

Read the story

Deadly flash flooding has recently caused significant devastation in parts of Tibet. However, some of the most dramatic footage depicting the severe impact of these floods is being kept hidden from public view within China. This means Chinese citizens have very limited access to the full visual scope of the disaster. International news agencies and human rights groups report that details about the victims and the overall extent of the damage remain scarce. Such tight control over media by the government often leads to the suppression of critical or negative news. Consequently, the world knows very little about the actual number of casualties or the families deeply affected. This practice is widely believed to be an attempt to manage public perception and prevent unrest. Many people outside China are largely unaware of the true humanitarian crisis unfolding in the region. This restricted information flow raises serious concerns about freedom of the press and the public's right to information. Therefore, getting a complete picture of the Tibet floods and their consequences is extremely difficult through official channels.

Words and grammar

1. scarce (adjective): Not enough of something; very little is available or accessible.
2. control over media (noun phrase): The power or authority to decide what news and information is shown, published, or broadcast.
3. unaware (adjective): Not knowing or realizing that something is happening or that something exists.
4. press (noun): news organisations and the journalists who work for them
5. consequences (plural noun): results or effects of actions or events
6. humanitarian (adjective): Concerned with or seeking to promote human welfare, especially by providing aid and support to people in need.
7. casualties (noun (plural)): people killed or injured in a war, accident, or other disaster or incident.
8. affected (verb): had an influence on or caused a change in someone or something

Grammar Focus: The Passive Voice
We use the passive voice when the action is more important than the person or thing doing the action, or when we don't know or don't want to mention who performed the action. It is formed with a form of 'to be' + the past participle of the main verb. In the continuous form, it uses 'to be' + 'being' + past participle.
Example from the text: "some of the most dramatic footage depicting the severe impact of these floods is being kept hidden from public view within China."
