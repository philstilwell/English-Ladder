Lesson 01: Clarifying comfort complaints and thermostat readings
Original fictional case: An office manager says the meeting room is 22 degrees Celsius. The number is the thermostat setpoint, not a confirmed ambient-temperature reading. Staff report feeling warm there after 14:00. No cause or equipment fault has been established.
Language workshop: Ask which number is being reported
Communication goal: Separate a control target, a temperature observation, and a comfort complaint.
Language guidance: A thermostat display may show different values. Ask what the existing label says rather than directing control changes. Comfort descriptions are useful information, but they do not by themselves identify a system fault.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): Does that display show ___ or ___? / The complaint concerns ___ after ___ . / The target is ___; the measured condition remains ___.
Vocabulary: thermostat: A device that senses temperature and requests heating or cooling according to its configured controls. / setpoint: The target value requested by a control, distinct from a measured condition. / ambient temperature: The temperature of the surrounding air at an identified place and time. / thermal zone: An area treated as a unit for heating or cooling control or assessment.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: You are the manager. The display says target 22 degrees Celsius, and staff feel warm after 14:00.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: The manager asks whether lowering the setting will solve the complaint. Keep the exchange to information gathering, not operating advice.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Is 22 degrees the target shown on the thermostat, or a measured room-temperature reading? I will record it as the setpoint if that is the display label. The staff's report concerns feeling warm in the meeting-room zone after 14:00. We still need to distinguish that comfort report from measured conditions and any technical finding.
END REFERENCE

Lesson 02: Identifying equipment and linked components
Original fictional case: A school request names AC-4, but the roof plan labels outdoor unit CU-4 and the indoor equipment register labels air handler AH-4. Their relationship is not confirmed. The facilities manager has the equipment register; learners must not access a roof or inspect equipment to obtain information.
Language workshop: Verify the asset rather than trusting a similar label
Communication goal: Resolve equipment identity using existing records and clear references.
Language guidance: Site asset tags, manufacturer model numbers, and serial numbers identify different things. Similar suffixes do not prove components are paired. Work from supplied records; do not ask learners to access plant, roofs, or enclosed machinery.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): The request says ___; the register identifies ___ . / Is that an asset tag or a ___? / The component relationship remains ___.
Vocabulary: air handler: Equipment that conditions or circulates air through a building system using components such as fans and coils. / condensing unit: An assembly containing components that reject heat as refrigerant condenses, commonly located separately from the indoor equipment. / evaporator: A component where refrigerant absorbs heat as it evaporates. / asset tag: A site-assigned identifier linking a particular equipment item to its location and records.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: You are the facilities manager. You have register entries but no confirmed component pairing.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: The manager says a photograph of a different site's unit looks identical. Explain why appearance is not an asset reference.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Could you confirm which asset AC-4 refers to in the request? The records list outdoor unit CU-4 and air handler AH-4, but their matching numbers do not establish that they serve the same system. Please provide the relevant equipment-register entries so we can verify the relationship before attaching the service history.
END REFERENCE

Technician: Then the reviewer can assess the actual proposal without treating this discussion as technical approval or a handling plan.
Facilities buyer: I'll present the designation difference for review, not as a proposal to alter the old system.
