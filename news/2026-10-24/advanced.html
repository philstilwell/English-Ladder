France Prepares for Nationwide Marches Following Severe Turmoil

Advanced · C1+

📅 October 24, 2026 - France Prepares for Nationwide Marches Following Severe Turmoil

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

France is on high alert ahead of a new wave of nationwide marches, just a week after experiencing some of its most severe turmoil in decades. Although authorities have imposed sweeping bans on demonstrations, hundreds of thousands of participants are still expected to converge on Paris and other major cities. Facing accusations of failing to foresee the extent of the earlier disorder, the government is drastically ramping up security by dispatching 70,000 personnel. The interior minister warned that security forces would be uncompromising with violent offenders, citing numerous online calls for violence from radical factions. The initial movement was ignited by high school students demanding better educational environments and urgent solutions to staff shortages. Although the French president recently met with student representatives and pledged clear commitments to them, these delegates have urged their peers to continue to mobilise. Several key municipalities have taken an aggressive regulatory stance by announcing blanket bans on all public gatherings ahead of the weekend. In Paris, a march supported by labour groups has been officially ordered to adhere to a route that entirely bypasses the city centre. Nevertheless, prominent cultural institutions in the capital have opted to close pre-emptively as businesses board up their windows to mitigate potential damage. What began as a student-led initiative has rapidly expanded to become a wider anti-government outpouring across the nation. Meanwhile, the national government has promised to reconstruct a severely damaged civic building entirely, ensuring that local taxpayers will not have to foot the bill.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. turmoil (noun): A state of great disturbance, confusion, or uncertainty.
2. sweeping (adjective): Extensive or wide-ranging in scope, effect, or application.
3. converge on (phrasal verb): To move towards a specific location from different directions and gather there.
4. foresee (verb): To be aware of beforehand; to predict or anticipate an event.
5. ramping up (phrasal verb): Substantially increasing the level, amount, or speed of an activity or effort.
6. uncompromising (adjective): Showing an unwillingness to make concessions or negotiate, particularly when enforcing rules.
7. ignited (verb): Sparked or started a heated situation, such as an argument, movement, or conflict.
8. blanket bans (noun): Absolute prohibitions that apply universally to all cases or situations without exception.
9. pre-emptively (adverb): Done beforehand to prevent an anticipated event or to mitigate its effects.
10. outpouring (noun): A sudden, robust expression of strong emotion or widespread public dissent.
11. foot the bill (idiom): To pay for something, especially a large or unexpected expense.

Advanced Grammar: Participle clauses replacing adverbial clauses
Advanced writers often use a present participle (an "-ing" verb) to introduce a dependent clause that explains the cause, background, or result of the main action. In this example, "Facing accusations..." replaces an adverbial clause of reason (like "Because the government was facing accusations..."), making the sentence more concise and cohesive while keeping the focus on the main action.
Example from the text: "Facing accusations of failing to foresee the extent of the earlier disorder, the government is drastically ramping up security by dispatching 70,000 personnel."
