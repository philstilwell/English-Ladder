Harper Lee First Edition Discovered in Oxfam Donation Joins British Library

📅 September 22, 2026 - Harper Lee First Edition Discovered in Oxfam Donation Joins British Library

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

An original US first edition of Harper Lee's renowned novel, "To Kill a Mockingbird," was recently discovered within a donation to an Oxfam charity shop. This significant literary find, the seminal 1960 work, emerged from a box of American books at the charity's Portobello Road bookshop in west London. The valuable copy has since been acquired by the British Library, where it is destined to enrich its extensive national collection. Olivia Haysman-Walker, the shop manager, identified this particular US first edition, noting its potential to have been easily overlooked. She explained that despite the sheer volume of incoming donations, staff recognized the iconic novel and decided to scrutinize it more closely. Oxfam personnel quickly discerned they were in possession of a truly exceptional item, recognizing its profound historical significance. Although its dust jacket bore the marks of age and was somewhat worn around the edges, the internal pages remained in pristine condition. Haysman-Walker expressed her amazement that such a valuable artifact journeyed from their bookshop into the prestigious British Library. She characterized this as 'quite a journey' and a 'lovely reminder' during Second Hand September of the unsuspected treasures lurking in charitable donations. Oxfam's annual Second Hand September initiative actively promotes the environmental and economic benefits of purchasing second-hand items. Lee's Pulitzer Prize-winning book chronicles the lawyer Atticus Finch's valiant defense of Tom Robinson, a Black man wrongly accused of raping a white woman. This profound narrative of societal injustice and childhood innocence, set in 1934 Alabama, has sold over 40 million copies globally and been translated into 40 languages. The celebrated US novelist, a recipient of the Presidential Medal of Freedom, passed away in 2016 at the venerable age of 89.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. renowned (adjective): known and admired by many people for its quality or achievement; famous.
2. artifact (noun): an object of historical or cultural interest, often valuable and discovered through careful investigation or circumstance.
3. destined to enrich (verb phrase): certain or planned to add to the value, quality, or significance of something.
4. extensive (adjective): covering a large area; great in amount or scale.
5. sheer volume (noun phrase): the immense quantity or amount of something, emphasizing its largeness.
6. scrutinize (verb): to examine or inspect something closely and thoroughly.
7. discerned (verb, past tense): perceived or recognized something, often with difficulty, by using one's senses or intellect.
8. profound historical significance (noun phrase): deep and far-reaching importance in relation to past events or developments.
9. pristine (adjective): in its original condition; unspoiled or spotless.
10. prestigious (adjective): inspiring respect and admiration; having high status.
11. chronicles (verb): records a series of events in a factual and detailed way.
12. venerable (adjective): accorded a great deal of respect, especially because of age, wisdom, or character.

Advanced Grammar: Participle Clauses for Additional Information
Participle clauses, often introduced by a present participle (the -ing form) or a past participle (the -ed form), function to add extra information about the main clause, describing a consequence, reason, or simultaneous action. They offer a more concise and sophisticated way to combine ideas, particularly in formal or journalistic writing, by reducing the need for separate clauses joined by conjunctions.
Example from the text: "Olivia Haysman-Walker, the shop manager, identified this particular US first edition, noting its potential to have been easily overlooked."
