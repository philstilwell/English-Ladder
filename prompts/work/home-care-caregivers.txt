Lesson 01: Confirming the visit and the care plan
Original fictional case: Caregiver Maya arrives at 9:00 for a two-hour visit with Mr. Ellis. Her assignment lists breakfast support and light household tasks. He expects her to drive him to a 10:00 appointment, but transport is not listed and she has no authorization to provide it. The coordinator is available by phone. Maya must establish the discrepancy, contact the coordinator with the client involved as appropriate, and continue agreed support within her role. Neither a phone message nor a request for help confirms a transport booking.
Language workshop: The appointment is real; the transport is unconfirmed
Communication goal: Clarify scheduled support, acknowledge an unmet expectation, and request authorization without promising an unassigned service.
Language guidance: An appointment time is not a transport booking. A transport request is not authorization for the caregiver to drive. Keep the appointment, departure arrangements, transport provider, and booking status distinct when asking the coordinator to resolve the discrepancy.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): Good morning, Mr. Ellis. I am here for your nine-to-eleven visit. / My assignment lists breakfast support and light household tasks. / What were you expecting for your appointment this morning? / I understand that getting there at ten matters to you. / Transport is not listed in the assignment I have. / I do not have authorization to drive you. / I can contact the coordinator now to check the arrangement. / Would you like to be part of that conversation? / There is a difference between the expected support and my recorded assignment. / Has transport actually been booked, or only requested? / Who is confirming the transport arrangement? / Please confirm any access requirements through the agreed process. / I cannot confirm a driver until the coordinator verifies the booking. / While we clarify that, would you like help with breakfast? / Let me check: the appointment is at ten, and transport is still unconfirmed. / I will record the request, who was contacted, and the confirmed response.
Vocabulary: care plan: A record of assessed needs and agreed support arrangements. / assignment: The particular work allocated to a worker. / visit window: The specified period for an expected visit. / scheduled duration: The planned length of a visit or service. / care coordinator: The person coordinating relevant care arrangements. / support need: An area in which a person requires assistance. / ADLs: Activities of daily living, such as dressing or eating. / IADLs: Instrumental activities of daily living, such as shopping or household management. / personal care: Support with personal daily activities within an agreed role. / domestic support: Agreed help with household activities. / light housekeeping: Limited household tasks within the assigned service. / appointment: A planned meeting or service at a specified time. / transport authorization: Permission for the particular transport arrangement. / escort: A person accompanying someone under an agreed arrangement. / scope of duties: The tasks permitted within the worker's actual role. / service discrepancy: A difference between expected and recorded service. / unmet expectation: Something expected that has not been confirmed or provided. / booking confirmation: Evidence that a particular service reservation is accepted. / accessible transport: Transport suited to the person's relevant access needs. / contingency arrangement: A prepared alternative for a possible disruption. / contact route: The approved way to reach the appropriate person. / visit record: The factual account of support and events during a visit. / authorized change: A change agreed through the required process. / read-back: Repeating key information to check that it was understood correctly.
Speaking task: Read Mr. Ellis and Maya aloud, then swap roles. Acknowledge the appointment before explaining the transport limit. Correct the ten gaps. Read back nine-to-eleven visit, ten-o-clock appointment, and unconfirmed transport as separate facts. Repeat the coordinator call and breakfast offer without blaming the client or promising a private lift.
Partner: Mr. Ellis: Client / Maya: Caregiver
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A 14:00-15:00 visit includes household support. The client expects a parcel pickup, which is not assigned. The coordinator can clarify it; no pickup has been booked.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Mr. Ellis: Thank you. I can explain what I was told last week, and you can explain what is on your assignment this morning.
Maya: That will help us report the service discrepancy fairly. I will keep your account separate from what the current record says, so the coordinator can reconcile them.
Mr. Ellis: Please put the outcome in the notes as well. The next person should not have to start the same conversation from the beginning.
Maya: I will update the visit record with the request, contact, and confirmed response. Until we have that response, I will describe transport as unconfirmed rather than booked.
END REFERENCE

Give up to three focused suggestions. Ask me to retry the weakest turn; wait. Only then offer an alternative wording and a harder replay.

Dialogue 08: Explaining a service problem without blaming the client
Original fictional setting: A coordinator and caregiver reconstruct an access delay to give the client an accurate response.
Professional roles: Coordinator / Caregiver
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Coordinator: The client says we marked the visit as missed because she wasn't home, but she says she was waiting inside the whole time.
Caregiver: My actual observation was that the approved entry method failed and I received no response through the normal contact route at nine.
Coordinator: So the record should describe no access and no response, not establish that the client was away from the home at that time.
Caregiver: Correct. I cannot know where she was from that information alone. I need to correct the broader conclusion in the visit record.
Coordinator: Which steps did you take after that, and can you provide the times without adding a reason that nobody verified during the incident?
Caregiver: I contacted the coordinator on duty through the approved procedure at nine five and followed the instructions I received for the access problem.
Coordinator: The response to the client should acknowledge the disruption and clarify the inaccurate wording without claiming the entry issue was her fault.
Caregiver: I can explain that the record overstated what we knew, apologize for that wording, and describe the actual sequence through the authorized channel.
Coordinator: Good. Don't promise a particular complaint outcome or a future service change that has not been reviewed and approved through our process.
Caregiver: I'll confirm that the concern has been passed for review and identify how she will receive the next update about the access arrangement.
Coordinator: Please also ensure the next caregiver sees the verified entry instructions, not an informal suggestion from a neighbor or an assumption from this incident.
Caregiver: I'll use the approved record and request acknowledgment, so both the corrected account and the current authorized instructions reach the next person clearly.
Vocabulary in this exchange: observation: Something a worker directly sees, hears, or otherwise notices. / acknowledgment: Confirmation that a message or concern has been received, not necessarily resolved.
