Milburn Review Could Reshape Welfare Support for Under-25s

Advanced · C1+

📅 November 12, 2026 - Milburn Review Could Reshape Welfare Support for Under-25s

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

A review commissioned by the government is expected to recommend a radical overhaul that could reduce some young people's welfare entitlements. According to the Times, Alan Milburn's report will recommend moving under-25s off the main sickness and disability benefits. The government has tasked Milburn, a former Labour health secretary, with helping reduce the proportion of 18- to 24-year-olds outside education, employment or training, reported as one in eight. The report is expected to propose ending under-25s' eligibility for the health element of universal credit and for personal independence payment, both forms of state support. A replacement allowance would match the basic universal credit rate. Some people could receive additional help with everyday expenses associated with their conditions. Those with more serious disabilities who are unable to work would receive a new payment at a higher rate. Milburn is also expected to recommend billions in employment support, including work placements, job subsidies and individual advisers. Milburn frames the central choice as whether to prioritise young people's longer-term life chances or their immediate financial circumstances. He has warned that the changes would involve difficult consequences for both existing and new recipients. The proposed approach would combine incentives with sanctions, since young people could lose some support if they did not engage with the additional employment services. Milburn says substantial spending would be needed to rebuild the system from scratch. Were Milburn's recommendations to be approved, monetary savings for the British state could still take almost a decade to emerge. A government spokesperson has promised a safety net for the most vulnerable and those unable to work. The government is expected to pursue legislation implementing the plans in the new year.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. commissioned (verb, past participle): Formally requested by an organisation or authority to investigate an issue or produce a report.
2. radical overhaul (noun phrase): A thorough redesign that changes how a system operates, rather than adjusting individual details.
3. entitlements (plural noun): Forms of support that people have a right to receive under the applicable rules.
4. subsidies (plural noun): Public funds used to cover part of a cost, here to encourage employment opportunities.
5. frames (verb, third-person singular present): Presents an issue in a particular way that directs attention towards certain choices or considerations.
6. incentives (plural noun): Opportunities or advantages intended to encourage a particular action.
7. sanctions (plural noun): Penalties for failing to meet requirements, here the possible loss of some welfare support.
8. from scratch (adverbial phrase): Starting again with the fundamental design rather than simply modifying existing arrangements.
9. monetary savings (plural noun phrase): Reductions in the amount of money spent, distinguished from other possible improvements.
10. safety net (noun phrase): Support intended to prevent vulnerable people from being left without essential help.

Advanced Grammar: Conditional inversion with “were” and a to-infinitive
Use “were + subject + to-infinitive” in place of “if + subject + were to + infinitive” for a formal hypothetical future condition. Here, approval remains uncertain, while “could” also qualifies the projected timescale for savings.
Example from the text: "Were Milburn's recommendations to be approved, monetary savings for the British state could still take almost a decade to emerge."
