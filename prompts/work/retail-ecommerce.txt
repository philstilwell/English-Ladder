Lesson 01: Merchandising and Assortment Planning
Original fictional case: During the same four-week season, Range A sold 80 of its 100 opening units for $12,000. Range B sold 120 of its 200 opening units, also for $12,000. There were no receipts, returns, or stock adjustments. A sold at full price; B required markdowns. For this review, unit sell-through means units sold divided by opening units. Buyer Inez and planner Cal must discuss next season without assuming that equal revenue means equal performance. Product costs and customer-segment evidence are not yet available.
Language workshop: Equal revenue, different stock exposure
Communication goal: Compare product ranges using a defined sell-through measure and a balanced buying recommendation.
Language guidance: Sell-through definitions differ. This case uses units sold divided by opening units, with no other stock movements. A is 80%; B is 60%. Do not quietly substitute revenue or remaining units as the denominator.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): Both ranges generated $12,000 in the same four weeks. / Here, sell-through uses units sold divided by opening stock. / Range A sold through at eighty percent. / Range B still has eighty units on hand. / B may serve a different customer segment. / More units sold does not necessarily mean more revenue. / B reached the same revenue with markdowns. / Can we add product costs before comparing gross profit? / This is not enough evidence to delist the whole range. / Let us examine B by size and colorway. / The rate is lower even though unit sales are higher. / Keep both ranges on the same four-week basis. / I recommend a more targeted buy, subject to the missing evidence. / A proposal is not a released purchase order. / We still need to understand which customers bought B. / The same revenue came with different stock and price exposure.
Vocabulary: assortment: The selection of products offered to customers. / range: A related group of products offered for sale. / stock-keeping unit (SKU): An internal identifier for a specific stocked item or variant. / sell-through: Units sold as a proportion of a defined stock base. / opening stock: Inventory held at the start of a period. / residual stock: Inventory remaining after the measured selling period. / markdown: A reduction from an earlier selling price. / full-price sale: A sale at the regular undiscounted price. / option count: The number of distinct product choices in a range. / assortment breadth: The variety of categories or product choices offered. / assortment depth: The variety of options within a product category or line, distinct from units held per option. / size curve: The planned distribution of units across sizes. / colorway: A particular color version of a product. / core line: A recurring product line central to the offer. / seasonal line: A line intended for a particular selling season. / range review: A structured assessment of the product selection. / buying budget: Funds allocated to purchasing merchandise. / open-to-buy: A planned merchandise purchasing allowance after commitments. / stock turn: A measure of how frequently inventory is sold or replaced. / weeks of cover: Stock expressed in weeks at a specified sales rate. / gross margin return on inventory: Gross profit relative to average inventory cost. / delist: Remove an item from the active product selection. / range rationalization: Simplifying the assortment using stated commercial criteria. / customer segment: A group of customers with relevant shared characteristics.
Speaking task: Read turns 1-10; contrast units sold, sell-through, and remaining stock. Swap roles for turns 11-20; keep the next buy conditional on the cost and variant review. Complete the transfer and check each range against its own opening stock.
Partner: Inez: Buyer / Cal: Merchandise planner
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: Range C sells 45 of 60 opening units. Range D sells 60 of 120 opening units. There are no other stock movements. Use the same unit sell-through definition.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Inez: Good. Keep the four-week dates visible so we do not compare a full season with a partial one.
Cal: The range review will show common dates, both denominators, the price reductions, and the outstanding analysis. That makes the recommendation easier to challenge constructively.
Inez: Hold the repeat order, then. Bring me the size and color breakdown with costs before we decide which options to keep.
Cal: Correct. We can revisit assortment depth after that review, alongside quantities per option. Equal revenue is the starting observation, not the final buying decision.

Lesson 02: Pricing, Promotions, and Margin
Original fictional case: In the baseline week, a product sold 100 units at $50 each. During the promotion it sold 120 units at $42.50 each, a 15% price reduction. Product cost remained $30 per unit. These are complete comparable weekly sales with no returns. Taxes, shipping income, operating expenses, and other promotional costs are excluded.
