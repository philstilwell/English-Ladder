📅 October 04, 2026 - Kyiv Bridges Targeted in Drone Attacks as German Chancellor Visits

Read the story

The latest Russian unmanned aerial vehicle attack on Kyiv's Pivnichyi (Northern) Bridge coincided with a surprise visit from German Chancellor Friedrich Merz to the Ukrainian capital. Video verified by the BBC shows a drone hitting Pivnichnyi Bridge as vehicles cross, producing a fireball and black smoke. This attack marked the fourth successive day that major bridges in Kyiv have been targeted by Russian aerial assaults, as Moscow ramps up attacks on critical infrastructure in Ukraine's capital. Two individuals sustained non-fatal injuries when the Pivnichyi Bridge was struck for the second day in a row, with the Pivdennyi (Southern) Bridge also having been targeted multiple times earlier in the week. Other images show a gaping hole in a traffic lane on the bridge. Many of Kyiv’s estimated three million residents travel between the Dnipro’s banks, relying heavily on metro lines and trains. Although both damaged bridges remain standing, damage and traffic restrictions have at times caused gridlock in parts of Kyiv. A resident told the BBC that travelling between the river’s banks had become extremely difficult. Repair teams are actively addressing the damage, and officials are exploring contingency measures like temporary pontoon bridges, ferry services, and even a river tunnel to ensure Kyiv remains connected. Beyond the bridge attacks, other Russian aerial bombardments on Sunday damaged a heating main, causing a temporary hot water shortage in the Desnyanskyi district. Additionally, Russian drones struck a warehouse in Sumy, claiming one life, as reported by President Zelensky. Ukraine's Air Force reported that out of 135 unmanned aerial vehicles launched overnight by Russia, including 52 jet-powered variants, 125 projectiles were successfully intercepted. As emergency services responded, German Chancellor Merz met President Zelensky in Kyiv, and they laid wreaths for Ukrainian soldiers killed in the war. Merz reaffirmed Germany’s support, saying Ukraine could rely on his country. He also described the assistance as a matter of self-interest because Germany’s security depended on the war’s outcome. Since Merz became chancellor in May 2025, Germany has become Ukraine’s largest single donor, or benefactor. President Zelensky has consistently appealed to Western allies for more sophisticated military assistance, specifically requesting advanced interceptors capable of shooting down newer jet-powered Russian unmanned aerial vehicles and ballistic missiles. Russia's defense ministry declared its intention to intensify its campaign across Ukraine, framing it as a response to Zelensky's earlier remarks about escalating attacks on Russian oil refineries. The Russian foreign ministry reiterated a warning for foreign citizens, including diplomats, to leave Kyiv. EU ambassador Katarína Mathernová said the EU delegation would stay despite the warning. German ambassador Boris Ruge also said his delegation would stay. Experts assess that while Russian aerial assaults are unlikely to demolish the bridges outright, sustained targeting risks forcing their closure and weakening their structural integrity, ultimately rendering them unusable.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. ramps up (verb phrase): Increases the amount or intensity of something; makes something more active.
2. gaping hole (noun phrase): A very large, wide-open hole, often implying significant damage or emptiness.
3. relying heavily on (verb phrase): Depending significantly or predominantly on something or someone.
4. gridlock (noun): A situation where a network of roads becomes so congested with traffic that all movement stops.
5. contingency measures (noun phrase): Plans or actions taken to prepare for possible future events or problems.
6. reaffirmed (verb): Stated again in a definite way; confirmed a previous commitment or belief.
7. self-interest (noun): Concern for one's own advantage and well-being.
8. benefactor (noun): A person or organization that gives money or other help to a person or cause, often financial.
9. interceptors (noun): Aircraft or missiles designed to detect and destroy enemy aircraft or missiles.
10. ballistic missiles (noun phrase): Missiles that travel along a high, curved path for much of their flight.
11. reiterated (verb): Said again or repeated, often for emphasis or clarity.

Advanced Grammar: Participle Clauses for Results
An “-ing” clause can describe a result. In this example, “producing a fireball and black smoke” gives the result of the collision, not of the BBC checking the video. Other “-ing” clauses may describe accompanying actions; their meaning depends on context.
Example from the text: "Video verified by the BBC shows a drone hitting Pivnichnyi Bridge as vehicles cross, producing a fireball and black smoke."
