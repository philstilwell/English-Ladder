📅 October 25, 2026 - Mass Protests and Clashes Grip France Ahead of Elections

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

French officials confirmed that nearly 1,000 arrests were made as hundreds of thousands of people participated in another day of protests across the nation. Approximately 70,000 police officers were deployed to prevent the latest protests from descending into the kind of chaos that previously saw officers outnumbered. Officials have so far managed to avoid rioting and looting on the scale seen last weekend, averting the worst scenes of disorder. Furthermore, President Emmanuel Macron decreed that only demonstrations organised by recognised trade unions would be legally permitted. Despite these measures, the interior minister noted that several thousand radical activists gathered at the rear of the march. Tensions rose as police baton-charged groups of masked youths who were smashing windows and pelting officers with various objects. In the southern port of Marseille, rubbish bins were set ablaze and metal sheets were used to block streets. Meanwhile, authorities in Lyon utilised non-lethal grenades to scatter a crowd that had reportedly gathered in an area deemed off-limits by the local prefecture. In Barcelona, over 100 people were arrested after a student protest resulted in what local authorities described as gratuitous violence. The continuing unrest comes ahead of presidential elections in six months' time, with leading contenders levelling the blame directly at Macron.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. deployed (verb): Positioned or distributed military or police personnel strategically for action.
2. descending into (phrasal verb): Gradually worsening or changing into a state of severe disorder.
3. rioting (noun): The act of taking part in a violent public disturbance alongside a crowd.
4. decreed (verb): Officially ordered or commanded an action by a formal legal authority.
5. radical (adjective): Characterised by a desire for extreme political or social reform.
6. pelting (verb): Repeatedly throwing items or objects at someone or something.
7. ablaze (adjective): Burning fiercely or intensely on fire.
8. off-limits (adjective): Legally or officially restricted from access by the general public.
9. gratuitous (adjective): Done without good reason; uncalled for or lacking valid justification.
10. contenders (noun): Individuals or groups who are competing for a championship or political position.

Advanced Grammar: "With"-participle clauses
The preposition "with" can introduce a participle clause to describe circumstances happening at the same time as the main action. In this structure, "with" is followed by a noun phrase and an -ing or -ed participle. This efficiently provides crucial background context without needing a completely separate sentence.
Example from the text: "The continuing unrest comes ahead of presidential elections in six months' time, with leading contenders levelling the blame directly at Macron."
