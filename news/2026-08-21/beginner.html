Israel Re-establishes West Bank Settlement

Read the story

Israel has reopened a place in the West Bank. This place is called a settlement. The settlement was closed for a long time. Now, Israel re-establishes it. Many countries around the world disagree with this. There are international protests about the decision. Thirty pioneer families have come to live in the settlement. The Israeli government supports these new families. People living nearby, who are Palestinian residents, are worried. They feel fearful about the future.

Words and grammar

1. settlement (noun): A place where people start to live, often in a new area.
2. re-establishes (verb): To start something again that was stopped or closed before.
3. international protests (noun phrase): When many people from different countries show they are unhappy about something.
4. pioneer families (noun phrase): The first families to move to and live in a new area.
5. fearful (adjective): Feeling afraid or worried about something bad happening.

Grammar Focus: Simple Present Tense
We use the simple present tense to talk about facts, habits, or things that are true now. We also use it for things that happen regularly.
Example from the text: "Now, Israel re-establishes it."
