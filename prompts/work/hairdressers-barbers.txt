Lesson 01: Turning a reference photo into a clear request
Original fictional case: A client shows a chin-length bob but wants to keep enough length for a low ponytail. No cut has started. The photo has a straight fringe; the client has not requested one. Clarify which features matter and confirm a collarbone-length outline.
Language workshop: Ask about features, not just the whole photograph
Communication goal: Resolve conflicting length preferences before a haircut.
Language guidance: A photograph contains several choices. Separate length, outline, and fringe; use an indirect question to find the feature the client means.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): Which part of the photo would you like to keep? / When you say short, do you mean ___? / Could you confirm whether you want ___?
Vocabulary: consultation: A discussion with a client to establish the desired hair service, preferences, and limits. / reference photo: An image used to discuss a desired hairstyle, not a guarantee of an identical result. / perimeter: The outer outline of a haircut, including the visible line at the ends. / fringe: Hair cut to fall over the forehead; commonly called bangs in US English.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Client: you like the photo's neat outline, not its short length or fringe; you need a low ponytail.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: The client points to the fringe while saying 'this shape.' Ask whether the gesture changes the earlier preference.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Your photo has a shorter outline and a straight fringe, but keeping a low ponytail matters to you. Which feature of the photo do you want to keep? We can discuss a collarbone-length outline instead. Before we start, please confirm whether you want any change around your forehead.
END REFERENCE

Lesson 02: Describing shape, texture, and everyday styling
Original fictional case: A client wants less fullness at the neckline but does not want exposed skin at the sides. Photo A, labelled 'low taper,' shows visible hair covering the sides. Photo B, labelled 'skin fade,' shows exposed skin at the shortest area of the sides. The client spends five minutes styling each morning. Compare these supplied appearances, not cutting methods or guaranteed upkeep.
Language workshop: Compare the feature that matters
Communication goal: Use the same appearance and routine criteria for two styles.
Language guidance: Names alone do not fix an exact length. Compare what is visible in the supplied images, then check the client's priorities without teaching a cutting method.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): In this photo, ___, whereas in that one, ___. / Both give ___, but only this image shows ___. / How much time do you normally spend on ___?
Vocabulary: taper: A gradual change from longer to shorter hair, often around the sides and neckline. / fade: A haircut with a blended transition from very short hair to longer hair above. / layers: Sections cut at different lengths to create shape or movement within a hairstyle. / natural texture: The hair's usual straight, wavy, curly, or coily pattern without deliberate reshaping.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Client: prefer covered sides and less fullness at the neckline; you style for five minutes in the morning.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: The client assumes 'low' means the same length in both photos. Clarify the actual area being compared.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: In these photos, the low taper keeps more visible hair at the sides, whereas the skin fade exposes skin at the shortest area. You said you want less fullness at the neckline without that exposed look. Let's discuss the taper image as a reference and check how it fits your usual five-minute routine.
END REFERENCE
