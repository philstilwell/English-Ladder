Lesson 01: Confirming the delivery destination
Original fictional case: Driver Leo is safely parked and calls receiving contact Hana about shipment 418 for Alder Books, 26 Mill Street, suite 4. The route note says rear annex. Hana confirms that the rear annex belongs to suite 6 and that suite 4 receives through its staffed front desk. Leo must report the conflict to dispatch for correction. The shipment has not been handed over, and this call does not itself update the route record or authorize a different delivery destination.
Language workshop: The right address, the wrong entrance
Communication goal: Resolve conflicting location information through specific questions, a complete read-back, and a request to correct the record.
Language guidance: The street address is not the dispute. Suite 4 and suite 6 use different receiving points. The shipment remains for Alder Books in suite 4; identifying the front desk does not transfer the shipment to another consignee.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): I am calling about shipment 418 for Alder Books. / Is the address 26 Mill Street? / The shipment lists suite 4; is that your unit? / My route note says rear annex. / The rear annex belongs to suite 6. / Where does suite 4 receive deliveries? / We use the staffed front desk. / Alder Books, suite 4, staffed front desk; correct? / The recipient has not changed. / Suite 6 is a different unit. / I will ask dispatch to correct the note. / The route record has not been amended yet. / The shipment has not been handed over. / Are you the receiving contact for suite 4? / I want to avoid treating the annex as your receiving point. / I have the corrected information to report to dispatch.
Vocabulary: consignee: Named party intended to receive a shipment. / shipper: Party sending the goods. / carrier: Business transporting the shipment. / shipment reference: Identifier used to locate a particular shipment. / delivery address: Stated location to which goods are to be delivered. / suite number: Identifier for a separate unit within a building. / receiving point: Specific place where the intended recipient receives goods. / route note: Written information supplied for a delivery stop. / rear annex: Additional part of a building located at the back. / staffed front desk: Front reception point with personnel present. / entrance: Access point into a building or site. / address line: Separate part of a written address. / dispatch: Team coordinating vehicles, drivers, and delivery instructions. / receiving contact: Person contacted about receiving a shipment. / location conflict: Inconsistency between two descriptions of a destination. / read-back: Spoken repetition used to check exact information. / destination correction: Amendment to incorrect destination information. / site instruction: Direction applying at a particular receiving location. / delivery record: Recorded information about a delivery and its status. / handover: Transfer of goods to the appropriate receiving party. / stop number: Identifier for one location in a planned route sequence. / vehicle profile: Vehicle-specific dimensions and restrictions entered for route planning. / clearance: Available space around or above a vehicle; height must match the specific route. / record amendment: Authorized change to existing recorded information.
Speaking task: Read the full destination: Alder Books, 26 Mill Street, suite 4, staffed front desk. Contrast suite 4 with suite 6 and the rear annex in turns 4 and 8. Read the final record status without claiming dispatch has amended it or goods were handed over.
Partner: Leo: Delivery driver / Hana: Receiving contact
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: Shipment 529 names Birch Print, suite 2 at 14 Oak Lane. A side-door note belongs to suite 3. The receiving contact confirms suite 2 uses the main reception. Dispatch correction is pending.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Leo: Thanks. I'll keep the delivery open while I speak with dispatch. This call hasn't completed the stop.
Hana: Right, no handover has taken place. You've confirmed where our receiving point is.
Leo: So I have corrected information to pass on, but the old route note is still there until dispatch amends it.
Hana: Exactly. The record amendment is pending. Suite four, staffed front desk is the receiving information to report.

Lesson 02: Checking shipment counts
Original fictional case: At Finch Office Supplies, driver Priya and receiver Ben compare a bill of lading listing twelve cartons with their physical count of eleven. They agree on eleven present cartons. The contents have not been checked, and dispatch has not located a twelfth carton. The difference is one carton against the listed quantity, not a verified count of missing individual products. They need a factual discrepancy report and a dispatch follow-up, not a guess about where the carton was lost.
Language workshop: Twelve on paper, eleven at receiving
Communication goal: Compare document and physical quantities, distinguish cartons from contents, and report an unresolved shortage without assigning blame.
Language guidance: Twelve listed cartons minus eleven counted cartons gives a one-carton discrepancy. That calculation says nothing about the number of individual products missing or the contents of the cartons present, because no contents check has occurred.

Lesson 08: Closing a route with a clear handoff
Original fictional case: Route 12 is ending. Driver Luis tells evening dispatcher Jo that stop 7 was received by Mei at 16:05, but the receipt has not uploaded. Stop 8 was refused because the customer reported a duplicate order; that reported reason has not been independently verified. Jo accepts responsibility for checking the stop-7 receipt upload and contacting customer service about stop 8. Neither follow-up is completed during this handover, and no refund, replacement delivery, or final route closure is established.
Language workshop: One missing upload, one refusal
Communication goal: Hand over distinct delivery outcomes and unfinished records, attributing a reported reason and assigning each follow-up explicitly.
Language guidance: Stop 7 was received by Mei at 16:05; its receipt upload is missing. Stop 8 was refused with a customer-reported duplicate-order reason. These are different outcomes, not two undelivered shipments or two completed follow-ups.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): I am handing over the open items from route 12. / Stop 7 and stop 8 have different issues. / Mei received stop 7 at 16:05. / The receipt has not uploaded. / The missing upload does not mean the goods were not received. / Stop 8 was refused. / The customer reported a duplicate order. / That reason has not been independently verified. / Please check the stop-7 receipt upload. / Please contact customer service about stop 8. / I will take both follow-ups. / Taking ownership does not complete either check. / Please repeat the two actions and their current status. / No refund or replacement delivery is confirmed. / Keep 16:05 with the stop-7 receiving record. / Jo owns both open items; their outcomes remain pending.
Vocabulary: route handoff: Transfer of route information and unfinished responsibilities. / route closure: Completion of the required route-ending process. / stop outcome: What actually happened at an individual delivery stop. / received by: Phrase naming the person who received goods. / receipt timestamp: Time associated with the receiving event. / delivery receipt: Record associated with the receiving event. / proof of delivery: Evidence of delivery under the applicable process, often POD. / receipt upload: Transfer of the receipt into the required electronic system. / upload pending: Status indicating the electronic transfer is unfinished. / synchronization: Updating records between devices or systems. / record completeness: Whether all required information and records are present. / missing document: Required record not currently available where expected. / refused delivery: Delivery the intended receiving party declines to accept. / refusal reason: Explanation given for declining a delivery. / reported duplicate: Alleged repetition of an order, not independently verified here. / customer service: Team handling customer queries and resolution processes. / independent verification: Check separate from the original person's statement. / open item: Task or question still needing action. / action owner: Person responsible for the next defined task. / ownership acceptance: Agreement to take responsibility for follow-up. / completion status: Whether a task has actually been finished. / route summary: Concise account of outcomes across a delivery route. / read-back confirmation: Repetition that checks the accuracy of a handoff. / resolution: Established outcome addressing an open issue.

Dialogue 08: The evening desk challenges a vague status
Original fictional setting: A driver and evening dispatcher resolve an ambiguous end-of-day route entry.
Professional roles: Dispatcher / Driver
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Dispatcher: Your final stop says done, but a contents query remains open.
Driver: The physical handover is complete. The customer received six cartons, while the order query concerns contents they expected separately.
Dispatcher: Did you verify the contents yourself, or are you passing on the customer's report?
Driver: I'm passing on their report. We confirmed six cartons, but I didn't verify the expected items inside them.
Dispatcher: Then done is too broad for our handoff. We need to distinguish completed delivery from an unresolved order question.
Driver: Agreed. I'll state six cartons received by Omar at five ten, with a separate reported contents query outstanding.
Dispatcher: I can send that query to customer service. What reference should they use to connect it to the receipt?
Driver: Use shipment Q14. The receipt is already available, so this isn't a missing-upload issue like yesterday's stop.
Dispatcher: I have Q14 and its proof of delivery. I'll link the query to that shipment.
Driver: Please don't tell the customer their whole order is complete solely because the delivery receipt is available.
Dispatcher: Understood. I'll take the customer-service follow-up and keep the contents query open until they respond.
Driver: That accurately separates what happened at the door from what customer service still needs to establish.
Vocabulary in this exchange: delivery receipt: A record acknowledging delivery and any recorded qualifications about it. / proof of delivery: A record supporting a delivery event, such as named receipt or other evidence required for that service.
