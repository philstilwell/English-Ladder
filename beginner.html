📅 July 27, 2026 [0 days, 0 hours old] - Oil Price Change: US and Iran Talks
The price of oil went down because the US and Iran stopped fighting to talk.

Beginner ESL: International News: Oil Prices and Diplomacy

I. The News Brief

The price of oil went down today. This happened because of news from the US and Iran. The US and Iran will pause attacks for now. They want to have talks about the situation. The US said it halted military actions. They stopped to give space for these talks. Many people have hopes for peace. This decision gives hopes that the conflict can end. A peaceful resolution might be possible soon. This news helps calm the situation between the two countries.

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. pause attacks (verb phrase): To stop fighting or hurting someone for a short time.
2. talks (noun): Formal discussions between people or countries to solve problems.
3. halted (verb): Stopped completely; brought to a stop.
4. hopes (noun): Feelings of wanting good things to happen in the future.
5. conflict (noun): A serious disagreement or fight between people or groups.

Grammar Focus: Simple Present Tense for News
We often use the Simple Present tense to talk about news headlines or current events that are happening now or are generally true. It makes the news sound fresh and immediate.
Example from the text: "The price of oil went down today."

📅 July 26, 2026 [1 day, 1 hour old] - Berlin Pride Attack: Police Search for Suspect
Police are looking for a person after a car hit people at a Pride event in Berlin, Germany.

Beginner ESL: Safety at Public Events and Police Investigations

I. The News Brief

A bad event happened in Berlin, Germany. It was on Saturday night. Many people were at a Pride event. A car rammed into the crowd of people. This made many people scared. Police started a manhunt for a suspect. The suspect drove the car. Police are now looking for this person. The event happened in the German capital. Police want to find out why this happened.

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. rammed (verb): hit something very hard with force
2. crowd (noun): a large group of people together in one place
3. manhunt (noun): a big search by police for a person
4. suspect (noun): a person who police think did a crime
5. capital (noun): the most important city in a country, where the government is

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense (Regular Verbs)
We use the simple past tense to talk about actions or events that are finished. For many verbs, we add -ed at the end to make them past tense. For example, 'walk' becomes 'walked', 'start' becomes 'started', and 'ram' becomes 'rammed'.
Example from the text: "A car rammed into the crowd of people."

Example from the text: "The shooting happened in April."

📅 July 24, 2026 [3 days, 1 hour old] - Wildfires Spread in France: Thousands Evacuated
Thousands of people left a French tourist area because of big fires.

Beginner ESL: Wildfires and Evacuations in France

I. The News Brief

Big fires are burning in France. These are called wildfires. The wildfires started several days ago. They spread quickly in the area. This area is a popular tourist spot. Many people were on holiday there. Officials told people to leave. They ordered everyone to evacuate. Tens of thousands of people left the area. The fires are still a big danger.

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. wildfires (noun): Large, uncontrolled fires that burn in natural areas, often in forests or dry fields.
2. spread (verb): To move or extend over a larger area, or to become wider or more common.
3. tourist spot (noun phrase): A place that many people visit for holidays, recreation, or sightseeing.
4. officials (noun): People who have an important position or authority, especially in a government or organization.
5. evacuate (verb): To leave a dangerous place quickly and safely, often because of an emergency like a fire or flood.

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense
We use the Simple Past tense to talk about actions that started and finished at a specific time in the past. We often add '-ed' to regular verbs, but many common verbs have irregular forms, like 'tell' becoming 'told'.
Example from the text: "Officials told people to leave."

Beginner ESL: International Cooperation on Nuclear Energy

I. The News Brief

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

3. co-operation (noun): The act of working together with others to achieve a shared goal or to help each other.
4. access (noun): The ability or right to enter, use, or get something, like a place or an opportunity.
5. nuclear energy (noun): Power that is created when atoms are split apart, often used to produce electricity.

Grammar Focus: Simple Present Tense for facts
We use the Simple Present tense to talk about things that are always true or happen regularly. It is used for facts and general statements that do not change.
Example from the text: "The US Department of Energy says this is important."

Beginner ESL: International Trade Disputes and Tariffs

President Trump made a new decision. He put new taxes on goods from Canada. These taxes are called tariffs. The tariffs are very high, 50%. This is a big problem for trade. Trade tensions are growing between the two countries. Canada and the USA are North American neighbours. Mark Carney wants to talk more. He wants to intensify trade talks. 3. trade (noun): The buying and selling of goods and services between people or countries.
4. tensions (noun): A feeling of anger or disagreement between people or countries.
5. intensify (verb): To make something stronger or more serious.

Grammar Focus: Simple Present Tense
We use the simple present tense for things that happen regularly, or for facts and general truths. It describes what is true now. For example, 'The sun rises every day.'
