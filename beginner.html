📅 August 16, 2026 [0 days, 0 hours old] - News from Ukraine: Attacks and Accusations
Russia launched new attacks in Ukraine, and five people died.

Beginner ESL: War in Ukraine

I. The News Brief

Russia launched new aerial attacks. Five people were killed in these attacks. The attacks happened across Ukraine. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky spoke about the attacks. He accuses Russia of targeting civilian infrastructure. Civilian infrastructure means things like houses and roads. Moscow reports that a warehouse was hit. Aerial attacks come from the air. Zelensky said Russia attacked places where people live, while Moscow said a warehouse was hit. So, Ukraine accuses Russia, while Russia reports a different story.

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. accuses (verb): to say that someone did something wrong.
2. reports (verb): to give information about something, often in the news.
3. aerial (adjective): from or in the air.
4. across (preposition): in or to every part of a place.
5. civilian infrastructure (noun phrase): buildings and systems that help people in a city, like homes, roads, and hospitals.

Grammar Focus: Using 'while' to show contrast
We use the word 'while' to connect two different ideas or actions that happen at the same time. It shows a contrast between them.
Example from the text: "Zelensky said Russia attacked places where people live, while Moscow said a warehouse was hit."

📅 August 15, 2026 [1 day, 0 hours old] - Indonesia Earthquake: Rescue Efforts Underway

Beginner ESL: Natural Disasters: Earthquakes and Rescue

I. The News Brief

A powerful earthquake hit Indonesia. It was a 7.7-magnitude earthquake. This earthquake killed at least 38 people. Many people died in the disaster. The earthquake happened in eastern Indonesia. The place is an island called Flores. Now, a search and rescue operation is under way. Workers want to find survivors. They are looking for people who are still alive. This operation is very important for the people of Flores.

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. powerful (adjective): very strong; having great strength or force.
2. earthquake (noun): A sudden violent shaking of the ground, sometimes causing great destruction.
3. killed (verb (past tense)): Caused someone or something to die; took life away.
4. rescue operation (noun phrase): Organized work to save people from danger or a difficult situation.
5. survivors (noun): People who are still alive after a dangerous event or accident.

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense (Regular Verbs)
We use the simple past tense to talk about actions that started and finished in the past. For many verbs, we add '-ed' to the end to make them past tense.
Example from the text: "This earthquake killed at least 38 people."

📅 August 14, 2026 [1 day, 23 hours old] - Heavy Rain in Japan: Deaths and Disruptions
Strong rain in Japan caused problems, including deaths and power cuts.

Beginner ESL: Natural Disasters and Weather Events

I. The News Brief

Japan had very heavy rain recently. This rain was "unprecedented", meaning it was very unusual. Eight people were killed because of the strong rain. The weather caused a big storm. This storm cut power to many homes. More than 20,000 households lost electricity. Also, 7,000 people were stranded at Narita airport. They could not leave the airport. The strong rain made many problems for people. It shows how dangerous bad weather can be.

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. unprecedented (adjective): Never happened before; very unusual.
2. killed (verb (past tense)): Caused someone to die.
3. storm (noun): Very bad weather with strong wind and rain.
4. cut power (verb phrase): Stopped electricity from working.
5. stranded (adjective): Unable to leave a place because of a problem.

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense
We use the simple past tense to talk about actions that finished in the past. We often add '-ed' to regular verbs. Irregular verbs change their form, like 'is' becoming 'was' or 'are' becoming 'were'.
Example from the text: "Eight people were killed because of the strong rain."

📅 August 13, 2026 [2 days, 23 hours old] - Nobel Winner: Putin Cannot Win in Ukraine
A Nobel Peace Prize winner says Russia's leader cannot win the war in Ukraine.

Beginner ESL: The war in Ukraine and statements by a Nobel Peace Prize winner.

I. The News Brief

Dmitry Muratov is a Nobel Peace Prize winner. He had an interview with the BBC. He talked about the war in Ukraine. This war has continued for five years. Muratov said that Russia's leader can only destroy Ukraine. He believes Russia cannot conquer Ukraine. The leader of Russia cannot claim victory. This news is from a very respected person. The war is very serious for everyone. Muratov shared his strong opinion.

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. Nobel Peace Prize winner (noun phrase): A person who gets a special international award for working to make peace in the world.
2. interview (noun): A meeting where someone asks questions to another person to get information.
3. war (noun): A period of fighting between countries or groups, often with armies.
4. destroy (verb): To break something completely so it cannot be used or exist anymore.
5. conquer (verb): To take control of a country or a city by fighting and winning.

Grammar Focus: Modal Verb: Can (Ability/Possibility)
We use the word 'can' to talk about what is possible or what people are able to do. It shows ability or a general truth. For example, 'I can swim' means I have the ability. 'It can be cold in winter' means it is possible.
Example from the text: "Russia's leader can only destroy Ukraine."

📅 August 11, 2026 [4 days, 23 hours old] - President Trump's Secret Plane Swap
President Trump used a clever plan to change planes secretly after a meeting in Turkey.

Beginner ESL: International News: President Trump's Secret Travel

I. The News Brief

President Trump was at a Nato summit in Turkey. After the summit, he needed to fly home. Reporters were told he would fly on Air Force One. But this was not true; it was a ruse. President Trump had a secret plan to leave. He hid inside a catering truck at the airport. The truck took him to a different plane. This plane swap was done very secretly. Reports said there was an Iran threat. The secret plane swap kept him safe from the threat.

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. Nato summit (noun phrase): A big meeting for leaders from different countries that are part of NATO, an alliance.
2. Air Force One (noun): The special airplane that the President of the United States uses for travel.
3. ruse (noun): A trick or a clever plan used to make someone believe something that is not true.
4. catering truck (noun): A vehicle that brings food and drinks to airplanes for passengers.
5. Iran threat (noun phrase): A danger or warning coming from the country of Iran.

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense
We use the simple past tense to talk about actions that happened in the past and are now finished. Many past tense verbs end with '-ed', like 'walked' or 'talked'. Some verbs are irregular, like 'was' (from 'is') or 'hid' (from 'hide').
Example from the text: "He hid inside a catering truck at the airport."
