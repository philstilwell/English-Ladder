# Conversation and meaning bank — release 1

300 original items, reviewed October 15, 2026. These are independent practice examples, not student utterances, transcript extracts, or formal test questions. No student information or paid content-generation service was used.

| Activity | Items | Six topic sets |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Conversation Rescue | 60 | Hearing clearly; Checking meaning; Finding words; Managing turns; Correcting misunderstandings; Handling delicate moments |
| Meaning Switch | 60 | Time and events; Rules and possibilities; Amount and frequency; Focus and reference; Verbs and prepositions; Conditions and reasons |
| Ask the right question | 60 | People, things and places; Time, amount and frequency; Choosing the verb form; Subjects and objects; Polite and indirect questions; Precise follow-up questions |
| Story builder | 60 | Everyday tasks; Getting around; Learning and messages; Plans and work; Solving problems; Reasons and consequences |
| Missing Details | 60 | Visits and appointments; Travel and collection; Orders and deliveries; Events and coordination; Choosing a suitable option; Handover and clarification |

Every item has four distinct choices, one keyed answer under its stated task, and an explanation. There are ten items per set, with Start/Build/Stretch stages of twenty each. These labels describe progression within the bank; they are not validated proficiency levels. Correct-option positions are balanced at fifteen per position in each bank and deterministically shuffled to avoid a repeating answer cycle. Story sentence order is shuffled separately, with the already-correct presentation excluded. Students may reveal the answer or retry without changing the original first choice.

Content review covered the correct answer, alternate readings, distractor usefulness, adult situations, and simple instructions. Conversation Rescue distractors were revised to represent different conversational purposes rather than absurd replies. Missing Details alternatives now ask plausible related questions that do not supply the detail required by the task. Ask the right question mixes common form errors with grammatical questions that ask for different information. A card-acceptance item explicitly distinguishes whether any cards are accepted from which cards are accepted. The library example now says “is closed on Sundays” to avoid confusing a closing time with an all-day closure.

Meaning Switch distinguishes possibility from obligation, amount from frequency, speaker intention from actual outcome, and related word forms. Wording such as “normally,” “more clearly,” and “in this context” avoids overstating tendencies as absolute rules. The “not all” explanation does not falsely imply that some must be on. “Everyone is not ready” is explicitly identified as ambiguous. An unclear “both/either” contrast and an unrelated tense-error item were replaced with clear “either/neither” and “despite/because of” contrasts.

Story builder uses four scrambled sentences rather than free-text grading. Each keyed chronology has event, reference, or cause-and-result clues: for example, a replacement file must be created before that replacement can open. Every displayed correct sequence is automatically checked against its authored chronological source. Missing Details includes a model reply supplying the requested information. These replies are fictional scenario details, not general policies of real businesses or services.

Automated checks cover 300 complete entries, numbering, six equal topic sets per bank, stage and key balance, distinct contexts/tasks and choices, agreement between authored sources and generated keys, all story permutations, and complete replies. Automated checks cannot prove linguistic quality; the editorial pass supplies that review. This first release has not been piloted with students or independently calibrated.

After release, preserve wording, options and keys under existing version/item identities. Extend with new numbers, or retain old banks/readers when issuing a deliberately revised version. Earlier student choices must never be interpreted against changed wording.
