Lesson 01: Design Intent and Client Requirements
Original fictional case: Client representative Amira asks architect Ben for a flexible meeting room. During briefing, she specifies workshops for 12 people at movable tables and presentations for 24 people in rows, at different times. Staff should be able to change the arrangement within 15 minutes. Remote participants must hear questions from anywhere in the room. These are requested performance targets, not verified design capabilities or approved occupant loads. Ben will prepare two layouts and seek audiovisual and code reviews. The room dimensions, storage provision, access requirements, and budget effects have not yet been assessed.
Language workshop: What does a flexible meeting room need to do?
Communication goal: Turn broad client language into specific functional requirements without claiming design approval.
Language guidance: Requested capacity is not approved occupant load. A layout drawing must still be assessed against the actual space and applicable requirements. Keep the client wish, proposed solution, and verified result distinct.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): When you say flexible, which activities do you need? / Will those activities happen at the same time? / The workshop arrangement needs twelve places at movable tables. / The presentation arrangement needs twenty-four seats in rows. / Staff should be able to change layouts within fifteen minutes. / That is a requested target, not a tested capability. / Where will unused furniture be stored? / Remote participants need to hear questions throughout the room. / Displaying slides does not by itself provide microphone coverage. / We need to assess views from the proposed seating positions. / The layouts still need access and code review. / I cannot confirm the approved occupant load from this brief. / We have not yet assessed the budget effect. / I will prepare two layouts for review. / The two activities occur at different times. / I will record these as requirements to be tested.
Vocabulary: design intent: The purpose and performance an intended design should achieve. / client brief: A statement of the client's needs, priorities, and constraints. / functional requirement: A description of what a space or system must enable. / space planning: Organizing space for activities, movement, and equipment. / occupant load: The number of occupants determined under applicable code provisions. / seating arrangement: The planned position and relationship of seats. / theater-style seating: Rows of seats generally facing a presentation area. / workshop layout: An arrangement supporting collaborative or practical group work. / reconfigurable furniture: Furniture that can be rearranged for different uses. / changeover time: The time needed to change between arrangements or uses. / circulation: Routes and space for movement within a building. / egress: Movement out of a building or space, particularly for emergency exit. / accessibility: Usability of spaces and facilities by people with differing abilities. / sightline: A line of vision from a viewing position to an object or area. / acoustic performance: How a space handles sound for its intended use. / reverberation: Persistence of sound after the original source stops. / speech intelligibility: How clearly spoken words can be understood. / audiovisual (AV) system: Equipment for presenting or transmitting sound and images. / microphone coverage: The area from which a microphone system can capture usable sound. / power provision: The planned availability and location of electrical power. / storage allowance: Space reserved for items that are not currently in use. / adjacency: The relationship between nearby spaces or functions. / performance target: A desired measurable result that must still be evaluated. / design constraint: A condition limiting the available design choices.
Speaking task: Read the corrected conversation, distinguishing 12 workshop places from 24 presentation seats. Switch roles and repeat turns 5-12; emphasize the 15-minute target, storage, and hearing questions from the whole room. Read the checked transfer twice using 10, 18, and 20. Keep requested capacity distinct from approved occupancy.
Partner: Amira: Client representative / Ben: Architect
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A separate client requests workshops for 10 people and presentations for 18, at different times. The desired changeover is 20 minutes. None of the capacities is approved.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Amira: What will you bring back to our next meeting? I need something concrete enough for staff to discuss without assuming the design is already settled.
Ben: Two proposed layouts and an explicit list of design constraints. I will identify the outstanding dimensional, storage, access, AV, code, and budget reviews beside the requested performance.
Amira: That works. Please preserve the twelve and twenty-four figures, different-time use, and fifteen-minute target, while clearly showing that feasibility remains to be established.
Ben: Agreed. Those will form the recorded functional requirements. The next step is to test them against the actual room and project constraints, not to label them approved capabilities.

Lesson 02: Drawings, Specifications, and RFIs
Original fictional case: Site coordinator Lena finds a finish discrepancy in Room 205. Drawing A601 revision C identifies wall finish F2; specification S09 revision B identifies F3 for the same wall. Lesson 08: Claims, Disputes, and Documentation
Original fictional case: Delivery records show that a project item arrived at the end of working Day 11 rather than the planned end of Day 6, a five-working-day difference. Commercial coordinator Saira has $2,400 of reported extra labor costs, but the time records and causal link have not been reconciled. The impact on project completion, any concurrent delay, contract notice requirements, and entitlement to payment or time have not been reviewed. Project manager Theo finds a draft saying the delay automatically entitles us to compensation. They must prepare a factual internal summary for the authorized contract team, not make a legal determination.
Language workshop: The delay is recorded; entitlement is not established
Communication goal: Describe a delay and supporting records without presenting an unreviewed claim as a proven right.
Language guidance: A five-day delivery variance is not automatically a five-day delay to project completion. The schedule logic, available time, actual effects, and other events require analysis. An event record and a contractual entitlement answer different questions.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): Delivery occurred at the end of working Day 11. / The plan showed the end of working Day 6. / The recorded delivery variance is five working days. / The effect on project completion has not been assessed. / Extra labor of two thousand four hundred dollars is reported but unreconciled. / Link the labor entries to the relevant work and dates. / We still need to establish which costs arose from this event. / Entitlement to time or payment remains under review. / Remove automatically entitled from the unreviewed summary. / The authorized team must review the contract and notice requirements. / Keep the planned date, actual date, and supporting delivery record together. / Do not rewrite an earlier observation as a later proven conclusion. / Check whether other delays affected the same period. / Describe verified steps taken without inventing their effect. / I am not authorized to settle this dispute. / Please review the event, claimed effect, records, and applicable contractual basis.
Vocabulary: claim: A request or assertion seeking a contractual or legal remedy or entitlement. / entitlement: A right to a remedy or benefit under the applicable basis. / causation: The connection between an event and a claimed consequence. / quantum: The amount of a claimed or assessed monetary remedy. / contemporaneous record: A record made at or near the time of the relevant event. / chronology: An ordered account of events and their dates or times. / delay event: An occurrence alleged or shown to affect progress or timing. / concurrent delay: Overlapping delay effects considered under the applicable contract and analysis. / disruption: Interference with the planned manner or efficiency of performing work. / prolongation: An extension of the time spent on a project or activity. / mitigation: Reasonable measures to limit adverse effects under the relevant circumstances. / notice provision: A contract term governing notification of specified matters. / time bar: A limitation linked to meeting a deadline under the applicable legal or contractual rules. / reservation of rights: A statement intended to preserve a position, whose effect depends on the circumstances. / substantiation: Evidence and reasoning supporting an assertion or amount. / cost reconciliation: Checking reported costs against supporting records and the relevant basis. / labor allocation: Assignment of labor time or cost to particular activities. / daily report: A record of the day's work, conditions, events, and resources. / delivery docket: A record accompanying or confirming a delivery. / correspondence register: An organized record of project communications. / liability: Legal responsibility determined under the applicable rules and facts. / disputed amount: A monetary figure whose validity or payment remains contested. / settlement authority: Authority to agree a resolution on behalf of a party. / dispute resolution: The process for addressing disagreements under the applicable arrangement.
Speaking task: Read the corrected exchange, keeping the five-working-day delivery variance separate from completion impact. Switch roles for turns 11-20; distinguish reported $2,400, causation, entitlement, and settlement authority. Read the checked transfer twice with three working days and $900 reported, without presenting either as an agreed remedy.
Partner: Saira: Commercial coordinator / Theo: Project manager
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A delivery planned for the end of working Day 4 arrives at the end of Day 7. Reported additional labor is $900. Completion impact, cost reconciliation, and entitlement are unreviewed.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Theo: The internal package will include the chronology, delivery records, labor entries, unresolved schedule effects, and questions for authorized reviewers.
Saira: Include verified mitigation steps where the records support them. Do not assume that discussing a recovery option proves it was implemented or that it prevented a particular amount of loss.
Theo: I will remove the automatic-entitlement statement and circulate the factual summary internally. I will not offer a settlement or communicate a final legal conclusion.
Saira: Good. Settlement authority is separate from preparing records. We can make the event and unresolved questions precise while leaving contractual decisions with the people authorized to make them.
