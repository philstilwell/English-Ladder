Talking about jobs: a or an?

Introduce a person’s job with “a” or “an”, choosing the article by the next word’s first sound.

She is an engineer. He works as an artist.

Understand

The useful distinction

When introducing one person’s job, we usually use “a” or “an” before the singular job noun: “She is a teacher”; “She is an engineer”. Keep the article after “work as” too: “He works as a designer”. “A” and “an” are articles: small words that introduce the noun phrase. In these ordinary introductions, do not say “I work as teacher”.

Choose by the first sound of the word immediately after the article. Use “a” before a consonant sound: “a teacher”, “a designer”. Use “an” before a vowel sound: “an engineer”, “an artist”. An adjective can change the choice: “a teacher” but “an experienced teacher”. The first sound of “experienced”, not “teacher”, now decides the article.

Sound and spelling do not always match. “University” starts with the consonant sound at the beginning of “you”, so say “a university teacher”. For plural job nouns, leave out “a/an”: “They are teachers”. Use “the” when identifying a particular person, as in “She is the teacher I told you about”. Titles and appointment expressions can have other patterns, such as “elected president”; this lesson practises ordinary job introductions.
