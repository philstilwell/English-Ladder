Lesson 01: Confirming seats and order details
Original fictional case: Table 12 has three covers. Seat one orders mushroom pasta, seat two orders a salad with dressing separately, and seat three has not chosen. The phrase 'no onions' was spoken without a clear seat reference. Clarify before sending the order.
Language workshop: Attach each change to a dish
Communication goal: Clarify an ambiguous modifier and confirm the complete order.
Language guidance: A modifier needs a dish and a diner. 'On the side' means separately, not omitted. Ask a focused question before reading back the order.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): Was ___ for your ___ or for ___? / I have ___ for seat ___, with ___. / You're still deciding on ___; is that right?
Vocabulary: cover: One diner counted for restaurant table service. / seat number: A numbered position at a table used to match an order to a diner. / modifier: An order detail that changes the standard dish, such as sauce on the side. / read-back: A spoken repetition of an order so the guest can correct it.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Guest at seat two: confirm that no onions applies to your salad, not the pasta. Seat three needs another minute.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: Seat one also wants dressing for a side salad. Keep that request separate from seat two's main salad.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Before I send table twelve's order, could I check who requested no onions? I have mushroom pasta for seat one and a salad with dressing on the side for seat two. Seat three is still deciding. I'll attach the onion request to the correct dish and read those details back.
