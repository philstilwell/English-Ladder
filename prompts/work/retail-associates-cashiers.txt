Lesson 01: Finding the item the shopper actually means
Original fictional case: Shopper Casey asks associate Hana for the blue shirt in an advertisement. The advertisement contains two blue shirts: linen L214 and cotton C218. Casey wants the cotton version in medium, not the linen version or another size. Local availability has not been checked. Hana confirms C218, blue, medium, and cotton before beginning a stock inquiry. No item has been located, reserved, or sold, and the advertisement does not prove that the requested variation is available at this branch.
Language workshop: Which blue shirt?
Communication goal: Use a reference, material, and size to identify a product before making an unverified local-stock claim.
Language guidance: The exact request is blue cotton C218 in medium. Linen L214 is a different item even though it is also blue. Confirming the product does not establish stock, a reservation, or permission to substitute another variation.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): The ad shows two blue shirts; do you mean cotton or linen? / Which size are you looking for? / The cotton shirt is C218. / L214 is the linen version. / Blue cotton C218 in medium; is that right? / That's the right shirt; let me check whether we have your size here. / The advertisement does not confirm stock at this branch. / I will use that exact reference and size for the stock inquiry. / I would ask before offering a different size or material. / Nothing has been reserved yet. / Are you asking about availability at this branch? / The next step is to check the requested variation. / Medium cotton, not medium linen. / I cannot confirm available or sold out before the check. / Please tell me if I have the size or material wrong. / The item is confirmed; its stock status is still unknown.
Vocabulary: item reference: Identifier used to distinguish a product. / stock-keeping unit: Internal identifier for a stocked product variation, often abbreviated SKU. / barcode: Machine-readable code used to identify an item or record. / product variation: Specific version differing in size, color, or another feature. / colorway: Color version offered within a product range. / size: Labelled dimension category such as medium. / cotton: Textile fiber identified for the requested shirt in this case. / linen: Different textile fiber used in the other advertised shirt. / fiber content: Information describing the fibers used in a textile. / advertised item: Product featured in a promotion or advertisement. / product image: Picture of a product, not proof of local stock. / range: Group of related products or variations. / local availability: Whether the exact variation is available at the relevant branch. / branch: Particular store location within a retailer. / shop floor: Customer-accessible area where merchandise is displayed. / stockroom: Storage area for merchandise not on the selling floor. / display sample: Item shown for viewing, not necessarily available for sale. / stock inquiry: Request to check whether a specific item is available. / stock record: System record of recorded merchandise quantities or status. / physical check: Verification of the actual item and its location. / reservation: Confirmed arrangement to hold an item under the store process. / substitution: Replacement with a different item or variation requiring agreement. / specification: Set of details defining the requested item. / planogram: Diagram assigning products and display space on a fixture.
Speaking task: Read turns 2, 6, and 16 with C218 stressed. Your partner points to the cotton choice, not L214. Repeat the complete request using the four facts: C218, blue, medium, cotton. Read turns 14 and 20. Keep locating stock and reserving it as separate steps.
Partner: Casey: Shopper / Hana: Retail associate
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: An advertisement shows red wool W310 and red cotton C314 sweaters. The shopper wants red cotton C314 in large. Local stock is unchecked, and no item is reserved.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Casey: That's right. If there's only linen, please let me know. I wouldn't want it swapped without checking with me.
Hana: Any substitution would be your choice. I'll check the shirt you asked for first.
Casey: Thanks. I'll stay nearby while you find out. I don't need you to look for a different size.
Hana: I'll start the stock inquiry for blue cotton C218 in medium at this branch. We don't have the stock result yet.

Lesson 02: Comparing fit, materials, and label information
Original fictional case: Shopper Luis needs a machine-washable jacket and compares two $60 options with associate Priya. Jacket A is unlined and its label permits machine washing. Jacket B is lined and its label says dry-clean only. No other care details, durability tests, or fit measurements are supplied. A meets the stated washing requirement on the available label information; B does not. Luis asks to look at A next but has not tried it on or purchased it.
