5. Finish with two new four-choice collocation questions for later review, with a clearly separate answer key. Do not claim to schedule a reminder.

Lesson 01: Clarifying the service and the intended length
Original fictional case: A client booked an overlay but calls it a full set and points to a long reference image. They actually want to keep their current short length. No work has begun. Clarify length and explain why the image does not automatically mean extensions.
Language workshop: Check length before interpreting a service label
Communication goal: Distinguish a client's visual preference from a booking name.
Language guidance: Terms such as full set can be used loosely. Ask about added length explicitly rather than treating a photograph as a complete instruction.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): Do you want added length or ___? / Which part of the photo do you mean? / Your booking says ___; let's confirm ___.
Vocabulary: manicure: A cosmetic service for fingernails and hands, with the included elements set by the salon menu. / pedicure: A cosmetic service for toenails and feet, not medical treatment of foot conditions. / overlay: An enhancement product applied over the natural nail without deliberately adding length. / extension: An artificial addition that lengthens the nail beyond its natural length.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Client: want the pictured pink color but no additional length; used full set without a precise meaning.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: The client says overlay and extension sound interchangeable. Explain the length distinction plainly.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: You've booked an overlay, and you said you want to keep your current short length. The photograph shows added length, so let's separate its color from its shape. Are you asking for the pictured color on your existing length, or do you want extensions?
END REFERENCE

Lesson 02: Comparing shape and practical length
Original fictional case: A client likes a long almond sample but needs a short result for keyboard work. A short squoval sample is also available. Compare the sample silhouettes and lengths without promising that either shape will prevent breakage or discomfort.
Language workshop: Compare like-for-like features
Communication goal: Distinguish shape from length when discussing samples.
Language guidance: Two samples can differ on more than one dimension. Name the outline and length separately so a preference for one does not silently select the other.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): This outline is ___, whereas that one is ___. / The lengths differ as well as ___. / Which feature matters most for ___?
Vocabulary: free edge: The part of a natural nail that extends beyond the fingertip. / squoval: A nail outline combining a relatively square end with softened corners. / almond shape: A nail outline that narrows toward a softly pointed, almond-like tip. / swatch: A sample showing a nail color or finish for comparison.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Client: like the almond outline but do not want the sample's long length.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: The client assumes selecting the short sample guarantees it will never snag. Avoid an unsupported promise.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: The almond sample narrows toward the tip, whereas this short squoval sample has a flatter end with softer corners. Their lengths differ too, so shape alone isn't the comparison. Since you prefer a short result for keyboard work, let's discuss the shorter reference before deciding what suits your nails.
