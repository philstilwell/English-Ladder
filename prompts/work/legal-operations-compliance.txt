Lesson 01: Contract Intake and Workflow
Original fictional case: Ben submits request L17 with the subject Urgent contract. It lacks an agreement type, business owner, and required decision date. Attached is an unsigned vendor service proposal for a two-month, $40,000 software pilot; an earlier nondisclosure agreement is also attached. Intake coordinator Mina must establish the transaction purpose, correct parties, required documents, personal-data involvement, and actual timing. Receipt is not legal approval. The business has not confirmed who can authorize spending or sign. Mina can coordinate triage but cannot decide contract terms or promise immediate signature.
Language workshop: Urgent is a signal, not a complete instruction
Communication goal: Clarify the agreement, owner, decision need, and document status before routing a contract request.
Language guidance: The earlier NDA and the proposed services arrangement serve different purposes. Do not assume the NDA approves the pilot, spending, data processing, or signature. Reviewers need the relevant document package and actual parties, not an intake label alone.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): I have received L17 and the attached proposal. / To route it correctly, I need the agreement type, business owner, and decision date. / What services will the two-month pilot actually include? / Which legal entities would enter the agreement? / The earlier NDA does not replace the service documents. / Who is accountable for the business need? / Who approves the spend, and who is authorized to sign? / What decision is needed by what date, and what happens if it is delayed? / Is that the review deadline or the desired signature date? / Will the pilot involve personal data or access to customer systems? / The proposal is unsigned and has not completed review. / Please include the current proposal and all referenced terms. / We need the scope and reviewer availability before confirming timing. / I will flag the data-processing details for the appropriate reviewer. / Keep the current version and status in the intake record. / I will confirm the route, missing items, and next contact once triage is complete.
Vocabulary: intake: Receiving and organizing information needed to handle a request. / matter: A legal or operational issue tracked as a defined item of work. / triage: Assessing a request to determine its route, priority, and next action. / business owner: The person accountable for the underlying business need. / requester: The person submitting a request, not necessarily its decision-maker. / counterparty: The other party to the relevant agreement or transaction. / legal entity: An organization or person recognized as capable of relevant legal rights and duties. / agreement type: The category of contract or document being requested. / NDA: Nondisclosure agreement; terms governing specified confidential information. / MSA: Master services agreement; overarching terms for specified service arrangements. / SOW: Statement of work; a document specifying particular work and related terms. / order form: A document setting out a particular purchase and its relevant terms. / contract package: The related documents that together describe the proposed arrangement. / unsigned proposal: Proposed terms that have not been signed in the supplied record. / signature authority: Permission to execute the relevant agreement on behalf of a party. / spend approval: Authorization to commit the relevant funds. / decision deadline: The time by which a specified decision is needed. / target signature date: A desired signing date whose commitment status must be clear. / review scope: The issues and documents included in an assessment. / routing rule: A criterion determining where a request is sent. / CLM: Contract lifecycle management; processes and systems for managing contract stages. / version control: Management of document revisions and their status. / turnaround estimate: An estimate of how long a defined response or task will take. / legal approval: Approval within the defined legal review process, distinct from other authorizations.
Speaking task: Read Ben and Mina aloud, then swap roles. Separate the sixth-of-October decision from the hoped-for ninth-of-October signature. Correct the ten gaps. Reread the party-name check and the unresolved customer-data flow without inventing either answer. Repeat the handoff with Nell as business owner and spending and signature authority still unconfirmed.
Partner: Ben: Business requester / Mina: Legal intake coordinator
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: Request M22 attaches an unsigned services proposal. Dana owns the business need. A review decision is needed on 5 May; signature is desired on 9 May. The spend approver and authorized signer are not yet confirmed.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Ben: The supplier keeps chasing. Could we say today, even though the reviewer is not confirmed?
Mina: I cannot give that turnaround estimate yet. I can acknowledge receipt and confirm that we are clarifying the route. Once the scope and reviewer availability are known, we can communicate a supported timing expectation.
Ben: I will get Nell's confirmation, the party details, the latest proposal, and the data-handling information into the same record.
Mina: Good. I will complete triage, identify the missing approvals, and confirm the next contact. We can respond to the urgency while keeping receipt, review, approval, and signature as separate statuses.
