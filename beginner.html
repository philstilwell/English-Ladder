📅 September 22, 2026 - Rare Harper Lee Book Found in Oxfam Shop
An old and special book by Harper Lee was found in an Oxfam shop in London.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

A rare book was found in an Oxfam shop in London. It is a first edition of the novel 'To Kill a Mockingbird' by Harper Lee. The book was donated to the charity's bookshop. A shop manager found this special book and knew it was important. The outside cover was old, but the pages inside were in beautiful condition. Now, the book is part of the national collection at the British Library. This discovery reminds people about treasures found in second-hand shops. Oxfam's 'Second Hand September' campaign encourages buying used items.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. rare (adjective): Not common; not seen or found often.
2. found (verb (past simple)): Discovered by chance; saw or learned about something for the first time.
3. shop (noun): A place where you can buy things.
4. donated (verb (past simple)): Gave something, like money or goods, to a charity or a person who needs help.
5. manager (noun): A person who is in charge of a business or a part of a business.
6. special (adjective): Better or more important than usual; unique.
7. condition (noun): The state of something, how good or bad it is.
8. campaign (noun): A series of actions planned to achieve a goal, like selling a product or helping a cause.

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense
We use the simple past tense to talk about actions or events that happened in the past and are now finished. For example, 'was found' means someone found it before now.
Example from the text: "A rare book was found in an Oxfam shop in London."

Read the story

He also said his government would stop bad actors who use AI for wrong things. Some experts say AI development should slow down.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. plans (noun): ideas about what someone will do in the future
2. force (noun): a group of people working together for a special purpose
3. leader (noun): a person who guides or controls a group
4. government (noun): the group of people who control a country
5. warnings (noun): information that tells you about a possible danger or problem
6. important (adjective): having great value or meaning
7. slow down (verb phrase): to make something move or happen more slowly

Grammar Focus: "Will" for Future Actions
We use "will" to talk about future plans, decisions, or predictions. It shows something is expected to happen. For example, "They will travel next month" or "The store will open soon."
Example from the text: "Trump said his government will not stop AI from growing."

Local elections in Germany are happening and could affect Chancellor Merz's political future.

German elections are happening today, and they could decide Chancellor Merz's future. The chancellor is feeling strong political pressure, which will grow if his party struggles in elections. He says he will continue, but many people do not approve of him. The country's economy is not strong, and energy costs are high. These problems make people feel worried about the country's future.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. elections (noun): A time when people vote to choose their leaders or representatives.
2. decide (verb): To make a choice or come to a conclusion about something after thinking about it.
3. chancellor (noun): The leader of the government in some countries, like Germany.
4. pressure (noun): A difficult situation or feeling that makes you worried or stressed.
5. struggles (verb): Has problems or finds something very difficult to do or deal with.
6. approve (verb): To think that something or someone is good or acceptable.

Grammar Focus: Using 'if' to talk about possible results
We use the word 'if' to talk about a condition and its possible result. The part with 'if' describes the condition, and the other part tells what will happen if that condition is true.
Example from the text: "The chancellor is feeling strong political pressure, which will grow if his party struggles in elections."
