Lesson 01: Taking a precise electrical service request
Original fictional case: A client reports that the office sockets are dead. Their account concerns two desk receptacles in Room 204, noticed at 08:30. Other areas have not been assessed. A panel directory says office, but its accuracy has not been verified. Clarify the service request without asking the client to test or operate anything.
Language workshop: Locate the report without expanding it
Communication goal: Turn a broad complaint into an accurate service-call description.
Language guidance: Ask about existing observations, room labels, and timing. A client report is not a verified circuit finding. Do not direct the client to operate equipment, repeat a fault, open an enclosure, or perform electrical checks.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): Which ___ are you referring to? / You noticed ___ at ___; is that right? / That label suggests ___, but identification remains ___.
Vocabulary: service call: A visit requested to assess or address a reported electrical issue. / receptacle: The electrical contact device into which a plug is inserted; commonly called a socket or outlet. / distribution board: An assembly containing devices that distribute and protect electrical circuits; also called a panelboard in some settings. / circuit identifier: A label or reference used to distinguish a particular electrical circuit in project records.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: You are the client. You noticed the issue at 08:30 but have not investigated other rooms.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: A manager calls this a floor-wide outage. Restate the known scope without ruling out unassessed areas.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: When you say the office sockets, do you mean the two desk receptacles in Room 204? I will record that you noticed the issue at 08:30. The directory's office label does not yet confirm which circuit serves them. Please describe what you already observed; the electrician will assess the circuit identification and condition.
