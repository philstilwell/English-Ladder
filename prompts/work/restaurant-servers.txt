Lesson 01: Confirming seats and order details
Original fictional case: At fictional table 12, three guests are ordering. Seat one wants mushroom pasta; seat two wants salad with dressing separately; seat three has not chosen. Someone says "no onions," but server Lena cannot tell which dish the request belongs to. She has not sent the order. Guest Sam at seat two confirms that the onion request belongs to the salad. The dialogue follows Lena and section lead Cam checking that clarified request before transmission. Seat three still needs time. No allergy is reported in this case, and the onion request must not be treated as a confirmed allergy or a rule for every dish.
Language workshop: Who asked for no onions?
Communication goal: Attach each dish and modifier to the correct guest, then read back the order before sending it.
Language guidance: No onions needs a clear dish reference. Do not attach it to every plate because the speaker was unclear. A modifier describes the requested change; a reported allergy requires its own accurate communication and the actual allergy process.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): Which dish should have no onions? / Is that request for your salad? / Seat one is the mushroom pasta. / Seat two is the salad with dressing on the side. / Seat three is still deciding. / I have noted no onions for your salad. / I will not apply that request to the other dishes. / May I check one detail before I send this? / You would like the dressing served separately, correct? / I will confirm any preparation question through our kitchen process. / The seat reference and modifier need to stay together. / No allergy has been reported in this exchange. / Have I matched the dishes and requests correctly? / I will give seat three another moment to choose. / The order has not been sent yet. / Salad for seat two, no onions, dressing on the side.
Vocabulary: cover: One diner counted for restaurant service or planning. / seat number: Identifier linking an order to a place at a table. / table number: Identifier used to locate a dining table. / order pad: Written surface used to record guest orders. / point of sale: System for entering orders and recording sales, often abbreviated POS. / order ticket: Record of dishes and instructions sent for preparation. / modifier: Instruction changing an item from its standard preparation or presentation. / omission: Requested removal of an ingredient or component. / substitution: Replacement of one component with another when available and permitted. / on the side: Served separately rather than mixed into or placed on the dish. / read-back: Spoken repetition of recorded details to check accuracy. / seat reference: Link showing which guest a detail concerns. / course: Stage of a meal, such as a starter or main. / main: Principal dish in a meal. / starter: Dish served before the main course in the relevant menu format. / garnish: Item added to finish or accompany a dish. / accompaniment: Food served alongside a principal item. / preparation request: Guest instruction about how an item should be prepared. / dietary requirement: Food-related need that must be described accurately and handled appropriately. / preference: Stated choice that must not automatically be labeled a medical condition. / pending choice: Selection that has not yet been made. / send the order: Transmit a confirmed order through the restaurant's system. / order sequence: Agreed order in which information or dishes are handled. / confirmation: Explicit agreement that repeated details are correct.
Speaking task: Check the cloze answers. Read Lena and Cam's exchange, stressing table twelve, seat one, seat two, and seat three. Switch roles. Say the final read-back without changing the pasta or inventing the third main. Complete the table 8 transfer and check its key. Repeat with a partner: fish, seat two, soup unchanged, two covers.
Partner: Lena: Server / Cam: Section lead
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: Table 8 has two covers. Seat one orders tomato soup; seat two orders grilled fish. A lemon-separately request is clarified as belonging to the fish. No third diner or allergy is reported.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Lena: I will return to seat three now and ask what they would like. Then I can repeat the complete order to the table.
Cam: Get that last confirmation from the guest. Leave Sam's salad request as it is unless Sam changes it.
Lena: Understood: pasta unchanged, salad with its two requests, third main still open. I will come back if the guest needs a kitchen check.
Cam: All right. Follow our process when you send the order. Keep the dish, seat, and instructions together so the kitchen receives what the guests actually asked for.

Lesson 02: Explaining menu choices and prices
Original fictional case: The fictional lunch set is $24 and includes a main and dessert. Choosing steak adds a $5 supplement to that set price. The a la carte steak is $27 without dessert. Both steak options include potatoes. Guest Jordan initially thinks the steak set must cost $24, then wonders whether potatoes cost extra.
