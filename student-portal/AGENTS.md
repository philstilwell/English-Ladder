# Student feedback rules

- Write all student-facing assessments, feedback, summaries, headings, and practice instructions in short, clear English suitable for A1–A2 readers. Use common words and one idea per sentence. Explain necessary grammar terms with a simple example. This is a reading-register preference, not a claim about a student's assessed level. Keep the tone respectful and adult. Harder vocabulary being taught may remain, with an easy explanation.
- Put words and phrases being discussed inside quotation marks or italics, including in headings and practice instructions. For example: “Phil showed two ways to use ‘access’.” Clearly distinguish actual lesson quotations from new practice examples; never turn a paraphrase or new example into a claimed transcript quote.
- Use only a student's first name in prose. An explicitly chosen nickname may be used as a profile label. Never display or publish full names on student-facing pages. The owner explicitly authorized full names, Zoom names, contact details, and course records only in the authenticated administrator directory at phil.englishladder.com; keep its data separate from every student API and from AI authoring inputs.
- Every student's session feedback must focus exclusively on English as a second language: usage, vocabulary, grammar, comprehension, conversational skills, and language practice. Do not summarize or evaluate personal lives, work performance, beliefs, health, or unrelated conversation topics.
- Find every matching session in the requested period, including known display-name variants. Read the complete available transcript for each session, not just Zoom's generated summary. Record its coverage and identify missing transcripts honestly.
- Ground feedback in timestamped learner utterances and teacher exchanges. Distinguish explicit in-session teaching from additional practice. Treat automatic transcription errors cautiously; do not infer pronunciation quality or proficiency scores from text.
- Explain language accurately even when a conversational explanation in the source was oversimplified. Label new model sentences and exercises as practice rather than transcript quotations. Keep quoted examples brief and remove unnecessary personal details.
- Store actual student feedback only in the private per-student server storage. Keep publishing inputs in the ignored private directory with restrictive file permissions. Never commit student records, raw transcripts, credentials, or provisioning inputs; never place them in public assets or browser storage.
- Test account isolation, authentication, teacher-only writes, logout cleanup, and mobile access whenever changing record handling. Use fictional records in tests.
- Overall assessments follow the same ESL-only and privacy rules. Support recurring patterns with references across the full transcripts, balance them with successful uses, and label practice targets as goals rather than current scores. Link to private feedback pages, never Zoom meeting notes. Display dates only in assessment references, including accessible labels; omit elapsed transcript times and coverage ranges from student pages while retaining them in private records for verification.
- Assessment study plans are optional suggestions for independent study, never promises about a future session or tasks assigned to Phil. Use the heading “Study on your own”. Give each topic a short copyable AI prompt that introduces the specific language, explains it in simple English, gives examples, and asks one question at a time with feedback. Keep prompts free of student names, personal details, transcript quotations, timestamps, and links.
- Beside each study prompt, keep a ChatGPT action that fills its composer and a Gemini copy-and-open action, with clear paste instructions until Gemini supports a verified prefill link. Preserve manual copying. AI destinations open only on the student's action, in a new tab without an opener or referrer; include only the neutral study prompt, never student records, and never automatically submit it.
- Quizzes should practice patterns from the student's assessment with simple instructions and feedback after each choice. Label quiz items as new practice, use quotation marks or italics for the English being taught, and do not present quiz results as a proficiency level. Keep authored quizzes in private student storage and clear in-memory attempts on sign-out or expiry.
- Save student quiz choices privately after each answer. Preserve the original first choice, source lesson references, and question wording for each attempt. Teacher previews must never count as student attempts. Show first-try mistakes and dated progress only to the authenticated teacher; do not treat practice scores as a proficiency level. Show only plain source lesson dates in the quiz introduction, with no links. Keep short answer instructions beside the questions.
- Show the Quiz menu item only when a reviewed quiz is available. If there is not enough lesson evidence to author one, omit the quiz and its menu entry instead of inventing questions or showing an empty tab.
- When automatic quizzes have been requested, follow QUIZ_AUTOMATION.md. Read each new full transcript and the saved original first choices from earlier attempts. Publish one seven-question quiz per eligible completed session, with simple instructions and choice-by-choice feedback. For the authorized nightly page updates, refresh the assessment with new evidence and preserve its earlier version privately. Always preserve saved attempts. Keep scheduling checkpoints private; do not invent mistakes when no results exist.

- Student registration and Zoom mappings are managed in the private StudentDirectory service. Preserve established student IDs, course records, learning history, and first-try results. Imported spreadsheet rows remain drafts by default. When Phil explicitly requests transcript matching and activation, activate clear matches supported by actual transcript speaker identities; ask about ambiguous names. Do not infer an alias merely because another student mentions a name. Preserve an explicit owner instruction that a student is expired or paused, even if the spreadsheet still lists a later end date.
- Phil can assign, reveal, and copy student passwords on private account pages, as explicitly requested. Keep passwords out of the directory list, ordinary save responses, student APIs, automation inputs, public assets, logs, and Git. Only the dedicated login-details endpoint on the administrator host may return a student password, after checking the host-bound teacher session; never return verification records or the universal teacher password. Store recoverable custom passwords encrypted with the separate Worker encryption secret, bound to their account and current verifier. Blank password fields preserve both records. Changing a password revokes that student's sessions and does not affect teacher access or other students. Restoring an encrypted copy of a verified existing password must preserve its verifier and sessions. Clear revealed/copied details on navigation, logout, and session expiry; discard late responses. Never set a student's password to the universal administrator password.

- When importing the New-Students spreadsheet, include its checkbox column C. Exclude any row with a checked box and any course whose end date is before today in America/New_York. A course ending today remains eligible through today. Never import a header-only selection without the checkbox column, because that would bypass the owner’s exclusion rule.

- For an explicitly requested historical backfill, inventory the whole date range, deduplicate identical Zoom transcripts, and resolve actual meeting dates from meeting records. Preserve existing reviewed pages and chosen nicknames. Fill empty nickname fields only with respectful suggestions supported by transcript evidence; keep the supporting reasons in private authoring records. Do not invent assets or nicknames for students with no usable evidence. Publish reviewed historical sets through the teacher-only atomic import; keep the current quiz tied to the latest session and preserve all first-try results. Historical imports do not change the nightly cutoff or spending limits.

- Paused students belong in a default-closed section at the bottom of the administrator list. Search and the explicit paused filter may expand it. Keep account links and quiz status available inside the section.
- Account deletion is available only through the authenticated administrator host, with exact-origin checks, the current record revision, and typed confirmation of the saved student ID (or full name for drafts without an ID). Close access and nightly updates before removing the account, password, and all per-student learning records. Keep a minimal deletion receipt, retired address, and import fingerprints so retries are safe and imports or delayed writes cannot restore deleted accounts. Incomplete cleanup stays visibly paused and retryable. Never reuse a deleted student address. Preserve original Zoom transcripts and spreadsheet records. Test deletion only with fictional records; never delete real accounts merely to verify the interface.

- Student-page and administrator main menus use an edge-to-edge dark bar with white lettering, the logo’s blue underline with a thinner white line directly above it for the current item, and remain visible while scrolling. Keep Sign out inside both fixed menu bars, aligned to the right and reachable on mobile. Do not include Quiz results in the student menu, including in teacher view; preserve the private teacher results route and its link from the administrator account page. Keep sticky side panels and section jumps below the menu, including when mobile menu items wrap.
