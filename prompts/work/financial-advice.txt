Lesson 01: Advisor English: Discovery, Scope, Trust, and Boundaries
Original fictional case: Leah tells adviser Sam, "I want to retire comfortably." She has not supplied a target date, spending estimate, or current asset and liability summary. The introductory meeting covers discovery and the proposed planning service; no engagement scope or investment recommendation has been finalized. Leah asks whether a single savings target can be supplied immediately. Sam can explain the process and request information through the approved secure channel, but cannot responsibly fill the missing facts with assumptions presented as her circumstances.
Language workshop: Turning a retirement wish into usable planning facts
Communication goal: Clarify an imprecise client goal and explain the information needed before an individualized recommendation.
Language guidance: Comfortably is a useful expression of a goal, not a defined spending amount. A date and spending estimate need a basis: today's money or future amounts, household or individual, before or after tax. Do not silently choose those meanings for the client.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): You want retirement to feel financially secure and manageable. / What timing should we use for your target retirement date? / Does that estimate cover the household or only your own spending? / Are these amounts in today's money or projected future amounts? / Which costs are essential, and which could change? / Please provide current asset, liability, and pension information through the secure channel. / An initial illustration would use assumptions that we need to verify. / We will confirm the information and service scope before a personalized recommendation. / I cannot give a reliable single target from the goal alone. / The proposed scope does not automatically include tax or legal advice. / Reviewing the information does not authorize an investment change. / The dates and spending basis affect how we interpret the target. / We can record a provisional range without treating it as confirmed spending. / Who relies on the income included in this plan? / The goal is clear; the amount, timing, and current position remain incomplete. / Does this summary distinguish your confirmed facts from our open questions?
Vocabulary: discovery meeting: An initial discussion to understand a client's circumstances and aims. / fact-find: Structured collection of information relevant to planning or advice. / planning scope: The agreed topics and services included in a planning assignment. / engagement agreement: Terms describing the professional relationship and services. / financial goal: A desired outcome expressed with relevant amount, timing, and priority. / target retirement date: The date or period the client aims to stop or reduce work. / spending estimate: An approximation of expected outgoings on a stated basis. / essential spending: Costs the client treats as necessary for basic or committed needs. / discretionary spending: Outgoings the client has greater ability to adjust. / household cash flow: Money received and paid by a household over a period. / asset summary: An account of owned resources and their relevant characteristics. / liability summary: An account of debts and other obligations. / net worth: Assets less liabilities on a defined valuation basis. / pension statement: A record of pension information issued by a plan or provider. / income source: An identified origin of household receipts. / dependant: A person relying on the client for financial support in context. / emergency reserve: Resources intended for unexpected needs, with access and amount requiring context. / time horizon: The period before or over which money is expected to be needed. / constraint: A condition limiting available choices. / priority: Relative importance assigned to a goal or need. / planning assumption: A provisional input used to explore possible outcomes. / information gap: Missing data relevant to the assessment. / secure channel: An approved protected route for exchanging sensitive information. / scope limitation: A boundary on what the engagement or analysis covers.
Speaking task: Read Leah and Sam's corrected discovery exchange. Keep the retirement date, spending basis and current financial summary as missing facts, not invented estimates. Switch roles. Stress the requests for household scope and today's-money amounts, and the separate need to agree the planning service. Read the corrected transfer twice. Preserve all three missing qualifiers: currency, household coverage and before- or after-tax basis.
Partner: Leah: Prospective client / Sam: Financial adviser
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A client says they need "forty thousand a year" but has not identified the currency, household scope, or whether the amount is before or after tax. No target has been approved.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Leah: I sometimes help a family member with bills. The amount varies. How should we include that without pretending it is a fixed monthly expense?
Sam: Include that commitment with its uncertainty and importance to you. We should understand your priorities rather than assume every optional-looking payment is something you would comfortably stop.
Leah: I will bring the records and work out what I spend now. Please leave the investments as they are while we clarify the plan.
Sam: Correct. We will review the information and proposed engagement agreement before finalizing the scope and an individualized recommendation. Today's goal is a clear starting point, not an unsupported answer.
END REFERENCE
