📅 September 07, 2026 - Title: Welsh Government Seeks Input on New State-Owned Renewable Energy Entity
The Welsh government is seeking public consultation on a new state-owned energy entity designed to develop renewable assets, aiming to reduce long-term energy costs and foster community ownership, though it will not directly control consumer bills.

Read the story

The Welsh government is soliciting public input on a new state-owned renewable energy entity. This initiative aims to mitigate energy costs over time by developing and owning renewable energy assets and supporting local and community ownership. Furthermore, it would endeavor to attract investment into Welsh energy infrastructure and facilitate more organizations using locally generated energy. Currently, ministers are proposing to integrate Trydan Gwyrdd Cymru (TGC), an existing onshore wind and solar developer, with other Welsh energy schemes like Ynni Cymru. The consolidation seeks to create a single entity possessing the necessary expertise and critical mass of resources to effect significant change in the energy sector. Despite its objective to reduce expenses, the consultation states that the company would not replace existing energy suppliers or directly determine household electricity prices. Enterprise Minister Adam Price articulated a vision for Wales to have an energy system that is "clean, cheap and owned by us." Price contrasted this modern ambition with Wales's historical position as an "energy giant" during the Industrial Revolution, lamenting that the benefits of coal were not primarily derived by its own populace. He emphasized the imperative to avoid repeating such oversights with the immense potential presented by the contemporary renewable energy revolution. While many energy policy levers are determined at a UK level, Price suggested a public energy company could facilitate lower prices through specific agreements with large local energy consumers. The consultation also explores establishing a Welsh Energy Wealth Fund, intended to ensure energy project benefits reach vulnerable individuals and communities affected by project implementation. Furthermore, the new entity might acquire stakes in substantial energy ventures, including offshore wind, marine energy, and nuclear power. However, the proposals have faced scrutiny and criticism from political opponents such as the Welsh Conservatives and Reform parties. Janet Finch-Saunders, Welsh Conservative energy spokesperson, challenged the government's focus, stating that another state-owned company and more turbines are not the answer amidst manufacturing job losses. She asserted that Wales requires "cheap, reliable energy to keep factories open, businesses competitive and household bills down." Jason O'Connell, Shadow Cabinet Minister, echoed similar concerns, questioning the priority of building a publicly owned energy entity over actively lowering current high energy bills for Welsh families.

Words and grammar

1. soliciting (verb): Asking for or trying to obtain something from someone, often formally or persistently.
2. entity (noun): A thing with distinct and independent existence, especially a distinct organization or legal body.
3. acquire (verb): To obtain ownership or control of something, such as an asset or a share in a project.
4. mitigate (verb): To make something less severe, serious, or painful; to reduce the adverse effects or impact of something.
5. endeavor (verb): To try hard to do or achieve something, implying effort and often difficulty.
6. integrate (verb): To combine one thing with another so that they become a whole, or to become part of a whole.
7. consolidation (noun): The action or process of combining a number of things or entities into a single, more effective, or coherent whole.
8. critical mass (noun phrase): The amount of resources, expertise or support needed for an activity to become effective or successful.
9. articulated (verb): Expressed an idea or feeling fluently and coherently; gave clear expression to.
10. lamenting (verb): Expressing regret or disappointment about something that has happened.
11. imperative (noun): An essential or urgent thing; a command or duty that is extremely important.
12. facilitate (verb): To make an action or process easy or easier.
13. scrutiny (noun): Critical observation or examination.

Advanced Grammar: Concession: 'despite' with a noun phrase
Despite introduces a noun phrase presenting a fact that contrasts with the main clause. Here, the goal of reducing costs contrasts with limits on the company's role. Despite does not make the goal a guaranteed outcome; the opening can be expanded to 'although the company aims to reduce expenses'.
Example from the text: "Despite its objective to reduce expenses, the consultation states that the company would not replace existing energy suppliers or directly determine household electricity prices."

📅 September 06, 2026 - Title: Weather and air travel
Could changing weather mean more problems for travelers?

Read the story

The report examines whether climate change will bring more frequent disruption to aviation. Storms near London and across northern Europe caused extensive delays during a busy summer travel period. Controllers had to guide aircraft around storm cells, reducing the usable airspace. Congestion consequently left some departures waiting on the ground. Some planes might need to change their routes. Diverting to another airport creates additional demands for aircraft parking and passenger facilities. Pilots have access to weather radar and forecasts, but rapidly moving storms can still require decisions late in a flight. Longer diversions may increase fuel consumption and add to airlines’ operating costs. Flooding presents another risk by disrupting airport operations or blocking passengers’ access by road and rail. These concerns describe potential pressures on the system, rather than a prediction that every flight will be affected.

Words and grammar

1. aviation (noun): the activity and industry of flying aircraft
2. extensive (adjective): covering a large area or involving many parts
3. usable airspace (noun phrase): the part of the sky available for aircraft to use safely in the situation described
4. consequently (adverb): as a result of what happened before
5. fuel consumption (noun phrase): the amount of fuel used during an activity
6. operating costs (noun phrase): the expenses required to keep a business or service running
7. diversions (plural noun): changes that take aircraft away from their intended route or destination
8. storm cells (plural noun): distinct areas of storm activity that aircraft may need to avoid
9. potential pressures (noun phrase): possible demands or strains that could affect how a system operates
10. airport operations (noun phrase): the organised activities needed to keep an airport functioning

Advanced Grammar: Might for possibility
Might need to means a change is possible. It does not say every plane will change its route.
Example from the text: "Some planes might need to change their routes."

Read the story

Iran has unequivocally stated its readiness to counter intensified economic sanctions recently imposed by the United States. Washington describes its latest punitive measures as an "economic D-Day," signaling a severe escalation in financial pressure. However, Tehran contends it possesses a comprehensive strategy to navigate these significant challenges. The Islamic Republic asserts that it has developed various mechanisms to mitigate the impact of these unilateral restrictions effectively. This declaration comes amidst persistent tensions between the two nations regarding Iran's nuclear ambitions and regional influence. Iranian officials have consistently maintained that such coercive tactics will not compel them to alter their sovereign policies. Instead, they view these actions as a direct assault on their national sovereignty and economic resilience. The government claims to have meticulously studied past sanctions regimes to formulate a robust defense against current and future pressures. Furthermore, authorities indicate that they are prepared for a prolonged period of economic confrontation with the West. This proactive stance underscores Iran's determination to resist external economic pressure and maintain its independent trajectory.

Words and grammar

1. contends (verb): To assert something as a position in an argument or to struggle to surmount a difficulty or opposition.
2. comprehensive strategy (noun phrase): A detailed and far-reaching plan designed to address all aspects of a particular situation or achieve a specific long-term goal.
3. resilience (noun): The capacity to recover quickly from difficulties; toughness, or the ability of an economy or system to withstand or recover from shocks.
4. escalation (noun): A rapid increase in intensity, seriousness, or scope, often referring to conflicts or crises.
5. intensified (verb (past participle)): Made or become more extreme, severe, or stronger; often referring to efforts, operations, or phenomena.
6. underscores (verb): Emphasizes, highlights, or draws particular attention to a fact, idea, or situation.
7. amidst (preposition): In the middle of or surrounded by; in the atmosphere or a state of.
8. unequivocally (adverb): in a way that leaves no uncertainty about the stated position
9. sovereignty (noun): Supreme power or authority; the full right and power of a governing body over itself, without external interference.
10. trajectory (noun): the direction in which events or policies appear to be developing

Advanced Grammar: Present Participle Clauses for Consequence/Result
A present participle clause (starting with -ing form of a verb) can be used to describe an action or event that happens as a result or consequence of the main clause's action. It typically follows the main clause and adds explanatory detail, indicating what the result of the preceding statement is, creating a more concise and sophisticated sentence structure.
