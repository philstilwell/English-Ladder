Course: Pharmacy Technician English
Professional audience: Pharmacy technicians and pharmacy support staff
Scope: All patients, orders, amounts, times, and incidents are fictional language-practice examples, not medication, insurance, legal, dispensing, or technical training. Duties and supervision vary by jurisdiction and workplace. Pharmacists and other qualified professionals make clinical and regulated decisions. These exercises do not authorize dispensing, transfers, substitutions, handling methods, or changes to medication instructions.
Lesson 01: Prescription intake without assumptions
Original fictional case: An order's strength field is unclear. The patient remembers a blue label and says their prescriber usually orders the same thing. The technician cannot select a strength. Pharmacist Maya can take the clarification; no completion time is known.
Language workshop: Separate recollection from confirmation
Communication goal: Identify an unclear prescription detail and explain who can clarify it.
Language guidance: Name the exact field that needs checking. Attribute remembered information to the patient without treating it as a verified current order. 'Awaiting clarification' describes a status, not a cancellation.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): The current prescription shows ___, but ___ is unclear. / You remember ___; we still need to confirm ___. / The order is awaiting ___, not ___.
Vocabulary: prescription: An order for a medicine issued by a prescriber, subject to the pharmacy's required checks. / patient profile: The pharmacy record linking a patient's verified identifiers with prescription and other relevant information. / prescriber: A healthcare professional permitted to issue the medicine order concerned. / strength: The amount of active ingredient in a specified unit or concentration of a medicine.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Patient: your previous label was blue. You expect the same prescription, but cannot read the strength on today's order.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: Ask whether awaiting clarification means the prescription has been cancelled.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: The current prescription's strength field is unclear. Your memory of the blue label gives us some background, but it does not confirm this order. Maya, our pharmacist, can handle the clarification. For now, the order is awaiting review; it has not been cancelled, and I cannot give you a collection time yet.
END REFERENCE

Lesson 02: Insurance claims in plain English
Original fictional case: The claim response says prior authorization required. No plan decision or accepted claim has arrived. The patient thinks this is a permanent exclusion. Billing colleague Inez has accepted the status query and can explain the request route.
Language workshop: Explain a requirement without predicting the decision
Communication goal: Distinguish a coverage review, a claim response, and a patient's payment.
Language guidance: Use one plain-English explanation before returning to the technical term. 'Required' does not mean 'approved.' An authorization decision and the amount on a processed claim answer different questions.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): This response requires ___; it does not yet confirm ___. / The plan's review concerns ___, whereas the claim amount concerns ___. / The current claim shows ___; the previous amount was ___.
Vocabulary: claim rejection: An insurer's response saying that a submitted pharmacy claim was not accepted as submitted. / prior authorization: A health plan's review-and-approval requirement for certain coverage requests. / formulary: A health plan's list of medicines, often organized with coverage conditions and cost-sharing categories. / copayment: A fixed amount the patient pays for a covered medicine or service under the applicable plan.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Patient: you believe a request for prior authorization means permanent exclusion. Ask who is handling the query.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: Ask whether receiving an approval would automatically make the prescription free.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: This response says prior authorization is required. That is a request for the plan's review, not the final coverage decision. We have neither an approval decision nor an accepted claim yet. Inez is handling the status query and can explain how the request proceeds; I cannot promise coverage or a final copayment.

Dialogue 08: A correction must reach the listener
Original fictional setting: Fictional pharmacy debrief: an internal note was corrected, but a patient still has an inaccurate delivery date.
Professional roles: Pharmacist / Technician
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Pharmacist: The record now says delivery unconfirmed. Has the patient heard that correction, or only the afternoon team?
Technician: Only the team. The patient still has my earlier message giving Friday as a definite date.
Pharmacist: Then the record is more accurate, but the communication problem remains. Who can contact the patient today?
Technician: I can, using the recorded preference. I'll identify my earlier wording rather than blame the supplier.
Pharmacist: What will you say if the patient asks whether the corrected date is now Saturday?
Technician: There isn't a confirmed replacement date. I'll say the Friday guarantee was wrong and arrival remains unconfirmed.
Pharmacist: Good. Please acknowledge that the patient may have arranged transport, without assuming whether they actually did.
Technician: I'll ask whether the message affected their plans and explain the next supplier-status check at noon tomorrow.
Pharmacist: Can you own tomorrow's update as well, or do we need a handoff before you leave?
Technician: I'm scheduled here tomorrow and can own it. I'll keep that contact separate from the stock enquiry.
Pharmacist: Then the outstanding tasks are today's correction call and tomorrow's update, not a promised delivery.
Technician: I'll make today's call now and record what was communicated, including any contact attempt that doesn't connect.
END REFERENCE
