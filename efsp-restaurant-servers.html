Your goal

Clarify an ambiguous modifier and confirm the complete order.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

Table 12 has three covers. Seat one orders mushroom pasta, seat two orders a salad with dressing separately, and seat three has not chosen. The phrase 'no onions' was spoken without a clear seat reference. Clarify before sending the order.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

cover
One diner counted for restaurant table service.
seat number
A numbered position at a table used to match an order to a diner.
modifier
An order detail that changes the standard dish, such as sauce on the side.
read-back
A spoken repetition of an order so the guest can correct it.

Useful word combinations

  • take an order: record what each diner requests
  • confirm the seat number: match the dish to the correct diner
  • put the sauce on the side: request a separate serving of sauce
  • read the order back: repeat the details before sending them

03 · Notice the language

Attach each change to a dish

A modifier needs a dish and a diner. 'On the side' means separately, not omitted. Ask a focused question before reading back the order.

  • Was ___ for your ___ or for ___?
  • I have ___ for seat ___, with ___.
  • You're still deciding on ___; is that right?

Improve this: Two salads, one different.

See a clearer version

One salad for seat two, dressing on the side and no onions.

The revised order identifies the diner and both changes.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Guest: The dressing should come separately, not be left out.
Server: I'll request it ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. on the side

  1. A. mixed through the salad Mixing it through contradicts the request for separate service.
  2. B. poured over the salad Pouring it over the salad doesn't keep it separate.
  3. C. without dressing This removes the dressing rather than serving it separately.
  4. D. on the side Correct. The dressing arrives in a separate serving.
2. Server: I heard 'no onions,' but not which dish.
Guest: It was my salad.
Server: Could you confirm ____?

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. which seat you are sitting in

  1. A. in which seat are you sitting Moving the preposition doesn't remove the need for embedded statement order.
  2. B. which seat are you sitting in This uses direct-question word order after an introductory question.
  3. C. which seat do you sit in After 'confirm', use statement order rather than 'do you'.
  4. D. which seat you are sitting in Correct. The embedded question keeps the subject before its verb.

05 · Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Whose onion request?

Table 12: the three-cover order in the case.

  1. Server: I caught the request for no onions, but which dish does that apply to?
  2. Guest: My salad, please. My friend wants the mushroom pasta exactly as it normally comes.
  3. Server: Thank you. Are you also the person who asked for dressing on the side?
  4. Guest: Yes, I want the dressing separately, not removed from the order altogether, please.
  5. Server: Then seat two has the salad, no onions, with dressing in a separate serving.
  6. Guest: That's right. The person beside the window hasn't chosen a main course yet.
  7. Server: I have seat one down for pasta and seat three as still deciding.
  8. Guest: Could you give seat three another minute before you send the main course order?
  9. Server: Certainly. I'll wait here a moment; would another menu help with the choice?
  10. Guest: Yes, please. The third menu is underneath our coats, and another would be useful.
2. Moving chairs after ordering

Two diners have swapped seats before a runner arrives.

  1. Runner: I have the vegetable tart for seat one. Did anyone change seats after ordering?
  2. Guest: Yes, we swapped so my colleague could sit farther away from the speaker.
  3. Runner: Thanks for telling me. Who ordered the tart with the small green salad?
  4. Guest: I did, and I'm now sitting beside the aisle rather than the window.
  5. Runner: Then this plate belongs here. The pasta is for your colleague by the window?
  6. Guest: Correct. Neither of us changed the dishes; we only moved to different chairs.
  7. Runner: I'll tell your server about the move so the dessert orders reach you correctly.
  8. Guest: Please do. We're ordering different desserts and would rather not switch plates again.
  9. Runner: I'll identify the dishes as I bring them, as well as checking the updated seats.
  10. Guest: That works. You can put the tart here, with the salad beside it.
3. A phrase lost in the noise

A server checks an incomplete order during a busy lunch.

  1. Server: I'm sorry, the music covered your last words. Was that soup and a sandwich?
  2. Guest: Soup instead of a sandwich, actually. I don't need both items for lunch.
  3. Server: Thank you for correcting that. Which soup would you like from today's two choices?
  4. Guest: The tomato soup, please, with the bread that appears beside it on the menu.
  5. Server: I have one tomato soup with bread. Would you like the small or large portion?
  6. Guest: The small one. I only have time for a light lunch before my meeting.
  7. Server: Let me read that back: one small tomato soup with bread, and no sandwich.
  8. Guest: Exactly. Could you also check whether my colleague's drink has already been ordered?
  9. Server: Your colleague asked me for sparkling water earlier, and that order is already in.
  10. Guest: Great. Then the soup is the only item you still need to add.

Try a harder second round

Seat one also wants dressing for a side salad. Keep that request separate from seat two's main salad.

Scenario 2 · Two similar side dishes

A table orders one baked potato and one portion of mashed potatoes. The server hears 'without butter' but cannot identify the dish.

Role A: Server: clarify the dish and repeat both side orders.

Role B: Guest: the baked potato is yours and you prefer it without butter; the mashed potatoes belong to your companion.

Possible opening: “Was the no-butter request for the baked potato or the mashed potatoes?”

Success checks
  • Obtain the dish name.
  • Attach the preference to the baked potato only.
  • Read back both orders without inventing an ingredient claim.
Add a complication

The guest points instead of naming the dish, and both diners are holding menus.

Studying alone? Speak both roles aloud. For each scenario, prepare for two minutes, speak for one minute, then answer a follow-up. Switch roles and repeat using fewer notes. Use only the supplied facts; identify missing information instead of inventing it.

Compare scenario 1 with a model response
Before I send table twelve's order, could I check who requested no onions? I have mushroom pasta for seat one and a salad with dressing on the side for seat two. Seat three is still deciding. I'll attach the onion request to the correct dish and read those details back.

This is one possible spoken response, not the only acceptable wording. Preserve the case facts when you try your own version.

Check your reasoning

Review, then try again

  • Uses understandable sentences and explains specialist terms when the listener needs it.
  • Responds to the other person's question, checks understanding, and uses an appropriate tone.
  • Makes the requested action or unresolved question clear without inventing authority or facts.

Revise one sentence and repeat the response. A useful response can be clear even with a few grammar errors; judge meaning and task completion, not accent.

Vocabulary for this lesson

a la carte
Ordered as a separate menu item with its own price.
set menu
A group of courses offered together for a stated price and stated choices.
supplement
An extra charge for a specified menu choice.
side dish

Answer: A. not canceled

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

remake
A newly prepared replacement for a dish that needs correction.
replacement
An item supplied in place of another item.
check-back
A brief return to the table to ask whether the food and service meet the guest's request.
service recovery
Actions taken to address a service problem and restore the guest's confidence.

Useful word combinations

  • acknowledge the mix-up: recognize that an order was confused

Correct the order and offer a choice

A useful apology names the error. Give the available option and its wait before asking for a choice; a replacement isn't automatically a refund.

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Guest: I ordered pasta, not risotto.
Server: I'm sorry ____ the wrong dish.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. we brought

  1. A. we bringing A finite verb is missing.
  2. B. we have bring The past participle would be 'brought'.
  3. C. we bring Present tense does not identify this completed mistake.
  4. D. we brought Correct. Past tense acknowledges what happened.
2. Guest: Does 'remake' mean you remove the charge?
Server: Here, it means ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. a newly prepared replacement dish

  1. A. the charge removed from the bill Removing a charge is a bill adjustment, not the remake itself.
  2. B. the existing dish reheated Reheating an existing dish isn't the promised fresh preparation.
  3. C. the original order canceled Canceling ends the order rather than supplying its replacement.
  4. D. a newly prepared replacement dish Correct. The kitchen prepares a fresh replacement for the dish.

Your goal

Distinguish equal payment shares from item-based bills.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

Three diners have a fictional $72 total with all listed charges included. They request 'three ways': this could mean equal payments or separate checks by item. The restaurant supports either before payment. Ask which they mean; equal shares would be $24.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

itemized bill
A bill showing separate dishes, drinks, and other charges.
split payment
Payment of one bill using more than one payment contribution.
separate checks
Individual bills for different diners or groups at the same table.
service charge
A charge added for service under the restaurant's stated policy, distinct from an optional extra tip.

Useful word combinations

  • split the bill evenly: divide the amount into equal shares
  • allocate an item: place a specific charge on a particular check
  • query a charge: ask why an amount appears on the bill
  • bring a corrected bill: provide an updated statement after an error is confirmed

03 · Notice the language

Check the meaning of 'split'

'Three ways' doesn't identify a division method. Confirm the method and visible charges before presenting the payment request; never ask learners to handle card credentials.

  • Would you like equal shares or ___?
  • This charge refers to ___ on the bill.
  • Before payment, could we confirm ___?

Improve this: I'll put a third on each card.

See a clearer version

Would you prefer equal thirds or separate checks by item?

The question avoids deciding a financial preference for the diners.

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Guest: We want everyone to pay for their own dishes.
Server: Then you want ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. separate checks by item

  1. A. three equal contributions Equal contributions ignore different item totals.
  2. B. an even split Equal shares may not match each person's items.
  3. C. one shared total only A shared total alone doesn't meet the request.
  4. D. separate checks by item Correct. Items are assigned to the person paying for them.
2. Guest: Our total is seventy-two dollars, and we'd like equal thirds.
Server: That's ____ each.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. twenty-four dollars

  1. A. twenty-four dollars Correct. Three equal shares of 72 are 24.
  2. B. thirty-six dollars That would split the total into two shares.
  3. C. seventy-two dollars That repeats the whole total.
  4. D. eighteen dollars That would split the total into four shares.

01 · Read the situation

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

pre-bus
Remove used items during a meal after checking that diners have finished with them.
place setting
The tableware arranged for one diner.
sharing plate
A plate provided so a diner can take a portion of a shared dish.
crumb
A small loose piece of food, especially bread, on a table or plate.

Useful word combinations

  • clear an empty plate: remove a plate the diner has finished using
  • leave the serving dish: keep the shared dish on the table
  • reset a place: arrange tableware for the next diner or course
  • ask before reaching: get the guest's attention before entering their table space

03 · Notice the language

Ask about use, not appearance

An empty plate may still be in use. Use 'finished with' for an object; 'finished eating' asks about the person's meal.

  • Are you finished with ___?
  • Shall I leave ___ for now?
  • May I reach across to remove ___?

Improve this: You're done, right?

See a clearer version

Are you finished with this serving dish, or would you like it left here?

A specific, neutral choice avoids pressure to finish.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Guest: I'm using this empty plate for more vegetables.
Server: I'll ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. leave it here

  1. A. clear it now The guest explicitly says the plate is still needed.
  2. B. leave it here Correct. Leaving it respects the guest's stated use.
  3. C. replace it before you continue The guest hasn't requested a different plate or interruption.
  4. D. clear it with the serving dish Both items remain in use for the next portion.
2. Server: Are you finished ____ the bread plate?
Guest: No, please leave it.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. with

  1. A. from 'From' does not form this expression.
  2. B. by 'By' would introduce a deadline or agent in other structures.
  3. C. with Correct. 'Finished with' means no longer using something.
  4. D. at 'Finished at' would usually introduce a time or place.
