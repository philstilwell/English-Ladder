📅 September 02, 2026 - Europe responds to an accusation
Choose what you read. This report includes distressing events. You can choose an everyday story on Discover instead.

Read the story

Germany has blamed Russia for a failed drone attack on Leipzig airport, prompting calls for a stronger European response. Moscow rejects the allegation that it targeted a Ukrainian cargo aircraft. An explosive drone at the airport was made safe using a robot. Leipzig is an important Nato hub and is regularly used by Ukrainian transport aircraft. The European Commission and Nato have vowed to increase pressure on Russia. Nato’s leadership has also affirmed its support for Germany. Berlin announced plans to close a Russian consulate and a Russian cultural institution. Moscow responded by announcing the closure of German cultural centres in Russia. European ministers are considering tighter enforcement of sanctions alongside additional visa restrictions. Russia is accused of acting more recklessly.

Words and grammar

1. allegation (noun): a claim of wrongdoing that is being reported or disputed
2. vowed (verb): promised very strongly to do something.
3. affirmed (verb): states or confirms a position clearly
4. consulate (noun): an office representing a country and assisting its citizens abroad
5. enforcement (noun): the process of putting rules or restrictions into effect
6. cultural institution (noun phrase): an organisation established to support cultural activities or exchanges
7. closure (noun): the ending of an institution’s or facility’s operations
8. targeted (verb (past participle)): Singled out or directed at a specific object, person, or area, often with hostile intent or as an objective.
9. alongside (preposition): together with or in addition to another measure
10. prompting (verb): causing or encouraging a response or further action

Advanced Grammar: Reporting an accusation
“Is accused of” reports a claim about wrongdoing. It does not establish that the claim is true.
Example from the text: "Russia is accused of acting more recklessly."
