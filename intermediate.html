📅 October 09, 2026 - €5 Million Wine Heist at Italian Producer Marchesi Antinori
Thieves recently took 30,000 bottles of valuable wine from Marchesi Antinori, a prestigious Italian producer, in what is believed to be the largest such robbery in Italy.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Reports indicate that thieves took 30,000 bottles of wine valued at €5 million from an Italian producer. This large-scale robbery, or "heist," happened at a prestigious wine producer based in Florence, Italy. Marchesi Antinori, a family-owned producer, confirmed the incident at its storage facility in Tuscany. The incident occurred on a Saturday as the producer was preparing a consignment for international customers. At least seven masked men are believed to have deactivated alarms and disabled cameras during the heist. They reportedly used forklifts belonging to the producer to load valuable Tuscan red wines onto two large trucks. The chief executive stated this was the largest heist of its kind in Italy. The producer suspects the criminals were wine connoisseurs and had inside knowledge, given the specific bottles chosen.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. Reports indicate (verb phrase): To show or suggest something based on available information or news.
2. valued at (prepositional phrase): Having a particular monetary amount or worth.
3. prestigious (adjective): Highly respected and admired because of its importance or high quality.
4. consignment (noun): A batch of goods that is sent or delivered for sale or storage.
5. deactivated (verb): Made a device or system stop working or become inactive.
6. disabled (verb): Made a machine, system, or security feature unable to function.
7. forklifts (noun): Powerful industrial vehicles used for lifting and moving heavy loads, often on pallets.
8. connoisseurs (noun): People who have expert knowledge and appreciation of a particular subject, especially fine food, drink, or art.

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice: 'be believed to have'
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action rather than who performed it. When reporting beliefs or opinions about past events, we can use the structure 'subject + be + believed + to have + past participle'. This construction is common in news reporting to talk about what people think happened.
Example from the text: "At least seven masked men are believed to have deactivated alarms and disabled cameras during the heist."

📅 October 08, 2026 - US and Lebanon Shelter Syrian Official Linked to Abduction
A BBC investigation has revealed that US and Lebanese authorities are sheltering a former Syrian military official linked to serious crimes in exchange for intelligence.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

A BBC investigation says a former Syrian military official wanted over serious crimes is being sheltered by US and Lebanese authorities. Bassam al-Hassan ran Syria’s chemical weapons programme and advised former President Assad; he is now being harboured in Lebanon. An investigation found Hassan can remain there under an arrangement with the FBI and other US and Lebanese authorities. He is willing to provide information about the abduction of American journalist Austin Tice in 2012, and other intelligence. The BBC revealed Tice's abduction was a planned operation, with Hassan in the chain of command. Syria's new government wants Hassan returned to face trial, but US authorities wish him to remain in Lebanon. Hassan was offered 'residency', 'protection', and help with his 'living needs' in exchange for information, mainly about Tice. However, people close to the Tice investigation report little evidence of significant progress since Hassan began providing information. Some sources are concerned that this arrangement with the US could continue indefinitely, with high costs and little benefit.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. military official (noun phrase): a person who holds a position of authority in a country's armed forces.
2. sheltered (verb): Given a place to stay and protection; here, protection from being sent back to face trial.
3. harboured (verb): Given shelter and protection, especially when being sought by authorities; the word often carries a negative tone.
4. arrangement (noun): a plan or agreement that has been made, often informally.
5. abduction (noun): the act of taking someone away illegally by force, typically for a ransom or other purpose.
6. chain of command (noun phrase): a system in an organization where instructions are passed down from the highest level of authority to the lowest.
7. residency (noun): the legal right to live in a particular country or place.
8. significant (adjective): Large or important enough to matter; here, meaningful progress in the investigation.
9. indefinitely (adverb): for an unlimited or unspecified period of time.

Grammar Focus: The Passive Voice
The passive uses a form of “be” + a past participle. “Is being harboured” puts Hassan, the person receiving shelter, in the subject position. An active version would name those sheltering him: “The authorities are harbouring Hassan”.
Example from the text: "Bassam al-Hassan ran Syria’s chemical weapons programme and advised former President Assad; he is now being harboured in Lebanon."

The recent summit between Trump and Xi in Washington was notable for what was not explicitly said. On Monday, China released details of some concrete outcomes from the talks. However, the summit produced no joint statements and few major breakthroughs. Instead, both governments issued separate accounts, suggesting they remain wary of each other. Beijing and Washington agreed to launch a new dialogue on artificial intelligence. They also established a communication channel for AI-related incidents to prevent broader confrontation. Chinese state media confirmed the trade truce has been extended until January 10. However, Beijing had hoped for a much longer extension to this truce, making it a modest gain. China secured lower tariffs on $30bn of goods entering the US, with reciprocal reductions expected. Strikingly, there was no mention of increasing China's rare earth mineral exports to the US. The White House factsheet made no mention of Taiwan, although it was clearly a priority for Xi Jinping. According to Beijing, Xi urged Trump to formally oppose Taiwan independence. The US State Department continues to classify travel to China as a Level 2 advisory, urging increased caution. Ultimately, contentious issues from advanced technology to Taiwan remain unresolved after the summit.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. notable (adjective): Important or interesting; deserving attention.
2. concrete outcomes (noun phrase): Results or achievements that are definite and clearly visible, not just ideas or plans.
3. breakthroughs (noun): Important discoveries or developments that help solve a problem.
4. wary (adjective): Feeling or showing caution about possible dangers or problems.
5. dialogue (noun): A formal discussion between two groups or countries, especially to resolve problems.
6. confrontation (noun): A hostile or argumentative meeting or situation between opposing parties.
7. trade truce (noun phrase): A temporary stop or pause in a trade war or disputes between countries.
8. tariffs (noun): Taxes imposed by a government on imported or exported goods.
9. priority (noun): A thing that is regarded as more important than others.
10. unresolved (adjective): Not solved or settled; still needing an answer or a decision.

Grammar Focus: Present Perfect Passive
The present perfect passive is used to describe an action that happened at an unspecified time in the past and has a connection to the present, focusing on the action itself rather than who performed it. It is formed with "has/have been" + the past participle of the main verb. For example, in the sentence "The trade truce has been extended," we know the truce is now longer, but the focus is on the extension, not specifically on who extended it.
Example from the text: "Chinese state media confirmed the trade truce has been extended until January 10."
