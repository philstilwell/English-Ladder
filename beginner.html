📅 November 04, 2026 - New Graduates Use AI at Work
Some new graduates use AI at work, but they also need other skills.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Lotte Underwood has finished university and uses AI tools for much of her work. Her team's AI tools are computer programs that help with tasks like research and making pictures. She checks their work and shares it with other people in her team. She also joins meetings to learn how these tools can help customers. Underwood wants to learn other skills for her job too. Patel builds AI tools that give information to different teams. Patel also looks for tasks that AI could do, such as work that people repeat. Some graduates work with experienced staff to learn their jobs. They move to a different area of work every six months. Claire Reed believes too much AI work may not help graduates build their future careers. She says workers need AI skills and knowledge of what their customers want.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. tasks (plural noun): Pieces of work that someone needs to do.
2. checks (verb): Looks at something to see if it is correct.
3. shares (verb): Gives something to other people so they can use it too.
4. meetings (plural noun): Times when people come together to talk about something.
5. skills (plural noun): Things you can do well because you have learned or practised them.
6. staff (noun): The people who work for a company.

Grammar Focus: “Can” followed by a verb
Use “can” to say that someone or something is able to do something. Put the verb in its simple form after “can”, without “to”. Use the same form with every person: “I can help”, “she can help” and “they can help”.
Example from the text: "She also joins meetings to learn how these tools can help customers."

📅 November 03, 2026 - Collins Chooses Mogging as Its Word of the Year
Learn about a popular word and how people use it.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Collins English Dictionary chose mogging as its word of the year. Mogging means doing better than someone or getting more attention than them. People often use this word to talk about how someone looks. It became much more common in 2026. Young people use it online and in everyday conversation. It can be a joke about someone or a way to say something nice about them. How people understand it can change with the situation. Collins uses social media to learn how English is changing. Its dictionary team checks how often people use words.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. chose (verb, past simple): Picked one person or thing from several.
2. attention (noun): The interest people show when they watch or listen to someone.
3. common (adjective): Used or seen often.
4. online (adverb): On the internet.
5. conversation (noun): A talk between people.
6. situation (noun): What is happening at a particular time and place.

Grammar Focus: Can for possibility
Use “can” to say that something is possible. Put the basic verb after “can”, as in “can be” or “can change”. This verb stays the same with “he”, “she” and “it”. Do not add “to” before the verb.
Example from the text: "It can be a joke about someone or a way to say something nice about them."

📅 November 02, 2026 - Oil Kills Birds and Closes Park
Read about a bird park and the people helping after oil entered its pond.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

A bird park in County Antrim has closed while workers remove oil from its pond. Someone damaged a nearby oil tank, and oil from it killed 13 birds. The oil was a type that people use to heat their homes. More than 20 birds went to an animal care centre for special help. The team is helping geese, ducks and swans. They washed the birds and gave them special care as quickly as possible. The water is much cleaner now, but workers still need to do more. Most birds at the centre cannot return until the pond is completely clean. Police are asking people to share any information about what happened.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. pond (noun): A small area of water with land around it.
2. damaged (verb, simple past): Broke something or made it work less well.
3. nearby (adjective): Not far away.
4. heat (verb): To make something warm or hot.
5. care (noun): Help to keep people or animals healthy and safe.
6. washed (verb, simple past): Cleaned with water.

Grammar Focus: Simple past
Use the simple past to talk about finished actions. For many verbs, add “-ed”, as in “washed”. Some verbs change their form: “give” becomes “gave”. Use these forms with “they”, “he” and “she”.

1. fund (noun): A sum of money saved or collected for a special purpose.
2. government (noun): The group of people who officially control a country.
3. billion (noun): The number 1,000,000,000.
4. green energy (noun): Power that comes from nature and does not harm the environment.
5. Officials (noun): People who hold important jobs in a government or business.
6. missing (adjective): Lost, or not in the right place.

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense
We use the simple past tense to talk about finished actions in the past. Regular verbs usually end in "-ed" in the past tense. For example, "calculated" is the past tense of "calculate".
Example from the text: "Officials calculated the possible value of a missing British fund."
