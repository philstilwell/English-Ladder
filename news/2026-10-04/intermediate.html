Read the story

Major bridges in Kyiv have been affected by Russian aerial vehicle strikes for four consecutive days. Two people were injured after the Pivnichnyi Bridge was struck on Sunday morning. This recent assault occurred during German Chancellor Friedrich Merz's unexpected journey to Kyiv. Russia's military announced it would increase attacks on Kyiv and other Ukrainian regions. Video recordings of Sunday's incident showed cars on Pivnichnyi Bridge when an aerial vehicle crashed into it, creating an explosion. Other videos showed a noticeable opening in a traffic lane on the bridge. The two impacted bridges are still standing, but damage has caused significant traffic problems in parts of the capital. While emergency services worked on the incidents, Germany's Merz held a meeting with President Zelensky in Kyiv.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. affected (verb): To have an influence on someone or something, often causing a change or impact.
2. strikes (noun): Attacks, especially military ones, that involve hitting a target with force.
3. consecutive (adjective): Following one after another without interruption.
4. assault (noun): A strong verbal or physical attack on someone or something.
5. unexpected (adjective): Not anticipated or predicted; happening suddenly without prior warning.
6. increase (verb): To become greater in amount, number, or degree; to make something larger.
7. incident (noun): An event or occurrence, often one that is unpleasant or unusual.
8. explosion (noun): A sudden burst of energy or force, often with a loud noise, causing destruction.
9. noticeable (adjective): Easily seen or observed; prominent enough to attract attention.
10. significant (adjective): Sufficiently great or important to be worthy of attention; noteworthy.

Grammar Focus: The Passive Voice
We use the passive voice to talk about an action when we want to emphasize the object of the action or when the doer of the action is unknown or less important. The structure often uses a form of "to be" + the past participle of the main verb. For example, in news reports, the passive voice focuses on what happened, rather than who did it.
Example from the text: "Major bridges in Kyiv have been affected by Russian aerial vehicle strikes for four consecutive days."
