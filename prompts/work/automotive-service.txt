5. Finish with two new four-choice collocation questions for later review, with a clearly separate answer key. Do not claim to schedule a reminder.

Lesson 01: Capturing the customer's concern
Original fictional case: Customer reports an occasional rattle from the rear after the car has been parked overnight. They do not know its cause. Two household cars are on the account; today's booking is the blue hatchback. No technician assessment has occurred.
Language workshop: Record the concern without rewriting it as a diagnosis
Communication goal: Clarify vehicle identity and reported circumstances.
Language guidance: Customer descriptions guide the handoff but do not establish a mechanical cause.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): Which vehicle does this concern relate to? / You notice ___ when ___; is that accurate? / I'll record ___ as reported, with the cause unconfirmed.
Vocabulary: repair order: The job record linking a vehicle, customer concern, agreed work, and service results. / intermittent concern: A reported problem that occurs sometimes rather than continuously. / symptom description: The customer's account of what they noticed, without a confirmed explanation of cause. / vehicle identification number: The vehicle's unique identifying code, commonly shortened to VIN.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Customer: the blue hatchback rattles on some mornings after overnight parking; you cannot identify a component.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: Customer suggests a component name from an online video. Record it as their suggestion, not a diagnosis.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Let me confirm that this concern is for the blue hatchback, not the other car on your account. You hear an occasional rear rattle after the car has been parked overnight. Have I captured that accurately? I'll record your description for the technician without naming a faulty component before an assessment.
