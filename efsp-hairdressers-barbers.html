01Turning a reference photo into a clear requestAsk about features, not just the whole photograph · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Resolve conflicting length preferences before a haircut.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A client shows a chin-length bob but wants to keep enough length for a low ponytail. No cut has started. The photo has a straight fringe; the client has not requested one. Clarify which features matter and confirm a collarbone-length outline.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

consultation
A discussion with a client to establish the desired hair service, preferences, and limits.
reference photo
An image used to discuss a desired hairstyle, not a guarantee of an identical result.
perimeter
The outer outline of a haircut, including the visible line at the ends.
fringe
Hair cut to fall over the forehead; commonly called bangs in US English.

Useful word combinations

  • keep the length: retain the overall length the client identifies
  • take off the ends: shorten the ends by an agreed amount
  • frame the face: arrange the front shape around the face
  • agree on the outline: confirm the visible shape before starting

C Notice the language

Ask about features, not just the whole photograph

A photograph contains several choices. Separate length, outline, and fringe; use an indirect question to find the feature the client means.

  • Which part of the photo would you like to keep?
  • When you say short, do you mean ___?
  • Could you confirm whether you want ___?

Improve this: I'll do it like the picture.

See a clearer version

You like the outline, but want more length and no fringe. Have I understood?

The revised response checks individual choices instead of assuming every detail is wanted.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Client: I like the photograph, but I need a ponytail.
Stylist: Could you clarify ____?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. which feature you want to copy

  1. A. which feature you do wants to copy 'You do wants' is not a grammatical verb form.
  2. B. which feature want you to copy The subject 'you' must precede 'want' here.
  3. C. which feature you want to copy Correct. The indirect question uses subject before verb.
  4. D. which feature do you want to copy Remove 'do' after 'clarify' in this indirect question.
2. Client: Keep the front long; I don't want hair across my forehead.
Stylist: So you don't want a ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. fringe

  1. A. ponytail The client specifically needs to retain a ponytail.
  2. B. fringe Correct. A fringe falls across the forehead.
  3. C. perimeter The perimeter is the overall outer outline.
  4. D. parting A parting separates the hair into sections on the head.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. A bob photograph with a ponytail requirement

A first consultation before any cutting.

  1. Stylist: What appeals to you about this reference photo: the length, the fringe, or the outline?
  2. Client: Mostly the neat outline. I still need to tie my hair back at work.
  3. Stylist: The pictured length ends at the chin. That conflicts with your low ponytail requirement.
  4. Client: I hadn't noticed how short it was. Could we keep it near my collarbone?
  5. Stylist: Let's discuss that longer perimeter. Are you also asking for hair across your forehead?
  6. Client: No fringe, please. I wear glasses, and I like keeping the front tucked away.
  7. Stylist: So the photograph guides the outline, not the length or the front section.
  8. Client: Exactly. Could you show me the proposed length in the mirror before starting?
  9. Stylist: Yes, here is the point we discussed. Does this match what you meant?
  10. Client: It does. Keep that length, and leave the front long enough to tuck.
2. What does a tidy-up include?

A barber clarifies a returning client's brief request.

  1. Barber: When you say a tidy-up today, which parts feel too long or untidy?
  2. Client: Around my ears, mainly. I'm growing the top out for a different style.
  3. Barber: Then shortening the top isn't part of your request. What about the neckline?
  4. Client: That needs attention too, but I don't want the whole cut much shorter.
  5. Barber: Understood. Your last appointment note says a short all-over cut; today's preference differs.
  6. Client: Yes, please don't use that old description. I've been growing it for weeks.
  7. Barber: I'll record that you're keeping the top and changing the outline around the ears.
  8. Client: Could you also check the back with me? I can't describe it very well.
  9. Barber: Certainly. We'll use the hand mirror to agree on the outline together first.
  10. Client: That helps. The top stays long, and we agree on the edges before starting.
3. Bangs and fringe mean the same area

A visitor uses a different regional word at the salon.

  1. Stylist: You mentioned bangs on the phone. Our booking note uses fringe; that's the same area.
  2. Client: Good, because I wondered whether I'd booked a completely different kind of haircut.
  3. Stylist: No, the words vary. What length do you have in mind across your forehead?
  4. Client: I want it below my eyebrows, not as short as the display photograph.
  5. Stylist: Thank you. Would you like the center and the sides discussed separately today?
  6. Client: Yes. I like longer pieces at the sides, but I don't know their name.
  7. Stylist: You don't need the technical name. Point to the areas you want to retain.
  8. Client: These pieces here. I usually tuck them behind my ears when I'm working.
  9. Stylist: I see. We'll keep those longer and confirm the center length in the mirror.
  10. Client: That's the distinction I was trying to explain. Please keep those side pieces.

F Say it

Two scenarios to practice

Scenario 1 · Turning a reference photo into a clear request

A client shows a chin-length bob but wants to keep enough length for a low ponytail. No cut has started. The photo has a straight fringe; the client has not requested one. Clarify which features matter and confirm a collarbone-length outline.

Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.

Partner's role and follow-up

Client: you like the photo's neat outline, not its short length or fringe; you need a low ponytail.

Try a harder second round

The client points to the fringe while saying 'this shape.' Ask whether the gesture changes the earlier preference.

Scenario 2 · Two photographs, one preferred feature

A client brings one photo for the back outline and another for the long front. Both show shorter overall hair than the client wants. The client needs shoulder length.

Role A: Stylist: identify one feature from each photo and confirm the retained length.

Role B: Client: choose the rounded back from photo A and long front from photo B; reject their overall length.

Possible opening: “Can I combine the back from this picture with the front from that one?”

Success checks
  • Identify the chosen feature in each photo.
  • Confirm shoulder length independently of the photographs.
  • Restate the combined request aloud before proceeding.
Add a complication

The client calls both pictures 'the shorter one.' Ask them to point to A or B.

Studying alone? Speak both roles aloud. For each scenario, prepare for two minutes, speak for one minute, then answer a follow-up. Switch roles and repeat using fewer notes. Use only the supplied facts; identify missing information instead of inventing it.

Compare scenario 1 with a model response
Your photo has a shorter outline and a straight fringe, but keeping a low ponytail matters to you. Which feature of the photo do you want to keep? We can discuss a collarbone-length outline instead. Before we start, please confirm whether you want any change around your forehead.

This is one possible spoken response, not the only acceptable wording. Preserve the case facts when you try your own version.

Notice: Resolve conflicting length preferences before a haircut. Identify the wording that does this, then name one detail from the case that the response preserves.

Apply it to this case

In “Turning a reference photo into a clear request”, choose one confirmed detail from the situation and one item that still needs clarification. Say a two-sentence response using “Which part of the photo would you like to keep?”. Keep the known detail accurate and ask about the missing one.

Check your reasoning

Compare with the case above and the model response. Can you point to the words that support your factual statement? Is your question about something the case leaves open? If you introduce a possible outcome, clearly label it as a possibility.

Review, then try again

  • Keeps the case facts accurate; clearly separates confirmed and unknown information.
  • Uses understandable sentences and explains specialist terms when the listener needs it.
  • Responds to the other person's question, checks understanding, and uses an appropriate tone.
  • Makes the requested action or unresolved question clear without inventing authority or facts.

Revise one sentence and repeat the response. A useful response can be clear even with a few grammar errors; judge meaning and task completion, not accent.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Client: How do these two photographs differ?
Barber: This one keeps hair at the sides, ____ that one exposes skin.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. whereas

  1. A. unless 'Unless' makes a condition that the photographs do not establish.
  2. B. whereas Correct. 'Whereas' contrasts the two appearances.
  3. C. because 'Because' would claim a cause, not a contrast.
  4. D. therefore 'Therefore' wrongly presents the second appearance as a consequence.
2. Client: I don't straighten my curls for work. What do you call my usual curl pattern?
Stylist: Your ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. natural texture

  1. A. natural texture Correct. This refers to the usual curl pattern.
  2. B. overall density Density describes how closely hairs grow together, not their curl pattern.
  3. C. strand diameter Strand diameter concerns the thickness of individual hairs, not their pattern.
  4. D. current length Length describes how long the hair is, not whether it curls.

A salon consultation separates internal shape from the outside outline.

  1. Stylist: You asked about layers but also said the overall length must stay the same.
  2. Client: Right. I thought layers always meant making the whole haircut much shorter than it is.
  3. Stylist: Layers concern different lengths within the shape; the outside outline is a separate choice.
  4. Client: Can I have more movement without making it shorter overall?
  5. Stylist: Yes. Which reference image shows the kind of movement you're describing around the front?
  6. Client: This one does, though I don't like the very short pieces near the face.
  7. Stylist: That's important. We can discuss the longer sections without copying those shorter front pieces.
  8. Client: Please show me which parts you mean. I'm better with pictures than technical descriptions.
  9. Stylist: Here and here. Does that match your request to keep the outer length visible?
  10. Client: Yes, keep the overall length. Show me where the shorter layers would be.

  1. Stylist: The reference photograph shows a smooth finish. Do you usually wear your hair straight?
  2. Client: No, I wear my natural curls almost every day and rarely spend time straightening them.
  3. Stylist: Then we should discuss how you want the shape to look with your usual texture.
  4. Client: I like the photograph's outline, but I wasn't asking for that exact straight finish.
  5. Stylist: Thank you for clarifying. Would seeing a curly reference help us discuss the same outline?
  6. Client: Yes, particularly at the sides. That's where I want the shape to feel less wide.
  7. Stylist: This second picture may be a better discussion point, although your result won't be identical.

1. A Friday target is not a confirmed result

A client attends a color consultation.

  1. Stylist: Your photo shows a cool tone. Is that what you want, rather than extra lightness?
  2. Client: Yes, especially before Friday. Can I book exactly that result for the party?
  3. Stylist: First we need your color history. Do you know which home product you used?
  4. Client: I remember the box being red, but I don't remember the product name.
  5. Stylist: That leaves an important detail unknown. The photo can't confirm what is suitable here.
  6. Client: I thought today's consultation included the color service. Was that my mistake when booking?
  7. Stylist: The booking is for discussion only. We haven't assessed the service time or cost.
  8. Client: I'll look for the packaging when I get home. Can we still discuss the target?
  9. Stylist: Certainly. We can identify your preferred tone without promising a particular result by Friday.
  10. Client: Then let's do that. I won't treat this consultation as a confirmed color appointment.
2. Warmth is not the same as darkness

A client compares two salon shade samples.

  1. Stylist: You called both samples dark, but pointed to the golden one as your concern.
  2. Client: I struggle to describe it. The other sample looks calmer, even at similar depth.
  3. Stylist: Are you referring to the warm golden tone rather than how light it is?
  4. Client: Yes, that's closer. I don't necessarily want my hair made lighter this time.
  5. Stylist: Then we can discuss a less warm target without assuming a change in lightness.
  6. Client: Would calling it ash guarantee the exact shade on this sample ring for me?
  7. Stylist: No. The sample helps identify a preference; your own result needs an individual assessment.
  8. Client: That makes sense. Could you keep these two ideas separate in the consultation note?
  9. Stylist: Yes: less warmth requested, with no automatic request for lighter hair or new highlights.
  10. Client: That's the wording I needed. It avoids making the change sound larger than intended.
3. Remembering the missing appointment history

A stylist asks a returning client about an incomplete record.

  1. Stylist: Our note lists your last color visit. Is today's request about regrowth or the colored lengths?
  2. Client: The regrowth near my roots. I also used temporary color at home before my holiday.
  3. Stylist: Please include it in your history without guessing how it affects today's options.
  4. Client: I don't know the name, but my sister ordered it and may have the receipt.
  5. Stylist: That could help identify it. Shall we leave that part marked as unknown for now?
  6. Client: Yes. Please don't record that I haven't used anything just because I can't remember.
  7. Stylist: I'll state that a home product was used and its name remains unconfirmed.
  8. Client: Could we discuss haircut options today while the color question is still open?
  9. Stylist: We can discuss them separately. That doesn't decide whether a color service is suitable.
  10. Client: Good. I'll ask for the receipt, and today's conversation won't erase that missing detail.

Your goal

Negotiate appointments without disguising a timing conflict.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A haircut appointment at 2:00 is running twenty minutes late. The usual service duration is forty minutes. The client must leave by 2:45. A 10:00 appointment tomorrow is available; the stylist cannot promise a shortened service today. This salon waives the change fee when its delay causes this rescheduling.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

walk-in
A client seeking service without a booked appointment.
appointment slot
A period reserved in the salon schedule for a specified visit.
waiting list
A list of clients who want to be contacted if a suitable appointment opens.
service duration
The estimated time allocated to a particular salon service.

Useful word combinations

  • run behind schedule: begin later than planned
  • allow time for consultation: include discussion in the booking
  • offer a cancellation slot: propose time freed by another client
  • confirm the finish-time constraint: check when a client must leave

C Notice the language

Offer a choice that fits the available time

State the delay and the client's departure separately. A shorter remaining window does not make the service itself shorter.

  • We are running ___ behind.
  • That would take us past ___.
  • Would tomorrow at ___ work instead?

Improve this: We'll squeeze everything in.

See a clearer version

Today's remaining window is too short for the usual service; tomorrow at ten is available.

The client can choose using an honest time comparison.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Client: Must I stay until three?
Receptionist: With a 2:20 start and forty-minute service, the estimated finish is ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. 3:00

  1. A. 2:40 That allows only twenty minutes.
  2. B. 2:45 That is the client's departure constraint, not the estimated finish.
  3. C. 3:20 That adds an hour rather than forty minutes.
  4. D. 3:00 Correct. Forty minutes after 2:20 is 3:00.
2. Client: Does joining the waiting list reserve a place?
Receptionist: It means we'll ____ if a suitable slot opens.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. contact you

  1. A. move your existing booking automatically Joining this list does not authorize changing an existing booking.
  2. B. extend your existing service automatically A waiting-list entry does not automatically extend an existing service.
  3. C. contact you Correct. A waiting list offers contact if space opens.
  4. D. confirm your appointment automatically An available slot still needs the client's acceptance before confirmation.

1. The departure time cannot move

Reception discusses the delayed 2:00 haircut.

  1. Receptionist: I'm sorry, we're running twenty minutes behind your two o'clock appointment this afternoon.
  2. Client: I need to leave by quarter to three. Is the full haircut still possible?
  3. Receptionist: The usual forty-minute service from 2:20 would finish around three, beyond your departure.
  4. Client: Could the stylist just work faster? I already arranged transport for today.
  5. Receptionist: I can't promise a compressed service. There is a full slot tomorrow at ten.
  6. Client: Tomorrow is possible, but would changing it count as my cancelling at short notice?
  7. Receptionist: This change is due to our delay; I can move it without a change fee.
  8. Client: Then please move it. I don't want to feel rushed through the consultation.
  9. Receptionist: You're booked tomorrow at ten for the full slot, and today's appointment is replaced.
  10. Client: Thanks for stating the timing clearly. I'll rearrange my ride for the new appointment.
2. A walk-in between booked clients

A barber explains current availability to a visitor.

  1. Barber: Are you looking for a haircut today, or checking our next available booking first?
  2. Client: I was hoping to walk in. I can see an empty chair by the window.
  3. Barber: That chair has a booking starting shortly; an empty chair doesn't always mean available time.
  4. Client: I only need a small change. Could I take part of that appointment slot?
  5. Barber: We need enough time for the complete visit. The next open haircut slot is four.
  6. Client: I could return at four. Would I still have to join the walk-in queue then?
  7. Barber: If we book that slot now, you'll have an appointment rather than a queue place.
  8. Client: Let's do that. Can you confirm which barber I'm seeing when I return?
  9. Barber: You'll see Jordan at four. Please allow the full booked period for your visit.
  10. Client: Great. I'll come back for the appointment instead of waiting beside the empty chair.
3. An offer is not a booking

A salon receptionist calls a client on the waiting list.

  1. Receptionist: A cancellation has opened tomorrow at eleven. Are you still interested in that time?
  2. Client: Possibly, but I need to check whether my colleague can cover my shift first.
  3. Receptionist: I can hold this offered slot until four today while you check with them.
  4. Client: Does that mean it is already booked, even if I don't call you back?
  5. Receptionist: No. It is temporarily held; we need your confirmation before four to book it.
  6. Client: Understood. My colleague will be back at three, so I can answer after that.
  7. Receptionist: Would a call to this number be the easiest way to confirm your decision?
  8. Client: Yes. If I can't take it, I'd still like to stay on the waiting list.
  9. Receptionist: That's fine. Today's hold expires at four, but your waiting-list request can remain.
  10. Client: I'll call with a definite answer after three rather than assuming the hold is permanent.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. pause

  1. A. speed up Speeding up does not address discomfort.
  2. B. sum up Summing up means summarizing words, not stopping the activity.
  3. C. pause Correct. 'Pause' means temporarily stop.
  4. D. finish up Finishing does not describe the requested immediate pause.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Around

  1. A. Throughout 'Throughout' refers to an entire area or duration, not the cape's position here.
  2. B. Around Correct. 'Around' describes the cape surrounding the neck.
  3. C. Across from 'Across from' means opposite, not surrounding.
  4. D. Beyond 'Beyond' means farther away, not surrounding the neck.

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

service menu
A salon list describing available services and their stated prices or price ranges.
starting price
The lowest listed price for a service whose actual charge may vary with stated factors.
add-on
An extra service offered in addition to the originally selected service.
itemized receipt
A receipt listing individual services or products and their separate charges.

Useful word combinations

  • quote the total: state the price for the agreed scope
  • check what is included: establish which services a price covers
  • decline an add-on: choose not to purchase an optional service
  • break down the charge: explain the separate items making up a total

C Notice the language

Explain the full price before agreement

Use a clear breakdown and ask for a choice. Avoid treating interest in an add-on as agreement to pay for it.

  • The price includes ___, but not ___.
  • Together, the total would be ___.
  • Would you like ___ alone or ___ as well?

1. Client: Is the fifteen-dollar finish compulsory?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. optional

  1. A. complimentary 'Complimentary' means free, but this finish costs fifteen dollars.
  2. B. optional Correct. The client may choose or decline it.
  3. C. inclusive 'Inclusive' concerns what a price covers.
  4. D. outstanding 'Outstanding' would describe an unpaid charge or unfinished task.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. is optional and separately charged

  1. A. is optional and separately charged Correct. Declining the separately priced finish leaves the haircut-only choice available.
  2. B. is included at no extra charge The menu identifies a separate fifteen-dollar charge for the finish.
  3. C. has already been paid for No payment for a finish has been made in this case.
  4. D. is required to receive the haircut The finish is optional, not a condition of receiving the haircut.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Vocabulary for this lesson

A salon discussion examining a client's concern and the possible response.

A difference in shape or apparent length between corresponding sides.

A discretionary service or benefit offered by a salon to acknowledge a problem.

Useful word combinations

  • check both sides: compare the visible result from agreed viewpoints
  • acknowledge the mismatch: recognize a difference between expectation and result
  • arrange a review: organize a discussion of the concern and possible response

Answer: D. service review

Vocabulary for this lesson

The salon's stored information about a client's visits and relevant stated preferences.

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Receptionist: Is next month's consultation confirmed?

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. inquired

  1. A. attended Attendance would imply a visit took place.
  2. B. rescheduled Rescheduling implies an existing booking was moved.
  3. C. completed payment No payment has been stated or confirmed in this scenario.
  4. D. inquired Correct. Asking about it does not establish a booking.
2. Client: Can you save the photo for my next consultation but not post it?
Stylist: You're permitting ____ only.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. the stated record use

  1. A. all future publication This is broader than the client's permission.
  2. B. a social-media advertisement Advertising is not the permitted consultation use.
  3. C. the stated record use Correct. Permission is limited to the described record use.
