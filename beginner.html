📅 October 08, 2026 - US and Lebanon Protect Wanted Syrian General
The US and Lebanon are protecting a Syrian general wanted for crimes in return for information.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

A former Syrian general is being protected by the US and Lebanon. His name is Bassam al-Hassan. He is wanted for crimes against humanity. Mr. Hassan is in Lebanon. He is protected because he gives information to the US. This information is about an American journalist, Austin Tice, who disappeared in Syria. Mr. Hassan is linked to Mr. Tice being taken by force in 2012. Syria's new government wants General Hassan to return for a trial. But US authorities want him to stay in Lebanon. Austin Tice's family is upset about this protection.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. general (noun): A high-ranking officer in the army.
2. protected (verb, past participle): Kept safe from danger or harm.
3. wanted (verb, past participle): Sought by police because of a crime.
4. information (noun): Facts or details about something.
5. journalist (noun): A person who writes news stories for others to read or watch.
6. disappeared (verb, past tense): Went missing; could not be found.

Grammar Focus: Using 'because' to show reason
We use 'because' to explain why something happens or why someone does something. It connects an action or a situation to its reason.
Example from the text: "He is protected because he gives information to the US."

📅 October 07, 2026 - Students Protest for Better Schools in France
Students and other people protested across France to ask for better schools and an end to police actions.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Crowds of mainly teenagers called for more resources for schools. They also wanted an end to what they called police repression. A wave of protests by high-school pupils spread to their parents, university students and unions. Police clashed with demonstrators in Lyon. Police used tear gas in Paris and Marseille. The government said that 215 students and 85 staff had been hurt. Alissa, a student, said that schools were falling apart. Her friend, Sacha, said the ceilings in Marseille's schools were leaking and conditions were poor. Students will keep protesting until good measures are taken.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. protests (noun): events where people show they are unhappy about something, often in a public place
2. resources (noun): things that are available and can be used, like money, staff, or materials for schools
3. police (noun): the official organization that makes sure people follow the law and catches criminals
4. spread (verb): to move and cover a larger area, or to become known by more people
5. clashed (verb): had a fight or argument with someone, often between groups of people
6. hurt (verb): to cause pain or injury to a person or animal
7. conditions (noun): the state of something, especially regarding its quality or health

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense
The Simple Past Tense shows actions that happened and finished in the past. We often add "-ed" to regular verbs to make them past tense, like "called" or "clashed." Irregular verbs change in different ways, like "spread."
Example from the text: "Crowds of mainly teenagers called for more resources for schools."

📅 October 06, 2026 - US Moves Bombers from UK Base After Threat
The US moved its bombers from England because of a security threat.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The US moved its bombers from England. President Trump said this was because of 'some threats'. The Pentagon confirmed the bombers went back to America. Mr Trump said they moved the planes to avoid danger. Police arrested some men near the base before. This decision was a response to that incident. US leaders still trust the security of their bases.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. moved (verb): To change the position of something.
2. bombers (noun): Large military aircraft that carry bombs.
3. threats (noun): Possible dangers or bad things that might happen.
4. Pentagon (proper noun): The US military's main office in America.
5. confirmed (verb): Said that something was true or definite.
6. danger (noun): The possibility of something bad happening, like harm or injury.
7. arrested (verb): Police caught and held someone because they thought they did something wrong.
8. incident (noun): An event or happening, often something unpleasant.

Grammar Focus: Using "because of"
We use the phrase "because of" to show the reason for something. It always comes before a noun or a noun phrase.
Example from the text: "President Trump said this was because of 'some threats'."
