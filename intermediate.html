📅 October 22, 2026 - The Trillion-Pound Oil Fund the UK Could Have Built
The UK Treasury calculated that the country could have built a trillion-pound financial asset if it had invested its early North Sea oil revenues.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The UK government recently announced a new energy wealth fund with an initial investment of £2bn. Following the announcement, the Treasury calculated how much the UK could have raised by investing early North Sea proceeds. According to internal modelling, the nation could have built a massive financial asset worth a trillion pounds. Over the years, several energy exporting countries have used their oil and gas revenues to create epic national savings accounts. For example, neighbouring Norway has amassed a huge financial reserve from the exact same North Sea resources. Critics argue that early revenues were squandered on wasteful spending rather than being saved for the future. While the new British fund plans to invest domestically, Norway is obliged to invest its money abroad. Furthermore, tax collections from UK energy are forecast to dwindle as North Sea production gradually slows down. Although nobody expects the new £2bn fund to reach a trillion pounds soon, experts still wonder what might have been.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. calculated (verb): Discovered a number or amount using mathematical methods.
2. asset (noun): Something valuable that is owned by a person, company, or country.
3. revenues (noun): Amounts of money that a government or company receives regularly from its business activities.
4. amassed (verb): Gathered a large amount of something, such as money, over a long period of time.
5. squandered (verb): Carelessly wasted money, time, or valuable opportunities.
6. domestically (adverb): Within a particular country rather than internationally.
7. obliged (adjective): Required or forced to do something by a rule, law, or necessity.
8. dwindle (verb): To gradually become smaller in size, amount, or strength.

Grammar Focus: Past Modals for Imagined Situations
We use 'could have' followed by a past participle (like 'raised' or 'built') to talk about things that were possible in the past but did not actually happen. In this story, it describes the theoretical money the UK missed out on saving.
Example from the text: "Following the announcement, the Treasury calculated how much the UK could have raised by investing early North Sea proceeds."

📅 October 21, 2026 - American Released by Russia During Talks in St Petersburg
An American man jailed in Russia has been released and is returning home without a prisoner exchange.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Russia has released a 74-year-old American man who was jailed for allegedly fighting for Ukraine. Stephen Hubbard was sentenced to almost seven years in a penal colony after Russian forces captured him in 2022. President Donald Trump stated that Hubbard was on his way home and that no prisoner exchange was required. This release happened as President Vladimir Putin met with a US representative for talks in St Petersburg. The US government had previously classified the man as wrongfully detained. Reports indicated that Hubbard was in poor health and would travel to Texas for medical care. During the meeting, the US representative also asked Putin to consider restarting peace negotiations with Ukraine. Furthermore, Trump recently suspended some sanctions on Russia, arguing that these penalties would have stopped the talks.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. allegedly (adverb): used to state that something is claimed to be true, although it has not been proven
2. sentenced (verb): given a formal punishment by a judge in a court of law
3. captured (verb): caught and taken prisoner by force
4. exchange (noun): the act of giving someone or something and receiving something else instead
5. representative (noun): a person chosen to speak or make decisions on behalf of another person or group
6. wrongfully (adverb): done in a way that is unfair, unjust, or illegal
7. negotiations (noun): formal discussions aimed at reaching a mutual agreement
8. suspended (verb): officially stopped something for a temporary period of time

Grammar Focus: Past Simple Passive
The past simple passive is formed using "was" or "were" plus the past participle of the main verb. It is used to focus on an action that happened to the subject, rather than who did the action. In the example sentence, "was sentenced" focuses on what happened to Stephen Hubbard, while the active verb "captured" focuses on what the Russian forces did.
Example from the text: "Stephen Hubbard was sentenced to almost seven years in a penal colony after Russian forces captured him in 2022."

Trump suggested that it was "time for Ukraine to get a new president." For many months, Ukraine and Russia have been exchanging strikes on each other's energy infrastructure.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. stated (verb): said something clearly and formally.
2. initially (adverb): at the beginning; at first.
3. announcement (noun): a public statement giving information about something new or important.
4. urging (verb): strongly advising or trying to persuade someone to do something.
5. infrastructure (noun): the basic systems and structures, like roads and power plants, that a country needs.
6. fulfill (verb): to do or complete what is required or promised.
7. undoes (verb): cancels the effect of something; reverses a previous action.
8. criticism (noun): comments expressing disapproval of someone or something, often pointing out faults.
9. facilities (noun): buildings, services, or equipment provided for a particular purpose.

Grammar Focus: Reporting Verbs
Reporting verbs are used to tell someone what another person said, thought, or felt without directly quoting them. They can introduce reported speech or ideas. Common reporting verbs include "said," "stated," "claimed," "accused," and "suggested." The tense of the reported clause often shifts (e.g., present becomes past) if the reporting verb is in the past tense.

📅 October 09, 2026 - €5 Million Wine Heist at Italian Producer Marchesi Antinori
Thieves recently took 30,000 bottles of valuable wine from Marchesi Antinori, a prestigious Italian producer, in what is believed to be the largest such robbery in Italy.

Read the story

Reports indicate that thieves took 30,000 bottles of wine valued at €5 million from an Italian producer. This large-scale robbery, or "heist," happened at a prestigious wine producer based in Florence, Italy. Marchesi Antinori, a family-owned producer, confirmed the incident at its storage facility in Tuscany. The incident occurred on a Saturday as the producer was preparing a consignment for international customers. At least seven masked men are believed to have deactivated alarms and disabled cameras during the heist. They reportedly used forklifts belonging to the producer to load valuable Tuscan red wines onto two large trucks. The chief executive stated this was the largest heist of its kind in Italy. The producer suspects the criminals were wine connoisseurs and had inside knowledge, given the specific bottles chosen.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. Reports indicate (verb phrase): To show or suggest something based on available information or news.
2. valued at (prepositional phrase): Having a particular monetary amount or worth.
3. prestigious (adjective): Highly respected and admired because of its importance or high quality.
4. consignment (noun): A batch of goods that is sent or delivered for sale or storage.
5. deactivated (verb): Made a device or system stop working or become inactive.
6. disabled (verb): Made a machine, system, or security feature unable to function.
7. forklifts (noun): Powerful industrial vehicles used for lifting and moving heavy loads, often on pallets.
8. connoisseurs (noun): People who have expert knowledge and appreciation of a particular subject, especially fine food, drink, or art.

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice: 'be believed to have'
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action rather than who performed it. When reporting beliefs or opinions about past events, we can use the structure 'subject + be + believed + to have + past participle'. This construction is common in news reporting to talk about what people think happened.
Example from the text: "At least seven masked men are believed to have deactivated alarms and disabled cameras during the heist."
