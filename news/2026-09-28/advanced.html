Trump-Xi Summit: What Was Left Unsaid

Advanced · C1+

News archive →
📅 September 28, 2026 - Trump-Xi Summit: What Was Left Unsaid

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The most revealing aspect of the recent summit in Washington may be found in what the two leaders chose not to discuss, and what remains unknown. However, the talks produced no joint statements and few significant advancements. Instead, both governments issued separate accounts of the meeting, perhaps indicating that despite initial gestures, Washington and Beijing maintain a degree of caution towards each other. Beijing announced an agreement with Washington to initiate a new artificial intelligence discussion forum, with further consultations scheduled for late November. This move suggests both nations recognize the necessity of establishing certain safeguards around AI technology, which is increasingly integral to sectors from commerce to military planning. They also committed to creating a communication channel for AI-related incidents, intending to prevent misunderstandings or unforeseen events from escalating into a broader conflict. Chinese state media further confirmed that a temporary cessation of new tariffs has been extended until January 10. Beijing, however, had sought a more substantial extension to the pause in trade disputes, rendering the two-month reprieve a limited concession rather than a significant achievement. China successfully secured reduced import duties on $30bn (£23bn) of goods entering the US, with reciprocal reductions anticipated from Beijing. Strikingly, crucial subjects were entirely omitted from public statements. There was no mention of boosting China's rare earth mineral exports to the US, nor any sign that restrictions on advanced US semiconductors would be relaxed. The White House factsheet omitted any reference to Taiwan, yet the issue clearly held significant importance for Xi Jinping. According to Beijing's reports of the discussions, Xi urged Donald Trump to formally oppose Taiwan independence and manage the situation with prudence. This summit generated extensive discussions, yet yielded minimal shifts in policy for the time being. The most contentious issues, ranging from advanced technology to Taiwan, persist without full resolution. While both leaders affirm their commitment to stable relations, ongoing US-China communication appears significantly more straightforward than demanding either superpower to make substantial compromises.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. revealing (adjective): Providing insights or information that was previously hidden or unknown.
2. advancements (plural noun): Progress or developments in a particular field, often significant.
3. indicating (verb): Suggesting or showing something, often indirectly.
4. maintain (verb): To continue to possess or keep in existence a state or condition.
5. initiate (verb): To cause a process or action to begin; to start.
6. integral (adjective): Essential or fundamental to the completeness of something; forming a necessary part.
7. escalating (verb): Increasing rapidly in intensity or seriousness; worsening.
8. cessation (noun): The formal or complete stopping of an activity; an end.
9. reprieve (noun): A temporary escape from an undesirable situation or event; a postponement.
10. reciprocal (adjective): Given, felt, or done in return by each of two people or groups; mutual.
11. omitted (verb): Left out or excluded, either intentionally or by oversight.
12. prudence (noun): The quality of acting with careful good judgment, especially concerning practical matters.
13. contentious (adjective): Causing or likely to cause disagreement or argument; controversial.
14. affirm (verb): To state or assert positively; to declare something to be true.
15. substantial (adjective): Considerable in extent, amount, or importance; significant.

Advanced Grammar: Modals of Deduction and Speculation
C1-Higher learners often need to express degrees of certainty or possibility in formal contexts. Modals like "may," "might," "could," "must," "should," along with phrases like "perhaps indicating" or "suggests," are crucial for expressing deduction or speculation about events or situations when direct evidence is incomplete or ambiguous. These forms allow for nuanced communication, distinguishing between strong likelihood and mere possibility, which is vital in reporting and analysis.
Example from the text: "The most revealing aspect of the recent summit in Washington may be found in what the two leaders chose not to discuss, and what remains unknown."
