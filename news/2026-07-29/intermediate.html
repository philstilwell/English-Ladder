Japan Struck by Powerful Quake and Over 100 Aftershocks

Read the story

Japan recently experienced a powerful earthquake which tragically resulted in 13 fatalities. The initial tremor was followed by more than 100 aftershocks, causing ongoing concern among residents. Authorities have since issued warnings, urging the public to remain vigilant. These aftershocks have the potential to reach a significant magnitude, possibly up to 7. People in the affected regions are advised to stay alert for any further seismic activity. The country's officials are closely monitoring the situation to ensure public safety. Residents have been asked to prepare for potential emergencies and understand evacuation routes. Buildings and infrastructure are being carefully inspected for damage caused by the tremors. Many families are facing displacement and disruption to their daily lives due to the ongoing quakes. The government emphasizes the importance of following safety guidelines during this critical period.

Words and grammar

1. earthquake (noun): A sudden violent shaking of the ground, often causing great destruction, as a result of movements within the Earth's crust.
2. aftershocks (noun): Smaller earthquakes that follow the main shock of a large earthquake and originate at or near the same location.
3. vigilant (adjective): Keeping careful watch for possible danger or difficulties; alert and watchful.
4. magnitude (noun): A measure of the size or power of an earthquake.
5. seismic (adjective): Relating to or caused by earthquakes or other vibrations of the Earth's crust.

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action or the receiver of the action, rather than the doer. We often use it when the agent (the person or thing doing the action) is unknown, unimportant, or obvious from the context. It is formed using a form of 'to be' + past participle (e.g., was followed, are advised).
Example from the text: "The initial tremor was followed by more than 100 aftershocks, causing ongoing concern among residents."
