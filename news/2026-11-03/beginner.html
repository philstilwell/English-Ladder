Collins Chooses Mogging as Its Word of the Year

📅 November 03, 2026 - Collins Chooses Mogging as Its Word of the Year

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Collins English Dictionary chose mogging as its word of the year. Mogging means doing better than someone or getting more attention than them. People often use this word to talk about how someone looks. It became much more common in 2026. Young people use it online and in everyday conversation. It can be a joke about someone or a way to say something nice about them. How people understand it can change with the situation. Collins uses social media to learn how English is changing. Its dictionary team checks how often people use words.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. chose (verb, past simple): Picked one person or thing from several.
2. attention (noun): The interest people show when they watch or listen to someone.
3. common (adjective): Used or seen often.
4. online (adverb): On the internet.
5. conversation (noun): A talk between people.
6. situation (noun): What is happening at a particular time and place.

Grammar Focus: Can for possibility
Use “can” to say that something is possible. Put the basic verb after “can”, as in “can be” or “can change”. This verb stays the same with “he”, “she” and “it”. Do not add “to” before the verb.
Example from the text: "It can be a joke about someone or a way to say something nice about them."
