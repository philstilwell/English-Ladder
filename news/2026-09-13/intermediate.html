📅 September 13, 2026 - Hidden Catastrophic Impact of Summer Fires on Wales's Ecosystems

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Summer fires across Wales this year have resulted in "catastrophic" impacts, according to researchers. Researchers from Bangor University report that the actual scale of destruction is much larger than what people can see. Extreme heat and very dry weather made landscapes like heathland and moorland highly vulnerable to fast-spreading blazes. This led to one of the UK's worst fire seasons in recent memory. One expert noted that many only see blackened plants, but the significant effects are actually hidden underground. At Sychnant Pass, a rare ecosystem globally important for specialist plants and insects, suffered significant losses. Specifically, 4,000 years of organic carbon from the top layer of earth were lost there in just days. The loss of this essential organic matter contributes to climate change and makes the natural environment much weaker. Bangor University's team is now taking samples at various sites before winter rains wash away what remains. This research involves comparing burned land with nearby untouched areas to measure changes in carbon, nutrients, and erosion risk. Dr. Kara Marsden emphasized that rapid onsite work was crucial to measure conditions while the fires' impacts were still fresh. She explained that early green growth does not mean full recovery, as deeply rooted plants like bracken sprout quickly, but shallower-rooted plants are often completely gone. Jones warned that without swift restoration and better preparation for future fires, much of this natural environment could be lost permanently.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. catastrophic (adjective): causing sudden, extreme harm or suffering to a large degree.
2. scale (noun): the size or extent of something, especially when very large.
3. blazes (noun): very large, strong, and often destructive fires.
4. vulnerable (adjective): easily harmed or damaged.
5. ecosystem (noun): all the living things in a particular area and the way they affect each other and the environment.
6. organic carbon (noun phrase): carbon that comes from living or once-living organisms, important for healthy earth.
7. contributes to (verb phrase): helps to cause or is one of the reasons for something.
8. erosion risk (noun phrase): the possibility of earth or rock being gradually worn away by natural forces like water or wind.
9. crucial (adjective): extremely important or necessary.
10. restoration (noun): the act of returning something to its former or original condition.
11. permanently (adverb): in a way that lasts forever or for a very long time without changing.

Grammar Focus: Reporting Verbs
Reporting verbs are used to tell what someone said, thought, or felt. They can introduce a direct quote or a paraphrased statement. Common reporting verbs include "said," "stated," "reported," "explained," "warned," and "emphasized." They are often followed by "that" and a clause to introduce the reported information.
Example from the text: "Researchers from Bangor University report that the actual scale of destruction is much larger than what people can see."
