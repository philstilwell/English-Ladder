📅 September 17, 2026 - Bank of England Holds Interest Charges, Signals Future Rise

Read the story

The Bank of England has held interest rates for the sixth time in a row. However, it said they are likely to rise if high energy prices caused by the conflict in the Middle East continue. The US-Israel war with Iran has disrupted global energy supplies, which led to a sharp increase in petrol and diesel prices. Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey said that if volatility in energy prices persists, it will greatly impact inflation. He stated that the Bank rate might need to rise to ensure inflation falls back to its 2% target. The UK's central bank aims to keep inflation at a target of 2%, but it has been above that for nearly two years. Official figures showed inflation rose to 3.1% in August from 2.9% in July. The Bank now forecasts that inflation will rise more than it previously thought. It also warned that the price cap on household gas and electricity bills for January is "now expected to rise substantially further." Households feel the impact of a rising Bank rate through higher borrowing costs, but can also benefit from more generous savings rates. The UK economy has been "more resilient" than expected, and the Bank improved its forecast for economic growth between July and September.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. persists (verb): to continue to happen or exist, especially for a long time or in a way that is difficult to stop
2. disrupted (verb): to interrupt an event, activity, or process by causing a problem
3. governor (noun): the official head of a public institution or organization, such as a central bank
4. ensure (verb): to make something certain to happen
5. Official figures (noun phrase): statistics or data formally released by a government or authoritative organization
6. forecasts (verb): to predict or estimate a future event or trend based on available information
7. resilient (adjective): able to quickly return to a previous good condition after being shocked or damaged
8. substantially (adverb): by a large amount or to a great extent
9. benefit (verb): to receive an advantage or something good from a situation

Grammar Focus: First Conditional (If + Present Simple, will/might + Base Verb)
The First Conditional describes real or very possible situations in the future and their likely results. It uses 'if' followed by a verb in the present simple tense, and then a future tense with 'will' or 'might' (or other modals like 'may' or 'can') and the base form of the verb for the result clause.
Example from the text: "However, it said they are likely to rise if high energy prices caused by the conflict in the Middle East continue."
