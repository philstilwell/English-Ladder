Lesson 01: Receiving against the expected shipment
Original fictional case: Receiving clerk Nia checks a shipment against purchase order 740, which expects twenty cartons. The physical count is twenty-two cartons, and the item labels match the ordered item. The supplier has not explained the additional two cartons. Nia can flag the overage to inventory coordinator Ben, but neither can treat this conversation as approval to change the order. Matching item labels do not settle quantity acceptance, payment, stock release, or the cause of the difference.
Language workshop: Two cartons over the order
Communication goal: Report an inbound overage with matching item labels while preserving the original order and the limit of your authority.
Language guidance: The expected count is twenty cartons and the observed count is twenty-two: an overage of two cartons. Matching item labels support product identity, not approval of the additional quantity or a change to purchase order 740.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): I am checking the shipment against purchase order 740. / The order expects twenty cartons. / I have counted twenty-two cartons. / That is an overage of two cartons. / The item labels match the ordered item. / Matching labels do not resolve the quantity difference. / The supplier has not explained the additional two. / I cannot call the extra cartons a free addition. / I will report the overage for review. / Keep twenty as the original ordered quantity. / I cannot approve an order change. / The order has not been changed to twenty-two. / This count does not approve payment for the extras. / The count alone does not authorize stock release. / Twenty expected, twenty-two counted, item labels matching. / The extra quantity still needs an explanation and a decision.
Vocabulary: purchase order: Buyer document specifying ordered goods and quantities, often PO. / inbound shipment: Goods arriving at a warehouse or receiving location. / expected quantity: Amount required by the reference order or record. / received count: Number physically counted on arrival, distinct from approval status. / overage: Quantity above the expected or ordered amount. / carton: Outer package used as the counting unit here. / item label: Label identifying the goods or product reference. / item match: Correspondence between the observed and ordered product identity. / quantity exception: Difference requiring review against the stated quantity. / supplier: Business providing the ordered goods. / receiving clerk: Worker checking and recording incoming shipments. / inventory coordinator: Person coordinating stock records and related checks. / order line: Individual entry specifying an item and quantity on an order. / packing slip: Supplier document describing the shipment contents. / delivery note: Document accompanying delivered goods and their descriptions. / advance shipping notice: Electronic notice of expected shipment details, often ASN. / quantity variance: Difference between expected and observed amounts. / supplier explanation: Supplier account of why a discrepancy occurred. / order amendment: Authorized change to an existing purchase order. / approval limit: Boundary of a person's authority to accept or change something. / acceptance status: Whether the goods or quantity have been accepted under the process. / stock release: Authorization to make goods available for the relevant use. / invoice quantity: Number of units billed by the supplier. / reconciliation: Comparison of records and actual figures to explain differences.
Speaking task: Read the two quantities with their labels: twenty ordered and twenty-two counted. Repeat the two-carton overage and the separate item-label match. Read the closing handoff without changing the original order or approving payment.
Partner: Nia: Receiving clerk / Ben: Inventory coordinator
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: Purchase order 851 expects twelve cartons. Receiving counts fifteen with matching item labels. The extra quantity is unexplained, and the clerk cannot approve an order change.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Nia: I'll send that with PO 740 and the label match. The supplier's explanation is still the open question.
Ben: And retain your approval limit in the handoff. Someone reviewing the note needs to know what has not been authorized.
Nia: Read-back: twenty ordered, twenty-two counted, labels matching, two over. No explanation yet and no approved order change.
Ben: That's enough to start reconciliation. I'll take the discrepancy for review; don't describe it as resolved before we have the answer.
END REFERENCE

Lesson 02: Locating and identifying stock
Original fictional case: Picker Luis needs BK-14-B, blue folders, from bin A12. The bin sign names BK-14-B, but the stock in the bin is labelled BK-14-G, green folders. Inventory clerk Mei can check the putaway record. No stock move, bin-sign change, or substitution has been approved. The location mismatch does not prove why the green folders are there or establish where the blue folders are. Luis needs to report both complete codes and the location without picking the wrong color.
Language workshop: The bin sign and stock disagree

Dialogue 07: Credit approval and warehouse release are separate
Original fictional setting: A returns associate answers a customer-service query.
Professional roles: Customer service clerk / Returns associate
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Customer service clerk: C18 was credited this morning. Can its returned item become available stock?
Returns associate: Credit approval concerns the customer's account. It doesn't tell us whether the returned item meets the warehouse's release requirements.
Customer service clerk: I said approved without naming the decision; only the credit was approved.
Returns associate: The return is physically received, but its inspection hold remains. We have no confirmed disposition from the returns checker.
Customer service clerk: Then I should not tell sales a replacement order can use this unit simply because the customer has been credited.
Returns associate: Correct. Sales needs confirmed available-stock status, while the customer team may separately report that the account credit was approved.
Customer service clerk: Who owns the remaining warehouse decision, so I can direct the availability question to the right person?
Returns associate: Returns checker Omar owns the condition review, and the returns desk will communicate the resulting stock disposition.
Customer service clerk: I'll ask returns about availability, rather than asking finance to release stock.
Returns associate: Thanks. Keep C18 on the query so the physical return can be matched to the customer-account reference.
Customer service clerk: I'll report credit approved, warehouse release pending as two distinct statuses.
Returns associate: That is the distinction we need. We'll confirm availability only after the appropriate warehouse decision has been recorded.
Vocabulary in this exchange: inspection hold: A status preventing ordinary release while goods await a required condition check. / disposition: The approved decision about what happens next to goods, such as restocking or supplier return.
END REFERENCE

Dialogue 08: The incoming shift needs a bounded task
Original fictional setting: An outgoing counter hands an incomplete location review to the next clerk.
Professional roles: Counter / Incoming clerk
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Counter: I checked A01 through A04, but C90 also covers A05 and A06.
Incoming clerk: Which results are confirmed so far, and are there unresolved differences within the four locations you already counted?
Counter: A01 through A03 match; A04 is one short on its first count.
Incoming clerk: Then I have two kinds of remaining work: recount A04 and make the initial counts for A05 and A06.
Counter: Exactly. I don't want 'four locations checked' to imply the entire assignment or the A04 discrepancy is settled.
Incoming clerk: Please give me the count reference so I can continue the same assignment without creating a competing set of results.
Counter: It's count C90. No adjustments were approved, and I haven't assigned a cause to the A04 difference.
Incoming clerk: I'll acknowledge C90 with those limits: three matching locations, one preliminary variance, and two locations not yet counted.
Counter: This partial count doesn't independently verify the entire system total.
Incoming clerk: Agreed. I'll report completion only after the remaining counts and the discrepancy review reach their actual stages.
Counter: Who receives the completed count if a variance investigation remains open?
Incoming clerk: The inventory desk will receive my count summary, with any unresolved variance explicitly retained for its separate review.
