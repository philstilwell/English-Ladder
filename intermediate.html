Intermediate ESL: Extreme Weather in Japan: Fatalities, Power Outages, and Travel Disruptions

I. The News Brief

Japan experienced unprecedented heavy rainfall over the weekend, leading to severe disruptions across several regions. This extreme weather event unfortunately resulted in the deaths of eight individuals. The powerful storm cut power to a significant number of residents in affected areas. More than 20,000 households were left without electricity due to the severe conditions. The intense rainfall also caused widespread travel chaos, affecting thousands of people. Approximately 7,000 individuals were left stranded at Tokyo's Narita airport. Many flights were cancelled, and train services were suspended, severely impacting travel plans. Local authorities issued urgent warnings for potential landslides and flooding as the rain continued. Emergency response teams worked tirelessly to restore essential services and assist those impacted by the storm. The Japanese government has initiated efforts to assess the full extent of the damage caused by these extreme weather conditions.

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. unprecedented (adjective): Never done or known before; something new and without an earlier example.
2. cut power (verb phrase): To stop the supply of electricity to an area or building.
3. households (noun): All the people who live together in one house or flat.
4. stranded (adjective): Left in a place without any way of leaving it, usually in a difficult situation.
5. airport (noun): A place where aircraft take off and land, with buildings for passengers and cargo.

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action or the receiver of the action, rather than the doer. We often use it when we don't know or don't want to say who performed the action. It is formed with a form of the verb 'to be' followed by the past participle of the main verb.
Example from the text: "Approximately 7,000 individuals were left stranded at Tokyo's Narita airport."

Former US President Donald Trump reportedly used a deceptive plan involving a catering truck and a secret plane swap to leave a NATO summit in Turkey, misleading reporters due to concerns over a potential threat from Iran.

Intermediate ESL: Presidential Security and Deception Tactics

I. The News Brief

Former US President Donald Trump reportedly used an unusual method to depart a NATO summit in Turkey secretly. He was involved in a ruse, a deceptive plan, to mislead reporters about his travel arrangements. Journalists were falsely told he would be on Air Force One with them as they left the summit. However, a secret plane swap was carried out, allowing for his discreet departure. According to reports, Trump hid inside a catering truck during this complex operation. This particular maneuver was reportedly prompted by concerns about a potential threat from Iran. The extraordinary lengths taken highlight the intense security considerations for world leaders. Reporters, who were led to believe he was on their plane, did not discover the deception until later. The main goal was to ensure the president's safety, especially when leaving a sensitive area. Such an incident illustrates the elaborate strategies sometimes necessary for presidential security protocols.

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. ruse (noun): A trick or act that is used to fool someone or to hide the truth.
2. discreet (adjective): Careful in what you say or do, so that you do not embarrass or offend people or tell secrets; not drawing attention to itself.
3. maneuver (noun): A movement or series of moves requiring skill and care, often used to achieve a particular goal or avoid something.
4. prompted (verb (past participle)): Caused someone to do something or caused something to happen; to provoke or inspire an action.
5. deception (noun): The act of hiding the truth, especially to get an advantage or to make someone believe something that is not true.

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the action of the verb is more important than who or what is performing the action, or when the agent is unknown or less important. The subject of the sentence receives the action rather than performing it. It's often formed with a form of 'to be' (e.g., is, are, was, were) followed by the past participle of the main verb.
Example from the text: "Reporters, who were led to believe he was on their plane, did not discover the deception until later."
