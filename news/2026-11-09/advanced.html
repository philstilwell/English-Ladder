Hazel dormice show signs of recovery in England and Wales

📅 November 09, 2026 - Hazel dormice show signs of recovery in England and Wales

Read the story

Hazel dormouse numbers show tentative signs of recovery after decades of decline. The National Dormouse Monitoring Programme, led by the People’s Trust for Endangered Species, has monitored sites in England and Wales since 1998. Its data indicate that dormouse numbers have fallen by almost 60 per cent since 2000, equivalent to an average annual decline of 3.5 per cent. Although the monitoring data point to a possible increase of nearly 10 per cent between 2020 and 2025, researchers stress that there is considerable uncertainty around that estimate. The apparent upturn coincided with springs and summers that were warmer and drier than average. Hazel dormice typically hibernate from around October to May, spending the winter in nests on the ground. In spring and summer, cold, wet weather or a shortage of food can prompt dormice to enter torpor, a short spell of deep sleep that conserves energy. Warmer summers may reduce the need for torpor and leave dormice with more energy for feeding and reproduction, researchers suggest. Ecologist Charlotte Armitage cautioned that it remains unclear how warmer, wetter winters might affect dormice. The researchers say the potential longer-term effects of climate change on the species warrant further investigation. Once widespread, the hazel dormouse is now largely confined to southern England and parts of Wales. Hazel dormice have become much rarer amid widespread woodland loss, and native populations have disappeared from around 20 English counties. Conservationists stress that safeguarding the woodlands and hedgerows where dormice live remains essential to their long-term survival.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. tentative (adjective): Based on evidence that suggests a possibility but still leaves room for doubt.
2. equivalent to (adjective followed by a preposition): Corresponding in value when expressed on another scale or in another form.
3. point to (prepositional verb): Suggest a conclusion on the basis of available evidence, leaving room for uncertainty.
4. upturn (noun): A shift towards improvement, especially a rise after a period of falling numbers or weaker results.
5. coincided with (prepositional verb in the past tense): Occurred during the same period as something else, with any causal relationship left open.
6. torpor (uncountable noun): A temporary state like deep sleep in which an animal becomes much less active and uses less energy.
7. conserves (verb, third-person singular present simple): Keeps more of a resource available by reducing how much is used.
8. warrant (verb): Provide sufficient reason to take an action or examine something more closely.
9. confined to (past participle followed by the preposition ‘to’): Restricted in range to a particular area or set of places.
10. safeguarding (verb in the “-ing” form): Taking steps to prevent damage or loss.

Advanced Grammar: Concession with an opening “although” clause
An opening clause with “although” acknowledges a point that might lead readers to expect a different conclusion. A comma separates it from the main clause. In the reading, this order gives the researchers’ caution the final emphasis.
Example from the text: "Although the monitoring data point to a possible increase of nearly 10 per cent between 2020 and 2025, researchers stress that there is considerable uncertainty around that estimate."
