Air Traffic Chaos: Passengers Lose Money and Holidays

Beginner · A1–A2

News archive →
📅 September 10, 2026 - Air Traffic Chaos: Passengers Lose Money and Holidays

Read the story

Problems with air traffic control caused chaos for many thousands of air passengers. Catie and Phil Allen lost money because their holiday to Slovenia was cancelled. Their flight to Ljubljana never took off due to the disruption. If a flight is cancelled, airlines must get customers to their destination quickly or give a refund. However, extra compensation is not always paid if airlines are not at fault for the problem. Catie and Phil booked accommodation and excursions separately, so they lost money on these. Some other passengers ended up sleeping on floors at Heathrow Airport after their flights were cancelled. Airlines should supply delayed passengers with food and a hotel stay.

Words and grammar

1. chaos (noun): a state of great confusion and disorder; everything is mixed up and not organized.
2. disruption (noun): a problem that stops something from continuing as normal or planned.
3. passengers (noun): people who travel in a car, bus, train, or airplane but are not the driver or pilot.
4. cancelled (verb): stopped; decided not to do something that was planned, like a trip or an event.
5. refund (noun): money that is given back to you, especially when you return something you bought or a service is not provided.
6. separately (adverb): apart from other things or people; not together.
7. destination (noun): the place where someone or something is going.

Grammar Focus: Using 'because' for reasons
We use the word 'because' to show the reason or cause for something. It tells us why something happens. For example, 'I am happy because it is sunny' means the sunny weather is the reason for happiness.
Example from the text: "Catie and Phil Allen lost money because their holiday to Slovenia was cancelled."
