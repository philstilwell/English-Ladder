📅 September 03, 2026 [0 days, 0 hours old] - El Niño: A Big Weather Event
The UN and experts say a big natural weather event called El Niño might cause problems around the world.

Beginner ESL: Global weather changes and economic impact

I. The News Brief

The UN warns about El Niño. El Niño is a natural weather phenomenon. It means big changes in weather on Earth. Experts say this El Niño is 'supersized', so it will be very strong. Many countries are getting ready for it. The World Meteorological Organization (WMO) also gave a warning. El Niño could bring big problems. These problems are called disruption. The disruption might affect global economies. Countries need to prepare for its impact, meaning its effects.

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. natural weather phenomenon (noun phrase): A weather event that happens in nature, not caused by people.
2. warns (verb): To tell someone about a possible danger or problem so they can be careful.
3. disruption (noun): A problem that stops something from continuing as usual; a big disturbance.
4. global economies (noun phrase): How money and trade work in all countries around the world.
5. impact (noun): The strong effect or influence that something has on another thing.

Grammar Focus: Using 'could' for possibility
We use the word 'could' to talk about things that are possible in the future, but not certain. It means something might happen. For example, 'It could rain tomorrow' means rain is possible, but not 100% sure.
Example from the text: "El Niño could bring big problems."

5. blamed (verb, past simple): Said someone was responsible for something bad.

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense
We use the simple past tense to talk about actions that happened and finished in the past. We often add '-ed' to regular verbs. For example, 'blame' becomes 'blamed' when the action is finished.

📅 September 01, 2026 [1 day, 23 hours old] - People Forced to Return to Afghanistan: A Difficult New Start

Beginner ESL: Forced returns to Afghanistan under the Taliban

This year, one million more people returned. They must start over in Afghanistan. The Taliban group controls Afghanistan now. Some deportees say, "I've never been to Afghanistan." II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

2. deportees (noun): People who are made to leave a country by the government.
3. return (verb): To go back to a place where you were before.
4. start over (verb phrase): To begin something again from the beginning, usually after a problem or change.
5. controls (verb): To have power over a place, a group of people, or a situation.

Grammar Focus: Simple Present Tense
We use the simple present tense to talk about things that are always true, facts, or habits. We also use it to talk about situations happening now or regularly.
Example from the text: "The Taliban group controls Afghanistan now."

📅 August 29, 2026 [5 days, 13 hours old] - Tibet Floods: Hidden News
Floods happened in Tibet, but people in China cannot see all the news.

Beginner ESL: Natural Disasters and Information Control

I. The News Brief

Tibet recently had big floods. These floods caused much damage. Many people were victims of the floods. There is video footage about these events. This footage shows very bad scenes. But this video is hidden in China. People in China cannot see it. It is not for public view. So, we know little about the victims. Getting information about the floods is difficult.

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. floods (noun): Too much water that covers land that is usually dry.
2. victims (noun): People who are hurt or suffer because of an accident, crime, or disaster.
3. footage (noun): Part of a film or video recording that shows an event.
4. hidden (adjective): Kept secret or put in a place where it cannot be seen or found.
5. public view (noun phrase): When something is shown to many people, not kept secret or private.

Grammar Focus: "There is" and "There are"
We use "There is" for one thing, and "There are" for many things. It tells us that something exists or is in a place. For example, "There is a book on the table." "There are two cats in the garden."
Example from the text: "There is video footage about these events."

Beginner ESL: Glaciers, Floods, and Climate Change in the Himalayas

Scientists say the floods were very bad. The floods destroyed many things. Ice in the Himalayan mountains is melting fast. This melting Himalayan ice is a serious issue. II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. collapsed glacier (noun phrase): A large piece of ice that broke into pieces and fell down from a mountain.
2. likely caused (verb phrase): Probably made something happen or was the main reason for an event.
3. devastating floods (noun phrase): Very destructive overflows of water that cause great harm and damage.
4. scientists say (verb phrase): Researchers or experts share their findings or give their professional opinions.
5. melting Himalayan ice (noun phrase): Frozen water in the high mountains of the Himalayas that is turning into liquid water.

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense
We use the simple past tense to talk about actions or events that started and finished at a specific time in the past. For regular verbs, we often add '-ed', but many verbs have special past forms.
Example from the text: "The collapsed glacier likely caused devastating floods there."
