Critics Welcome Nigella Lawson to Bake Off

Advanced · C1+

News archive →
📅 September 08, 2026 - Critics Welcome Nigella Lawson to Bake Off

Read the story

Early reviews have lauded Nigella Lawson’s arrival as a judge on The Great British Bake Off. The seventeenth series premieres on Channel 4 on 22 September, following a preview for television reviewers. Lawson succeeds Prue Leith, who said she wanted to reduce her workload and enjoy more free time. Ben Dowell of the Times portrayed the production team as delighted by Lawson’s presence. His comparison with royalty conveys admiration rather than a claim about her actual status. The Telegraph’s Anita Singh depicted Lawson as a source of material likely to circulate on social media. Her metaphor, “a walking meme factory,” emphasizes the potential for Lawson’s comments to become widely shared online jokes. The announced roster brings together a doctor, a sprinter, a hairdresser and a drag artist. Drawing on his Nigerian roots, Moyin incorporates distinctive flavours into his bakes while combining university studies with athletics. Yannis, a doctor, hopes to counter the stereotype that Bangladeshi men do not belong in the kitchen. Connie, who works in hospital discharge services, describes baking as a way to unwind after a demanding day. These accounts describe the bakers’ interests and aspirations; they do not establish how well each person will perform in the competition.

Words and grammar

1. lauded (verb, past participle): Praised enthusiastically in public; more formal and emphatic than simply saying someone was liked.
2. premieres (verb): Has its first public broadcast or performance; an earlier private preview is not the public premiere.
3. portrayed (verb, past simple): Presented someone or something in a particular way; a portrayal reflects the writer’s perspective.
4. conveys (verb): Communicates an idea or feeling, sometimes indirectly through a comparison or choice of words.
5. depicted (verb, past simple): Represented someone in words or images; here, described Lawson in a particular way.
6. circulate (verb): Spread from person to person or place to place; here, be shared online.
7. roster (noun): A list of the people taking part; more common in organized teams and events than in casual conversation.
8. incorporates (verb): Includes something as part of a larger whole; here, puts particular flavours into the finished bakes.
9. stereotype (noun): A fixed, oversimplified idea about a group that ignores differences between individuals.
10. aspirations (plural noun): Hopes or ambitions for the future; aspirations describe aims, not proven results.

Advanced Grammar: An introductory -ing clause
An introductory -ing clause can describe an action or circumstance connected with the main clause. Its understood subject must match the subject that follows. Here, Moyin both draws on his roots and incorporates flavours. A different following subject could wrongly suggest that someone or something else is doing the drawing.
Example from the text: "Drawing on his Nigerian roots, Moyin incorporates distinctive flavours into his bakes while combining university studies with athletics."
