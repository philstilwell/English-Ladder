Your goal

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

An account application lacks requested ownership information. The applicant asks staff to open the account before the review is complete.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

KYC
Know-your-customer process used to verify a customer's identity, ownership, and risk profile.
CIP
Customer Identification Program: a bank's program for identifying customers under applicable US requirements.
beneficial owner
Natural person who ultimately owns or controls an entity or receives its economic benefit.
risk rating
A category or score assigned using defined criteria to describe a risk.

03 · Notice the language

Ask for an actionable next step

State the action and the information needed. Explain why it matters and specify a deadline only when one is given or agreed. 'Could you' is polite; repeated apologies can obscure a legitimate request. Urgent situations may require a direct request.

  • Could you provide ... so that ...?
  • Please confirm ... by [agreed time].
  • If that timing is not possible, please let me know ... .

Improve this: Please revert soonest with the necessary.

See a clearer version

Could you send the missing document and confirm when it will be available?

The revision names the document and requested response. It avoids expressions that may be unfamiliar or ambiguous for an international audience.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. What does 'Please send it by Thursday' normally mean?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. Send it no later than Thursday.

  1. A. Send it on Thursday, but not earlier. “By Thursday” permits earlier delivery; it is not restricted to that day.
  2. B. Keep sending it regularly until Thursday. This interprets a deadline as a continuing activity, which would use “until”.
  3. C. Send it no later than Thursday. 'By' sets the latest point, while 'after' refers to a later time.
2. Which request gives the recipient enough detail to act?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review?

  1. A. Could you confirm that you received my earlier message? This checks receipt, not which version is required for the review.
  2. B. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review? The requested action and information are explicit.
  3. C. Could you confirm that the review is important? This checks importance rather than identifying the needed document version.

05 · Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Ownership information is still required for review

Fictional account-opening exercise: this application's existing request asks for Mira Chen's ownership percentage, which is missing. The application review is incomplete. This case-specific request is not a universal banking requirement; actual decisions follow current authorized procedures.

  1. Account-opening specialist: Could you provide the requested ownership information through our approved channel so the application review can continue?
  2. Company finance officer: Can you open the account first? Our team needs it before the review is finished.
  3. Account-opening specialist: I can't treat the incomplete review as approved. I'll explain the outstanding request clearly.
  4. Company finance officer: Which information is missing? I don't want to resend unrelated documents or guess your requirements.
  5. Account-opening specialist: Please provide Mira Chen's ownership percentage, as requested for this application. When can you supply it?
  6. Company finance officer: Does beneficial owner simply mean the person who signed the application for the company?
  7. Account-opening specialist: Don't assume they're identical. The applicable definition and requested details need the authorized KYC review.
  8. Company finance officer: I'll ask our authorized company contact to provide the specified information and confirm availability.
  9. Account-opening specialist: Thank you. That supports know-your-customer review without promising approval or assigning a risk rating here.
  10. Company finance officer: I'll explain that providing the missing information advances review, but doesn't guarantee account opening.
2. Identity evidence does not settle ownership

Separate fictional onboarding case: an applicant's identity document is received, while requested company ownership information remains outstanding.

  1. Relationship coordinator: We received the signer's identity document. Can we mark all account-opening checks complete?
  2. Onboarding reviewer: No. The requested ownership information is still outstanding, so the overall review remains open.
  3. Relationship coordinator: Could you identify the next request clearly so I don't ask the client for everything again?
  4. Onboarding reviewer: Please ask for the outstanding ownership information specified in the current application request.
  5. Relationship coordinator: Does completing an identity check under the customer identification program, CIP, establish beneficial ownership too?
  6. Onboarding reviewer: We shouldn't equate those checks. Follow the applicable review requirements for each outstanding item.
  7. Relationship coordinator: I'll keep the received identity document separate from the missing company information in our status update.
  8. Onboarding reviewer: Good. Use the approved channel and avoid circulating identification material in general internal messages.
  9. Relationship coordinator: I'll request an achievable delivery point from the client rather than promise account activation today.
  10. Onboarding reviewer: That gives a clear action and purpose without bypassing review or overstating what one document establishes.
3. A changed ownership chart needs a verified version

Separate fictional application case: two company ownership charts differ; neither is identified as current, and no onboarding decision is available.

  1. Onboarding analyst: Could you confirm which ownership chart is current? The two submitted versions show different information.
  2. Corporate relationship manager: I can ask the authorized company contact. Should I simply use the one received most recently?
  3. Onboarding analyst: Receipt order alone doesn't establish current ownership. We need a verified explanation of the difference.
  4. Corporate relationship manager: I'll request the current chart and the relevant clarification through the approved document channel.
  5. Onboarding analyst: Please identify both versions so the client can address the actual discrepancy instead of resending blindly.
  6. Corporate relationship manager: Does the mismatch mean we should assign the highest risk rating before the review?
  7. Onboarding analyst: That determination belongs to the authorized process. Here we should describe the discrepancy factually.
  8. Corporate relationship manager: I'll avoid labeling it deception or promising acceptance after a corrected chart arrives.
  9. Onboarding analyst: Good. Ask when the verified information is available, keeping any formal requirements unchanged.
  10. Corporate relationship manager: I'll return the client's confirmed status and preserve the unresolved ownership question until reviewed.

06 · Say it

Two scenarios to practice

Scenario 1 · Account Opening and KYC

An account application lacks requested ownership information. The applicant asks staff to open the account before the review is complete.

Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.

Partner's role and follow-up

You can help but need a clear request and its purpose. Ask what is required and whether the timing is fixed or negotiable.

Try a harder second round

The other person cannot meet the requested timing. Clarify an alternative without inventing authority to change a formal deadline.

Scenario 2 · A translated document needs the specified review

A fictional business applicant supplies a document in a language the onboarding reviewer cannot assess. The current process's translation requirements have not been checked. Request an authorized clarification rather than inventing a requirement or approving the application.

Role A: Onboarding coordinator: ask the review lead to confirm the applicable translation requirements.

Role B: Relationship manager: explain the applicant needs a specific request and realistic timing.

Possible opening: “Could you confirm the applicable translation requirements so we give the applicant an accurate document request?”

Success checks
  • Avoid interpreting unverified ownership information.
  • Request the applicable requirement from the authorized source.
  • Keep approval and timing unpromised.
Add a complication

Someone suggests guessing the ownership details from familiar names in the document.

Studying alone? Speak both roles aloud. For each scenario, prepare for two minutes, speak for one minute, then answer a follow-up. Switch roles and repeat using fewer notes. Use only the supplied facts; identify missing information instead of inventing it.

Compare scenario 1 with a model response
Could you provide the outstanding ownership information through the approved channel? I can explain what is missing, but the application still needs to follow the bank's review process.

This is one possible spoken response, not the only acceptable wording. Preserve the case facts when you try your own version.

Check your reasoning

  • Uses understandable sentences and explains specialist terms when the listener needs it.
  • Responds to the other person's question, checks understanding, and uses an appropriate tone.
  • Makes the requested action or unresolved question clear without inventing authority or facts.

Revise one sentence and repeat the response. A useful response can be clear even with a few grammar errors; judge meaning and task completion, not accent.

structuring

Suspicious Activity Report: a confidential regulatory report filed under applicable financial-crime reporting requirements.

03 · Notice the language

Match certainty to evidence

Say what the evidence shows, identify what is still unknown, and limit the conclusion to that evidence. Words such as 'may', 'suggests', and 'in this sample' are useful when the uncertainty is real. Do not soften an urgent, verified safety concern.

  • This may indicate ..., but ... .
  • We have not yet established whether ... .

See a clearer version

The pilot worked in the tested setting; performance elsewhere remains untested.

The revision states the finding and its scope. It avoids turning limited evidence into a universal claim.

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence preserves uncertainty about an unconfirmed cause?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. The change may have contributed to the delay.

  1. A. The change definitely caused the delay. 'Definitely' presents the cause as certain.
  2. B. The change may have contributed to the delay. 'May have contributed' presents a possibility without claiming the cause is established.
  3. C. The change must have caused the delay. 'Must have' expresses a strong inference that the evidence may not support.
2. A result comes from a small pilot in one location. Which phrase accurately limits the claim?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. In this pilot, the observed result was ...

  1. A. In this pilot, the observed result was ... This locates the finding in the setting actually studied.
  2. B. In similar locations, we can now expect the same result ... Similarity alone does not establish that the result will transfer.
  3. C. Across the service, the observed result was ... A pilot in one location does not establish a service-wide observation.

  2. B. Could you confirm when does the review start? Remove 'does' and use statement word order after 'confirm'.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. Has the report been approved, or is it ready for review?

  1. A. Can you send the report to me this afternoon? This asks about delivery timing, not whether approval has happened.
  2. B. Who will present the report at the meeting? This asks about the presenter, not the report's approval status.
  3. C. Has the report been approved, or is it ready for review? This question distinguishes two meanings of 'ready' that affect the next action.

Answer: B. Delivery is planned for Friday; the carrier has not confirmed it.

Answer and explanations

1. Signed off does not mean every balance matched

Fictional operational-control discussion: a reconciliation is signed off while one unmatched balance remains under investigation; sign-off meaning has not been clarified.

  1. Operations reporting manager: The reconciliation is signed off, so can I say every balance has been matched?
  2. Control analyst: One unmatched balance remains under investigation. We need to clarify what the sign-off actually records.
  3. Operations reporting manager: Could you explain reconciliation in plain English for the colleague preparing the management slide?
  4. Control analyst: It's a comparison of records to identify differences; signing a review doesn't necessarily remove every difference.
  5. Operations reporting manager: Then completing a review and resolving an unmatched balance are distinct statuses in this case.
  6. Control analyst: Exactly. The current label shouldn't hide the open difference or its assigned follow-up.
  7. Operations reporting manager: Does that open item prove the control never operated or that someone committed fraud?
  8. Control analyst: Neither conclusion follows automatically. We need evidence about operation, findings, and the investigation.
  9. Operations reporting manager: I'll explain the open balance and request clarification of the sign-off and remediation status.
  10. Control analyst: Good. That communicates operational risk accurately without treating a signature as proof of a fully reconciled position.
2. A control description is not evidence of operation

Separate fictional risk review: a team has documented a daily control but has not supplied records showing it operated during the review period.

  1. Risk coordinator: The daily control is described in the manual. Does that prove it operated throughout the review period?
  2. Control assurance specialist: No. The description explains what should happen; operating evidence shows what actually happened.
  3. Risk coordinator: So a written control design and documented performance are different things we need to explain.
  4. Control assurance specialist: Yes. We should request relevant records rather than infer completed checks from the manual.
  5. Risk coordinator: Would missing evidence prove the team never performed any of those daily checks?
  6. Control assurance specialist: Not by itself. It establishes a gap in the evidence supplied, which needs further review.
  7. Risk coordinator: I'll describe the manual as available and the operating-evidence assessment as still open.
  8. Control assurance specialist: Good. Keep any finding or remediation plan tied to the actual review, not assumptions about staff.
  9. Risk coordinator: I'll ask the record owner for the specified period's support through the approved channel.
  10. Control assurance specialist: That distinction helps management understand both the documented process and what remains unverified about its operation.
3. Fixing one item versus changing the process

Separate fictional operational-risk case: one erroneous internal entry has been corrected, but the process that produced it has not yet been assessed.

  1. Operations supervisor: We corrected the entry. Can we say the operational-risk issue has been permanently remediated?

Your goal

Make a request with a clear action, purpose, and realistic timing.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

An examination request asks for evidence of a control operating over a stated period. The team has only the policy document ready.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

examiner
A person conducting an official review of an institution's activities or records.
audit evidence
Records, observations, or other information used to support an audit conclusion.
finding
A documented result or conclusion of a review or investigation.
management response
Management's documented reply to a finding, including proposed or completed actions.

03 · Notice the language

Ask for an actionable next step

State the action and the information needed. Explain why it matters and specify a deadline only when one is given or agreed. 'Could you' is polite; repeated apologies can obscure a legitimate request. Urgent situations may require a direct request.

  • Could you provide ... so that ...?
  • Please confirm ... by [agreed time].
  • If that timing is not possible, please let me know ... .

Improve this: Please revert soonest with the necessary.

See a clearer version

Could you send the missing document and confirm when it will be available?

The revision names the document and requested response. It avoids expressions that may be unfamiliar or ambiguous for an international audience.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. What does 'Please send it by Thursday' normally mean?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. Send it no later than Thursday.

  1. A. Send it on Thursday, but not earlier. “By Thursday” permits earlier delivery; it is not restricted to that day.
  2. B. Keep sending it regularly until Thursday. This interprets a deadline as a continuing activity, which would use “until”.
  3. C. Send it no later than Thursday. 'By' sets the latest point, while 'after' refers to a later time.
2. Which request gives the recipient enough detail to act?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review?

  1. A. Could you confirm that you received my earlier message? This checks receipt, not which version is required for the review.
  2. B. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review? The requested action and information are explicit.
  3. C. Could you confirm that the review is important? This checks importance rather than identifying the needed document version.

1. Request evidence for the stated examination period

Fictional examination preparation: a request asks for control-operation evidence over a stated period; only the policy document is ready.

  2. Operations control owner: The policy is ready. Do you need something beyond the description of how the control should work?
  3. Examination coordinator: Yes. The request concerns actual operation, so we need the relevant records for that stated period.
  4. Operations control owner: Can I send today's completed check as proof that all earlier checks were performed?
  5. Examination coordinator: Not as proof of the whole period. Match the evidence to the request and identify any gaps.
  6. Operations control owner: I'll check available records and confirm realistic retrieval timing through the approved review channel.
  7. Examination coordinator: Thank you. Please keep original dates and avoid recreating missing checks as if contemporaneous.

  9. Examination coordinator: That outcome belongs to the review. Our immediate task is an accurate, specific evidence response.

Your goal

Acknowledge a communication problem and give a corrected message.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A customer asks why a fee was charged. The representative has a current fee schedule but has not checked the transaction details.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

complaint
An expression of dissatisfaction requiring attention under a relevant process.
fee disclosure
Information explaining charges and how they apply under a service arrangement.
error resolution
The process for investigating and addressing a reported transaction or service error.
fair treatment
Handling people consistently and appropriately under relevant rules and circumstances.

03 · Notice the language

Repair a misunderstanding

Name what was unclear or wrong, acknowledge its effect, and correct it. A brief apology can help, followed by a practical next step. Avoid explaining away the other person's reaction or promising an outcome you cannot control.

  • My earlier message did not make ... clear.
  • I'm sorry for ... . The correct information is ... .
  • Let me check that the revised explanation addresses ... .

Improve this: I'm sorry if you failed to understand.

See a clearer version

I'm sorry my message did not explain the timing clearly.

The revision takes responsibility for the message without blaming the listener. It identifies what needs to be corrected.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which apology takes responsibility for unclear wording?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. I'm sorry my earlier wording was unclear.

  1. A. I'm sorry we could not meet the original date. This acknowledges a missed date, not the communication problem in the question.
  2. B. I'm sorry my earlier wording was unclear. This acknowledges the speaker's contribution to the problem.
  3. C. I'm sorry the process took longer than expected. This acknowledges duration, but does not take responsibility for the unclear wording.
2. What should follow an apology for an incorrect date?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. The corrected date and the current confirmation status.

  1. A. The corrected date and the current confirmation status. A repair needs usable corrected information, including any uncertainty.
  2. B. A longer explanation that never states the correct date. The reader still lacks the information they need.
  3. C. A new guaranteed date even if it is unverified. An unsupported new promise can create a second misunderstanding.
