Anthropic's Stock Market Debut Tests Confidence in AI

📅 November 20, 2026 - Anthropic's Stock Market Debut Tests Confidence in AI

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Anthropic became one of the world's most valuable public companies after its shares began trading on the stock market. The artificial intelligence (AI) company became publicly listed, allowing people to buy its shares. Its valuation, or total market value, reached roughly $1.76 trillion. Its debut, or first appearance, on the Nasdaq stock exchange was an important test for an industry receiving huge investment. The share price quickly reached $111.53 before dropping to $97.17 by the end of the day. Although Anthropic's shares gained roughly 17% during the day, the wider technology market did not perform as well. A few major technology companies spent more than $1 trillion on developing AI in the last year alone. The rapid growth in investment has raised concerns about a financial bubble, when prices rise beyond a reasonable value. Ben Barringer, who leads technology research at Quilter Cheviot, expects significant price swings, or large movements up and down. Neil Wilson said there was no obvious similar company to compare Anthropic with. In the US, several politicians have said they want rules to make sure AI tools are safe. The company, which makes the AI chatbot Claude, will face close attention as people question whether AI is worth its cost.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. valuation (noun): An estimate of how much a company is worth.
2. debut (noun): A first public appearance, here a company's first day on the stock market.
3. investment (noun): Money put into a business or activity with the hope of gaining a future benefit.
4. perform (verb): To have particular results, either good or bad.
5. concerns (plural noun): Worries about a problem or something that might go wrong.
6. financial bubble (noun phrase): A situation in which prices rise beyond a reasonable value and may later drop sharply.
7. price swings (plural noun phrase): Large changes in prices as they move up and down.
8. compare (verb): To examine things together to understand how they are similar or different.

Grammar Focus: Relative clauses with “which”
A relative clause with “which” adds information about a thing or company. Put commas around this extra information when the sentence already identifies the company. The main sentence still makes sense without the clause.
Example from the text: "The company, which makes the AI chatbot Claude, will face close attention as people question whether AI is worth its cost."
