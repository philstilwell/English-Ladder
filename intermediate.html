📅 October 31, 2026 - Romesh Ranganathan and Sebastian Croft Win Celebrity Traitors
Explore how voting decisions shaped the Celebrity Traitors final and learn language for describing choices and results.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The second series of BBC One's The Celebrity Traitors reached its final on Friday. For four weeks, famous contestants faced suspense and suspicion as they tried to win through clever thinking. More than 15 million people have been watching this series. The group of 21 original contestants was reduced to seven before the final. Comedian Romesh Ranganathan and actor Sebastian Croft became the two winners, both belonging to the team called the faithful. The faithful team succeeded in removing the remaining traitors, Maya Jama and King Kenny, and the winners shared £83,000 for charity. During the final stage, contestants gathered around a fire pit to decide whether any traitors were still in the game. After a tied vote between James Blunt and Kenny, Croft persuaded Ranganathan to vote Kenny out, securing victory for the faithful. James then chose another round of voting and was removed himself, leaving Ranganathan and Croft as the final two. James could have shared the win if he had chosen to finish the game, as Ranganathan and Croft did. The winners will divide their money between two refugee charities, the Refugee Council and Choose Love.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. suspense (noun): An excited or worried feeling while waiting to discover what will happen.
2. suspicion (noun): A feeling that someone may be dishonest or doing something wrong.
3. contestants (plural noun): People who take part in a competition.
4. reduced (verb, past participle): Made smaller in size or number.
5. remaining (adjective): Still present after others have left or been removed.
6. persuaded (verb, past tense): Caused someone to agree to do something by giving reasons or discussing it.
7. round (noun): One stage of an activity that can happen several times, such as voting.
8. tied vote (noun phrase): A vote in which two choices receive the same number of votes.

Grammar Focus: Past simple passive
Use “was” or “were” followed by a past participle to focus on what happened to someone or something. For example: “The group of 21 original contestants was reduced to seven before the final.” Here, “was reduced” focuses on the change in the group's size without naming who caused it.
Example from the text: "The group of 21 original contestants was reduced to seven before the final."

📅 October 30, 2026 - Netanyahu faces criticism over election claims
Israeli parties disagree over claims of illegal foreign funding after an election leaves the main political groups without a majority.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Netanyahu's coalition, a group of parties governing together, lost its majority in Israel's election. Final results showed that neither Netanyahu's bloc, or political group, nor the main Jewish opposition won more than half the seats in parliament. The two Arab party lists won a record 19 of parliament's 120 seats following a sharp rise in turnout among Arab voters. Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats, while the bloc led by his rival Gadi Eisenkot won 48. These figures could give the Arab parties an important role in deciding who forms the next government. Likud, Netanyahu's party, accused the Arab-led parties of stealing the election. The possibility of illegal funding from Qatar and Turkey is being investigated by Israel's home security agency, Shin Bet. Qatar denied the allegations and accused Israeli politicians of spreading false information about the country. Both Arab parties rejected the claim of wrongdoing, and the Joint List said it had no basis. The agency later clarified that its investigation did not accuse an entire community whose members had voted legally. An election official said there was no basis for claims of a pattern of actions breaking rules protecting fair elections. Eisenkot said it was too early to know whether anything had happened and accused Netanyahu of trying to weaken trust in the results. Netanyahu's critics said he was trying to prevent Eisenkot from agreeing to join forces with the Arab parties.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. coalition (noun): A group of political parties that work together to govern.
2. majority (noun): More than half of the total, here referring to seats in parliament.
3. turnout (uncountable noun): The number or percentage of people who vote in an election.
4. accused (verb, past tense): Said that someone had done something wrong, without necessarily proving it.
5. funding (uncountable noun): Money provided to pay for an activity or organisation.
6. allegations (plural noun): Claims that someone has done something wrong which have not been proved.
7. investigation (noun): A process of carefully examining information to find out what happened.
8. join forces (verb phrase): Work together to achieve a shared aim.

Grammar Focus: Present continuous passive
Use “is” or “are” + “being” + a past participle to describe an action happening now when the person or thing receiving the action is the focus. In the example sentence, “is being investigated” describes an examination that is still in progress; it does not mean the claim has been proved. The phrase beginning with “by” identifies who is carrying out the investigation.
Example from the text: "The possibility of illegal funding from Qatar and Turkey is being investigated by Israel's home security agency, Shin Bet."

Extensive demonstrations regarding schools and financial difficulties led to violent clashes, vandalism, and numerous arrests across France.

Read the story

Violence broke out across France on Saturday during demonstrations against hardship in schools and financial difficulties. In Paris, there were significant clashes into the evening with riot police, who used tear gas to control crowds. Widespread vandalism targeted businesses, and looting was reported in several major cities. As the day continued, some groups torched vehicles in the city streets. Local police said hostile individuals prevented firefighters from safely tackling the blaze. Hundreds of thousands of students have also demonstrated recently over staff shortages and poor facilities in secondary schools. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu stated that 150 police officers were injured in the unrest. Meanwhile, Macron promised that individuals who attacked public buildings would be prosecuted.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. hardship (noun): Severe suffering or difficulty, especially regarding money or living conditions.
2. clashes (noun): Violent confrontations or physical arguments between two or more opposing groups.
3. vandalism (noun): The crime of intentionally damaging buildings or property belonging to other people.
4. looting (noun): The criminal activity of stealing goods from shops and homes during a violent public event.
5. blaze (noun): A very large, dangerous, or fiercely burning fire.
6. shortages (noun): Situations where there is not enough of a necessary resource or personnel.
7. unrest (noun): A state of anger, dissatisfaction, and violent public action among a group of people.
8. prosecuted (verb): Officially charged with a crime and judged in a court of law.

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice (Past Simple)
We use the passive voice when the action itself, or the receiver of the action, is more important than who performed it. To form the past simple passive, use "was" or "were" followed by the past participle of the main verb. It is very common in formal news reporting when the identity of the person doing the action is unknown or less important.
Example from the text: "Widespread vandalism targeted businesses, and looting was reported in several major cities."
