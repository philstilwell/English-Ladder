📅 September 10, 2026 - UK Air Traffic Control Shutdown: Holiday Plans Ruined, Rights Questioned

Read the story

A four-hour shutdown of the UK's air traffic control system caused widespread flight disruption and led to thousands of passengers being affected. Many people experienced ruined holidays and faced significant financial losses due to unused accommodation and excursions. Catie and Phil Allen, for instance, lost an estimated £1,300 after their flight to Slovenia never took off. When a flight is cancelled, airlines must transport customers to their destination as quickly as possible at no extra cost, even if it is with another carrier. Alternatively, passengers can choose a full refund, which should also be offered for delays longer than five hours. Extra compensation is usually paid if airlines are at fault, but not in this case, as air traffic issues were beyond their control. The Allens had booked accommodation and excursions separately, meaning they lost money for these items because their travel insurance policy did not cover air traffic control problems. Unlike package holidays, which are fully covered if a flight is cancelled, individual bookings often come with different terms. Some passengers had to sleep on airport floors because airlines could not provide enough accommodation nearby. Aviation Minister Keir Mather stated that passengers are entitled to care and assistance during delays, but airport capacity was a key lesson to learn.

Words and grammar

1. widespread (adjective): Happening or existing in many places or among many people.
2. affected (verb, past participle): Influenced or changed by something.
3. ruined (verb, past participle): Completely spoiled or destroyed.
4. financial losses (noun phrase): Situations where you lose money, or the amount of money lost.
5. accommodation (noun): A place for someone to stay or live.
6. excursions (noun): Short journeys or trips, especially for pleasure.
7. estimated (adjective): Guessed or calculated approximately.
8. at fault (adjective phrase): Responsible for a mistake or problem.
9. beyond their control (prepositional phrase): Unable to be managed or influenced by them.
10. policy (noun): A plan or course of action, often a formal agreement like an insurance contract.
11. entitled to (verb phrase): Having a right to something.

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice (Present Simple)
The passive voice is used when the action is more important than the performer of the action, or when the performer is unknown or obvious. In the present simple passive, we use 'is/are' + the past participle of the main verb. It shifts the focus from who does something to what is done.
Example from the text: "Extra compensation is usually paid if airlines are at fault, but not in this case, as air traffic issues were beyond their control."
