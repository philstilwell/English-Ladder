Lesson 01: Matching an invoice to its supporting records
Original fictional case: Fictional supplier Alder bills twelve desk lamps on invoice A184. Purchase order P61 lists twelve lamps, but receiving record R9 lists ten. The remaining two may relate to another delivery; no second receiving record is available. Accounts payable clerk Mina asks purchasing colleague Joel to investigate the missing support. No complete match, payment approval, supplier overcharge, or confirmed second delivery has been established.
Language workshop: A184: two lamps without a receiving record
Communication goal: Explain an invoice-matching exception by comparing three records and requesting the missing evidence without assuming a completed delivery.
Language guidance: A184 bills twelve lamps and P61 orders twelve, but R9 supports ten received. The difference is two lamps. The agreement between invoice and order does not remove the mismatch with the receiving record.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): I am querying Alder invoice A184. / It relates to purchase order P61. / The invoice bills twelve desk lamps. / The purchase order also lists twelve. / Receiving record R9 lists ten. / Two lamps are not supported by the available receiving record. / They may relate to another delivery. / Can you locate a second receiving record? / The three records do not yet fully agree. / I will not change R9 to match the invoice. / This difference does not by itself establish overbilling. / We have not established payment approval. / Please include A184, P61, and R9 in the response. / Ten are documented as received in the available record. / The remaining two still need support. / Please check the delivery evidence and report what is actually documented.
Vocabulary: accounts payable: Records and processes concerning amounts owed to suppliers; abbreviated AP. / supplier invoice: Supplier document requesting payment for stated goods or services. / purchase order: Order document specifying requested items and terms; abbreviated PO. / receiving record: Evidence recording goods received under the stated process. / three-way match: Comparison of invoice, order, and receipt information. / matching exception: Difference preventing the required records from agreeing. / invoice quantity: Number of units billed on the invoice. / order quantity: Number of units requested on the purchase order. / received quantity: Number of units documented as received. / supporting document: Record providing evidence for an entry or decision. / line item: Separately identified entry on a transaction document. / unit price: Price stated for one item or unit. / supplier account: Record grouping transactions for a particular vendor. / invoice reference: Identifier for a supplier bill. / order reference: Identifier linking a transaction to the purchase request. / receipt reference: Identifier for the receiving evidence. / quantity variance: Difference between compared unit counts. / partial delivery: Delivery of fewer than all ordered units. / unmatched balance: Portion not yet supported by the required matching evidence. / query status: Current state of an unresolved inquiry. / payment hold: Status preventing payment pending the relevant process. / approval evidence: Record establishing the permission actually given. / overbilling: Billing more than is properly chargeable under the relevant facts. / document chain: Linked records supporting a transaction from order to receipt and bill.
Speaking task: Complete the invoice-matching dialogue. Read the three references and quantities aloud: A184 twelve, P61 twelve, R9 ten. Then read the request for evidence of the remaining two. Complete the transfer, check the explained key, and reread the query without claiming either overbilling or a complete match.
Partner: Mina: Accounts payable clerk / Joel: Purchasing colleague
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: Complete the matching query without assuming a second receipt or payment approval.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Mina: Please say whether you found the missing record. Handled wouldn't tell me whether the exception is resolved.
Joel: I will report the actual query status. If support is still missing, the reply will say so and retain the two-lamp difference against the same document references.
Mina: I'll leave twelve billed and ordered, ten documented, two requiring support, possible second delivery unconfirmed.
Joel: That captures the unmatched balance in quantity terms. I will follow up on the evidence and keep any later conclusion tied to the records that support it.

Lesson 02: Explaining receipts and outstanding customer balances
Original fictional case: Fictional customer Pine has invoice 410 for $600 and invoice 411 for $400. A $600 receipt is identified as Pine's, but it contains no invoice reference. Both invoices remain open in the ledger pending allocation. Receivables clerk Ava asks customer contact Luis for the missing reference. The two invoices total $1,000 and the identified receipt is $600, leaving a $400 net difference across these stated items; that arithmetic does not establish which invoice is settled.
Language workshop: The money arrived; the invoice allocation did not

Language guidance: J14 is a $180 invoice shown twice in tracker version 3 because the same file was imported twice. Two displayed entries would total $360, with one extra $180 entry. That tracker arithmetic does not establish two payments, a $180 cash loss, or a recovery.

Bookkeeper: I will close the quantity query and identify the price check separately for our purchasing colleague.
Supplier: That's helpful. Our team will treat the delivery question as resolved and await any specific pricing question.

Original fictional setting: A fictional bookkeeper explains a correction to a manager who worries money moved twice.
Professional roles: Manager / Bookkeeper

Manager: The revised cash report moves a payment from Thursday to Friday. Did the date correction send it again?
Bookkeeper: No. It changed the report's date label. We have no evidence of a second payment.
Manager: Why was the original date wrong? Please distinguish the report edit from the actual bank activity.
Bookkeeper: The import used preparation date in the bank-date column. The bank record shows Friday.
Manager: So Thursday's report included it too early under that column's meaning. Is that the confirmed issue?
Bookkeeper: Yes. The corrected version uses bank date and links the original report, preserving the explanation.
Manager: Has the weekly total changed, or does the amount move between days within the week?
Bookkeeper: The weekly total is unchanged. Daily totals shift by this item's two hundred twenty dollars.
Manager: I'll correct my earlier suggestion of a second payment. The evidence supports a reporting-date correction.
Bookkeeper: Thank you. Please mention both the unchanged weekly total and changed daily allocation when explaining it.
Manager: Which version should the department use now, and how will they identify the old one?
Bookkeeper: Version five is current; four is marked superseded. The history retains both and explains this date correction.
