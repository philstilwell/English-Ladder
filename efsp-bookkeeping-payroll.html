01Matching an invoice to its supporting recordsLocate the exact mismatch · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Clarify which document supports a billed quantity.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

Fictional supplier Alder bills twelve desk lamps on invoice A184. Purchase order P61 lists twelve, but receiving record R9 lists ten. The remaining two may be on another delivery; no second record is available.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

invoice
A supplier's or seller's bill listing goods or services and the amount requested.
purchase order
A buyer's purchasing document specifying the goods or services ordered.
receiving record
A record of the goods or quantities actually received by an organization.
credit note
A seller's document reducing an amount previously billed, with a reference to the relevant charge.

Useful word combinations

  • match an invoice to an order: compare the supplier's bill with the purchasing reference
  • query a quantity difference: ask why billed and received quantities differ
  • link a credit note: connect a reduction to the original invoice
  • confirm receipt of goods: establish what arrived, not what was merely ordered

03 · Notice the language

Locate the exact mismatch

Name the invoice, order, and receiving record separately. Agreement between two documents does not settle a third document's different quantity. Ask about the missing evidence without assuming either a supplier error or an unrecorded delivery.

  • The invoice shows ___, whereas the receiving record shows ___.
  • Could you confirm whether ___ arrived separately?
  • Which reference supports the remaining ___?

Improve this: Your invoice is wrong.

See a clearer version

The invoice lists twelve lamps, but our available receiving record lists ten. Was there a separate delivery?

The revised question identifies the evidence gap without treating an unexplained difference as a confirmed error.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Bookkeeper: The order lists twelve and R9 lists ten.
Buyer: Please confirm ____ before we call the invoice fully matched.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. whether the other two lamps were received

  1. A. when the invoice becomes due The due date concerns timing, not receipt.
  2. B. who requested the twelve lamps The requester does not establish how many arrived.
  3. C. whether the other two lamps were received Correct. Receipt of the remaining two is the missing fact.
  4. D. the unit price on the original quotation Price does not resolve this quantity difference.
2. Supplier: Are you saying we overbilled you?
Bookkeeper: No; the difference is ____ until we check for another delivery.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. unresolved

  1. A. credited Credited would imply a credit note already exists.
  2. B. unresolved Correct. Unresolved preserves the open question.
  3. C. settled Settled would imply the issue is resolved.
  4. D. overdue Overdue describes a missed due date, not this evidence gap.

05 · Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Two lamps without a receiving reference

A fictional bookkeeper calls the buyer about invoice A184 and receiving record R9.

  1. Bookkeeper: Could you clarify the two-lamp difference on Alder's invoice? The receiving record shows only ten.
  2. Buyer: We ordered twelve, so I expected the invoice to show twelve as well.
  3. Bookkeeper: It does, but the order confirms our request rather than the quantity actually delivered.
  4. Buyer: That's fair. The warehouse mentioned another delivery, although I haven't seen its receiving record.
  5. Bookkeeper: Was that another lamp delivery, or could they have meant the chairs ordered separately?
  6. Buyer: I don't know yet. I can ask for the item description and delivery reference.
  7. Bookkeeper: Please do. For now, shall we describe two lamps as awaiting receipt confirmation?
  8. Buyer: Yes, that is more accurate than calling the supplier's bill incorrect at this stage.
  9. Bookkeeper: I've marked that specific difference for review. The other ten match the available record.
  10. Buyer: I'll check the second delivery with the warehouse and return the reference, not another order copy.
2. A credit note for the wrong invoice

A fictional supplier representative and clerk compare a credit note with two similar invoices.

  1. Clerk: Your credit note mentions invoice seventy, but our damaged shelf was billed on invoice seventeen.
  2. Supplier representative: Both invoices were for shelving. Could you tell me which delivery contained the damaged item?
  3. Clerk: The West office delivery on Monday. Invoice seventeen lists six shelves for that location.
  4. Supplier representative: I see the confusion. Invoice seventy belongs to the East office delivery the following week.
  5. Clerk: Then the amount alone won't establish which invoice the credit note should reduce, will it?
  6. Supplier representative: No. The amounts happen to match, but the delivery and invoice references are different.
  7. Clerk: Could you confirm the intended link before we apply the credit to either balance?
  8. Supplier representative: The intended invoice is seventeen. I will request a corrected credit note with that reference.
  9. Clerk: Thank you. I'll keep the existing note linked to this query until its replacement arrives.
  10. Supplier representative: Please label it pending correction; I have confirmed the intended link, not issued the replacement yet.
3. Cases or individual folders

A fictional invoice query concerns a supplier's unit of measure.

  1. Accounts clerk: The invoice shows four at thirty dollars each, but the receiving record lists forty folders.
  2. Purchasing assistant: That looks like a quantity difference. Did the supplier perhaps omit a zero?
  3. Accounts clerk: Before we conclude that, could you check whether four means cases rather than individual folders?
  4. Purchasing assistant: The purchase order says four cases, each containing ten folders. The price is per case.
  5. Accounts clerk: That explains forty individual folders. Does the invoice also identify the unit as a case?
  6. Purchasing assistant: Yes, in the description beside the item code. It's easy to miss in this layout.
  7. Accounts clerk: Then the quantities agree after comparing the same units: four cases equal forty folders.
  8. Purchasing assistant: Exactly. The price extension is one hundred twenty dollars for the four cases.
  9. Accounts clerk: I'll note the unit conversion in the query so the reviewer can follow the comparison.
  10. Purchasing assistant: And I'll ask receiving to include both cases and pieces next time, where the form permits.

06 · Say it

Two scenarios to practice

Scenario 1 · Matching an invoice to its supporting records

Fictional supplier Alder bills twelve desk lamps on invoice A184. Purchase order P61 lists twelve, but receiving record R9 lists ten. The remaining two may be on another delivery; no second record is available.

Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.

Partner's role and follow-up

Buyer: you ordered twelve lamps and can see only R9. The warehouse can check whether a second delivery occurred.

Try a harder second round

The supplier sends the purchase order again. Explain why it confirms the order quantity but not receipt.

Scenario 2 · A service invoice without a completion reference

Fictional invoice S12 bills two training sessions. The booking email confirms two sessions, but the completion record lists only one. The coordinator can check attendance records; the second session's status is unknown.

Role A: Bookkeeper: distinguish booked sessions from completed sessions and request the missing reference.

Role B: Training coordinator: one attendance list is available; the instructor holds the other session's records.

Possible opening: “The booking covers two sessions, but I can currently support completion of only one.”

Success checks
  • Name the missing second-session evidence.
  • Avoid calling the bill wrong before checking.
  • Agree who will obtain the completion record.
Add a complication

The supplier resends the booking confirmation instead of completion evidence.

Studying alone? Speak both roles aloud. For each scenario, prepare for two minutes, speak for one minute, then answer a follow-up. Switch roles and repeat using fewer notes. Use only the supplied facts; identify missing information instead of inventing it.

Compare scenario 1 with a model response
Invoice A184 and purchase order P61 both show twelve lamps, while receiving record R9 shows ten. Could you confirm whether the other two arrived separately or remain outstanding? I need the related delivery reference before I can describe the invoice as fully matched. At present, the two-lamp difference is unresolved.

This is one possible spoken response, not the only acceptable wording. Preserve the case facts when you try your own version.

Check your reasoning

Compare with the case above and the model response. Can you point to the words that support your factual statement? Is your question about something the case leaves open? If you introduce a possible outcome, clearly label it as a possibility.

Review, then try again

  • Keeps the case facts accurate; clearly separates confirmed and unknown information.
  • Uses understandable sentences and explains specialist terms when the listener needs it.
  • Responds to the other person's question, checks understanding, and uses an appropriate tone.
  • Makes the requested action or unresolved question clear without inventing authority or facts.

Revise one sentence and repeat the response. A useful response can be clear even with a few grammar errors; judge meaning and task completion, not accent.

03 · Notice the language

Separate receipt from allocation

A receipt records money received. Allocation connects it to a particular balance. Do not equate an unallocated receipt with nonpayment, or assume the customer's intended allocation from the amount alone.

  • We have received ___, but it has not been allocated to ___.
  • The open invoices total ___; the separate receipt is ___.
  • Which invoice does your remittance identify?

Improve this: You still owe us a thousand dollars.

See a clearer version

The open invoices total a thousand dollars, and we also have your six-hundred-dollar receipt awaiting allocation.

The correction acknowledges cash already received instead of presenting the gross invoice balance as the entire position.

Your goal

Explain a preview difference while separating arithmetic from approval.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

Fictional preview A shows Morgan's gross pay of $1,200, deductions of $200, and net pay of $1,000. Preview B shows the same gross pay, deductions of $250, and net pay of $950. A new $50 deduction line is present; its supporting record is pending.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

gross pay
An employee's earnings amount before deductions for the specified payroll period.
deduction
An amount subtracted from gross pay, recorded under a specified reason or category.
net pay
The amount remaining after deductions are subtracted from gross pay.
payroll register
A payroll report listing employee earnings, deductions, and payment amounts for a processing run.

Useful word combinations

  • compare payroll previews: check two versions before final processing
  • trace a deduction line: locate the record supporting an amount withheld
  • reconcile the headcount: explain a difference in the number of employees listed
  • identify a one-time payment: distinguish a single payment from a recurring amount

03 · Notice the language

Compare like-for-like payroll figures

Keep the employee and pay period constant. Name gross pay, deductions, and net pay instead of using 'pay' for all three. A figure that adds up may still need supporting documentation.

  • Gross pay is unchanged, whereas ___ differs by ___.
  • The arithmetic difference comes from ___; the support is ___.
  • Are both previews for the same ___?

Improve this: Morgan's salary has gone down.

See a clearer version

Gross pay is unchanged; the preview shows fifty dollars more in deductions and fifty dollars less in net pay.

The revision avoids describing a deduction change as a change in the underlying gross earnings.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Employee: Did my gross pay change between these previews?
Clerk: No; gross pay is unchanged, ____ deductions increased by fifty dollars.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. whereas

  1. A. unless Unless introduces a condition absent from the facts.
  2. B. before Before introduces a time sequence not supplied.
  3. C. whereas Correct. Whereas contrasts unchanged gross pay with changed deductions.
  4. D. because Because would claim the deduction increase caused unchanged gross pay.
2. Reviewer: The figures add up. Is the new deduction approved?
Clerk: Its arithmetic is explained, but the supporting record is ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. pending

  1. A. balanced Balanced does not mean evidence has been provided.
  2. B. pending Correct. The record has not yet been confirmed.
  3. C. recurring Recurring describes frequency rather than review status.
  4. D. net Net describes a remaining amount, not a missing record.

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

close checklist
A list tracking required checks and review tasks at the end of an accounting period.
posting period
The accounting period to which an entry is assigned in the ledger.
adjusting entry
An accounting entry made to correct or update recorded balances, subject to the applicable review.
trial balance
A listing of ledger account balances used to compare total debits and credits at a stated point.

Useful word combinations

  • complete a close checklist: finish and record the specified closing checks
  • confirm the posting period: establish the accounting period assigned to an entry
  • submit an adjustment for review: present a proposed correction to the reviewer
  • tie a schedule to the ledger: compare a supporting total with the recorded account balance

Invoice date, service period, and posting period can differ. Preserve them separately and name the person who will obtain the accountant's decision. A balanced trial balance does not answer every period question.

Your goal

Repair an inaccurate status and preserve the distinction between an entry and a payment.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

In fictional tracker version 3, invoice J14 for $180 appears twice because the same file was imported twice. The controller confirmed one duplicate. No ledger correction has been posted. A previous message wrongly said the duplicate payment had been recovered; no duplicate payment is established.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

audit trail
A traceable history linking a transaction to its source, changes, and review records.
reversal
An entry that offsets an earlier accounting entry, rather than merely deleting its visible record.
duplicate entry
A second recording of the same transaction where only one recording was intended.
version history
A record of successive document or data versions and their changes.

Useful word combinations

  • trace the original entry: locate the transaction being corrected
  • cross-reference a correction: link the new record to the earlier one
  • identify the current version: distinguish the active record from superseded copies
  • explain the correction reason: state what changed and why

03 · Notice the language

Correct the claim, not just the number

State exactly what the earlier message got wrong. A duplicate document, entry, and payment are different findings. Refer to the original record and correction status without teaching a universal reversal method.

  • I incorrectly described ___ as ___.
  • The confirmed finding is ___; we have not established ___.
  • The correction refers back to ___ and is currently ___.

Improve this: The duplicate payment is recovered.

See a clearer version

A duplicate invoice entry is confirmed. A duplicate payment has not been established, and the ledger correction is pending.

The corrected statement removes unsupported claims about both payment and recovery.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Controller: Did the bank send money twice?
Clerk: We have confirmed a duplicate ____, not a duplicate payment.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. entry

  1. A. refund Refund implies money returned, which is unconfirmed.
  2. B. disbursement Disbursement means money paid out, the claim being corrected.
  3. C. settlement Settlement implies payment of an obligation, not the repeated record.
  4. D. entry Correct. The confirmed issue is a repeated invoice entry.
2. Colleague: Which tracker should I use?
Clerk: Version four ____ version three; the earlier version remains in the history.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. supersedes

  1. A. precedes Precedes would put version four before version three, reversing the sequence.
  2. B. matches Matches suggests equivalent content rather than identifying the current replacement.
  3. C. supersedes Correct. Supersedes identifies the newer version as the current one.
  4. D. duplicates Duplicates means repeats, not replaces as the current version.
