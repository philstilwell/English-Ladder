Lesson 01: Demand Planning and Forecast Bias
Original fictional case: Demand planner Aisha and sales lead Martin review monthly forecasts of 120, 130, and 140 units against verified demand of 100, 110, and 120. For this case, signed error means forecast minus actual demand. Next month's baseline is 120 units. Sales proposes a 30-unit promotional uplift but has no order evidence for it. Aisha and Martin must describe the three-month pattern and preserve the promotion as a scenario without treating either historical bias or future uplift as a certainty forever.
Language workshop: Three optimistic forecasts and a new promotion
Communication goal: Explain signed forecast error, distinguish bias from error size, and keep an unconfirmed promotional uplift separate from the baseline.
Language guidance: With error defined as forecast minus actual, the three errors are +20, +20, and +20 units. Total error is +60 and mean error is +20. Positive means overforecasting under this convention; other systems may reverse the sign.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): Here we define signed error as forecast minus actual demand. / Each of the three forecasts exceeded actual demand by 20 units. / Mean signed error is plus 20 units over these three months. / This pattern does not prove every future forecast will be high. / Absolute error measures the miss without its sign. / The next-month baseline remains 120 units. / Sales proposes a 30-unit promotional uplift. / That produces a 150-unit scenario, not confirmed demand. / What order or response evidence supports the proposed increment? / A future promotion does not change the earlier forecast errors. / Compare the forecast version that existed at the agreed planning point. / A sales target is not automatically an unbiased demand estimate. / Is the uplift additional demand or partly shifted from another item? / Keep the adjustment, owner, and rationale visible. / Assess whether the adjustment improves the defined forecast measure. / Present baseline, upside, and supporting evidence separately.
Vocabulary: demand forecast: An estimate of future demand for a defined item and period. / actual demand: The observed demand under the stated measurement definition. / forecast bias: A tendency for forecasts to be systematically too high or too low. / signed error: The forecast difference calculated with an explicitly stated direction. / mean error: The average signed forecast error across the defined observations. / absolute error: The size of a forecast error without its positive or negative sign. / mean absolute error: The average absolute error across the defined observations. / overforecast: A forecast greater than the corresponding actual demand. / underforecast: A forecast below the corresponding actual demand. / baseline forecast: The reference forecast before a separately identified adjustment. / promotional uplift: Additional demand expected because of a promotion. / incremental demand: Demand additional to the specified base rather than displaced from elsewhere. / cannibalization: A gain in one product's demand at the expense of another's. / seasonality: A recurring demand pattern tied to a calendar or cycle. / trend: A sustained directional movement in a series over time. / forecast horizon: The future period covered by a forecast. / forecast lag: The time between making a forecast and the period being forecast. / forecast snapshot: A saved version of the forecast at a specified time. / consensus forecast: A forecast agreed through the relevant cross-functional planning process. / demand signal: Information indicating demand behavior or potential change. / booked order: An order recorded under the organization's order definition. / scenario assumption: A premise used to construct a possible future case. / forecast override: A deliberate adjustment to a model or baseline forecast. / forecast value added: Improvement in a defined forecast measure from a specified planning step.
Speaking task: Read the corrected forecast dialogue in pairs. Stress plus twenty, mean, and absolute when comparing the historical errors. Switch roles. Read turns 9-20, distinguishing the 120-unit baseline, proposed 30-unit uplift, and 150-unit scenario. Complete and check the transfer. Read 80 minus 100 as minus 20, and keep the 105-unit next-month scenario separate from orders.
Partner: Aisha: Demand planner / Martin: Sales lead
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A forecast is 80 units and actual demand is 100. Error is defined as forecast minus actual. Next month has a 90-unit baseline and a proposed 15-unit uplift without orders.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Martin: When the next results arrive, let's evaluate the forecast version we actually used. Revising old numbers after seeing demand would make the performance comparison misleading.
Aisha: Preserve the forecast snapshot and its planning date. We need a consistent lag and definition if we want to learn whether the adjustment helped.
Martin: I will present one hundred twenty as baseline and one hundred fifty as the promotion scenario, with the evidence gap stated. The historical errors will remain unchanged.
Aisha: Good. Record the proposed forecast override with its owner and rationale. That gives the opportunity a fair assessment without disguising uncertainty or treating the scenario as a customer commitment.

Language workshop: Wednesday is still an estimate
Communication goal: Give a useful delay update while separating a carrier estimate, confirmed transport events, appointment timing, and an authorized contingency.
Language guidance: An ETA is an estimate for a stated event and place. It is not proof of departure or a guarantee that goods will be usable for a production start. A warehouse appointment and a confirmed arrival are also different events.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): The carrier currently estimates warehouse arrival on Wednesday. / Departure has not yet been confirmed. / I cannot confirm arrival before your 14:00 production start from the available information. / I understand that the uncertainty affects your production plan. / This estimate refers to your warehouse, not merely the destination terminal. / Please provide the confirmed departure event and its source. / A receiving slot is not proof that the vehicle will arrive in that slot. / We are assessing expedited routing, not reporting a completed booking. / Any premium cost needs the appropriate authorization. / Space must be confirmed before we rely on the alternative. / The next carrier status update is Tuesday at 16:00. / I will update you then even if the arrival remains uncertain. / A forecast departure is not actual departure. / The agreed trade rule does not itself guarantee Wednesday arrival. / State which milestone, estimate, or condition has changed. / Use Wednesday as the current estimate, with the departure dependency explicit.
