Lesson 01: Confirming the assignment and its limits
Original fictional case: Cleaner Maya has a commercial assignment reading conference room: tables and floor. It covers table surfaces and the floor, not high windows or personal equipment. Client Jordan asks for everything before a 3:00 meeting and points to the high windows and laptops on the tables. Supervisor Rosa can clarify and authorize any additional work through the site process. No extra work has been approved, and the deadline does not expand permission. Maya must acknowledge the meeting, name the actual scope, and arrange clarification without promising all requested tasks.
Language workshop: What does everything include?
Communication goal: Restate a defined cleaning assignment, clarify an extra request, and preserve authorization despite a deadline.
Language guidance: Before three identifies the requested deadline. It does not mean all tasks are authorized or feasible. Preserve the difference between the current assignment, an additional request, and an approved change.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): I understand the meeting starts at three. / My task list says table surfaces and the floor. / Which additional areas do you mean by everything? / High windows are not in my current assignment. / Personal devices are outside the stated scope. / I can ask Rosa to clarify the extra request. / That request has not been approved yet. / I cannot confirm all of that from this assignment. / Does the work order include that surface? / I understand the deadline; I still need approval for the extra tasks. / What is the approved arrangement for clearing the table surfaces? / Rosa can confirm whether we can add that work. / Table surfaces and floor remain the assigned tasks. / I will not give a completion time I have not checked. / I will pass on the clarified instruction. / I understand the request; I am checking the change before agreeing to it.
Vocabulary: assignment: Work specifically allocated to a worker or team. / scope of work: Defined tasks and boundaries of an assignment. / task list: Written list of required work items. / table surface: Exposed working area of a table. / floor area: Specified section of floor within the assignment. / high window: Window area requiring access above ordinary floor-level work. / personal equipment: Devices or items belonging to individual users. / client contact: Person communicating requirements for the customer organization. / site supervisor: Person overseeing assigned work at the location. / additional work: Work beyond the current assignment. / authorization: Actual permission from the relevant responsible person or process. / work order: Record describing requested or assigned work. / service specification: Description of the service and required outcome. / exclusion: Item or task explicitly outside an assignment. / deadline: Time by which a task is requested or required. / meeting setup: Arrangement of a room for a meeting. / surface access: Ability and permission to reach the relevant surface. / approved change: Change accepted through the required process. / priority: Relative importance or order of assigned tasks. / capacity: Available time, resources, and ability for the work. / completion estimate: Approximate expected finishing time. / cleanable area: Measured area included in the specified cleaning task, which may differ from total building area. / labor-hour: One hour of work by one person, distinct from elapsed clock time for a crew. / read-back: Repetition of the understood instruction for confirmation.
Speaking task: Read the scope conversation, emphasizing tables and floor versus windows and devices. Repeat Maya's request to Rosa with both extra tasks and the meeting deadline. Complete the reception scenario without treating the exterior signs as approved work.
Partner: Jordan: Client contact / Maya: Cleaner
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A work order covers reception counters and the lobby floor. The client adds exterior signs before noon. Supervisor Leon can review changes, but no extra work is approved.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Jordan: So I should not tell everyone the extra work will be finished by three?
Maya: Correct. The deadline is noted, but I have not confirmed extra work or a completion estimate for it. Rosa needs to clarify those points.
Jordan: Understood. Please ask about the windows, the laptops, and the three o'clock deadline.
Maya: That is an accurate read-back. I will take the clarification to Rosa and communicate the actual outcome, without treating the request itself as permission to proceed.

Lesson 02: Respecting access and occupied spaces
Original fictional case: At 10:00, guest Alex in room 312 declines cleaning because a call is expected to last until 11:00. Attendant Lina can return between 11:15 and 11:30 or offer fresh towels at the door now. Alex chooses the return window and does not request towels at the door. Lina must record that plan accurately without promising exactly 11:00 or treating the end of the call as permission to enter. Actual room-access procedures still apply when the attendant returns. No room entry or cleaning completion occurs in this conversation.
Language workshop: Not during the call

Dialogue 08: An inspection request is not a release decision
Original fictional setting: An attendant clarifies a room-status question from a reception colleague.
Professional roles: Reception colleague / Attendant
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Reception colleague: Room 512 shows cleaned. Can I send the waiting guest up?
Attendant: I can confirm cleaning is complete, but the supervisor's inspection hasn't been recorded as finished.
Reception colleague: I saw the inspection request sent ten minutes ago. Doesn't that mean the supervisor accepted and completed it?
Attendant: Not necessarily. I'll check whether the supervisor has accepted the inspection request.
Reception colleague: Please do. I need a reliable status rather than a guess based on how long the request has existed.
Attendant: The supervisor has accepted the inspection and is heading there now. No completion or release is confirmed yet.
Reception colleague: Thank you. I'll tell the guest we're still awaiting the final room-status update, without promising an exact minute.
Attendant: That matches what we know. Would you like me to relay the supervisor's confirmed result to your desk?
Reception colleague: Yes, please. If they find an issue needing rework, tell us that instead of using a general 'ready'.
Attendant: I will. The update will identify the inspection outcome and leave any release decision with the responsible colleague.
Reception colleague: I'll keep the guest downstairs until we hear the confirmed result from the supervisor.
Attendant: I'll call when the supervisor confirms the result.
Vocabulary in this exchange: cleaning: Removing dirt and other unwanted material from a surface. / inspection: A check of completed work against the relevant task requirements. / rework: Work repeated or corrected because the first result didn't meet the specified requirement.
