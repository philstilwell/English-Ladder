Lesson 01: Strategy Role, Problem Framing, and Executive Diagnosis
Original fictional case: Executive sponsor Daniel asks strategy lead Rina for a growth strategy. Last year the business reported $20 million revenue and $2 million operating profit. The sponsor has not chosen whether the primary objective is revenue growth, operating-profit improvement, or entry into a new market. No target, horizon, or spending limit has been agreed. A workshop is scheduled for Monday. Rina must establish the decision question and missing inputs rather than assume that an acquisition or a 20% revenue target has already been authorized.
Language workshop: Growth is an ambition, not yet a clear assignment
Communication goal: Clarify an executive mandate before selecting a growth option or commissioning broad analysis.
Language guidance: Revenue and operating profit are different measures. The historical figures imply a 10% operating margin on the stated basis, but do not tell the team which outcome to prioritize. A growth label should not silently become a revenue target, acquisition mandate, or permission to spend.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): Which outcome should the growth strategy prioritize? / Last year revenue was $20 million and operating profit was $2 million. / Revenue growth and profit improvement can point to different choices. / Over what period should we evaluate the result? / We have not yet agreed a spending limit or scope. / Acquisition is an option to assess, not the mandate itself. / Who will approve the objective and the final recommendation? / Let us confirm the mandate before Monday's options discussion. / What evidence explains the gap between current performance and the desired outcome? / I will label that premise as unverified until its owner confirms it. / We should test the cause before designing the response. / Which questions would actually change the decision? / A 20% revenue figure has not been approved as the goal. / The initial option set should not assume one route is inevitable. / We have a historical baseline, but no agreed target or horizon. / Does this question capture the choice you need the team to support?
Vocabulary: strategic mandate: The authorized purpose and boundaries of a strategy assignment. / problem statement: A precise description of the issue to be addressed. / decision question: The choice the analysis is intended to support. / executive sponsor: The senior person accountable for backing and directing an initiative. / objective: The outcome an effort is intended to achieve. / target: A specified desired level of performance or outcome. / baseline: The starting reference against which a change is assessed. / time horizon: The period over which a decision or outcome is evaluated. / scope boundary: A limit on what the assignment includes. / constraint: A condition restricting feasible choices. / diagnosis: An evidence-based explanation of the central problem or challenge. / symptom: An observed sign that may have more than one underlying cause. / hypothesis: A proposition requiring testing against evidence. / issue tree: A structured breakdown of a problem into questions. / driver analysis: Examination of factors contributing to an observed result. / evidence gap: Missing information needed to support a conclusion. / assumption register: A record of premises, evidence status, and owners. / fact base: The verified information supporting an analysis. / stakeholder map: A description of relevant parties and their interests or roles. / alignment: Shared understanding of the relevant goal or decision. / decision rights: The authority to make or approve specified choices. / success criterion: A condition used to determine whether the effort meets its goal. / strategic option: A distinct possible course of action. / recommendation: A proposed course of action supported by reasoning.
Speaking task: Check the cloze key, then read the sponsor discussion. State $20 million revenue and $2 million operating profit as last year's baseline, not next year's targets. Switch roles for the mandate questions. Preserve the unresolved objective, horizon, scope, spending limit, and decision rights instead of inventing answers. Complete and check the overseas-office exchange. Contrast an option with an objective, and retain the absence of lease authority.
Partner: Daniel: Executive sponsor / Rina: Strategy lead
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A sponsor proposes opening an overseas office but has not stated the intended business outcome or budget. The team has no authority to sign a lease.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Daniel: I should confirm the outcome and limits, then the team can compare routes. Who else needs a decision role should be explicit as well.
Rina: Yes. Clear decision rights will prevent a workshop preference from being reported as authorization. We can name the sponsor decisions separately from the analytical work assigned to the team.
Daniel: Send me a one-page mandate before Monday. Leave the target open for my decision; the twenty-percent figure mentioned earlier was not an agreement.
Rina: I will mark the open success criteria and frame the decision for confirmation. The workshop can then compare options against the same objective instead of debating several different meanings of growth.
END REFERENCE

Lesson 02: Strategic Choices: Ambition, Where to Play, How to Win, and Tradeoffs
Original fictional case: For the next quarter, a service business has 160 specialist-weeks available after existing commitments.
