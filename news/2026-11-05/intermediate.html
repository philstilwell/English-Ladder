📅 November 05, 2026 - Bank of England Expected to Increase Interest Rates

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The Bank of England is expected to increase its main interest rate on Thursday, for the first time in over three years. Most analysts predict a rise from 3.75% to 4%, making loans more expensive for individuals and businesses while helping savers. The rate is below its 2023–24 peak of 5.25%, and it has stayed the same at the last six meetings. Analysts suggest that inflation, or rising prices, driven mainly by petrol and diesel costs will lead to a rate increase. The annual inflation rate reached 3.5% in September 2026, compared with 3.1% the previous month and the Bank's 2% target. The Bank's main rate influences both the cost of home loans, called mortgages, and the interest paid on savings. If the Bank rate rose, it would have an immediate impact on 510,000 people with tracker mortgages, which follow changes in that rate. For someone with a typical tracker mortgage, the expected rise would add £31 to monthly repayments, according to UK Finance. Lenders and the Bank must judge the UK's economic outlook, which is closely connected to international events.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. analysts (plural noun): People who study information carefully to explain situations and predict changes.
2. peak (noun): The highest level reached during a particular period.
3. driven mainly by (verb phrase): Caused mostly by a particular thing.
4. annual (adjective): Measured over a period of one year.
5. influences (verb, present simple): Affects how something develops or what happens to it.
6. impact (noun): An effect that an event or change has on someone or something.
7. repayments (plural noun): Amounts of money paid back regularly to cover a loan.
8. outlook (noun): The situation that people expect in the future.

Grammar Focus: Second conditional for an imagined future situation
Use “if” plus the past simple and “would” plus the base form of a verb to describe an imagined situation and its result. Here, the past form presents a possible future rate rise as imagined; it does not mean the rise has already happened.
Example from the text: "If the Bank rate rose, it would have an immediate impact on 510,000 people with tracker mortgages, which follow changes in that rate."
