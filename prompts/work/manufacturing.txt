Lesson 01: Production Flow and Daily Management
Original fictional case: Production lead Mei and operations manager Daniel review yesterday's line output: 840 completed units against a 1,000-unit target. The log records 40 minutes of downtime, but the cause and its contribution to the shortfall are still being checked. The briefing supplies no planned production time, ideal cycle time, or quality breakdown. A dashboard labels 84% as overall equipment effectiveness. Mei and Daniel must correct the metric and give a useful update without claiming the downtime explains every missing unit.
Language workshop: Eight hundred forty is not the whole story
Communication goal: Report an output shortfall precisely while separating observed downtime, unconfirmed causes, and different performance measures.
Language guidance: 840 divided by 1,000 is 84% target attainment. The shortfall is 160 units, or 16% of target. Neither result supplies OEE, availability, or first-pass yield. Those measures require their own defined inputs and calculation bases.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): We completed 840 units against a target of 1,000. / The shortfall is 160 units, or 16% of target. / Eighty-four percent is output target attainment. / The supplied figures do not support an OEE calculation. / The log records 40 minutes of downtime. / The reason for that downtime is still being checked. / We have not established how much of the shortfall the stop explains. / What planned production time was used in this calculation? / Throughput needs a quantity and a time period. / We have not yet confirmed which step limited total output. / Separate speed, availability, and quality information. / The current log does not establish an operator cause. / Report the known gap and the next evidence check separately. / A recovery proposal is not a guarantee that the missing output will be recovered. / Mei will reconcile the log and report the supported loss breakdown. / Keep the metric definition beside the percentage.
Vocabulary: production target: The planned output quantity for a defined period. / actual output: The quantity actually produced in the stated period. / target attainment: Actual output expressed relative to the stated target. / shortfall: The amount by which a result falls below its target. / throughput: The quantity passing through a process per unit of time. / cycle time: The elapsed time required for a defined process cycle. / takt time: Available production time divided by required demand for the same period. / lead time: The elapsed time from a defined request or start to completion. / work in process: Material that has entered production but is not yet finished. / bottleneck: The step that limits the output of the overall process. / constraint: A limiting factor on the system's performance. / line balance: Distribution of work across production steps. / downtime: Time during which equipment or a process is not producing as defined. / planned stop: A scheduled interruption included in the operating plan. / unplanned stop: An interruption not included in the intended operating schedule. / availability: The share of planned production time in which equipment is running. / performance loss: Output-speed loss relative to the defined ideal running rate. / quality loss: Output that does not meet the defined good-product criteria. / overall equipment effectiveness: Availability multiplied by performance and quality, with defined factors; abbreviated OEE. / daily management: Routine review of performance, problems, and follow-up actions. / tier meeting: A short escalation meeting at a defined organizational level. / visual board: A shared display of current measures, status, and actions. / loss attribution: Assignment of a measured loss to supported contributing factors. / recovery plan: A defined proposal for addressing a performance or schedule gap.
Speaking task: Read the production review. State 840 against 1,000, 84% target attainment, and a 160-unit shortfall. Swap roles. Keep the forty-minute stop separate from its unconfirmed cause and output effect; do not label 84% as OEE. Read the corrected 450-unit transfer. State ninety-percent attainment and a fifty-unit gap without inventing a stop cause.
Partner: Mei: Production lead / Daniel: Operations manager
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A line produces 450 units against a target of 500. It logs 20 minutes of downtime. No cause or OEE inputs are supplied.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Daniel: For today's meeting, I need a concise update: result, known interruption, open questions, and responsibility for the next check. We can discuss recovery after that evidence is clearer.
Mei: I will update the visual board accordingly. It will show the output gap and logged stop separately, with me responsible for reconciling the records and reporting the supported breakdown.
Daniel: Good. We can acknowledge the missed target without guessing why it happened. Any proposal to recover output should make its assumptions and constraints clear.
Mei: Agreed. The recovery plan will remain a proposal until assessed and authorized. First, the board must use the right measure and keep the unfinished investigation visible.
END REFERENCE

2. After I choose, identify who you will play and the immediate communication goal. Play the other professional; where a meeting requires a third person, label each of your speakers clearly. 3. Let the exchange develop across six to ten learner turns, or end earlier if I type "feedback". Respond to what I actually say. Give up to three focused suggestions. Ask me to retry the weakest turn; wait. Only then offer an alternative wording and a harder replay.
Use voice replies when available. If I prefer selections, offer four plausible spoken replies and explain my choice before continuing. Do not require written compositions.

Language workshop: One thousand units, three different states
Communication goal: Reconcile output categories and explain yield, pending recovery, and scrap without double-counting or assuming a rework outcome.
Language guidance: 900 first-pass units plus 60 awaiting rework plus 40 scrap equals 1,000 inspected units. First-pass yield is 90%; approved scrap is 4% of this population. The remaining 6% is pending rework, not already recovered output.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): The report covers 1,000 inspected units from this batch. / Nine hundred met requirements on the first inspection. / Sixty units await authorized rework. / Forty units have approved scrap disposition. / These three exclusive states account for the full batch. / The 100 non-first-pass units are not all scrap. / If all 60 pass after rework, the accepted total would be 960. / No reworked units have been accepted yet. / First-pass yield is 90% of the inspected population. / The approved scrap quantity is 4% of these 1,000 units. / Two defects on one item are not two defective units. / Move accepted reworked units between states rather than add them twice. / Scrap cost and rework labor are different cost categories. / A rework plan does not remove the need for the required acceptance decision. / Mark 960 as conditional recovery, not actual good output. / Show current accepted, pending, and scrap quantities separately.
