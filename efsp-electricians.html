01Taking a precise electrical service requestLocate the report without expanding it · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Turn a broad complaint into an accurate service-call description.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A client reports that the office sockets are dead. Their account concerns two desk receptacles in Room 204, noticed at 08:30. Other areas have not been assessed. A panel directory says office, but its accuracy has not been verified. Clarify the service request without asking the client to test or operate anything.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

service call
A visit requested to assess or address a reported electrical issue.
receptacle
The electrical contact device into which a plug is inserted; commonly called a socket or outlet.
distribution board
An assembly containing devices that distribute and protect electrical circuits; also called a panelboard in some settings.
circuit identifier
A label or reference used to distinguish a particular electrical circuit in project records.

Useful word combinations

  • locate the reported issue: identify the room and device concerned
  • confirm the circuit reference: distinguish a label from a verified identification
  • describe the symptom: state what was noticed without diagnosing it
  • take a service history: ask when the issue occurred and what was reported

C Notice the language

Locate the report without expanding it

Ask about existing observations, room labels, and timing. A client report is not a verified circuit finding. Do not direct the client to operate equipment, repeat a fault, open an enclosure, or perform electrical checks.

  • Which ___ are you referring to?
  • You noticed ___ at ___; is that right?
  • That label suggests ___, but identification remains ___.

Improve this: "The whole office circuit has failed."

See a clearer version

The client reports an issue at two desk receptacles in Room 204; the circuit is not yet verified.

The report names its source and limits its extent.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Client: I mean the devices that receive the desk plugs, not the lighting controls.
Electrician: You are referring to the two ____ in Room 204.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. receptacles

  1. A. switches Switches control circuits or devices rather than receiving those desk plugs.
  2. B. junction boxes Junction boxes house wiring connections; they are not the described plug-in devices.
  3. C. luminaires Luminaires are lighting units, not devices that receive desk plugs.
  4. D. receptacles Correct. Receptacles receive the plugs supplying the desk equipment.
2. Dispatcher: Has the whole floor been assessed?
Electrician: No, the report ____ two desk receptacles only.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. concerns

  1. A. rules out The report describes those receptacles; it does not exclude them.
  2. B. depends of The correct expression is 'depends on', which would still change the meaning.
  3. C. concerns Correct. 'Concerns' identifies the limited subject of the report.
  4. D. confirms all of This would turn a limited report into a broader confirmation and fits the sentence poorly.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Which office sockets?

An electrician clarifies the fictional Room 204 service call by phone.

  1. Electrician: This service call says office sockets. Which room and devices do you mean?
  2. Client: The two desk receptacles in Room 204. I noticed the problem at eight thirty.
  3. Electrician: Thank you. Have you already observed anything elsewhere, without doing any further checks?
  4. Client: No, I only know about those two. Someone mentioned the whole floor.
  5. Electrician: I'll keep that broader suggestion separate from what you actually reported to us.
  6. Client: The distribution board's directory says office. Doesn't that confirm the circuit identifier?
  7. Electrician: It gives us a reference, but the circuit identification still needs verification.
  8. Client: Should I send you a photograph of the room label from the corridor?
  9. Electrician: Yes, if it's available without approaching equipment; no electrical investigation is needed from you.
  10. Client: I'll send that existing photograph and leave the service description limited to my observations.
2. Outlet means different things to different people

An electrician clarifies a shop manager's request about a wall device.

  1. Electrician: You asked us to relocate an outlet. Which item on this elevation do you mean?
  2. Shop manager: The round fitting above the display, where the light comes out near the sign.
  3. Electrician: That's a light fitting rather than a receptacle. Are you requesting a position change?
  4. Shop manager: Yes, the new sign would cover it. I used outlet because I couldn't name it.
  5. Electrician: No problem. We'll identify the fitting by its drawing tag so the request is clear.
  6. Shop manager: The tag is L7, and the sign company has a revised elevation available.
  7. Electrician: Please send that elevation with the request; a photograph alone won't define the new position.
  8. Shop manager: Can you price it now, or do you need the proposed location first?
  9. Electrician: We need the location and a review of the affected work before quoting.
  10. Shop manager: Then I'll obtain the elevation and describe this as relocating fitting L7, not a socket.
3. Yesterday's account is secondhand

An electrician checks the source of an intermittent complaint with a caretaker.

  1. Electrician: The service note says the lobby lights flickered yesterday. Did you see that yourself?
  2. Caretaker: No, the evening receptionist told me. I wasn't on site during that shift.
  3. Electrician: Do you know the time or which fittings they were describing in the lobby?
  4. Caretaker: They said around six, near the reception counter, but I don't have exact identifiers.
  5. Electrician: I'll record those details as their report and keep the locations approximate for now.
  6. Caretaker: Would it help if I asked them to clarify what they already noticed?
  7. Electrician: Yes, ask about the time and location, not for them to reproduce the issue.
  8. Caretaker: Understood. I'll also check whether there is an earlier service reference for those fittings.
  9. Electrician: That would help connect the history without assuming the same cause has returned.
  10. Caretaker: I'll send the reference if I find it, clearly separate from yesterday's secondhand account.

A business has Room 12 in both buildings A and B. A service request names Room 12 and a desk receptacle; the client confirms building B and a west-facing window. No circuit is identified.

Role A: Electrician: clarify the building and room without inventing a circuit assignment.

Role B: Client: confirm building B, Room 12 beside the west-facing window, and reception contact Dana.

  • Correct the appointment location verbally.

02Clarifying lighting specificationsSeparate appearance from light output · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Explain lighting units without converting them into an unsupported design recommendation.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A client selects two fictional luminaires, both listed at 1,200 lumens. One is 3,000 kelvins and the other 4,000 kelvins. The client assumes the larger kelvin number means more light. No product substitution is approved; driver compatibility remains for specialist review.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

luminaire
A complete lighting unit, including its light source or sources and associated components.
color temperature
A description, expressed in kelvins, of how warm or cool a light source appears.
lumen
A unit expressing the amount of visible light emitted by a source.
driver
An electronic component that supplies and controls electrical power for a compatible light source, commonly an LED.

Useful word combinations

  • compare the lighting schedule: check listed fitting requirements
  • confirm the color temperature: specify the requested warm or cool appearance
  • check the light output: distinguish emitted light from electrical power consumption
  • verify driver compatibility: obtain confirmation that the specified components work together

C Notice the language

Separate appearance from light output

Lumens describe emitted light; kelvins describe appearance. Neither figure alone establishes suitability, installed brightness, or compatibility. Explain the supplied comparison and leave selection and technical review distinct.

  • ___ describes ___, not ___ .
  • Both are rated at ___, while ___ differs.
  • That figure alone does not confirm ___.

Improve this: "Four thousand means it is brighter."

See a clearer version

Four thousand kelvins describes a cooler appearance, not a higher lumen output.

The correction separates two different properties.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Client: Which figure tells me how much visible light the source emits?
Electrician: Look at its stated ____ rating.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. lumen

  1. A. watt Watts describe electrical power, not light output directly.
  2. B. volt Volts describe electrical potential difference, not emitted light output.
  3. C. lumen Correct. Lumens measure the amount of emitted visible light.
  4. D. kelvin Kelvins describe color temperature, not the amount of emitted light.
2. Client: So four thousand kelvins is more light?
Electrician: It describes appearance ____ quantity of light.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. rather than

  1. A. in addition to This also incorrectly adds light quantity to what kelvins describe.
  2. B. rather than Correct. The contrast corrects the client's confusion.
  3. C. as well as This incorrectly says the figure describes both properties.
  4. D. because of This claims a causal relationship instead of distinguishing properties.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Kelvins are not lumens

An electrician compares the client's two proposed luminaires.

  1. Electrician: Both luminaires are listed at twelve hundred lumens. Their color temperatures are different.
  2. Client: I thought the four-thousand-kelvin one would give us more light than the three-thousand one.
  3. Electrician: The kelvin figure describes warm or cool appearance, not the amount of light emitted.
  4. Client: So the four-thousand option looks cooler, while their stated output is the same?
  5. Electrician: Yes. That's the comparison these figures support, without settling the room's lighting design.
  6. Client: Will equal lumens guarantee that the desk looks equally bright under either fitting?
  7. Electrician: No, the fitting's distribution and the room arrangement also matter to that assessment.
  8. Client: Then I'd like the designer to compare their appearance and suitability for this desk.
  9. Electrician: I'll include both data sheets and keep driver compatibility as a separate review point.
  10. Client: Good. My preference is cooler light, but that isn't approval of an unchecked substitution.
2. The fitting code has a suffix

An electrician checks a supplier's proposed lighting delivery.

  1. Electrician: The delivery note lists Lumenia forty B, while our approved schedule says forty D.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

centerline
An imaginary line through a component's center used as a positioning reference.
finished floor level
The level of the completed floor surface used as a reference for stated heights.
mounting height
The specified vertical position of a device, requiring a stated measurement reference.
reflected ceiling plan
A drawing representing the ceiling layout as if viewed in a mirror below it.

Useful word combinations

  • confirm the height reference: identify the datum and measured point
  • coordinate with the joinery elevation: compare device positions with cabinet details
  • mark the intended location: identify a proposed position for review
  • reconcile the ceiling layout: resolve conflicting fitting positions on drawings

C Notice the language

Ask for the point as well as the number

A height can refer to the center, edge, or another identified point. State the floor reference and device point separately. Drawings used in an exercise are not placement rules or accessibility standards.

  • Is ___ measured to the centerline or ___?
  • The floor reference is ___; the device reference is ___ .
  • These drawings agree on ___ but differ on ___.

Improve this: "It's at eleven hundred, just like the cabinet."

See a clearer version

The height is 1,100 millimeters, but the device measurement point needs clarification.

The revision avoids treating matching numbers as matching positions.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

connected load
The total rated electrical demand of connected equipment, distinct from any assessed simultaneous demand.
dedicated circuit
A circuit intended to serve specified equipment or a particular purpose rather than general shared use.
substitution
A proposed replacement for a specified product or component, requiring the relevant review.
change quotation
A priced proposal for an alteration to the agreed electrical work, with its stated inclusions and conditions.

Useful word combinations

  • obtain the equipment schedule: request a list of proposed equipment and ratings
  • review the added demand: ask for assessment of the proposed equipment's electrical needs
  • price a scope change: quote defined additional work
  • compare the full specification: examine more than appearance or model family

C Notice the language

Offer assessment without promising capacity

Appearance and socket shape do not establish electrical suitability. Ask for manufacturer information and intended use, then separate technical assessment, price, and approval. Do not calculate capacities or prescribe circuit arrangements.

  • The addition changes ___, so we need ___ .
  • We can assess ___ once ___ is available.

Your goal

Hand over documents and follow-up responsibilities without implying all checks are complete.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

At a fictional office handover, the as-built drawings are received. One circuit-directory entry still says old store although the room is now archive. The identification needs verification, not relabeling by assumption. Electrical lead Priya accepts follow-up and will report Thursday. No operational clearance is inferred.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

circuit directory
A labeled list intended to identify circuits and the areas or equipment they serve.
as-built drawing
A drawing intended to record the electrical installation as completed, including documented changes.
commissioning record
A record of specified verification activities and results associated with bringing an installation into its intended service.
outstanding item
A specific unresolved task, observation, or document requirement retained for follow-up.

Useful word combinations

  • reconcile the circuit directory: check the list against confirmed installation records
  • match the equipment reference: link a document to the correct asset
  • identify outstanding documents: name records still missing or incomplete
  • accept the follow-up: confirm who will obtain and report the unresolved information

C Notice the language

Transfer an unresolved record accurately

Separate received, reviewed, and reconciled. A room-name change may expose a documentation mismatch without proving the underlying circuit identity. Do not instruct clients to relabel, operate, or investigate electrical equipment.

  • We have received ___; ___ remains unresolved.
  • ___ will verify ___ and report ___ .
  • Please confirm your understanding of ___.

Improve this: "The handover pack is complete, so all labels are right."

See a clearer version

The drawings are received; one directory entry still needs verification.

The revision distinguishes document receipt from verified identification.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Client: Can we treat the old-store entry as a verified archive reference?
Electrician: Not yet. We need ____ to establish that identity.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. a check against the confirmed installation record

  1. A. a check against the confirmed installation record Correct. Reconciliation with confirmed installation information can establish the identity.
  2. B. a copy of the unchanged directory An unchanged copy preserves the information but does not verify it.
  3. C. a room-name change based only on recollection Recollection alone does not confirm which circuit the entry identifies.
  4. D. a new number matching the room number Matching a room number changes labeling without verifying the circuit.
2. Client: Who owns the follow-up?
Electrician: Priya ____ responsibility for the directory query.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. has accepted

  1. A. might have accepting After 'might have', use a past participle; this also weakens the known acceptance.
  2. B. has been accept The passive form would require 'accepted' and a different meaning.
  3. C. is accept 'Is accept' is not a valid verb phrase.
  4. D. has accepted Correct. Present perfect describes the confirmed acceptance.
