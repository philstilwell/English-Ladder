Title: Nobel Laureate Dmitry Muratov: Putin Cannot Claim Victory in Ukraine

Nobel Peace Prize winner Dmitry Muratov has stated that Russian President Vladimir Putin cannot achieve victory in Ukraine but can only destroy the country, with the conflict now in its fifth year.

Read the story

Dmitry Muratov, a distinguished Nobel Peace Prize winner, recently shared his strong views regarding the ongoing conflict in Ukraine. He spoke during an exclusive interview with the BBC’s Steve Rosenberg, offering critical insights into the war. Muratov firmly believes that President Putin can no longer claim victory in Ukraine, despite Russia's military efforts. The devastating conflict has now continued for a considerable period, entering its fifth year. According to Muratov's assessment, the objective of the Kremlin leader has fundamentally changed. He argues that Putin's forces are now primarily focused on the destruction of Ukraine. Muratov emphasizes that it is impossible for Russia to truly conquer the nation through military means. His perspective highlights the immense human cost and the unlikelihood of a decisive Russian triumph. The Russian journalist's comments underline the devastating nature of modern warfare and its impact. These statements provide an important voice against the conflict and its declared aims.

Words and grammar

1. Nobel Peace Prize winner (noun phrase): A person who has received the prestigious international award for outstanding contributions to peace.
2. exclusive interview (noun phrase): A discussion with a person, published or broadcast only by one media organization.
3. claim victory (verb phrase): To state officially or publicly that one has won a battle, game, or competition.
4. Kremlin leader (noun phrase): A common way to refer to the President of Russia, as the Kremlin is the official residence and workplace of the Russian head of state.
5. conquer (verb): To take control of a country or city by force, usually with an army; to defeat an enemy or opponent.

Grammar Focus: Reporting Verbs
Reporting verbs are used to convey what someone else has said or thought. Common reporting verbs include 'say,' 'tell,' 'believe,' 'argue,' and 'emphasize.' They introduce either direct speech (e.g., He said, 'It's late.') or indirect speech (e.g., He said that it was late.), often leading to changes in tense and pronouns in indirect speech.
Example from the text: "Muratov firmly believes that President Putin can no longer claim victory in Ukraine, despite Russia's military efforts."
