01Confirming the assignment and its limitsTurn 'everything' into named tasks · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Clarify the requested surfaces and the boundary of the current assignment.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A commercial assignment says 'conference room: tables and floor.' The client asks for 'everything' before a 3:00 meeting. The worker is assigned table surfaces and the floor, not high windows or personal equipment. The supervisor can clarify extra work; the meeting time doesn't expand permission.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

assignment sheet
A list identifying the rooms, areas, or cleaning tasks allocated to a worker.
service scope
The specific cleaning work included in an assignment or agreement.
touchpoint
A surface or item frequently touched by people, such as a handle.
spot cleaning
Cleaning a particular marked or affected area rather than the entire surface.

Useful word combinations

  • confirm the assigned area: identify exactly which space belongs to the task
  • clarify the service scope: check which work is included
  • prioritize the requested room: agree which assigned space comes first
  • distinguish a spot from the whole surface: define the extent of a cleaning request

C Notice the language

Turn 'everything' into named tasks

A broad request may contain extra work or items the cleaner should not move. Name the included work and ask about the rest.

  • My assignment includes ___, but not ___.
  • When you say ___, which surfaces do you mean?
  • I'll ask the supervisor whether ___ can be added.

Improve this: "I'll clean everything."

See a clearer version

I'll clean the listed tables and floor; the additional items need a scope check.

The revised statement gives a precise commitment.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Client: By everything, I also meant the high windows.
Cleaner: Those tasks are ____ my current assignment.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. outside

  1. A. outside Correct. The current assignment covers tables and floor, not high windows.
  2. B. included in 'Included in' would incorrectly claim the extra work was assigned.
  3. C. covered by 'Covered by' would also include work outside the stated scope.
  4. D. within 'Within' would put those extra tasks inside the listed scope.
2. Supervisor: Is the client asking for the whole carpet to be cleaned?
Cleaner: No, just one marked patch. The request is for ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. spot cleaning

  1. A. full carpet cleaning Full carpet cleaning extends beyond the patch the client identified.
  2. B. spot cleaning Correct. Spot cleaning targets the particular marked area.
  3. C. whole-room vacuuming This covers the whole room rather than the one patch.
  4. D. a general carpet inspection Inspection assesses the carpet; the request specifically asks for cleaning.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Everything has a boundary

The conference-room assignment in the case.

  1. Client: Could you clean everything in this conference room before our three o'clock meeting?
  2. Cleaner: My assignment sheet lists tables and floor. Which other items did you mean?
  3. Client: The high windows look marked, and there are laptops on some of the tables.
  4. Cleaner: Those aren't included in my task list. I can ask my supervisor about the additional request.
  5. Client: Please put the tables and floor first. The meeting can't move to another room.
  6. Cleaner: I can prioritize that assigned work, but I won't handle laptops without the site's instructions.
  7. Client: We'll ask their owners to clear them. Can you report any table area you couldn't reach?
  8. Cleaner: Yes. I'll describe any inaccessible surfaces rather than mark the whole room complete.
  9. Client: And please ask about the windows without promising they'll be finished before the meeting.
  10. Cleaner: I will. The listed table and floor work remains my assignment; the windows need review.
2. A spot or the whole carpet?

A cleaner clarifies a client's request about a marked carpet.

  1. Cleaner: You asked me to address the carpet. Do you mean this patch or the entire room?
  2. Client: This small patch beside the door is the issue. The rest is on the usual schedule.
  3. Cleaner: Thank you. I'll report a spot-cleaning request for that patch, not a whole-room treatment.
  4. Client: Can you guarantee the mark will disappear before the visitors arrive this afternoon?
  5. Cleaner: I can't promise that outcome. I'll ask the supervisor about the suitable work and timing.
  6. Client: Please tell them it's near the entrance, where visitors will see it first.
  7. Cleaner: I'll give the exact location and note that you haven't requested the whole carpet today.
  8. Client: That's right. I want the particular mark assessed without changing the full cleaning schedule.
  9. Cleaner: Is the visitor arrival time two o'clock, as shown on the room booking?
  10. Client: Yes, two o'clock. Please use that time when you discuss the request with your supervisor.
3. Touchpoints in the corridor

A team member clarifies the location of a daily task.

  1. Cleaner: The sheet says corridor touchpoints. Does that include the handles inside the private offices?
  2. Team lead: Today's assignment covers shared corridor handles and railings, not entry into the private offices.
  3. Cleaner: Thanks. I wanted to check the boundary before assuming the office doors were included.
  4. Team lead: The shared-side handles are included, but office access is a separate arrangement for another shift.
  5. Cleaner: I'll describe the task as the shared corridor touchpoints, with no private-office entry.
  6. Team lead: Exactly. Please also identify which corridor section you're covering so we don't duplicate the work.
  7. Cleaner: I'll take the east corridor between the lifts and the meeting-room entrance this shift.
  8. Team lead: Then I'll assign the west corridor to Noor and leave the private-office list separate.
  9. Cleaner: If a tenant asks me to add an office, I'll refer that extra request to you.
  10. Team lead: Please do. We can clarify access and scope before changing either worker's assignment.

F Say it

Two scenarios to practice

Scenario 1 · Confirming the assignment and its limits

A commercial assignment says 'conference room: tables and floor.' The client asks for 'everything' before a 3:00 meeting. The worker is assigned table surfaces and the floor, not high windows or personal equipment. The supervisor can clarify extra work; the meeting time doesn't expand permission.

Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.

Partner's role and follow-up

Client: you want table surfaces and floor ready first; windows are an extra request.

Try a harder second round

The client adds laptops to 'tables.' Clarify that cleaning around personal equipment differs from handling it.

Scenario 2 · Furniture is not part of the floor request

A client asks for a meeting-room floor cleaned but heavy display units block one corner. The cleaner isn't assigned to move them. The supervisor can review access.

Role A: Cleaner: explain the inaccessible corner.

Role B: Client: arrange staff assistance or accept a recorded limitation.

Possible opening: “I can reach the open floor, but that corner is blocked by the display units.”

Success checks
  • Name the blocked area.
  • Avoid assuming permission or ability to move heavy units.
  • Agree who resolves access and how completion is described.
Add a complication

The client assumes 'floor cleaning' includes moving all furniture.

Studying alone? Speak both roles aloud. For each scenario, prepare for two minutes, speak for one minute, then answer a follow-up. Switch roles and repeat using fewer notes. Use only the supplied facts; identify missing information instead of inventing it.

Compare scenario 1 with a model response
The assignment covers the conference-room table surfaces and floor. When you say everything, are you also asking about the high windows and personal equipment? Those aren't included in my current task list. I can complete the listed work and ask my supervisor to review the additional request before anyone promises it for three o'clock.

This is one possible spoken response, not the only acceptable wording. Preserve the case facts when you try your own version.

Apply it to this case

Check your reasoning

Compare with the case above and the model response. Can you point to the words that support your factual statement? Is your question about something the case leaves open? If you introduce a possible outcome, clearly label it as a possibility.

Review, then try again

  • Keeps the case facts accurate; clearly separates confirmed and unknown information.
  • Uses understandable sentences and explains specialist terms when the listener needs it.
  • Responds to the other person's question, checks understanding, and uses an appropriate tone.
  • Makes the requested action or unresolved question clear without inventing authority or facts.

Revise one sentence and repeat the response. A useful response can be clear even with a few grammar errors; judge meaning and task completion, not accent.

02Respecting access and occupied spacesOffer a return window without overriding privacy · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Agree a practical service choice with an occupant.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A guest in room 312 declines cleaning at 10:00 because of a call until 11:00. The attendant's return window is 11:15-11:30. The guest can choose a visit in that window or fresh towels at the door now. No room entry is authorized by this conversation alone.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

do-not-disturb notice
A sign communicating an occupant's request not to be disturbed; entry rules remain site-specific.
occupied room
A room currently in use by a guest or other occupant.
access window
An agreed period during which a space can be entered for assigned work.
service refusal
An occupant's decision to decline an offered cleaning visit or task.

Useful word combinations

  • request permission to enter: ask before entering where permission is required
  • agree a return time: arrange when to offer service again
  • respect a declined service: accept the occupant's stated choice
  • confirm the access window: verify when the room is available for work

C Notice the language

Offer a return window without overriding privacy

A return appointment and permission to enter are separate. State the available window instead of promising an exact minute you can't control.

  • Would ___ or ___ work better?
  • I can return between ___ and ___.
  • Would you prefer no room service today?

Improve this: "I'll come in after eleven."

See a clearer version

I can return between eleven fifteen and eleven thirty; would that suit you?

The guest gets a choice rather than an announced entry.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Guest: Your return is between eleven fifteen and eleven thirty?
Attendant: Yes, that's a ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. return window

  1. A. return window Correct. A window gives a range of times.
  2. B. completed visit The visit has not yet taken place.
  3. C. guaranteed exact minute A fifteen-minute interval is not one guaranteed minute.
  4. D. service refusal The guest is checking an offered visit, not refusing it.
2. Attendant: Would you prefer fresh towels ____ room cleaning today?
Guest: Yes, towels only.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. instead of

  1. A. along with This keeps both services rather than towels alone.
  2. B. instead of Correct. The guest chooses towels as the replacement service.
  3. C. as well as This would request both services, unlike 'towels only'.
  4. D. in addition to This adds cleaning rather than replacing it.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. A service choice at the door

Room 312 at 10:00, following the case.

  1. Attendant: Good morning. Is now a convenient time to clean your room, or should I return?
  2. Guest: I'm on a work call until eleven, so I can't have the room cleaned now.
  3. Attendant: I can return between eleven fifteen and eleven thirty, or bring towels to the door now.
  4. Guest: Towels only would be better today. I don't need a later cleaning visit.
  5. Attendant: Certainly. How many fresh towels would you like delivered without entering the room?
  6. Guest: Two bath towels, please. I can take them at the door once you return.
  7. Attendant: I'll bring two and record that you declined room cleaning today, not all service.
  8. Guest: Thank you. Please don't interpret my declining cleaning as permission to enter after the call.
  9. Attendant: Understood. I'll leave the room entry unchanged and knock when I bring the towels.
  10. Guest: That works. I can briefly answer the door for them without interrupting the whole call.
2. A meeting runs beyond the access window

A cleaner negotiates access with an office occupant.

  1. Cleaner: Our access window starts at six, but I can see your meeting is still running.
  2. Office occupant: It may take another twenty minutes. Could you start cleaning around us while we finish?
  3. Cleaner: I need to check whether occupied cleaning is suitable under this site's arrangement.
  4. Office occupant: Then could you work on the corridor first and return after we leave?
  5. Cleaner: The corridor is on my assignment, so I can change that order of work.
  6. Office occupant: Thank you. We expect to leave by six twenty, but I can't guarantee that yet.
  7. Cleaner: I'll return to ask at six twenty rather than assume the room has become available.
  8. Office occupant: Please do. I'll tell the team someone will check, not announce cleaning as already started.
  9. Cleaner: If you're still here, I'll report the access delay and discuss the remaining window with my lead.
  10. Office occupant: That sounds reasonable. You can complete the corridor while we finish the meeting.
3. A sign and a verbal message conflict

An attendant asks the supervisor about contradictory room-access information.

Your goal

Describe missing product information and request qualified help.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A bottle on the cart has an unreadable label. A colleague identifies it by color, but two products use similar bottles. The cleaner has not used it. The supervisor can identify it and provide the relevant product information or a clearly identified replacement. No chemical steps are taught.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

product label
The information attached to a cleaning product identifying it and its stated use and warnings.
Safety Data Sheet
A document describing a chemical product's hazards and related safety information; often shortened to SDS.
cleaning
Removing dirt and other unwanted material from a surface.
disinfection
A process intended to inactivate specified microorganisms under the product's stated conditions; not synonymous with ordinary cleaning.

Useful word combinations

  • identify the product: confirm the exact container and product name
  • consult the current label: use information for the product actually present
  • ask for the task standard: clarify what the assignment requires
  • report missing information: explain which required detail cannot be confirmed

C Notice the language

Ask for identification, not a guess

The same color or bottle shape can belong to different products. Knowing a nickname isn't the same as identifying the actual container.

  • The label doesn't let me confirm ___.
  • I haven't used ___ because ___.
  • Could you provide the current ___ for this product?

Improve this: "It's the blue one, so I know it."

See a clearer version

The label is unreadable; color alone doesn't identify this product.

The revised explanation names the unresolved fact.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Colleague: Both bottles are blue. Is that enough to identify this one?
Cleaner: Its identity is still ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. unverified

  1. A. matched to the label No readable label match has been obtained.
  2. B. confirmed by color The case gives two products with similar color.
  3. C. unverified Correct. Appearance hasn't established the actual product identity.
  4. D. printed clearly The label is explicitly described as unreadable.
2. Cleaner: This Safety Data Sheet names Product A, but the bottle label names Product B.
Supervisor: Please obtain ____ for the product-information check.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. the SDS matching Product B

  1. A. the room's assignment sheet The room assignment identifies work, not the product's hazard information.
  2. B. Product A's latest SDS A newer document for Product A still doesn't match Product B.
  3. C. the SDS matching Product B Correct. The Safety Data Sheet must identify the actual product being checked.
  4. D. Product B's delivery note A matching delivery note doesn't replace the product's Safety Data Sheet.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

hazard report
A report describing a condition that may cause harm and needs the appropriate response.
maintenance request
A request for the responsible team to assess or repair a building or equipment problem.
out-of-service notice
A sign identifying equipment or an area as unavailable for use.
affected area
The specific location impacted by a reported problem.

Useful word combinations

  • describe the observed condition: say what you saw without inventing a cause
  • give an exact location: identify floor, room, or nearby landmark
  • report a recurrence: say that the same observed condition appeared again
  • keep an area status clear: distinguish cleaned, restricted, and awaiting review

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Supervisor: Do you know where the water came from?
Cleaner: No. Its source is ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. unknown

  1. A. confirmed as a pipe leak No pipe fault has been established from the observation.
  2. B. traced to a spill No spill has been confirmed as the water's source.
  3. C. unknown Correct. The cleaner observed water without establishing its cause.
  4. D. linked to the earlier cleaning Earlier cleaning doesn't establish the cause of the present water.
2. Supervisor: Is the water inside washroom B?
Cleaner: No, it's ____ washroom B, beside the east stair door.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. outside

  1. A. above No water above the washroom has been reported.
  2. B. outside Correct. Outside locates the water beyond the washroom entrance.
  3. C. opposite Opposite means across from it, which the case doesn't establish.
  4. D. inside Inside contradicts the cleaner's correction and the stated location.

Your goal

Clarify a quality concern and agree a check of the specific area.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A client reports streaks on the interior glass panel beside office 8, cleaned earlier that day. The cleaner can acknowledge the reported result and ask the lead to arrange a check and appropriate rework. The cause is unknown; the client is available to point out the panel.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

inspection
A check of completed work against the relevant task requirements.
rework
Work repeated or corrected because the first result didn't meet the specified requirement.
residue
Material left on a surface after use or a cleaning task.
streak
A visible line or uneven mark on a surface such as glass.

Useful word combinations

  • acknowledge the specific concern: name the mark or missed area the person reports
  • ask for the exact location: identify which surface needs attention
  • check against the task requirement: compare the result with the assigned standard
  • confirm the correction: ask whether the reported issue has been addressed

C Notice the language

Address the result without arguing about effort

'It was cleaned' doesn't mean every result meets the task requirement. Listen for the exact surface and defect instead of defending how long the task took.

  • Could you show me the exact ___?
  • I can see the ___ you mean.
  • I'll ask for a check of ___, not assume ___.

Improve this: "We already did that."

See a clearer version

That panel was cleaned earlier; I can see the streaks you're pointing out.

The revision acknowledges both earlier work and the present concern.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Client: I mean visible wipe-like lines across the glass, not a coating, grooves, or missing pieces.
Cleaner: So you're describing ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. streaks

  1. A. chips Chips involve missing pieces of material, which the client excludes.
  2. B. residue Residue names material left behind, not the specific line pattern described.
  3. C. scratches Scratches are surface damage such as grooves, which the client excludes.
  4. D. streaks Correct. Streaks are the visible lines or uneven wipe marks described.
2. Cleaner: I'll report what I can see, ____ guess the cause.
Client: Thank you.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. rather than

  1. A. because of 'Because of' needs a noun phrase, not this verb form.
  2. B. rather than Correct. This contrasts observation with an unsupported explanation.
  3. C. as well as This would add guessing to the reported observation.
  4. D. in addition to This also adds guessing and requires a different verb form.

08Giving a clear end-of-shift handoverReport the stage, not a vague 'ready' · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Give each area a precise status and a named follow-up.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Rooms 301 and 302 have been cleaned; 302 awaits the supervisor's inspection. Room 303 was inaccessible and not cleaned. The incoming attendant can accept 303's access follow-up, while the supervisor owns 302's inspection. Report each status separately without deciding room release.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

room status
The recorded stage of a room's service, such as not started, in progress, or awaiting inspection.
outstanding task
Work that has not yet been completed.
completion record
A record showing which assigned work was actually completed and any relevant limitations.
sign-off
A recorded confirmation by the designated person that a specified check or task meets its requirement.

Useful word combinations

  • hand over the remaining area: identify unfinished work and its next owner
  • distinguish cleaned from inspected: keep task completion separate from a later check
  • record an access limitation: state what prevented work in a particular area
  • confirm who takes over: obtain acknowledgment from the receiving worker

C Notice the language

Report the stage, not a vague 'ready'

Cleaned, inspected, and released can be separate statuses under a site's arrangements. Do not turn a completed cleaning task into a broader authorization.

  • ___ is cleaned but awaits ___.
  • ___ hasn't started because ___.
  • Can you accept the follow-up for ___?

Improve this: "The third floor is ready."

See a clearer version

301 is cleaned; 302 awaits inspection; 303 remains uncleaned due to access.

The statuses let the next worker act without guessing.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Incoming attendant: Is 302's inspection complete?
Outgoing attendant: It's cleaned, but inspection is ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. still pending

  1. A. already signed off No inspection sign-off has been confirmed in the case.
  2. B. no longer required The required check remains open despite completed cleaning.
  3. C. scheduled and completed Scheduling a check doesn't establish that it was completed.
  4. D. still pending Correct. The inspection remains unfinished despite completed cleaning.
2. Outgoing attendant: Can you take 303's access follow-up?
Incoming attendant: Yes, ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. I'll confirm access and update its status

  1. A. I sent someone a note about access Sending a note doesn't identify who now accepts the task.
  2. B. I remember seeing the access request Remembering a request doesn't commit to carrying it out.
  3. C. I thought it belonged to the earlier shift This states a previous assumption rather than accepting responsibility.
  4. D. I'll confirm access and update its status Correct. This explicitly accepts the named follow-up and reporting task.

1. Three rooms, three statuses

The end-of-shift case on the third floor.

  2. Incoming attendant: What about three-oh-three? Its room status doesn't show a completed cleaning task.

  4. Incoming attendant: Yes, I'll check access for three-oh-three and update its status once I know more.

  9. Outgoing attendant: That's the current picture. Please report any change rather than carry yesterday's assumptions forward.
  10. Incoming attendant: I will. I'll start by contacting the person responsible for access to three-oh-three.
2. A completed area has one limitation

A commercial cleaner gives a precise end-of-shift report.

3. The receiver cannot take the task

An outgoing cleaner finds that the intended colleague is assigned elsewhere.

  2. Colleague: I can't accept it yet; I'm assigned to another building for the rest of tonight.
  3. Outgoing cleaner: Thanks for saying so. I won't mark it handed over just because we've discussed it.
  4. Colleague: Please ask the supervisor to reassign it. They know who is staying at this site.
  5. Outgoing cleaner: I'll keep it listed as outstanding and contact them before I finish my report.
  6. Colleague: Tell them the exact washroom and which check is missing so they can allocate it.
  7. Outgoing cleaner: It's washroom C's paper-towel replenishment check, not a new full-room cleaning request.
  8. Colleague: That distinction should help them choose the appropriate worker and avoid duplicating completed cleaning.
  9. Outgoing cleaner: The supervisor has accepted the reassignment question. I'll record that, with the check still unfinished.
  10. Colleague: Good. Someone has the allocation follow-up now, but the supply check itself remains outstanding.
