Lesson 01: Legal English Mindset: Facts, Issues, Rules, Risk
Original fictional case: A case note says the supplier breached the contract. The team has an email stating that delivery occurred on 18 May, but has not reviewed the signed agreement or amendments. The client says the promised date was 15 May. Ava, a paralegal, prepared the chronology; Daniel, supervising counsel, reviews the analysis. No legal conclusion about breach or remedy has been approved.
Language workshop: A late-delivery email is not the whole contract
Communication goal: Distinguish an allegation from a legal conclusion and identify missing evidence.
Language guidance: The email and client account are sources to assess, not interchangeable proof of the signed terms. Delivery on 18 May does not alone establish breach of a 15 May contractual obligation, particularly when the agreement and amendments remain unreviewed.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): The client reports that 15 May was the promised date. / The email records delivery on 18 May. / We have not yet established the applicable contractual deadline. / We need the signed agreement and any relevant amendments. / The potential breach analysis remains preliminary. / The question is which delivery obligation governed this shipment. / We cannot infer an available remedy from the date alone. / I will assemble the documents and identify each source in the chronology. / That supports the delivery date, not necessarily the promised date. / Please retain "the client reports" in the shortened summary. / Are we assuming the email varied the signed terms? / That is an inference from the sequence, not an express statement in the document. / Which part of the chronology conflicts with the source record? / The record may support that position, subject to the remaining review. / The claimed loss and recoverable damages are different questions. / Counsel has not approved a final conclusion on breach or remedy.
Vocabulary: fact: A circumstance treated as established on the relevant evidence. / allegation: An assertion that has not necessarily been established. / issue: A question requiring analysis or determination. / rule: A governing proposition whose applicability must be assessed. / application: Analysis connecting a rule to the relevant facts. / conclusion: A reasoned determination drawn from the analysis. / evidence: Information offered to support a factual proposition. / inference: A conclusion drawn from other information. / assumption: A proposition used provisionally without establishing it. / chronology: An ordered account of events over time. / source: The origin of a statement or piece of information. / corroboration: Additional support for an account or proposition. / agreement: An arrangement whose terms and legal effect require context. / obligation: A requirement imposed by a relevant legal or contractual source. / breach: Failure to comply with an applicable legal or contractual duty. / remedy: A form of relief potentially available for a legal wrong. / damages: Monetary relief sought or awarded for a legal wrong; its basis and purpose vary. / causation: The legally relevant connection between conduct and consequence. / materiality: The significance of a fact or term in its legal context. / qualification: A condition or limit attached to a statement. / reservation: A stated concern or limit on a position. / exposure: Potential adverse legal or financial consequence. / preliminary: Initial and subject to further review. / factual record: The assembled information relevant to the matter.
Speaking task: Check the dialogue. Attribute 15 May to the client and 18 May to the email; do not convert the interval into a breach finding. Switch roles. Request the signed agreement and amendments, keeping claimed loss distinct from recoverable damages. Complete the termination exchange. Retain the unreviewed clause and preliminary assessment in the circulated summary.
Partner: Ava: Paralegal / Daniel: Supervising counsel
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A client reports that a supplier ended service early. The team has the termination email but has not reviewed the governing termination clause or amendments. No conclusion on validity is approved.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Ava: My new opening will distinguish the reported fifteenth-of-May promise from the email's eighteenth-of-May delivery statement, with breach and remedies still under review.
Daniel: That is appropriately preliminary. Add who is requesting the missing documents so the note leads to a specific next step.
Ava: I'll request the signed agreement and relevant changes today, with the source references attached. Please review the revised note before any client assessment goes out.
Daniel: I will. Once the terms and evidence are assembled, we can complete the analysis without making the chronology itself carry an unsupported legal conclusion.
END REFERENCE

Lesson 02: Client Intake, Scope, Conflicts, and Confidentiality
Original fictional case: A prospective client, Mira, has emailed a detailed dispute account and asks whether to send a demand today. The conflicts review is incomplete. Leo is an intake coordinator, not the reviewing lawyer. The firm's procedure requires the parties' names and related entities before the assigned lawyer can decide whether the firm may accept the matter. No acceptance or advice has been authorized. Mira mentions a possible deadline but supplies no document establishing it.
Language workshop: An urgent inquiry before the conflicts review is complete
