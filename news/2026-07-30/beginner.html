Read the story

The United States launched new strikes. These strikes were on Iran. It happened after an attempted attack. American troops were the target. There was a short period of calm before. Hostilities between the two countries were paused then. But now, both sides have restarted missile attacks. The US wants to protect its troops. This situation can be dangerous. Many people hope for peace soon.

Words and grammar

1. strikes (noun): Sudden strong attacks, often by military planes or missiles.
2. attack (noun): When someone tries to hurt another person or country.
3. troops (noun): Groups of soldiers working together.
4. calm (noun): A time when things are quiet and peaceful, without fighting.
5. paused (verb (past tense)): Stopped for a short time, then started again.
6. launched (verb): To start or set in motion something, often a large-scale project or an attack.

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense
We use the simple past tense to talk about actions that happened and finished before now. For many verbs, we add '-ed' to the end. For others, the word changes.
Example from the text: "The United States launched new strikes."
