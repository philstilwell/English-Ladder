📅 September 20, 2026 - German Elections: Chancellor Merz's Future Hangs in the Balance
German Chancellor Friedrich Merz faces intense political pressure as crucial state elections could decide his future amidst declining approval and rising support for the far-right AfD.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

German elections are underway, which could determine the political future of Chancellor Friedrich Merz. He faces intense political pressure that is expected to escalate if his party performs poorly in Sunday's state polls. Local elections are taking place in Berlin and Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, where opinion polls suggest his Christian Democratic Union (CDU) is at risk of significant defeats. These polls indicate Merz's conservatives could lose power in Berlin as they tussle with Die Linke (The Left) for the leading position. In Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, the CDU is barely hovering above the 5% threshold required to enter the state parliament, potentially marking their worst-ever result. This current predicament follows a major victory for the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) party in Saxony-Anhalt two weeks ago, where the CDU's vote was halved. Some branches of the AfD are classed as right-wing extremist by domestic intelligence. Its anti-immigration, Russian-friendly, Germany-first agenda has found fertile ground in the country's former communist east. Merz is currently answering for his party's tumbling popularity, while the AfD, which he intended to counter, continues its ascent. Despite his vow to carry on, Merz is contending with poor approval ratings amid whispers of a chancellor swap, though his challengers appear hesitant. His blunt communication style, such as dismissing "work-life balance" and urging Germans to work more and take less sick leave, has also caused irritation among some. Voters' faith in his ability to fix these problems is apparently ebbing away. The country's long-stuttering economy, high energy costs, and fraught debates about immigration are feeding a sense of national gloom. Earlier this week, the leaders of eight German states backed Merz, stressing that restoring trust in the CDU lay in "listening and taking political action, not in debates over personnel." However, murmurings of discontent within his party have grown louder, though no one has openly challenged him for the top job. Martin Rohloff, a CDU candidate, thinks Merz does not have the "Fingerspitzengefühl, the feeling for the small persons." Rohloff also muses that while Merz "wants the right things," "he cannot communicate well." Rohloff even suggested Merz should go, stating, "If I have to answer yes or no, I would say yes. There are other people who maybe have better feelings for the public." The chancellor is reportedly due to meet senior party figures this evening, having already cancelled a planned trip to New York in an apparent effort to contain the crisis.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. crisis (noun): A time of intense difficulty, trouble, or danger, often requiring an immediate decision.
2. worst-ever (noun phrase): The lowest or most unfavorable outcome ever achieved by a political party or candidate in an election.
3. tussle with (verb phrase): To struggle or contend vigorously, especially to gain or maintain control over something.
4. hovering above (verb phrase): Remaining just above a particular level or threshold, often indicating a precarious position.
5. current predicament (noun phrase): A difficult, perplexing, or trying situation from which it is hard to escape.
6. fertile ground (noun phrase): A suitable environment or situation that encourages the growth or development of something.
7. tumbling popularity (noun phrase): A rapid and noticeable decline in public approval or support.
8. contending with (verb phrase): To struggle or deal with a difficulty, problem, or opponent.
9. blunt communication style (noun phrase): A manner of speaking that is direct, straightforward, and sometimes perceived as insensitive or ungracious.
10. ebbing away (phrasal verb): To gradually lessen or decline, like a tide going out.
11. fraught debates (noun phrase): Discussions or arguments filled with stress, anxiety, or emotional tension.
12. murmurings of discontent (noun phrase): Quiet or private expressions of dissatisfaction or unhappiness, often signalling growing opposition.

Advanced Grammar: Perfect Participle Clauses
A perfect participle clause, typically beginning with “having” + past participle, is used to show an action that was completed before the main action of the sentence. It can provide context, cause, or a preceding event, and often functions as an adverbial clause, making the sentence more concise and indicating sequence.
Example from the text: "The chancellor is reportedly due to meet senior party figures this evening, having already cancelled a planned trip to New York in an apparent effort to contain the crisis."

Ed Sheeran addressed the recent upheaval caused by rapper Macklemore's removal from his concert tour, speaking about free speech and the Israel-Gaza situation. Sheeran also voiced strong objections to the concept of venues "preapproving" performers or their content, a stance met with audience applause. Outside the venue, a few dozen protestors waved flags as ticket holders streamed in, yet the concert proceeded largely unfettered. Attendees interviewed by the BBC shared a common resolve to enjoy the music while simultaneously championing freedom of speech. Macklemore's original performance involved a song dedicated to pro-Palestinian protestors, in which he referred to "genocide" in Gaza, a term Israel disputes. Messina Touring Group indicated that Macklemore's departure was due to certain venues threatening to cancel concerts if he performed. Robert Kraft, owner of Gillette Stadium, subsequently confirmed his decision to bar Macklemore from performing at Sheeran's upcoming shows there.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. upheaval (noun): A violent or sudden change or disruption to something.
2. amid (preposition): In the middle of or surrounded by.
3. articulated (verb): Expressed or stated clearly and coherently.
4. disproportionate (adjective): Too large or too small in comparison to something else; lacking appropriate balance or relation.
5. conveyed (verb): Communicated or made known; transmitted.
6. profound (adjective): Very great or intense; having or showing great knowledge or insight.
7. systemic (adjective): Relating to a system, especially as opposed to a particular part; affecting a whole body or system.
8. objections (noun): Expressions or feelings of disapproval or opposition; reasons for disagreeing.
9. unfettered (adjective): Not bound by chains or shackles; unrestrained or unrestricted, allowing complete freedom.
10. championing (verb): Supporting the cause of; defending or advocating for.

Advanced Grammar: Participial Phrases for Adverbial Modification
Participial phrases, often beginning with an -ing form (present participle) or a past participle (e.g., 'interviewed'), can function as adverbial modifiers at the beginning or end of a sentence. They provide additional information about the main clause, such as cause, effect, purpose, or a related action, and efficiently condense information. On a positive note, the Bank reported that the UK economy proved "more resilient" than anticipated, revising its economic growth forecast for July to September to 0.4%. Furthermore, food price inflation is projected to rise by 4% by year-end, a reduction from the earlier 6-7% forecast, as higher energy costs have not yet spilled over into other sectors. The Bank also announced a halt to its "quantitative tightening" (QT) program, pausing the annual sale of government bonds. Instead, it will divest smaller portions of its bond stockpile over eight years, a shift from its earlier practice of offloading bonds, which contributed to higher yields. This change in strategy prompted an immediate market reaction, with yields on 30-year UK government bonds falling from 5.86% to 5.75% after the announcement.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. maintained (verb): To keep something at the same level or standard, preventing it from decreasing or changing significantly.
2. consecutive (adjective): Following one after another without interruption; in an unbroken sequence.
3. uptick (noun): A small increase or slight improvement, often temporary, in a trend or measurement.
4. magnify (verb): To make something appear larger, more intense, or more significant than it actually is; to amplify an effect.
5. anticipates (verb): Regards as probable or expects; to look forward to or prepare for something to happen.
6. elevated (adjective): Higher than the usual, normal, or expected level; increased in amount or degree.
7. implications (noun): The possible results or effects of an action or decision, which may not be immediately obvious.
8. benchmark (noun): A standard or point of reference against which things can be measured, compared, or assessed.
9. factored in (phrasal verb): Included or considered something as a relevant element when making a calculation, plan, or assessment.
10. volatility (noun): The tendency of something, especially prices or markets, to change rapidly and unpredictably, often for the worse.

Advanced Grammar: Conditional clauses without "if"
Advanced English often employs alternative structures to express conditions, rather than explicitly using "if." Prepositions like "with" or phrases such as "on condition that" can introduce conditions, implying a cause-and-effect relationship or a necessary prerequisite for a certain outcome to occur. This adds nuance and conciseness to complex arguments, allowing for a more sophisticated expression of dependency.

Bangor University researchers say the scale of damage from summer wildfires is far greater than what is immediately visible. The Bangor team has been racing to collect soil samples before heavy winter rainfall washes away the remains. Capturing baseline data now is crucial, as soil and vegetation properties can change rapidly. Dr. Kara Marsden warned that early green shoots do not necessarily reflect true or full recovery. Bracken sprouts almost immediately because its rhizomes sit deep in the soil, so the burn layer wouldn't maybe have impacted those. But the heather and gorse have much shallower roots, so you know they're completely gone, and that depth of soil they depend on could take thousands of years to form again. Wildfires also release carbon, nitrogen, and sulphur into the atmosphere, directly contributing to climate change. Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. immediately visible (adverbial phrase): able to be seen right away or on the surface, without closer inspection.
2. blackened vegetation (noun phrase): plants and trees that have been burned and turned dark, often appearing charred or scorched.
3. topsoil (noun): the uppermost layer of soil, typically rich in organic matter and nutrients, vital for plant growth.
4. critical (adjective): of extreme importance, especially in relation to the success or failure of something.
5. double whammy (noun): a situation in which two unpleasant or undesirable things happen at almost the same time, or one after the other, creating a more severe negative effect.
6. worsens (verb): makes something more severe, serious, or unpleasant; deteriorates.
7. fragile (adjective): easily broken, damaged, or destroyed; delicate and vulnerable.
8. baseline data (noun phrase): information gathered at the beginning of a study or project that can be used as a basis for comparison with future data.
9. rapidly (adverb): very quickly and at a great rate; with speed and urgency.
10. susceptible (adjective): likely or liable to be influenced or harmed by a particular thing; vulnerable.

Advanced Grammar: Participle Clauses for Consequence
Participle clauses, often starting with an '-ing' form, can concisely express an action or state that is a result or consequence of the main clause. They help to link ideas, showing a cause-and-effect relationship without using conjunctions like 'and as a result' or 'consequently', making sentences more fluid and advanced.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. lauded (verb, past participle): Praised enthusiastically in public; more formal and emphatic than simply saying someone was liked.
2. premieres (verb): Has its first public broadcast or performance; an earlier private preview is not the public premiere.
3. portrayed (verb, past simple): Presented someone or something in a particular way; a portrayal reflects the writer’s perspective.
4. conveys (verb): Communicates an idea or feeling, sometimes indirectly through a comparison or choice of words.
5. depicted (verb, past simple): Represented someone in words or images; here, described Lawson in a particular way.
6. circulate (verb): Spread from person to person or place to place; here, be shared online.
7. roster (noun): A list of the people taking part; more common in organized teams and events than in casual conversation.
8. incorporates (verb): Includes something as part of a larger whole; here, puts particular flavours into the finished bakes.
9. stereotype (noun): A fixed, oversimplified idea about a group that ignores differences between individuals.
10. aspirations (plural noun): Hopes or ambitions for the future; aspirations describe aims, not proven results.

Advanced Grammar: An introductory -ing clause
An introductory “-ing” clause can describe an action or circumstance connected with the main clause. Its understood subject must match the subject that follows. Here, Moyin both draws on his roots and incorporates flavours. A different following subject could wrongly suggest that someone or something else is doing the drawing.

The Welsh government is seeking public consultation on a new state-owned energy entity designed to develop renewable assets, aiming to reduce long-term energy costs and foster community ownership, though it will not directly control consumer bills.

Read the story

The Welsh government is soliciting public input on a new state-owned renewable energy entity. This initiative aims to mitigate energy costs over time by developing and owning renewable energy assets and supporting local and community ownership. Furthermore, it would endeavor to attract investment into Welsh energy infrastructure and facilitate more organizations using locally generated energy. Currently, ministers are proposing to integrate Trydan Gwyrdd Cymru (TGC), an existing onshore wind and solar developer, with other Welsh energy schemes like Ynni Cymru. The consolidation seeks to create a single entity possessing the necessary expertise and critical mass of resources to effect significant change in the energy sector. Despite its objective to reduce expenses, the consultation states that the company would not replace existing energy suppliers or directly determine household electricity prices. Enterprise Minister Adam Price articulated a vision for Wales to have an energy system that is "clean, cheap and owned by us." Price contrasted this modern ambition with Wales's historical position as an "energy giant" during the Industrial Revolution, lamenting that the benefits of coal were not primarily derived by its own populace. He emphasized the imperative to avoid repeating such oversights with the immense potential presented by the contemporary renewable energy revolution. While many energy policy levers are determined at a UK level, Price suggested a public energy company could facilitate lower prices through specific agreements with large local energy consumers. The consultation also explores establishing a Welsh Energy Wealth Fund, intended to ensure energy project benefits reach vulnerable individuals and communities affected by project implementation. Furthermore, the new entity might acquire stakes in substantial energy ventures, including offshore wind, marine energy, and nuclear power. However, the proposals have faced scrutiny and criticism from political opponents such as the Welsh Conservatives and Reform parties. Janet Finch-Saunders, Welsh Conservative energy spokesperson, challenged the government's focus, stating that another state-owned company and more turbines are not the answer amidst manufacturing job losses. She asserted that Wales requires "cheap, reliable energy to keep factories open, businesses competitive and household bills down." Jason O'Connell, Shadow Cabinet Minister, echoed similar concerns, questioning the priority of building a publicly owned energy entity over actively lowering current high energy bills for Welsh families.

Definitions in English.

2. entity (noun): A thing with distinct and independent existence, especially a distinct organization or legal body.
3. acquire (verb): To obtain ownership or control of something, such as an asset or a share in a project.
4. mitigate (verb): To make something less severe, serious, or painful; to reduce the adverse effects or impact of something.

7. consolidation (noun): The action or process of combining a number of things or entities into a single, more effective, or coherent whole.

11. imperative (noun): An essential or urgent thing; a command or duty that is extremely important.
