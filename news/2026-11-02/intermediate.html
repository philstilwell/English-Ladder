📅 November 02, 2026 - Oil Spill Kills Birds and Forces Wildlife Park Closure

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Deliberate damage to a home heating tank caused an oil spill that killed 13 birds from Broughshane Waterfowl Park. The attraction in County Antrim has been forced to close while teams continue to remove oil from its pond. More than 20 birds were taken for urgent specialist treatment, but the number of deaths has continued to rise. A volunteer arriving to feed the birds noticed an oil smell and saw black swans covered in oil. The park keeps 120 birds and also provides a home for wild birds. Environmental inspectors used special floating barriers that absorb oil to limit the damage to the ponds. The waterway has improved considerably, although further work will continue for as long as necessary. Rescue workers have bathed and treated the affected birds as quickly as possible because of the oil pollution. The team also gave the birds charcoal to help fight harmful substances they had swallowed. Birds can hide signs of illness, so symptoms may only become clear when it is too late. Most rescued birds are not native to the area, so they cannot return until the pond is completely clean. Police have appealed for anyone with information about the incident to come forward.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. urgent (adjective): Needing attention immediately.
2. specialist treatment (noun phrase): Medical help given by people with special knowledge of a particular problem.
3. absorb (verb): To take a liquid into a material and hold it there.
4. considerably (adverb): By a large amount or to a large degree.
5. affected (adjective): Harmed or changed by the event being discussed.
6. symptoms (plural noun): Signs that a person or animal has an illness.
7. appealed (verb, past participle): Made a serious public request for help or information.
8. come forward (phrasal verb): To offer information or help to the people who need it.

Grammar Focus: Present perfect passive
Use "has" or "have" followed by "been" and a past participle to connect a past action with its result now. The passive focuses on who or what receives the action. Here, "has been forced to close" explains why the attraction is closed.
Example from the text: "The attraction in County Antrim has been forced to close while teams continue to remove oil from its pond."
