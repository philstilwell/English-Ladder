01Capturing the customer's concernRecord the concern without rewriting it as a diagnosis · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Clarify vehicle identity and reported circumstances.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Customer reports an occasional rattle from the rear after the car has been parked overnight. They do not know its cause. Two household cars are on the account; today's booking is the blue hatchback. No technician assessment has occurred.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

repair order
The job record linking a vehicle, customer concern, agreed work, and service results.
intermittent concern
A reported problem that occurs sometimes rather than continuously.
symptom description
The customer's account of what they noticed, without a confirmed explanation of cause.
vehicle identification number
The vehicle's unique identifying code, commonly shortened to VIN.

Useful word combinations

  • record the customer's words: preserve what was actually reported
  • clarify when it happens: ask about the reported circumstances
  • verify the vehicle identity: match the booking to the right vehicle
  • separate symptom from diagnosis: distinguish experience from technical explanation

C Notice the language

Record the concern without rewriting it as a diagnosis

Customer descriptions guide the handoff but do not establish a mechanical cause.

  • Which vehicle does this concern relate to?
  • You notice ___ when ___; is that accurate?
  • I'll record ___ as reported, with the cause unconfirmed.

Improve this: "Your rear suspension is broken."

See a clearer version

You report an occasional rear rattle after overnight parking; the cause is unconfirmed.

The revised description preserves the symptom without inventing a fault.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Customer: It happens some mornings, but not every time.
Advisor: I'll describe the concern as ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. intermittent

  1. A. continuous Continuous means ongoing, which contradicts some mornings.
  2. B. intermittent Correct. Intermittent means it occurs only sometimes.
  3. C. resolved The customer has not reported a resolution.
  4. D. reproduced No technician has yet reproduced the concern.
2. Customer: The blue hatchback rattles; the other car on the account does not.
Advisor: I'll add your report to the hatchback's ____, without recording a confirmed fault.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. current repair order

  1. A. completed inspection finding An inspection finding would imply a technician's assessment.
  2. B. approved repair scope Describing a symptom does not define an approved repair scope.
  3. C. parts replacement request No diagnosis supports requesting a replacement part at this stage.
  4. D. current repair order Correct. The repair order can preserve the reported concern.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Two cars, one reported rattle

At reception, an advisor clarifies the customer's vehicle and concern.

  1. Advisor: Which car is today's concern about, the blue hatchback or your white sedan?
  2. Customer: The blue hatchback. I sometimes hear a rattle from the rear after overnight parking.
  3. Advisor: Does it happen every morning, or only occasionally when you first notice it?
  4. Customer: Only occasionally. I can't identify a component, and I haven't noticed a consistent pattern.
  5. Advisor: I'll record an intermittent concern, with your rear-rattle description and the overnight-parking detail.
  6. Customer: Please don't call it a suspension fault. That was just someone's suggestion online.
  7. Advisor: I won't. We need the technician's assessment before naming a cause on the repair order.
  8. Customer: Can you make sure the account's other car doesn't inherit this concern too?
  9. Advisor: Yes. We'll verify the vehicle identification number against this booking and vehicle record.
  10. Customer: Thank you. That keeps my symptom description attached to the car actually being assessed.
2. The sound word needs clarification

An advisor asks a customer to clarify an informal noise description.

  1. Customer: I wrote clicking on the booking, but rattling might describe the sound better.
  2. Advisor: Could you describe what you noticed without worrying about choosing a technical word?
  3. Customer: It was several quick little sounds, not one loud impact, near the passenger side.
  4. Advisor: I'll preserve that description rather than translate it into a component diagnosis.
  5. Customer: Please say it was my impression of the location, not something I verified.
  6. Advisor: Certainly. I'll mark passenger side as reported, with the exact source unconfirmed.
  7. Customer: Does changing my word from clicking to rattling mean the booking needs canceling?
  8. Advisor: No. We can clarify the concern on the existing repair order for this vehicle.
  9. Customer: Then include both my earlier wording and the clearer description we're discussing now.
  10. Advisor: I will. The technician can see how your description was clarified at intake.
3. Yesterday is not every day

An advisor checks the timing of a customer's concern.

  1. Advisor: The booking says the display goes blank daily. Is that frequency accurate?
  2. Customer: No. It happened yesterday once; I don't know why I selected daily online.
  3. Advisor: Thanks for correcting it. Had you noticed the same display issue before yesterday?
  4. Customer: Not that I remember. I don't want to claim it has never happened.
  5. Advisor: I'll record one episode reported yesterday, with no earlier episode recalled by you.
  6. Customer: That sounds right. Can the technician see that I corrected the original frequency?
  7. Advisor: Yes. I'll clarify the intake record so daily isn't treated as confirmed history.
  8. Customer: Please also distinguish the display from the whole electrical system. I can't assess that.
  9. Advisor: Agreed. Your report concerns the display only, and the cause remains unconfirmed.
  10. Customer: Then the symptom description now reflects what I actually observed rather than assumptions.

Scenario 2 · A relative's report

Customer booked a visit for a concern their partner noticed once on Monday. Customer has not experienced it personally; partner can provide details after 16:00.

Role A: Advisor: attribute the report and confirm who can clarify it.

Role B: Customer: distinguish your knowledge from your partner's experience.

Possible opening: “Did you notice this yourself, or are you relaying your partner's account?”

Success checks
  • Attribute the Monday report.
  • Avoid inventing frequency.
  • Identify the partner's available contact time.
Add a complication

Customer asks you to record it as happening every trip.

Studying alone? Speak both roles aloud. For each scenario, prepare for two minutes, speak for one minute, then answer a follow-up. Switch roles and repeat using fewer notes. Use only the supplied facts; identify missing information instead of inventing it.

Apply it to this case

Check your reasoning

Compare with the case above and the model response. Can you point to the words that support your factual statement? Is your question about something the case leaves open? If you introduce a possible outcome, clearly label it as a possibility.

Review, then try again

  • Keeps the case facts accurate; clearly separates confirmed and unknown information.
  • Uses understandable sentences and explains specialist terms when the listener needs it.
  • Responds to the other person's question, checks understanding, and uses an appropriate tone.
  • Makes the requested action or unresolved question clear without inventing authority or facts.

Revise one sentence and repeat the response. A useful response can be clear even with a few grammar errors; judge meaning and task completion, not accent.

02Clarifying the appointment and drop-offDistinguish reception timing from completion timing · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Correct a booking assumption before the customer relies on it.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Customer booked an 08:00 drop-off but expects work to begin at 08:00 and finish by 09:00. The booking confirms intake only. Advisor can offer a status update by 10:00, not a finish guarantee; customer prefers a phone call.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

drop-off appointment
A booking in which the customer leaves the vehicle rather than waiting throughout the visit.
waiting appointment
A booking arranged around the customer's plan to remain at the service location, without automatically guaranteeing completion time.
intake check
The reception-stage confirmation of the vehicle, concern, and agreed service arrangements.
contact preference
The customer's stated channel and availability for service communications.

Useful word combinations

  • confirm the appointment type: distinguish drop-off from waiting
  • set a callback window: agree when communication is expected
  • verify the contact preference: check channel and availability
  • clarify the intake check: separate reception from technician work

C Notice the language

Distinguish reception timing from completion timing

A booking time can identify intake rather than the start of technical work.

  • ___ is the drop-off time, not ___.
  • We can update you by ___ about ___.
  • Would you prefer contact by ___?

Improve this: "Eight means we'll start then."

See a clearer version

Eight is intake; a technician start time has not been confirmed.

The revision explains exactly what the appointment establishes.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Customer: Does eight o'clock guarantee work starts then?
Advisor: It confirms ____, not a technician start.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. drop-off intake

  1. A. diagnosis No assessment result is confirmed at intake.
  2. B. drop-off intake Correct. The booked time is for reception intake.
  3. C. parts arrival The booking says nothing about parts arrival.
  4. D. repair completion Completion has not been promised at eight.
2. Customer: Please call rather than text, but I haven't approved any repair.
Advisor: I'll record your ____, leaving work authorization unchanged.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. contact preference

  1. A. completion requirement The request concerns contact method, not a guaranteed finish.
  2. B. assessment finding No technical assessment result was provided by this request.
  3. C. contact preference Correct. A call preference changes communication, not work approval.
  4. D. repair decision The customer explicitly has not made a repair decision.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Eight is the drop-off time

An advisor clarifies an 08:00 booking before the customer leaves.

  1. Customer: My appointment is eight, so I expected the car finished by nine today.
  2. Advisor: Eight is your drop-off intake, not a confirmed technician start or completion time.
  3. Customer: I misunderstood. I booked a ride based on having the car back within an hour.
  4. Advisor: I'm sorry the timing wasn't clear. We can provide a phone update by ten.
  5. Customer: Will that call tell me the assessment status, or guarantee the work is finished?
  6. Advisor: It will give the actual status. It isn't a promise of completion by ten.
  7. Customer: Please call rather than text. I can answer at the number on my booking.
  8. Advisor: I've confirmed that contact preference. Is the revised timing clear before you decide?
  9. Customer: Yes. I understand the drop-off appointment and the callback, but no finish time is guaranteed.
  10. Advisor: That's the arrangement. We'll keep any later completion estimate separate from the intake time.
2. Waiting does not settle the finish time

A customer asks reception about remaining at the shop.

  1. Customer: I selected a waiting appointment. Does that mean you'll finish before my eleven-o'clock meeting?
  2. Receptionist: The booking records your plan to wait, but no eleven-o'clock completion is confirmed.
  3. Customer: I need to know that now rather than discover it after the intake check.
  4. Receptionist: Of course. I can ask the service advisor to review timing before you proceed.
  5. Customer: Please tell them the meeting is fixed, but I can choose another appointment.
  6. Receptionist: I'll give them that constraint and request available alternatives, not cancel this booking automatically.
  7. Customer: Could the advisor explain whether leaving the vehicle would change the timing estimate?
  8. Receptionist: Yes. That is a scheduling question for them; I can't guarantee a faster result.
  9. Customer: Then I'll wait for that explanation before deciding whether to keep today's appointment.
  10. Receptionist: I'll connect you now, with the current booking and your meeting time clearly stated.
3. A text cannot reach the customer

An advisor verifies a callback arrangement at drop-off.

  1. Advisor: Your booking lists text messages as the preferred contact method for today's updates.
  2. Customer: That number belongs to an old phone. Please use a call to my current number.
  3. Advisor: Let's confirm the current number through our booking check before changing the contact record.
  4. Customer: Certainly. I also can't answer between one and two because I'm presenting at work.
  5. Advisor: I'll record that availability limit alongside your preference for a phone call today.
  6. Customer: Does giving a new number authorize additional work if someone reaches me there?
  7. Advisor: No. Contact details only arrange communication; any work decision needs its own clear confirmation.
  8. Customer: Thank you. Please make that distinction if another advisor takes over this afternoon.
  9. Advisor: I'll include the verified number, call preference, and unavailable period in the handoff.
  10. Customer: Then the next advisor can reach me without assuming my contact update approves repairs.

03Explaining estimates and approval limitsAttach approval to a defined scope · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Check what 'go ahead' actually authorizes.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Fictional shop offers assessment for $95 under its stated booking terms. No repair is included. Customer says 'go ahead' but intends only the assessment. Advisor must read back the scope and route any later repair proposal for separate approval.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

estimate
A stated anticipated cost for a defined service scope, subject to its expressed terms.
diagnostic charge
A charge for agreed assessment work, distinct from charges for any later repair.
authorization limit
The stated amount or scope a customer has agreed may proceed under the applicable service arrangement.
additional work
Work beyond the currently agreed scope that requires its own clarification and approval.

Useful word combinations

  • explain the estimate scope: identify the work a stated amount covers
  • confirm the authorization limit: repeat the customer's agreed boundary
  • request approval for additional work: seek a decision on a defined proposal
  • distinguish assessment from repair: name what the current charge covers

C Notice the language

Attach approval to a defined scope

An amount without a task description can be misunderstood. This fictional arrangement is not a universal rule about charges or approval.

  • Your approval covers ___ for ___, correct?
  • That does not include ___.
  • Any proposed additional work needs ___.

Improve this: "You said go ahead, so everything is approved."

See a clearer version

You approved the stated assessment only; repairs would need a separate decision.

The read-back ties agreement to the customer's intended scope.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Customer: I approve the assessment, not a repair.
Advisor: I'll keep the authorization ____ to that scope.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. limited

  1. A. converted Converted does not express the required scope boundary.
  2. B. limited Correct. Limited preserves the customer's stated boundary.
  3. C. extended Extended would enlarge the scope they approved.
  4. D. unrelated Unrelated would disconnect approval from the task.
2. Customer: Does the diagnostic charge cover all repairs?
Advisor: In this booking it covers the ____ only.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. assessment

  1. A. completed repair No completed repair is included in the assessment.
  2. B. assessment Correct. The stated charge is for assessment only.
  3. C. replacement Replacement work is not included in this scope.
  4. D. installation Installation work is not part of this booking.

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Ninety-five dollars for assessment only

An advisor reads back a fictional shop's assessment terms.

  1. Customer: Ninety-five dollars is fine. Go ahead with the assessment, but not a repair.
  2. Advisor: To confirm, your approval covers the stated ninety-five-dollar assessment only under this booking.
  3. Customer: Yes. I want to hear any findings and proposed repair price before deciding.
  4. Advisor: I'll preserve that authorization limit. Additional work would need a separate discussion and decision.
  5. Customer: Does saying go ahead mean the technician can add other work up to that amount?
  6. Advisor: No. Your approval names the assessment scope, not any task costing less than ninety-five.
  7. Customer: Thank you. Please explain that distinction on the repair order for the technician.
  8. Advisor: I will. The diagnostic charge and any later repair proposal will remain separate.
  9. Customer: And the amount you stated applies to this particular assessment, not every service visit?
  10. Advisor: Correct. These are this booking's stated terms; other service scopes have their own terms.
2. Approval for one proposed item

A customer clarifies a decision after receiving two service proposals.

  1. Advisor: The technician's report includes two proposals, listed separately as item A and item B.
  2. Customer: I approve item A under the quoted terms, but I haven't decided about B.
  3. Advisor: Then I'll record approval for A only, with B awaiting your separate decision.
  4. Customer: Please don't mark the whole estimate approved just because I accepted one line.
  5. Advisor: I won't. Each proposal needs to retain its actual approval status on this record.
  6. Customer: Can I ask more questions about B without that being treated as permission?
  7. Advisor: Yes. I'll arrange an explanation; asking about an item isn't approval to proceed.
  8. Customer: That's helpful. My question is what the recommendation means, not when to start it.
  9. Advisor: I'll pass that question to the technician and leave B's authorization status unchanged.
  10. Customer: Then we agree: A approved as quoted, B unapproved while I seek clarification.
3. An estimate revision needs explanation

A customer queries two differently dated estimates.

  1. Customer: I have an earlier estimate for two hundred dollars and a newer one for two forty.
  2. Advisor: The newer document includes a proposed additional line. It doesn't establish that you approved it.
  3. Customer: I approved the earlier scope. Please explain what changed before seeking another decision.
  4. Advisor: I'll compare the two versions and identify that added line for your review.
  5. Customer: Does the revised total automatically replace the authorization limit from our earlier conversation?
  6. Advisor: No. We need to clarify the proposed change and your actual decision under the shop's terms.
  7. Customer: Then don't describe my request for an explanation as refusal of every service item.
  8. Advisor: I won't. Your earlier approval and the new proposal are separate parts of this record.
  9. Customer: Please give me the explanation with both dates so I can follow the comparison.
  10. Advisor: I'll arrange that now and preserve the additional line as pending your decision.

04Handing the concern to the technicianPreserve source, scope, and contact constraints · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Give a technician a usable handoff without inserting a diagnosis.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Job card 208 concerns a customer-reported intermittent cabin rattle after overnight parking. Customer approved assessment only, is unavailable from 12:00 to 14:00, and prefers a phone call after 14:00. Technician has not reproduced the concern. Advisor hands over these facts without a diagnosis.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

job card
The workshop record identifying the assigned vehicle, work scope, and relevant job details.
reported condition
A circumstance described by the customer that has not necessarily been independently observed.
technician finding
An observation or assessment actually made and recorded by the technician.
carryover job
A service job remaining incomplete when it passes to another work period or colleague.

Useful word combinations

  • hand over the job card: transfer the current scope and concern
  • attribute the reported condition: identify the customer's account
  • separate a finding from a report: distinguish technician evidence
  • read back the work scope: confirm the agreed task before handoff

C Notice the language

Preserve source, scope, and contact constraints

A job card should distinguish what the customer said from what the technician observed.

  • The customer reports ___ under ___.
  • The approved scope is ___.
  • Their contact restriction is ___; findings are ___.

Improve this: "The technician found the rattle already."

See a clearer version

The customer reported the rattle; the technician has not yet reproduced it.

The wording preserves the actual source of information.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Technician: Was the noise confirmed by another technician?
Advisor: No; it is customer-____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. reported

  1. A. resolved Resolved would imply the concern ended.
  2. B. repaired Repaired would imply completed technical work.
  3. C. reported Correct. The customer is the source of this account.
  4. D. replaced Replaced describes an item change, not a report.
2. Technician: Did someone establish the 'faulty speaker' written on the job card?
Advisor: No. It is ____; the customer only suggested it.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. an unverified customer hypothesis

  1. A. a confirmed technician finding No technician has established the proposed speaker fault.
  2. B. an approved repair instruction The suggestion is not permission to carry out a repair.
  3. C. an unverified customer hypothesis Correct. The explanation is the customer's unconfirmed suggestion.
  4. D. a completed diagnostic conclusion No completed assessment supports that diagnostic conclusion.

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Job card 208

An advisor hands an assessment-only job to a technician.

  1. Advisor: Job card two oh eight is assessment only for a reported intermittent cabin rattle.
  2. Technician: Has another technician reproduced it, or is the description entirely from the customer?
  3. Advisor: It is customer-reported after overnight parking; no technician finding is recorded yet.
  4. Technician: I'll keep that report distinct from whatever this assessment actually establishes or doesn't establish.

  2. Technician: Thanks for flagging it. What did the customer actually report experiencing in the vehicle?

Your goal

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Customer calls two days after service, reporting the same intermittent rattle. Advisor initially said 'that cannot be the same issue.' Cause and warranty coverage are unknown. Service lead Nia can call at 15:00 to review the report and next arrangements.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

repeat visit
A return to the service business about a continuing or recurring concern.
callback request
A request for a service-team member to contact the customer again.
warranty inquiry
A question about whether stated warranty terms apply, not a confirmed coverage decision.
case reference
The identifier used to link messages, visits, and review activity concerning an issue.

Useful word combinations

  • acknowledge a repeat concern: recognize the customer's ongoing experience
  • link the previous repair order: connect relevant history without assuming cause
  • refer a warranty inquiry: seek review of the applicable terms
  • confirm callback ownership: identify who will respond and when

C Notice the language

Correct a dismissive response

A repeat symptom does not prove the same cause, a failed repair, or warranty coverage. It deserves a factual review.

  • I spoke too definitively when I said ___.
  • You report ___ again; we need to review ___.
  • ___ will call at ___ to discuss ___.

Improve this: "That can't happen after our work."

See a clearer version

You have reported the concern again; we'll review it alongside the earlier job.

The revision removes an unsupported denial without inventing a cause.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Customer: Are you saying the repair definitely failed?
Advisor: No; the cause remains ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. unconfirmed

  1. A. completed Completion does not describe an unknown cause.
  2. B. confirmed No technical review has established the cause.
  3. C. replaced Replacement is an action, not the evidence status.
  4. D. unconfirmed Correct. The cause has not yet been established.
2. Customer: Does the callback approve warranty coverage?
Advisor: No; it arranges a ____ of your inquiry.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. review

  1. A. review Correct. A review considers the inquiry without deciding it.
  2. B. guarantee The callback is not a coverage guarantee.
  3. C. payment No payment decision has been confirmed here.
  4. D. replacement No replacement has been approved by this call.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Correcting an overconfident denial

An advisor repairs an earlier response to a repeat concern.

  1. Advisor: I spoke too definitively when I said it couldn't be the same issue.
  2. Customer: I heard the rattle again two days after service. I need that taken seriously.
  3. Advisor: We'll review your report alongside the earlier repair order without assuming its cause.
  4. Customer: Are you promising the next visit is covered by warranty, or is that undecided?
  5. Advisor: Coverage is unconfirmed. Nia can call at three to discuss the report and next arrangements.
  6. Customer: Please don't treat my warranty inquiry as proof that the earlier work failed.
  7. Advisor: I won't. A recurring symptom, its cause, and applicable coverage need separate review.
  8. Customer: Can the callback use the same case reference so I don't repeat everything?
  9. Advisor: Yes. I'll link the previous visit and today's report for Nia's callback.

2. The same symptom may have a different cause

A service advisor takes a repeat-visit inquiry.

  1. Customer: The display went blank again. Does that prove the same component failed twice?
  2. Advisor: I can't establish that from the symptom alone. The technician needs to review the recurrence.

  4. Advisor: I will. I'll record your new report with today's date and the earlier reference.
  5. Customer: I'd also like someone to explain whether any warranty terms apply to this concern.
  6. Advisor: I can refer that warranty inquiry, but I can't confirm coverage before review.
  7. Customer: Does asking the question automatically book another assessment or approve a diagnostic charge?
  8. Advisor: No. We need to discuss any proposed appointment, scope, and terms separately.
  9. Customer: Then for now I'm requesting a callback, not approving a new job.

A customer asks about a promised service response.

  1. Customer: I was promised a callback yesterday afternoon, but I didn't receive one.
  2. Advisor: I'm sorry we missed that contact commitment. Let me check the actual case status.
