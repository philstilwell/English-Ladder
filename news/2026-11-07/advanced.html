Katherine Ryan on Grief and Relief After Russell Brand’s Conviction

📅 November 07, 2026 - Katherine Ryan on Grief and Relief After Russell Brand’s Conviction

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Comedian Katherine Ryan described feeling both grief and relief after Russell Brand was convicted of rape and sexual assault. She said many women had been speaking privately after Thursday’s verdict and described their response as a nuanced mix of emotions. In an earlier documentary interview with Louis Theroux, Ryan had said there was a “predator” in the entertainment industry. In 2022, Ryan described naming the man as a “litigious minefield”. She said she had appeared on a television programme with him and had confronted him directly. There was speculation that Ryan was referring to her time on Roast Battle with Brand in 2018, but she has not confirmed that she meant him and did not name the programme. On her podcast, Ryan said the verdict had left her emotionally unsettled and that she was in awe of the survivors. She stressed that the survivors had come forward despite Brand’s wealth and influence and the prospect of attacks from his fans. Ryan acknowledged that some justice had been done. However much justice is done, Ryan said, those who were hurt will continue to live with the consequences. Brand was convicted on one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault. The court acquitted him of two other rape charges and an allegation of attempted indecent assault. Ahead of Monday’s sentencing, the judge told Brand to expect a substantial custodial term, meaning a lengthy period in prison. Before the trial, Brand made an unsuccessful bid to have the case dismissed. His lawyers argued that the publicity following the Dispatches programme was so prejudicial that he could not receive a fair trial. Police said Brand was also under investigation over further rape allegations made by two women. Former BBC One controller Lorraine Heggessey questioned how Brand had avoided being held to account for so long. Heggessey suggested that one factor was Brand’s ability to draw the young viewers many broadcasters covet.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. nuanced (adjective): Involving subtle differences or layers, as when several emotions coexist and a single label would oversimplify them.
2. litigious minefield (noun phrase): A situation in which what someone says or does could expose them to legal action, with risks that are difficult to anticipate or manage.
3. speculation (uncountable noun): Discussion about what might be true when the available information leaves the answer uncertain.
4. in awe of (idiomatic prepositional expression): Feeling deep admiration or respect for someone, here for the courage the survivors showed.
5. prospect (countable noun): The possibility of a future event that someone has reason to take seriously.
6. count (countable noun): An individual charge in a criminal case that can receive its own verdict.
7. custodial term (noun phrase): A formal legal expression for a period of imprisonment imposed as part of a sentence.
8. dismissed (past participle): Formally ended by a court, here in reference to the outcome Brand unsuccessfully sought before trial.
9. prejudicial (adjective): Likely to undermine the fairness of a legal proceeding, for example by influencing people’s views before they hear the evidence.
10. held to account (past-participial verb phrase): Made to answer for one’s conduct and face any consequences that follow.
11. covet (verb): Strongly want something considered valuable, here an audience of younger viewers.

Advanced Grammar: Concession with “however much”
Use “however much” before an uncountable noun to introduce a clause meaning “no matter how much”. The amount can vary without changing the outcome in the main clause. Here, the effects of harm persist whatever degree of justice is achieved.
Example from the text: "However much justice is done, Ryan said, those who were hurt will continue to live with the consequences."
