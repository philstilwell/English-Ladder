Graduate AI Roles Raise Questions About Professional Development

Advanced · C1+

News archive →
📅 November 04, 2026 - Graduate AI Roles Raise Questions About Professional Development

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Although AI is seen as a major factor behind graduate job cuts, companies are also establishing graduate positions specifically focused on the technology. Recent graduate Lotte Underwood works in public relations but spends more time using AI than carrying out conventional public relations work. Her remit includes managing AI agents, checking their results and passing material to colleagues who deal directly with clients and the media. The dedicated AI team has built around 30 agents for activities ranging from industry research and interview preparation to helping clients produce clearer briefs. At engineering software company Stride, graduate Dillan Patel was assigned to develop a chatbot designed to capture the knowledge of a senior employee transitioning into retirement. His subsequent role includes identifying manual or repetitive work that AI could perform across the company. Maxwell envisages graduates challenging an organisation to consider AI holistically, examining both potential applications and opportunities for business growth. If AI makes graduate jobs more integral to an organisation, Maxwell argues, employers can build a more loyal and engaged workforce whose members contribute meaningfully earlier. An analysis of 2.4 million US entry-level jobs found that the roles most influenced by AI were seven times more likely to require traditionally senior abilities, including motivational leadership and strategic decision making. PwC found that entry-level roles with higher expectations had grown by 35% since 2019, whereas other entry-level roles had declined by 10%. Clinton says greater opportunities to implement ideas have brought substantially more responsibility. Clinton argues that advancement is becoming less dependent on accumulated knowledge and more dependent on critical thinking and an understanding of processes. Underwood would like to gain traditional public relations experience beyond her current responsibilities, but remains confident about her future in the agency's growing AI team. Pratt describes a programme in which graduates move through four areas at six-month intervals, learning alongside senior relationship managers. Those graduates learn about financial products, prepare credit papers for lenders and develop customer relationships before progressing to managing entire transactions. Claire Reed cautions that graduate roles dominated by AI will not necessarily support long-term career progression. Reed nevertheless regards a baseline of AI literacy as a requirement for every graduate job. Only when AI expertise is coupled with an understanding of what clients actually want can it support a graduate's career, one contributor argues.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. remit (noun): The range of responsibilities and activities assigned to a particular role.
2. dedicated (adjective): Established specifically for a particular purpose, rather than handling it alongside unrelated work.
3. briefs (plural noun): Documents or instructions setting out what a client wants a piece of work to achieve.
4. transitioning into retirement (participial verb phrase): Moving from employment towards retirement, with emphasis on the process rather than a single departure date.
5. holistically (adverb): By considering how different parts of an organisation connect and affect the whole.
6. integral (adjective): Closely involved in an organisation's central activities and important to how it functions.
7. motivational leadership (noun phrase): The ability to encourage people to commit effort and work towards shared goals.
8. strategic decision making (noun phrase): Choosing actions with an organisation's broader priorities and longer-term direction in mind.
9. critical thinking (noun phrase): Carefully examining information, assumptions and reasoning before reaching a judgement.
10. career progression (noun phrase): Development towards greater responsibility or more advanced positions over a working life.
11. baseline (noun): A minimum starting standard expected of people in a particular situation.
12. coupled with (passive verb phrase): Combined with another quality or resource so that the two operate together.

Advanced Grammar: Inversion after a fronted “only when” clause
A clause beginning with “Only when” restricts the circumstances in which a claim applies. When it comes first, the following main clause places the auxiliary before the subject, as in “can it support”; the “when” clause keeps normal word order.
Example from the text: "Only when AI expertise is coupled with an understanding of what clients actually want can it support a graduate's career, one contributor argues."
