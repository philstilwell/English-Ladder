Course: Warehouse and Distribution English

Lesson 01: Receiving against the expected shipment
Original fictional case: Purchase order 740 expects twenty cartons. Receiving counts twenty-two with matching item labels. The supplier has not explained the additional two; the clerk can flag an overage but cannot approve an order change.
Language workshop: Report the extra quantity precisely
Communication goal: Separate receipt facts from a purchasing decision.
Language guidance: A matching label does not by itself approve an order increase.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): We expected ___; we counted ___. / The labels match, but ___ remains unclear. / Please refer the quantity difference to ___.
Vocabulary: advance shipping notice: A supplier's message describing goods expected to arrive, often shortened to ASN. / receiving discrepancy: A difference between an arriving shipment and its expected quantity, identity, or condition. / purchase order: A buyer's order identifying requested goods and quantities, often shortened to PO. / overage: A quantity received above the quantity expected for that shipment.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Buyer: only twenty were ordered; you must ask the supplier about the additional two.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: A colleague suggests replacing the expected quantity with the count. Explain why both figures matter.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Purchase order 740 expects twenty cartons, and our receiving count is twenty-two. The item labels match, but we haven't confirmed why there are two extra cartons. I'll report an overage against this receipt. Please keep the expected quantity unchanged until the purchasing contact clarifies whether the additional goods belong to this order.
END REFERENCE

Lesson 02: Locating and identifying stock
Original fictional case: A picker needs item BK-14-B, blue folders. Bin A12 contains BK-14-G, green folders. The bin sign says BK-14-B. Inventory clerk can check the putaway record; no move or substitution has been approved.
Language workshop: Read beyond a shared code prefix
Communication goal: Clarify an item-location conflict before relying on the location sign.
Language guidance: Product, variant, lot, and location identify different things. Repeat the full code.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): The task specifies ___; the carton shows ___. / Is ___ the location code or the item code? / Please check the record for ___.
Vocabulary: stock keeping unit: An identifier for a distinct stocked product or variant, commonly shortened to SKU. / bin location: The coded storage position assigned to stock within a warehouse. / lot number: An identifier linking units produced or supplied as one particular batch. / putaway: The placement and recording of received goods in their assigned storage locations.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Inventory clerk: task and sign say blue; carton labels say green; investigate the putaway history.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: A matching photograph is offered as proof. Ask for the full item identifier instead.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: The task calls for BK-14-B, blue folders, but the cartons in A12 are labeled BK-14-G, green folders. The bin sign agrees with the task, not the carton labels. Could you check the putaway record for these two variants? I haven't treated the shared BK-14 prefix as proof that they are interchangeable.
