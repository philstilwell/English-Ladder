5. Finish with two new four-choice collocation questions for later review, with a clearly separate answer key. Do not claim to schedule a reminder.

Lesson 01: Confirming the assignment and its limits
Original fictional case: A commercial assignment says 'conference room: tables and floor.' The client asks for 'everything' before a 3:00 meeting. The worker is assigned table surfaces and the floor, not high windows or personal equipment. The supervisor can clarify extra work; the meeting time doesn't expand permission.
Language workshop: Turn 'everything' into named tasks
Communication goal: Clarify the requested surfaces and the boundary of the current assignment.
Language guidance: A broad request may contain extra work or items the cleaner should not move. Name the included work and ask about the rest.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): My assignment includes ___, but not ___. / When you say ___, which surfaces do you mean? / I'll ask the supervisor whether ___ can be added.
Vocabulary: assignment sheet: A list identifying the rooms, areas, or cleaning tasks allocated to a worker. / service scope: The specific cleaning work included in an assignment or agreement. / touchpoint: A surface or item frequently touched by people, such as a handle. / spot cleaning: Cleaning a particular marked or affected area rather than the entire surface.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Client: you want table surfaces and floor ready first; windows are an extra request.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: The client adds laptops to 'tables.' Clarify that cleaning around personal equipment differs from handling it.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: The assignment covers the conference-room table surfaces and floor. When you say everything, are you also asking about the high windows and personal equipment? Those aren't included in my current task list. I can complete the listed work and ask my supervisor to review the additional request before anyone promises it for three o'clock.

START PROMPT
TASK: Interactive workplace role-play

2. After I choose, identify who you will play and the immediate communication goal. Play the other professional; where a meeting requires a third person, label each of your speakers clearly. 3. Let the exchange develop across six to ten learner turns, or end earlier if I type "feedback". Respond to what I actually say. Include a plausible clarification, disagreement or tradeoff without silently changing the starting facts. Mark any added constraint as a fictional second-round variation. 4. Stay in role during the exchange. If my meaning is unclear, ask for clarification naturally. Give a hint only if I ask or cannot proceed. Do not deliver a model conversation in advance.
5. At the debrief, quote two of my phrases: one successful choice and one worth improving. Check factual accuracy, language, register and whether the next step was clear. Give up to three focused suggestions. Ask me to retry the weakest turn; wait. Only then offer an alternative wording and a harder replay.
Use voice replies when available. If I prefer selections, offer four plausible spoken replies and explain my choice before continuing. Do not require written compositions.

Dialogue 02: A declined visit is not a complaint
Original fictional setting: An attendant checks how to report a guest's decision without adding an unsupported explanation.
Professional roles: Attendant / Supervisor
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Attendant: The guest in 206 declined cleaning today but accepted fresh towels. How should I describe the visit?
Supervisor: For the service refusal, record cleaning declined and two towels delivered. Did they ask for anything else?
Attendant: They asked us not to return later today. They didn't complain about the previous cleaning.
Supervisor: Then don't mark it as a cleanliness complaint or assume they're dissatisfied with the service.
Attendant: I had wondered whether they were upset, but that was only my interpretation of their brief answer.
Supervisor: Keep that interpretation out of the service record. The stated preference is enough for the next attendant.
Attendant: Should I leave the room on tomorrow's usual schedule, or does today's refusal cancel future visits too?
Supervisor: Today's statement concerns today. Follow the normal scheduling arrangement and check any separate request about later service.
Attendant: I'll tell the next attendant: two towels delivered, no return visit requested today.
Supervisor: Yes. That prevents another attendant from offering the same visit again this afternoon without a reason.
Attendant: The towels reached the guest at the door, and I didn't enter the room during that delivery.
Supervisor: Include that if our record asks for delivery location. It describes the visit without inventing a motive.
Vocabulary in this exchange: service refusal: An occupant's decision to decline an offered cleaning visit or task. / cleaning: Removing dirt and other unwanted material from a surface.

Dialogue 03: A shorthand instruction needs a task definition
Original fictional setting: A new cleaner asks the lead what an unfamiliar instruction actually requires.
Professional roles: Cleaner / Team lead
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Cleaner: The assignment says 'full treatment' for the meeting-room handles. I don't recognize that term on our task list.
Team lead: That's too vague for a new assignment. Which standard task names do you have in your training materials?
Cleaner: They distinguish cleaning from disinfection, but this note doesn't say which task was requested for the handles.
Team lead: I'll clarify the requested standard with the supervisor rather than assume the writer meant both tasks.
Cleaner: Thank you. The client also asked whether a clean appearance proves the second task was completed.
Team lead: It doesn't. We need the actual assigned work and its completion record, not a conclusion from appearance.
Cleaner: Should I tell the client nothing has been done, or explain which part is already complete?
Team lead: Tell them the handles were cleaned under the listed task, while the additional request is still being clarified.
Cleaner: I'll use those words. I don't want the vague phrase to make completed cleaning sound like unfinished work.
Team lead: Exactly. Nor should it turn completed cleaning into a claim about a different task that wasn't confirmed.
Cleaner: Once the supervisor clarifies the extra request, can you tell me who will perform it if it's assigned?
Team lead: Yes. I'll confirm the task and who will do it.
