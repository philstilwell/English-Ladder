📅 November 14, 2026 - Entertainment Abuse Stories Reflect Years of Change

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Recent abuse stories in entertainment reflect the consequences of changes that began years ago, rather than a sudden cultural change. The television programme Married at First Sight UK has been cancelled following allegations of rape. The men accused of rape and sexual misconduct on the programme deny all the allegations. The recent stories began with journalistic investigations that started years ago. Investigations usually take a long time because journalists gather evidence, check information and ask people to respond. According to broadcaster Kirsty Wark, seeing other women come forward has given more women the courage to share their experiences. In the past, women feared retaliation, meaning harmful action against them for reporting abuse. Unions say junior freelance workers, who often have an uncertain income, still find it difficult to report problems. Even well-known people may fear that raising concerns will cost them work, comedian Lucy Beaumont said. Social media has also helped encourage people to report their experiences. Harriet Wistrich from the Centre for Women's Justice says wealthy or famous people often use legal measures to discourage reporting. There is growing recognition that leaders of media organisations have a responsibility to address abuse, rather than leaving victims to act alone. Philippa Childs says the creative industries' current internal reporting systems are inadequate, meaning they do not work well enough.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. consequences (plural noun): Results of earlier actions, events or changes.
2. allegations (plural noun): Claims that someone has done something wrong, which still need to be proved.
3. investigations (plural noun): Careful efforts to discover the facts about a situation.
4. evidence (uncountable noun): Information or material that helps show whether something is true.
5. come forward (phrasal verb): Offer information about an experience, especially something harmful or difficult.
6. retaliation (uncountable noun): Action intended to harm someone because they have complained or reported a problem.
7. freelance workers (plural noun phrase): People who take separate jobs or projects instead of having a permanent job with one employer.
8. responsibility (noun): A duty to take action or make sure something is handled properly.
9. inadequate (adjective): Not good enough for what is needed.

Grammar Focus: Non-defining relative clauses with who
Use “who” to add information about people. Put commas around the extra information when it does not identify which people you mean. The main sentence still makes sense if you remove this extra information.
Example from the text: "Unions say junior freelance workers, who often have an uncertain income, still find it difficult to report problems."
