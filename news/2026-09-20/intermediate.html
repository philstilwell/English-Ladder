German Voting: Merz Under Strain, Future Uncertain

📅 September 20, 2026 - German Voting: Merz Under Strain, Future Uncertain

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Local voting is under way in two German states on Sunday, which could increase political strain on Friedrich Merz. Opinion polls suggest Merz's party, the Christian Democratic Union (CDU), is at risk of damaging defeats in Berlin and Mecklenburg-Vorpommerm. These recent votes follow a major win for the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) party in Saxony-Anhalt two weeks ago. There is speculation that Merz might be replaced as the country's leader mid-term if his party's political luck does not improve. He states he will continue, but he is facing low public ratings amid talk of a leadership change. Merz's conservatives might lose influence in Berlin as they compete with Die Linke (The Left) for the top position. The AfD aims to build on its recent big win by ranking first in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern. However, the centre-left SPD has made a comeback and is now competing for the highest place. The state premier, Manuela Schwesig, is trying to gather votes against the AfD. Merz's supporters say he has faced a difficult period with global events and has inherited many national problems. But any belief in his ability to solve these issues seems to be fading. His direct communication style has caused annoyance, for instance, his dismissal of 'work-life balance'. The national economy is struggling, energy costs are high, and intense discussions about immigration are causing widespread sadness. Merz has promised to carry on as his government plans to introduce economic, tax, and welfare reforms this autumn. This Sunday's vote presents a risk for Merz: that he is already out of time.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. political strain (noun phrase): strong demands or difficulties related to politics, often causing tension.
2. damaging defeats (noun phrase): losses in an election or competition that cause serious harm or problems for the losing side.
3. major win (noun phrase): a very important or significant success, especially in a competition or an election.
4. speculation (noun): guesses or theories about something without having all the facts, often about future events or reasons.
5. mid-term (adjective): happening in the middle of a period of office for a political leader or government, or in the middle of a school year.
6. leadership change (noun phrase): an event where a new person takes control of an organization or government, replacing the previous leader.
7. compete (verb): to try to win something or be more successful than others, often against rivals.
8. fading (verb): slowly disappearing or losing strength, color, or freshness over time.
9. annoyance (noun): the feeling of being slightly angry or bothered by something that is irritating.
10. dismissal (noun): the act of treating something as not important or not worth considering seriously.
11. widespread sadness (noun phrase): a feeling of unhappiness or gloom that is affecting many people or a large area.
12. introduce (verb): to formally bring a new law, system, or product into use or existence.

Grammar Focus: Relative Clauses with "which"
Relative clauses beginning with "which" give extra information about a thing or idea mentioned just before them. They connect two ideas into one sentence and are often used for non-essential information, set apart by a comma. In the example, "which could increase political strain on Friedrich Merz" tells us more about the "Local voting."
Example from the text: "Local voting is under way in two German states on Sunday, which could increase political strain on Friedrich Merz."
