01Policy Implementation and Program RulesMake a complex point clear · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Explain a useful distinction in plain English.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A resident mistakes draft eligibility guidance for a final rule. The office has published current guidance and a separate proposal.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

policy
Formal rule or standard that guides decisions and behavior.
eligibility
Rules that determine whether a person, account, provider, service, or request qualifies for a program, benefit, or action.
implementation
Putting an agreed plan, product, or process into operation.
public guidance
Official information explaining how a public program or process operates.

03 · Notice the language

Make a complex point clear

Start with the reader's question. Explain one unfamiliar term with familiar words, then give a relevant example or contrast. Check understanding without asking only 'Do you understand?'.

  • In this context, ... means ... .
  • The difference is that ... .
  • How would you explain that distinction to a colleague?

Improve this: We need to operationalize the implementation.

See a clearer version

We need to put the plan into practice.

The revised sentence names the action in familiar words. Specialist vocabulary is useful when it adds precision, but repeating abstract nouns can hide the meaning.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence explains 'backlog' rather than repeating it?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. The backlog is the work still waiting to be completed.

  1. A. The backlog must be reviewed at the backlog meeting. This describes a meeting but leaves the term unexplained.
  2. B. The backlog is the work still waiting to be completed. This gives the meaning in familiar words.
  3. C. The backlog is an important operational backlog. Repeating the same term does not define it.
2. Which follow-up best checks whether your explanation was clear?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. How would you describe the next step in your own words?

  1. A. How would you describe the next step in your own words? An open request lets the listener show their understanding.
  2. B. I have explained the next step, so shall we finish? Finishing the conversation gives no evidence of understanding.
  3. C. You understand everything now, don't you? This leading question makes it easy to agree without showing understanding.

05 · Say it

Practice with a partner

Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.

Partner's role and follow-up

You are unfamiliar with one technical term in the case. Ask for a plain-English explanation and then paraphrase it. Do not pretend to understand a term you cannot explain.

Try a harder second round

Your listener is new to this field. Replace two specialist expressions with clear explanations without changing the meaning.

Studying alone? Give both sides of the conversation aloud. Pause before answering the follow-up, then repeat with fewer notes.

02Public Meetings and Stakeholder InputHelp a group reach a clear next step · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Include relevant voices and clarify the decision process.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

At a public meeting, two speakers use most of the time and several people have not been heard. The meeting has an announced participation process.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

public comment
Input submitted by members of the public in a defined consultation process.
stakeholder
Person or group with an interest, risk, authority, or dependency in the work.
public record
A record held by a public body that is subject to applicable records rules.
facilitation
Guiding a discussion so people can contribute, understand differences, and reach useful next steps.

03 · Notice the language

Help a group reach a clear next step

Summarize the issue neutrally, invite missing perspectives, and distinguish discussion from decision. State who can decide and record any unresolved question. Do not assume silence means agreement.

  • Let's hear ... before we decide.
  • The point we still need to resolve is ... .
  • Who will make this decision, and what do they need?

Improve this: Nobody objected, so everyone agrees.

See a clearer version

Before I record agreement, does anyone have a concern or need more time?

The revised question gives people a way to raise a concern. Silence can have several meanings, especially in unfamiliar or unequal-power situations.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which phrase invites a missing perspective?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. We haven't heard from the remote team yet; what is your view?

  1. A. We haven't heard from the remote team yet; what is your view? It invites participation without interpreting silence as consent.
  2. B. The remote team has been quiet, so they must agree. Silence does not establish agreement.
  3. C. We can record their agreement without asking them. Recording agreement without checking may misrepresent the team's view.
2. Which closing statement records an unresolved decision accurately?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. Approval is pending; the sponsor will review the two options.

  1. A. We discussed the options, so approval is complete. Discussion does not itself constitute approval.
  2. B. Everyone spoke, so the decision must be final. Participation alone does not establish a decision.
  3. C. Approval is pending; the sponsor will review the two options. This distinguishes discussion from approval and identifies the next reviewer.

05 · Say it

Practice with a partner

Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.

Partner's role and follow-up

You hold a relevant concern that has not been discussed. Raise it when invited and ask who will decide. Help the facilitator state one unresolved question accurately.

Try a harder second round

A participant interrupts or the meeting is running out of time. Protect a turn to speak and close with an accurate decision record.

Studying alone? Give both sides of the conversation aloud. Pause before answering the follow-up, then repeat with fewer notes.

03Procurement and Vendor SelectionAsk a precise question · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Clarify missing information before responding.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A procurement evaluation note uses "best value" without linking the judgment to published evaluation criteria.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

RFP
Request for proposals: an invitation to submit an approach and offer for specified work.
evaluation criteria
The stated standards used to compare or assess options or performance.
bidder
A person or organization submitting an offer in a procurement process.
procurement rule
A requirement governing how purchasing or supplier selection must be conducted.

03 · Notice the language

Ask a precise question

Name the exact word, fact, or requirement you need clarified. Ask one focused question at a time. Repeat your understanding and give the other person a chance to correct it.

  • When you say ..., do you mean ...?
  • Could you clarify which ...?
  • So, my understanding is ... . Is that right?

Improve this: Could you explain what does this mean?

See a clearer version

Could you explain what this means?

After an introductory phrase such as 'Could you explain', the embedded question uses statement word order: what this means.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which indirect question has the usual word order?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. Could you confirm when the review starts?

  1. A. Could you confirm when does the review start? Remove 'does' and use statement word order after 'confirm'.
  2. B. Could you confirm when starts the review? Place the subject 'the review' before 'starts'.
  3. C. Could you confirm when the review starts? The embedded question uses subject then verb: the review starts.
2. Someone says the report is 'ready.' Which question best clarifies its approval status?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Has the report been approved, or is it ready for review?

  1. A. Who will present the report at the meeting? This asks about the presenter, not the report's approval status.
  2. B. Has the report been approved, or is it ready for review? This question distinguishes two meanings of 'ready' that affect the next action.
  3. C. Can you send the report to me this afternoon? This asks about delivery timing, not whether approval has happened.

Partner's role and follow-up

Try a harder second round

The other person uses an ambiguous word such as 'ready', 'approved', or 'urgent'. Clarify it before continuing.

04Grants, Compliance, and ReportingAsk for an actionable next step · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Make a request with a clear action, purpose, and realistic timing.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A grant report contains a total expenditure figure but lacks the requested supporting categories.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

grant
Funding provided for an agreed purpose subject to stated conditions.
subrecipient
An organization receiving a portion of an award to carry out part of a funded program.
allowable cost
An expense permitted under the rules or agreement governing a grant or program.
reporting
Communicating required or useful information in a defined form or process.

03 · Notice the language

Ask for an actionable next step

State the action and the information needed. Explain why it matters and specify a deadline only when one is given or agreed. 'Could you' is polite; repeated apologies can obscure a legitimate request. Urgent situations may require a direct request.

  • Could you provide ... so that ...?
  • Please confirm ... by [agreed time].
  • If that timing is not possible, please let me know ... .

Improve this: Please revert soonest with the necessary.

See a clearer version

Could you send the missing document and confirm when it will be available?

The revision names the document and requested response. It avoids expressions that may be unfamiliar or ambiguous for an international audience.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. What does 'Please send it by Thursday' normally mean?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Send it no later than Thursday.

  1. A. Send it on a date later than Thursday. A later date does not meet a 'by Thursday' deadline.
  2. B. Send it no later than Thursday. 'By' sets the latest point, while 'after' refers to a later time.
  3. C. Send it only after Thursday. This reverses the meaning of the deadline.
2. Which request gives the recipient enough detail to act?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review?

  1. A. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review? The requested action and information are explicit.
  2. B. Could you deal with the relevant matters soon? 'Relevant matters' and 'soon' leave the task unclear.
  3. C. Could you do the needful for the situation? This expression does not identify the specific action needed.

Practice with a partner

Partner's role and follow-up

You can help but need a clear request and its purpose. Ask what is required and whether the timing is fixed or negotiable.

Try a harder second round

The other person cannot meet the requested timing. Clarify an alternative without inventing authority to change a formal deadline.

Studying alone? Give both sides of the conversation aloud. Pause before answering the follow-up, then repeat with fewer notes.

05Interagency CoordinationTransfer information and responsibility · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Give a handoff that the receiving person can confirm.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

Two agencies are coordinating a service request. Each assumes the other will contact the resident.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

jurisdiction
The legal authority of a court or public body over a matter or area.
memorandum of understanding
A document recording parties' shared intentions or arrangements; legal effects depend on its terms.
data sharing
Making data available to another person or organization under appropriate conditions.
owner
Named person or role accountable for a decision, action, deliverable, or risk.

03 · Notice the language

Transfer information and responsibility

State the current situation, relevant background, open task, and receiving role. Ask for acknowledgment or repeat-back of the critical item. Sending a message and transferring responsibility are not always the same thing.

  • The current status is ...; the outstanding item is ... .
  • The record shows ... .
  • Please confirm who has accepted ... .

Improve this: Someone should follow up on this.

See a clearer version

Please confirm which team has accepted the follow-up task.

The revised sentence asks for an identifiable receiving team instead of leaving responsibility with an unspecified 'someone'.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence checks that responsibility has been accepted?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. Please confirm who will complete the outstanding check.

  1. A. Please confirm who will complete the outstanding check. It asks for an identifiable person or team responsible for the task.
  2. B. I mentioned the check in my earlier email. Mentioning a task does not show that anyone accepted it.
  3. C. The check appears somewhere in the notes. A written record alone does not identify who will act.
2. Which response best checks a critical handoff detail?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. So the result is pending and your team will follow up; is that correct?

  1. A. I assume all results are final, so we can finish. It changes pending information into a final result.
  2. B. I saw the message, so no clarification is needed. Receiving a message is not the same as confirming its meaning.
  3. C. So the result is pending and your team will follow up; is that correct? This repeats the status and responsibility and invites correction.

06Constituent Services and EscalationRepair a misunderstanding · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Acknowledge a communication problem and give a corrected message.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A resident has been redirected between offices without an explanation of who can address the issue.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

constituent
A person served or represented by a public official or institution.
casework
Work performed to help an individual navigate a service, issue, or administrative process.
appeal
A request for a decision to be reviewed through an established process.
authority
The recognized power to decide, act, or provide an authoritative basis for a conclusion.

03 · Notice the language

Repair a misunderstanding

Name what was unclear or wrong, acknowledge its effect, and correct it. A brief apology can help, followed by a practical next step. Avoid explaining away the other person's reaction or promising an outcome you cannot control.

  • My earlier message did not make ... clear.
  • I'm sorry for ... . The correct information is ... .
  • Let me check that the revised explanation addresses ... .

Improve this: I'm sorry if you failed to understand.

See a clearer version

I'm sorry my message did not explain the timing clearly.

The revision takes responsibility for the message without blaming the listener. It identifies what needs to be corrected.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which apology takes responsibility for unclear wording?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. I'm sorry my earlier wording was unclear.

  1. A. I'm sorry you did not read it correctly. This shifts blame to the reader.
  2. B. I'm sorry if you chose to misunderstand it. This attributes an unverified motive to the listener.
  3. C. I'm sorry my earlier wording was unclear. This acknowledges the speaker's contribution to the problem.
2. What should follow an apology for an incorrect date?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. The corrected date and the current confirmation status.

  1. A. A new guaranteed date even if it is unverified. An unsupported new promise can create a second misunderstanding.
  2. B. The corrected date and the current confirmation status. A repair needs usable corrected information, including any uncertainty.
  3. C. A longer explanation that never states the correct date. The reader still lacks the information they need.

07Transparency, Records, and EthicsMatch certainty to evidence · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Separate observations, assumptions, and conclusions.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A records request involves material that may require review for an exemption. An initial note says all material will automatically be released.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

public records
Records held by public bodies and governed by applicable access and retention rules.
exemption
A defined exception to a rule or requirement under the applicable process.
conflict of interest
A situation where another interest may improperly influence a person's duties or judgment.
ethics
Principles and standards used to judge responsible conduct.

03 · Notice the language

Match certainty to evidence

Say what the evidence shows, identify what is still unknown, and limit the conclusion to that evidence. Words such as 'may', 'suggests', and 'in this sample' are useful when the uncertainty is real. Do not soften an urgent, verified safety concern.

  • The available evidence shows ... .
  • This may indicate ..., but ... .
  • We have not yet established whether ... .

Improve this: The pilot proves this will work everywhere.

See a clearer version

The pilot worked in the tested setting; performance elsewhere remains untested.

The revision states the finding and its scope. It avoids turning limited evidence into a universal claim.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence preserves uncertainty about an unconfirmed cause?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. The change may have contributed to the delay.

  1. A. The change definitely caused the delay. 'Definitely' presents the cause as certain.
  2. B. The change may have contributed to the delay. 'May have contributed' presents a possibility without claiming the cause is established.
  3. C. The change must have caused the delay. 'Must have' expresses a strong inference that the evidence may not support.
2. A result comes from a small pilot in one location. Which phrase accurately limits the claim?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. In this pilot, the observed result was ...

  1. A. In this pilot, the observed result was ... This locates the finding in the setting actually studied.
  2. B. In every location, the result will be ... The pilot does not establish future results everywhere.
  3. C. Without any uncertainty, the result is ... This removes uncertainty rather than explaining its limits.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence explains 'backlog' rather than repeating it?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. The backlog is the work still waiting to be completed.

  1. A. The backlog is the work still waiting to be completed. This gives the meaning in familiar words.
  2. B. The backlog is an important operational backlog. Repeating the same term does not define it.
  3. C. The backlog must be reviewed at the backlog meeting. This describes a meeting but leaves the term unexplained.
2. Which follow-up best checks whether your explanation was clear?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. How would you describe the next step in your own words?

  1. A. I have explained the next step, so shall we finish? Finishing the conversation gives no evidence of understanding.
  2. B. You understand everything now, don't you? This leading question makes it easy to agree without showing understanding.
  3. C. How would you describe the next step in your own words? An open request lets the listener show their understanding.
