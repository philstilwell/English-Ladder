Fossil Fuel Dispute Leaves COP31 in Deadlock

📅 November 22, 2026 - Fossil Fuel Dispute Leaves COP31 in Deadlock

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Negotiations at the COP31 climate summit in Turkey remained in deadlock, with countries unable to agree even after discussions had overrun by more than a day. The EU and Pacific island nations opposed the latest text because it omitted any reference to reducing global demand for fossil fuels. A second sticking point concerned the African Group's request for stronger financial support for adaptation to climate impacts, which the EU had previously opposed. Delegations moving their return flights from Sunday to Monday provided a further indication that agreement remained elusive. Should countries fail to reach the required consensus, the negotiations could be suspended. The summit could then end without agreement and be reconvened next year, an outcome with only one precedent since these climate meetings began in 1995. Ministers sought to have a commitment to shift away from oil, gas and coal reinstated after fossil fuel producers led by Saudi Arabia pushed for its removal. References to carbon capture, use and storage, a technology that stores released emissions underground, were criticised as a licence to continue burning oil and gas. The proposed agreement could, for the first time at such a summit, acknowledge that the goal of keeping global temperature rise below 1.5C has been missed. Australia's climate change and energy minister, Chris Bowen, resisted calls to include language on fossil fuels. He argued that the summit should prioritise implementation, meaning putting measures into practice, and focus on the transition towards clean energy. Meanwhile, renewable energy was expanding at unprecedented rates, with solar panels making the largest contribution to meeting new global energy demand that year and outstripping oil and gas. One reported proposal would keep fossil fuels off the table in exchange for Saudi Arabia and other fossil fuel producers pledging to obtain 35% of their energy consumption from electricity by 2035. An earlier objective of increasing adaptation funding to $120 billion by 2035 had been removed from the text. Ghana's environment minister presented funding as a rightful claim on behalf of people bearing the brunt of developed countries' activities. The departure of negotiating teams could also leave too few nations present to pass a global agreement.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. deadlock (noun): A situation in which opposing positions prevent those involved from reaching agreement.
2. sticking point (noun phrase): A particular unresolved issue that obstructs a wider agreement.
3. reconvened (verb, past participle): Brought together again after a meeting has ended or been interrupted.
4. reinstated (verb, past participle): Restored to a document or position after previously being removed.
5. licence (noun): An apparent justification or permission to act, used critically here because the behaviour is considered harmful.
6. implementation (noun): The process of putting an agreed policy or measure into practice.
7. unprecedented (adjective): Not previously experienced or recorded in the relevant context, here describing the rate of growth.
8. outstripping (verb, present participle): Exceeding something else in a particular measure, here the contribution to meeting new energy demand.
9. off the table (idiomatic phrase): Excluded from the matters being considered or negotiated.
10. bearing the brunt (participial phrase): Experiencing the greatest share of something harmful or difficult.

Advanced Grammar: Formal conditional inversion with “should”
Place “should” before the subject and a base-form verb to introduce a possible future condition in a formal register. This replaces an “if” clause without predicting that the event will occur; “could” also leaves the consequence uncertain.
Example from the text: "Should countries fail to reach the required consensus, the negotiations could be suspended."
