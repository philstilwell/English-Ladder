📅 October 19, 2026 - Japan Aims to Retrieve Pristine Dust from Martian Moon Phobos
An ambitious Japanese space expedition prepares to retrieve a pristine sample from Mars' moon Phobos to uncover its origins.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

A pioneering space expedition is scheduled to depart on Monday, embarking on an ambitious endeavor to retrieve material from Mars' moon Phobos. The bold initiative, spearheaded by the Japanese Space Agency, aims to resolve the enduring mystery surrounding the formation of these Martian satellites. The automated rover will scout out a suitable location so that the main probe can subsequently touch down securely. Landing on such a small celestial body poses a formidable challenge because its remarkably weak gravity is dwarfed by that of the adjacent planet. The collected material will be securely packed for transit, culminating in a 2031 descent into the Australian desert. Experts stress that such a minuscule quantity provides ample material for comprehensive laboratory studies, proving that massive amounts are unnecessary. Scientists anticipate that examining the chemistry and mineral composition will produce distinct results to evaluate the conflicting hypotheses. Furthermore, experts harbor hopes that proximity has allowed ejected grains from the Martian crust to become embedded directly into Phobos. This could provide unprecedented, direct information regarding the chemical makeup and geological history of Mars itself. Securing uncontaminated matter is paramount for researchers, as existing Martian meteorites inevitably suffer terrestrial contamination upon atmospheric entry.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. endeavor (noun): A determined effort or serious attempt to achieve a challenging goal.
2. retrieve (verb): To recover or bring something back from a specific remote location.
3. spearheaded (verb): Led, directed, or initiated a significant undertaking or campaign.
4. formidable (adjective): Presenting a severe or daunting challenge that requires great effort to overcome.
5. dwarfed (verb): Made to seem much smaller or less significant by comparison to something substantially larger.
6. culminating (verb): Concluding an extended process or series of events with a significant final stage or climax.
7. minuscule (adjective): Exceptionally small in size, scale, or quantity.
8. ample (adjective): Plentiful and entirely sufficient for a specific purpose or requirement.
9. hypotheses (noun): Proposed scientific explanations or theories that require further testing and evidence.
10. embedded (verb): Fixed firmly and deeply within a surrounding substance or material.
11. paramount (adjective): Of supreme importance, outweighing all other considerations.

Advanced Grammar: Reduced Non-defining Relative Clauses
A past participle phrase like "spearheaded by the Japanese Space Agency" functions as a reduced non-defining relative clause (shortened from "which is spearheaded by"). Placed between commas, it adds essential background information about the subject without interrupting the main syntactic flow of the sentence.
Example from the text: "The bold initiative, spearheaded by the Japanese Space Agency, aims to resolve the enduring mystery surrounding the formation of these Martian satellites."

📅 October 18, 2026 - Violent Demonstrations Erupt Across France Amid Economic Pressures
Widespread disorder and violent confrontations have swept across France as over half a million people protest economic pressures and dilapidated facilities.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Widespread disorder erupted across France as more than half a million people joined demonstrations against economic pressures. The government characterised the disturbances as an escalation in violence, detaining at least 1,200 individuals who had taken to the streets. In Paris, significant confrontations occurred into the evening, prompting riot police to deploy tear gas and water cannon against the crowds. As the day wore on, some factions ignited fires in the street and torched vehicles in iconic central locations. Emergency crews also battled flames at the Limoges town hall, which the local prefecture confirmed caused part of the building to collapse. Furthermore, a historic tourist office was burnt in Lyon, a destruction the local mayor described as a tragedy. Hundreds of thousands of students have recently mobilised against inadequate staffing and dilapidated facilities in secondary schools. Their numbers have been bolstered by the prominent yellow vest movement and other demonstrating groups. This latest wave of turbulence arrives as France enters a period of deep political uncertainty. Consequently, the government has made apparent attempts at concessions, with the education minister pledging 3,000 additional supply teachers.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. erupted (verb): Broke out suddenly, violently, or dramatically.
2. characterised (verb): Described the distinctive nature or features of something in a specific way.
3. escalation (noun): A rapid or significant increase in the intensity, seriousness, or magnitude of a situation.
4. detaining (verb): Keeping individuals in official custody, typically for questioning or for participating in illegal activities.
5. confrontations (noun): Hostile, argumentative, or physical clashes between opposing parties.
6. deploy (verb): To bring resources or equipment into effective action.
7. torched (verb): Intentionally set fire to something in order to destroy it.
8. dilapidated (adjective): In a state of disrepair, ruin, or physical deterioration, often as a result of age or neglect.
9. bolstered (verb): Supported, strengthened, or increased in numbers or morale.
10. concessions (noun): Things that are granted, yielded, or compromised on, especially in response to demands.

Advanced Grammar: Using "with" + noun + participle
In advanced reporting, the preposition "with" followed by a noun (e.g., "the education minister") and an -ing participle (e.g., "pledging") is often used to efficiently add an accompanying circumstance or supporting detail to the main clause. Here, it explains exactly how the government is attempting to make concessions without requiring a separate sentence.
Example from the text: "Consequently, the government has made apparent attempts at concessions, with the education minister pledging 3,000 additional supply teachers."

In Advanced English, writers frequently reduce a time clause by combining a conjunction like "while" with an -ing participle (e.g., "while safeguarding"). This concise structure shows two simultaneous actions occurring, keeping the sentence flowing without repeating the subject.

Advanced Grammar: The structure "too + adjective + a/an + noun"
This advanced grammatical structure emphasizes that a quality is excessive to the point of being problematic or undesirable. Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. bankrolled (verb): To provide financial support for someone or something, often in a significant way.
2. supervised (verb): To observe and direct the execution of a task, activity, or group, ensuring it is done correctly.
3. entities (noun): Organizations, institutions, or groups with a distinct and independent existence.
4. seizure (noun): The action of forcibly taking something or someone, often by legal authority or by force.
5. underpins (verb): To form the basis, support, or foundation for something, strengthening its existence or validity.
6. leadership structure (noun phrase): The hierarchical arrangement of individuals in positions of authority and decision-making within an organization.
7. limited recourse (noun phrase): Having few available options or inadequate means to obtain remedy, assistance, or legal redress.
8. asserted (verb): To state a fact or belief confidently and forcefully, often in the face of contradiction.
9. constitutes (verb): To be or be considered as; to form or make up a particular part of something.
10. intermediary (noun): A person who acts as a link between two parties to facilitate an agreement, communication, or transaction.

Advanced Grammar: Participial Phrases of Consequence
An “-ing” clause can express the result of the preceding situation. Here, “leaving Damascus with limited recourse” describes the effect of the US position on Syria’s options. Replacing it with “because” would make the limited options a reason instead of a result; an “-ing” form by itself does not always express a result.

📅 October 07, 2026 - French School Demonstrations Intensify: Demands for Reform and Police Concerns
Across France, a wave of high school demonstrations has broadened to include university students and unions, driven by demands for better educational funding and mounting concerns over police conduct.

Read the story

Tear gas was used in Paris and Marseille as school protests grew across France. Initially, crowds consisting mainly of teenagers called for more resources for schools and an end to what they characterized as police repression. Hundreds of thousands of people have taken to the streets across France, as a wave of protests by high-school pupils spread to their parents, university students and unions. The government reported that over 250,000 demonstrators participated on Tuesday, with disruptions in almost a quarter of all lycées. Students' unions, however, claimed significantly higher numbers, putting total demonstrators at 450,000 and blockaded high schools at 1,800 on what the protesters dubbed "Act Three" of their movement. Police clashed with demonstrators in Lyon, using tear gas in Paris and Marseille and water cannon in Rennes, though serious trouble was less severe than in previous protests. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu condemned earlier violence, reporting 215 students, 85 staff, and 715 police officers had been hurt since the protests began. Education Minister Édouard Geffray appealed for dialogue over school blockades and warned against "ultra-violent elements" infiltrating student movements. Alissa described schools as “falling apart”, conditions she saw as necessitating protests. Her friend, Sacha, further detailed poor conditions, including leaking ceilings in Marseille's schools, hindering effective study. Widespread concern exists over allegations of "disproportionate force" by police, with France's IGPN police inspectorate investigating multiple cases. One police officer has been charged by prosecutors with "violence... resulting in mutilation or permanent disability" after firing a tear-gas grenade at a high school student. Another alarming incident involved a 15-year-old's hand being torn off after a so-called anti-encirclement grenade was fired in unclear circumstances near a school in Lens. Government efforts, including a website for grievances and internal school discussions, have made little impression on the students so far. With further union-backed mobilisations anticipated, the current wave of discontent appears unlikely to abate in the near future. Alain and Michèle, former teachers in their seventies, joined the Paris march, explaining that they understood the education system’s problems.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. characterized (verb, past tense): Described the distinctive nature or features of something or someone.
2. repression (noun): The act of suppressing something or someone by force, often with a sense of injustice or oppression.
3. dubbed (verb, past tense): Given a particular name or nickname to something or someone.
4. appealed for dialogue (verb phrase): Made a serious and urgent request for discussion and negotiation to resolve a situation.
5. infiltrating (verb, present participle): Entering or gaining access to an organization or place surreptitiously and often with hostile intent.
6. necessitating (verb, present participle): Making something necessary as a result or consequence of a particular situation.
7. allegations (noun): Claims that someone has done something wrong; the word itself does not establish whether the claims are supported or proved.
8. charged by prosecutors (verb phrase): Formally accused by legal officials of committing a crime, leading to legal proceedings.
9. unclear circumstances (noun phrase): Conditions or details surrounding an event that are not fully known, understood, or transparent.
10. impression (noun): An effect or feeling produced on someone's mind, often initially or subtly, by an experience or event.
11. unlikely to abate (idiomatic phrase): Not likely to become less intense, widespread, or severe in the near future.

Advanced Grammar: Present Participle Clauses (Adverbial)
The “-ing” clause “explaining that…” adds what Alain and Michèle said alongside the main report that they joined the march. Its understood subject is the couple. Such clauses can express accompanying actions, reasons or results; “-ing” alone does not establish exact timing.
