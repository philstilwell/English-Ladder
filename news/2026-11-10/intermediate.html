📅 November 10, 2026 - TV presenter Tommy Walsh dies at 69

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Tommy Walsh, a building and DIY expert who starred on several television shows, has died at the age of 69. His agent told BBC News that Walsh died peacefully after a long illness, with his family around him, and asked for privacy for the family. Walsh worked as a professional builder before becoming known to television audiences. He got his big break after restoring the home of a BBC producer who was developing ideas for a show that later became Ground Force. Walsh said he turned down the offer to appear on the show three times. He said he agreed after his eight-year-old daughter told him about a £10 bet she had made with a boy at school. She had bet that her father would appear on television. Walsh appeared on the BBC’s Ground Force with Alan Titchmarsh and Charlie Dimmock from 1997 to 2005. On Ground Force, Walsh’s co-stars carried out makeovers on gardens that belonged to members of the public. He went on to present Cowboys and Angels as well as Clean It, Fix It. In 2021, Walsh joined the daytime programme Homes Under the Hammer. Walsh also published several books about DIY projects that people can do themselves.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. agent (noun): Someone who represents another person in their working life and may speak to the media for them.
2. privacy (noun): The freedom to deal with personal matters without public attention.
3. big break (noun phrase): An important opportunity that helps someone become successful in their career.
4. restoring (verb, -ing form): Repairing a building to bring it back into good condition.
5. turned down (phrasal verb, past simple): Said no to an offer or invitation.
6. bet (noun): An agreement in which someone can win or lose money depending on whether a prediction comes true.
7. makeovers (plural noun): Changes that give places a new look.
8. went on to (verb phrase, past simple): Did something else later, often as the next step in a career.

Grammar Focus: Defining relative clauses with “who”
A defining relative clause with “who” tells us which person or kind of person we mean. Put it directly after the noun or noun phrase it describes, without commas. In the brief, the clause after “a BBC producer” tells us what the producer was working on.
Example from the text: "He got his big break after restoring the home of a BBC producer who was developing ideas for a show that later became Ground Force."
