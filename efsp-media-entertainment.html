03 · Notice the language

  • Could you clarify which ...?
  • So, my understanding is ... . Is that right?

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which indirect question has the usual word order?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. Could you confirm when the review starts?

  1. A. Could you confirm when the review starts? The embedded question uses subject then verb: “the review starts”.
  2. B. Could you confirm when does the review start? Remove 'does' and use statement word order after 'confirm'.
  3. C. Could you confirm when starts the review? Place the subject 'the review' before 'starts'.
2. Someone says the report is 'ready.' Which question best clarifies its approval status?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. Has the report been approved, or is it ready for review?

  1. A. Can you send the report to me this afternoon? This asks about delivery timing, not whether approval has happened.
  2. B. Who will present the report at the meeting? This asks about the presenter, not the report's approval status.
  3. C. Has the report been approved, or is it ready for review? This question distinguishes two meanings of 'ready' that affect the next action.

05 · Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. What does premium sound like?

The producer clarifies the lesson's incomplete video brief with the client creative lead; audience and delivery requirements remain unknown.

  1. Producer: Your creative brief says premium and energetic. Which qualities should viewers actually notice in the video?
  2. Client creative lead: I haven't selected examples yet. I realize those words leave considerable room for interpretation.
  3. Producer: Would you like to discuss the audience first, before we propose music or an editing rhythm?
  4. Client creative lead: Yes, although I still need our marketing team to confirm who the primary audience is.
  5. Producer: Then I will mark the audience as unresolved. Who can clarify the intended deliverable and channels?
  6. Client creative lead: Our campaign manager can answer that. Are those details necessary before you explore the tone?
  7. Producer: We can explore possibilities, but we cannot assess brand fit confidently without that context.
  8. Client creative lead: Please show alternatives as discussion material, not as an agreed creative direction.
  9. Producer: Understood. Could you arrange a briefing with the campaign manager and bring two reference examples?
  10. Client creative lead: I'll request that meeting and confirm availability. The creative direction remains open until we clarify those points.
2. A trailer or a complete story?

Separate fictional case: a museum commissions a thirty-second exhibit trailer for its lobby screen, without specifying whether it should include narration.

  1. Creative director: The brief calls this a documentary, but the deliverable is a thirty-second lobby trailer.
  2. Museum communications lead: We want visitors to understand the exhibit. Is documentary the wrong word for that?
  3. Creative director: It suggests a fuller story. Should this piece explain the exhibit or encourage people to enter?
  4. Museum communications lead: Encourage them to enter. We can leave the detailed explanation to the exhibit itself.
  5. Creative director: That helps define the tone. Should we develop a visual invitation rather than a narrated summary?
  6. Museum communications lead: Possibly, but I haven't checked whether sound is available on that screen.
  7. Creative director: Could you confirm that before we build a concept around a voice-over?
  8. Museum communications lead: Yes. Meanwhile, can you explore a version that communicates through images and short captions?
  9. Creative director: Certainly. I'll label sound as unconfirmed and focus the creative brief on attracting visitors.
  10. Museum communications lead: Good. I'll check the screen setup, then review the concept for accuracy and brand fit.
3. Two meanings of playful

Separate fictional case: a podcast producer and a commissioning editor discuss a gardening series for first-time apartment gardeners; the brief requests a playful tone.

  1. Podcast producer: When you ask for a playful tone, do you mean comedy or an approachable conversation?
  2. Commissioning editor: An approachable conversation. The listeners are first-time apartment gardeners, and we shouldn't make mistakes embarrassing.
  3. Podcast producer: So practical curiosity would fit better than jokes about people who cannot keep plants alive?
  4. Commissioning editor: Exactly. The brand fit depends on welcoming beginners, not presenting the host as superior.
  5. Podcast producer: Could we use a host who asks the questions a new gardener might hesitate to ask?
  6. Commissioning editor: Yes, provided the expert answers respectfully. What would you bring to the development review?
  7. Podcast producer: A sample opening and an episode outline, unless you expect a fully recorded pilot.
  8. Commissioning editor: An outline is sufficient. Please don't book talent or studio time for this review.
  9. Podcast producer: Then the deliverable is an outline with a sample opening, and no production booking.
  10. Commissioning editor: Correct. That gives us enough to test the tone before deciding whether to commission a pilot.

Scenario 2 · Which version belongs on the screen?

In a fictional cinema campaign, the client requests one poster animation for both a silent foyer screen and an online placement with sound. Only the foyer dimensions are supplied. Hold a clarification call and agree what must be confirmed before production.

Role A: You are the motion producer. Clarify whether one deliverable or two adapted versions are expected.

Role B: You are the campaign coordinator. You know the foyer is silent but must obtain the online specifications.

Possible opening: “Before we estimate the animation, could you clarify whether you need separate versions for the two placements?”

Success checks
  • Clarify the deliverables and sound requirements separately.
  • Keep the online specifications explicitly unresolved.
  • Agree who will obtain the missing specifications before production is scheduled.
Add a complication

The client calls the online version a simple resize, although its dimensions and duration remain unknown.

Studying alone? Speak both roles aloud. For each scenario, prepare for two minutes, speak for one minute, then answer a follow-up. Switch roles and repeat using fewer notes. Use only the supplied facts; identify missing information instead of inventing it.

A moment at work

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

production budget
The planned costs of making a media project or carrying out production work.
call sheet
A production document listing who is needed, where, and when for a shoot.
location
A specified place where work, filming, an event, or service occurs.
permit
Official permission for an activity under specified requirements.

03 · Notice the language

Give a useful status update

Lead with the current state, then the important change or open item. Use completed-action language only for completed work. Add a next update point when it is known; do not invent a date to make the message sound complete.

  • We have completed ...; ... remains open.
  • As of ..., the status is ... .
  • The next update will cover ... .

Improve this: Everything is progressing well.

1. Which sentence says a review is finished?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. The team has completed the review.

  1. A. The team is completing the review. 'Is completing' presents the work as in progress.
  2. B. The team plans to complete the review. 'Plans to complete' describes an intention, not completion.
  3. C. The team has completed the review. 'Has completed' states that the action is finished.
2. Which update distinguishes a plan from a confirmed fact?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Delivery is planned for Friday; the carrier has not confirmed it.

  1. A. The delivery plan proves that arrival will be Friday. A plan cannot prove a future arrival time.
  2. B. Delivery is planned for Friday; the carrier has not confirmed it. This explicitly labels the date as planned and identifies the missing confirmation.
  3. C. Delivery is confirmed for Friday because we planned it. A plan does not establish carrier confirmation.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Send it no later than Thursday.

  2. B. Send it no later than Thursday. 'By' sets the latest point, while 'after' refers to a later time.
  3. C. Send it on Thursday, but not earlier. “By Thursday” permits earlier delivery; it is not restricted to that day.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review?

  2. B. Could you confirm that the review is important? This checks importance rather than identifying the needed document version.
  3. C. Could you confirm that you received my earlier message? This checks receipt, not which version is required for the review.

Your goal

Compare options using the same criteria and state the tradeoff.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

Two distribution proposals offer different exclusivity periods and territories. A summary compares only the fee.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

windowing
Planning when and where media content is made available across distribution channels.
exclusivity
An arrangement limiting specified rights or activities to one party for an agreed scope.
platform
A foundational technology or system that supports several applications or products.
rights window
The period during which specified distribution or use rights apply.

03 · Notice the language

Explain a fair comparison

Use the same time period, scope, and measurement basis for both options. Explain a difference with 'whereas' or 'while'. State what improves and what is given up; do not hide the weaker side of a recommendation.

  • Both options ..., but ... .
  • Option A ..., whereas option B ... .
  • The comparison depends on ... .

Improve this: This option is more better.

See a clearer version

This option is better on cost, but slower to deliver.

'Better' already has comparative meaning, so it does not need 'more'. Naming the criterion and tradeoff makes the comparison useful.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence expresses a clear contrast?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. Option A is faster, whereas option B costs less.

  1. A. Option A is faster because option B costs less. 'Because' claims a causal connection that is not given.
  2. B. Option A is faster, so option B costs less. 'So' implies one fact causes the other.
  3. C. Option A is faster, whereas option B costs less. 'Whereas' marks a contrast between two options.
2. A rate increases from 6% to 7%. What is the absolute difference?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. An increase of one percentage point.

  1. A. An increase of seven percentage points. The new rate is 7%; the difference between the rates is one percentage point.
  2. B. An increase of one percentage point. Subtracting 6% from 7% gives one percentage point. The relative increase is about 16.7%.
  3. C. An increase of one percent relative to the original rate. A one-percent relative increase on 6% would be 6.06%, not 7%.

Your goal

Separate observations, assumptions, and conclusions.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A campaign video reaches more viewers but has a lower completion rate. A report labels it an unqualified success.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

reach
The number of distinct people or accounts exposed to content under a stated measurement method.
completion rate
The proportion of started or eligible activities that reach a defined end point.
retention
Keeping customers, users, staff, or records over a stated period; specify which meaning applies.
audience segment
A subgroup of an audience sharing relevant characteristics or needs.

03 · Notice the language

Match certainty to evidence

Say what the evidence shows, identify what is still unknown, and limit the conclusion to that evidence. Words such as 'may', 'suggests', and 'in this sample' are useful when the uncertainty is real. Do not soften an urgent, verified safety concern.

  • The available evidence shows ... .
  • This may indicate ..., but ... .
  • We have not yet established whether ... .

Improve this: The pilot proves this will work everywhere.

See a clearer version

The pilot worked in the tested setting; performance elsewhere remains untested.

The revision states the finding and its scope. It avoids turning limited evidence into a universal claim.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence preserves uncertainty about an unconfirmed cause?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. The change may have contributed to the delay.

  1. A. The change definitely caused the delay. 'Definitely' presents the cause as certain.
  2. B. The change may have contributed to the delay. 'May have contributed' presents a possibility without claiming the cause is established.
  3. C. The change must have caused the delay. 'Must have' expresses a strong inference that the evidence may not support.
2. A result comes from a small pilot in one location. Which phrase accurately limits the claim?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. In this pilot, the observed result was ...

  1. A. In this pilot, the observed result was ... This locates the finding in the setting actually studied.
  2. B. In similar locations, we can now expect the same result ... Similarity alone does not establish that the result will transfer.
  3. C. Across the service, the observed result was ... A pilot in one location does not establish a service-wide observation.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

brand safety
Measures intended to prevent advertising from appearing in unsuitable or harmful contexts.
product placement
Inclusion of a brand or product within media content under an agreed arrangement.
deliverable
A specified item of work to be provided to a customer or project recipient.
makegood
A replacement delivery or benefit offered to address an unmet advertising or sponsorship commitment.

03 · Notice the language

Offer a workable tradeoff

Acknowledge the goal and explain the available choices. Connect an offer to its actual conditions. A useful response makes room for discussion without promising approval or resources you do not control.

  • We can ..., provided that ... .
  • Would you prefer ... or ...?
  • I can take that proposal for review; I cannot yet confirm ... .

Improve this: If you will approve the scope, we can assess the schedule.

See a clearer version

If you approve the scope, we can assess the schedule.

For an ordinary future condition, the “if” clause uses present tense. The other clause can express the future consequence or possibility.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence presents a normal future condition clearly?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. If the scope changes, we will review the schedule.

  1. A. If the scope changes, we will review the schedule. The “if” clause uses present tense; the main clause states the future response.
  2. B. If the scope will change, we will review the schedule. For this ordinary future condition, use present simple in the “if” clause.
  3. C. If the scope changes, we reviewed the schedule. “Reviewed” places the response in the past instead of expressing the future response.
2. Which response negotiates a new request without silently promising extra work?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. We can assess the addition and explain its effect on the agreed scope.

  1. A. We have included it, although its scope has not been reviewed. It treats unreviewed work as already included.
  2. B. We guarantee the original date without assessing the addition. It promises an outcome without examining the consequences.
  3. C. We can assess the addition and explain its effect on the agreed scope. This offers a useful next step while keeping the approval and scope question open.

A moment at work

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

public statement
A message issued by an organization for a public audience.
backlash
A strong negative reaction from an audience or group.
legal review
Examination of an issue or document by the appropriate legal professional.
holding statement
An initial public message stating verified information while further facts are being established.

03 · Notice the language

Match certainty to evidence

Say what the evidence shows, identify what is still unknown, and limit the conclusion to that evidence. Words such as 'may', 'suggests', and 'in this sample' are useful when the uncertainty is real. Do not soften an urgent, verified safety concern.

  • The available evidence shows ... .
  • This may indicate ..., but ... .
  • We have not yet established whether ... .

Improve this: The pilot proves this will work everywhere.

See a clearer version

The pilot worked in the tested setting; performance elsewhere remains untested.

The revision states the finding and its scope. It avoids turning limited evidence into a universal claim.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence preserves uncertainty about an unconfirmed cause?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. The change may have contributed to the delay.

  1. A. The change must have caused the delay. 'Must have' expresses a strong inference that the evidence may not support.
  2. B. The change definitely caused the delay. 'Definitely' presents the cause as certain.
  3. C. The change may have contributed to the delay. 'May have contributed' presents a possibility without claiming the cause is established.
2. A result comes from a small pilot in one location. Which phrase accurately limits the claim?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. In this pilot, the observed result was ...

  1. A. Across the service, the observed result was ... A pilot in one location does not establish a service-wide observation.
  2. B. In this pilot, the observed result was ... This locates the finding in the setting actually studied.
  3. C. In similar locations, we can now expect the same result ... Similarity alone does not establish that the result will transfer.

1. Remove the unverified explanation

  4. Production spokesperson: Yes. We should separate what has been confirmed from what remains under review.
  5. Communications lead: The team worries that acknowledging uncertainty will make the statement seem evasive during the backlash.
