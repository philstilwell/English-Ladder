📅 October 28, 2026 - The Search for Quieter Delivery Drones
As delivery drones become increasingly familiar, residents report unwelcome disruption while companies develop ways to reduce their acoustic impact.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

For many people in the United States, delivery drones are ceasing to be a novelty and becoming part of everyday life. Ashley Schmidt, a resident of North Garland near Dallas, now spends little time in her garden. She attributes her discomfort to the sound of drones flying overhead with her neighbours' purchases. Whereas steady traffic may merge into the surrounding hum, a drone's sudden whine can cut through it. What Torija Martinez emphasises is that drone sound can be conspicuous even when overall sound levels are relatively low. He explains that communities with little previous exposure to aircraft noise may be especially sensitive to changes in their local soundscape, the combination of sounds around them. Research he cites suggests that reactions are stronger when a sound's source seems unfamiliar, intrusive or frequent, while people who benefit from drone services may be less troubled. He warns that currently localised complaints are likely to become more widespread as the sector expands. London-based Greenjets claims that its propulsion system, which generates the force to move a drone, can reduce drone noise by at least two thirds. Its design encloses spinning blades within a pod to muffle their sound and direct it away from the ground. The system also uses slower rotor speeds and materials that reduce sound caused by vibration. Greenjets' technology has helped conservationists in Africa seeking to monitor poachers from above without revealing their presence. For larger drones used in the Russia–Ukraine war, however, speed generally takes precedence over stealth, according to Tollast. Another propeller design features twisted, loop-shaped blades without pointed tips. Amazon says its latest drone designs use custom propellers that produce almost half the perceived sound of the previous generation, with a milder tone. Wing says that, alongside designing propellers to minimise sound, it varies flight paths to limit repeated overflights of individual homes or neighbourhoods. Schmidt would prefer deliveries in her area to stop until the service no longer causes a disturbance.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. novelty (noun): Something that attracts interest because it is new or unfamiliar, often with the implication that this interest will fade.
2. conspicuous (adjective): Standing out strongly from the surroundings; here, readily attracting attention through sound.
3. soundscape (noun): The combination of sounds that characterises a particular environment.
4. intrusive (adjective): Unwelcome because it enters someone's personal space or disrupts their activities or peace.
5. localised (adjective): Restricted to particular places rather than distributed across a wider area.
6. propulsion system (noun phrase): The equipment that generates the force needed to move a vehicle.
7. muffle (verb): To make a sound less clearly audible by enclosing or covering its source.
8. takes precedence (verb phrase): Receives greater importance than another consideration when priorities are decided.
9. stealth (noun): The ability to operate without attracting attention or being detected.
10. perceived (adjective): Experienced or judged by a person, rather than described solely through physical measurements.

Advanced Grammar: What-cleft sentences for information focus
A what-cleft organises a message around a clause beginning with “what”, followed by “be” and the information receiving emphasis; in the example, “What Torija Martinez emphasises” introduces his central point, while the clause after “is” states that point explicitly.
Example from the text: "What Torija Martinez emphasises is that drone sound can be conspicuous even when overall sound levels are relatively low."

📅 October 27, 2026 - James Bourne Says Brother's Kidney Donation Saved His Life
James Bourne credits his brother with a lifesaving kidney donation while acknowledging that recovery will take time.

Read the story

Busted musician James Bourne has credited his brother with saving his life by donating a kidney. He described a grave prognosis, an assessment of how an illness is likely to develop, saying doctors had given him only a few years to live. Bourne, 43, pulled out of the arena tour featuring Busted and McFly on the eve of its launch in September 2025. He attributed his departure to health problems, and his younger brother Chris stepped in as his replacement. Bourne later said he needed substantial surgery to extend his life, and he has since thanked Chris as both brothers recover from their operations. Although Bourne said both brothers were recovering well after surgery, he tempered this positive account by acknowledging that he still needed time. Chris marked the outcome with a brief message on X expressing their shared achievement. Bourne has not disclosed the precise nature of his illness. He rose to prominence in the early 2000s alongside Charlie Simpson and Matt Willis in Busted, whose hits included Year 3000. The group achieved four UK number one singles before it disbanded in 2005. Bourne and Willis subsequently joined forces with McFly to form McBusted in 2013. Simpson returned to Busted in 2015, completing the original trio's reunion. Beyond Busted, Bourne has written songs for McFly and 5 Seconds of Summer and fronted Son of Dork. He also recently released music from his stage musical Murder at the Gates.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. credited (verb, past participle): Publicly identified someone as responsible for a positive outcome; the pattern “credit someone with doing something” assigns recognition.
2. prognosis (countable noun): A medical assessment of how an illness is likely to develop, including the prospects for survival or improvement.
3. on the eve of (prepositional phrase): Immediately before an important event; here, specifically the day before the performances began.
4. attributed (verb, past simple): Identified something as the cause of an event; “attribute something to something” reports a causal explanation.
5. extend his life (verb phrase): Enable him to live longer than he otherwise might; the expression does not itself imply a complete cure.
6. tempered (verb, past simple): Made a statement or impression less absolute by adding a balancing qualification.
7. disclosed (verb, past participle): Made previously private information known; this relatively formal verb often concerns sensitive or confidential matters.
8. rose to prominence (verb phrase, past simple): Became widely known and attracted substantial public attention within a particular field.
9. disbanded (verb, past simple): Stopped operating together as an established group, without necessarily ruling out a later return.
10. fronted (verb, past simple): Served as the lead performer or principal public presence of a musical group, rather than merely writing material for it.

Advanced Grammar: Concessive clauses with “although”
“Although” introduces information that might lead readers to expect a different main-clause message: positive recovery might suggest that little further time is needed, yet Bourne qualifies that impression. The concessive relationship presents both statements as compatible while highlighting the tension between them.
Example from the text: "Although Bourne said both brothers were recovering well after surgery, he tempered this positive account by acknowledging that he still needed time."

George McMaster began his farming career milking eight cows and relying on visual judgement to detect health problems. He now watches a herd of 300 through an AI camera in the milking parlour at his farm near Islandmagee in County Antrim. The camera uses an algorithm, a set of computational rules, to identify individual cows, analyse daily footage and flag changes that may not yet be visible to a person. A farmer reports that the camera can identify lameness, or difficulty walking normally, earlier than ordinary daily checks had revealed it. A farmer also links healthier cattle with greater milk production and better animal welfare. In the robotic milking arrangement described, automation allows cows to enter and leave the machine when they choose, rather than being brought in at fixed times. For one farmer, obtaining high-speed broadband was a turning point that enabled him to open a farm shop accepting electronic payments. One farmer describes a lighter administrative burden, with information entered as work is done rather than written down both in the cattle house and in the office. A compliance section allows reports on medicines, feed and other farm information to be selected in preparation for inspections. Another stated priority is for AI to help reduce inputs, the resources farming uses, while increasing outputs, what it produces. Although one speaker is enthusiastic about the technology, he does not believe it will replace humans. One speaker explicitly rejects the prospect of AI taking over farmers' decision-making. One contributor argues that combining datasets could advance sustainability across its economic, environmental and social dimensions. Digitalisation is also presented as a way to help farmers meet their obligations while supplying good-quality, sustainable food.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. algorithm (countable noun): A set of rules a computer follows to process information, here to identify individual animals and detect changes.
2. automation (uncountable noun): The use of technology to perform tasks with less direct human involvement, without necessarily removing human responsibility.
3. administrative burden (noun phrase): The time and effort required to organise information and complete routine paperwork, viewed as a demand on someone's resources.
4. compliance (uncountable noun): The fulfilment of official rules or requirements; organised documentation can help demonstrate it.
5. inputs (plural noun): Resources used in production, considered in relation to the goods or quantities they help produce.
6. outputs (plural noun): The goods or quantities produced by an activity, considered in relation to the resources used.
7. decision-making (uncountable noun): The process of assessing information and choosing what to do, distinct from simply receiving information.
8. sustainability (uncountable noun): The capacity to maintain farming over time while addressing economic, environmental and social needs.
9. digitalisation (uncountable noun): The adoption of digital systems that changes how information is managed and work is carried out.
10. obligations (plural noun): Duties or requirements that someone is expected or required to fulfil, rather than optional ambitions.

Advanced Grammar: Concessive clauses with “although”
A concessive clause introduced by “although” acknowledges a fact that might suggest a different conclusion. Here, “is enthusiastic about the technology” might create an expectation of support for replacing humans, but the main clause rejects that expectation without denying the enthusiasm.
Example from the text: "Although one speaker is enthusiastic about the technology, he does not believe it will replace humans."

According to unnamed officials, Trump contacted Musk to express frustration that the network's data speeds remained severely capped. Although Trump refused to confirm whether he directly asked Musk to lift the limitations, he confidently asserted that the service had been successfully reinstated. For his part, Musk has consistently denied deliberately throttling the network, which is vital for targeting remote positions and protecting the country. Furthermore, the tech entrepreneur suggested that the US government might be responsible for the limitations, as the Pentagon fundamentally controls access to the secure platform. Nevertheless, the White House has categorically denied issuing any formal orders to curtail the crucial satellite service.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. hampered (verb): hindered, impeded, or created difficulties for the progress or movement of something.
2. connectivity (noun): the ability of a device or network to successfully link to the internet or other communication platforms.
3. waived (verb): officially chose not to enforce or apply a rule, penalty, or requirement.
4. sanctions (noun): official orders or economic penalties taken against a country to force it to obey international law.
5. capped (verb): had an upper limit strictly enforced, preventing speed or performance from increasing beyond a certain level.
6. interceptors (noun): fast aircraft or defensive missiles specifically designed to stop incoming enemy strikes before they reach their targets.
7. asserted (verb): stated a fact or belief confidently and forcefully.
8. throttling (verb): intentionally slowing down the speed or bandwidth of an internet connection or digital service.
9. fundamentally (adverb): at the most basic, essential, and foundational level; in a core or underlying way.
10. categorically (adverb): in a completely clear, direct, and explicit manner, leaving absolutely no room for doubt or confusion.

A present participle clause starting with an '-ing' verb can establish the political context, cause, or background for the main action. Here, it concisely explains why the subject made their decision without requiring a separate sentence.
