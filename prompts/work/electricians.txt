Lesson 01: Taking a precise electrical service request
Original fictional case: Client Rosa tells electrician Kai that the office sockets are dead. Her account concerns two desk receptacles in Room 204, noticed at 08:30. Other areas have not been assessed. A panel-directory entry says office, but its accuracy has not been verified. Kai must clarify the request from the information Rosa already has. He must not ask her to test or operate anything, expand the report to the whole office, or treat dead as proof of safe electrical status.
Language workshop: Which sockets, where, and since when?
Communication goal: Narrow a broad client description to named devices, location, and observation time without inferring technical status or requesting tests.
Language guidance: Dead is Rosa's informal description, not verified de-energized or isolated status. Her account identifies two desk receptacles in Room 204. Other areas are unassessed, so they must not be described as either working normally or affected.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): I will record what you have already observed. / Which room does your report concern? / You mean the two desk receptacles in Room 204. / Is 08:30 when you first noticed the issue? / We have not established exactly when it began. / The client reports that those two receptacles are not working as expected. / Other areas have not been assessed. / I will not describe this as a verified office-wide outage. / The directory entry says office. / The directory's accuracy has not been verified. / Dead is your description, not verified electrical status. / I cannot infer that equipment is de-energized or safe from this report. / You do not need to test or operate anything for this clarification. / The report concerns two devices, not an unspecified number. / Two desk receptacles, Room 204, noticed at 08:30, other areas unassessed. / I will preserve the unverified directory entry separately from your device report.
Vocabulary: receptacle: Device providing contacts for a plug connection. / socket: Common term for a receptacle or plug-in connection point. / outlet: Point where electrical energy is supplied to utilization equipment. / desk receptacle: Receptacle serving or located at a desk position. / service request: Report asking for attention to a stated issue. / client account: Description supplied by the customer. / reported symptom: Problem described by a person rather than established by a technical finding. / observation time: When a person noticed the described condition. / onset: Time when a condition began, if established. / affected location: Area named in the report as experiencing the issue. / unassessed area: Location whose condition has not been evaluated. / panel directory: List identifying circuits or areas associated with a panel. / directory entry: Individual item in a panel or circuit list. / circuit identification: Establishing which circuit corresponds to a device or area. / circuit: Electrical path or connected arrangement through which current may flow. / distribution panel: Assembly distributing electrical supply to outgoing circuits. / IP code: Enclosure protection code with separate digits for solid-object and water protection. / room reference: Identifier locating a room in the building. / verified status: Condition established through the appropriate authorized process. / de-energized: In a state without energization, requiring appropriate verification before reliance. / isolation status: Established state of separation from relevant energy sources under the actual procedure. / technical finding: Conclusion supported by the relevant professional assessment. / scope of report: Extent of the issue actually described by the reporting person. / readback: Repetition of important information to confirm accuracy.
Speaking task: Read turns 1-10, stressing two, Room 204, and noticed at 08:30. Switch roles for turns 11-20; keep dead attributed to the client, not a verified electrical state. Check and read the transfer without asking the client to operate or test anything.
Partner: Rosa: Client / Kai: Electrician
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: Complete the intake readback from the client information. Do not turn the word dead or an unverified label into a technical conclusion.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Rosa: Could you read back the room, count, and observation time? Those are the details I can confirm from what I already know.
Kai: The readback is: client reports two desk receptacles in Room 204, noticed at 08:30; exact onset unknown; other areas unassessed; directory wording unverified.
Rosa: Yes, that's accurate. Please retain office as unverified wording and keep it separate from the two devices I've reported.
Kai: I will. Circuit identification remains open for the appropriate process. The request will preserve your observations without asking you to carry out any electrical action or adding a technical finding.
END REFERENCE
