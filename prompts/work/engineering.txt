Lesson 01: Requirements and Constraints
Original fictional case: Customer representative Julia asks engineer Wei for a lightweight, durable enclosure. Their meeting proposes a maximum mass of 1.2 kg for the empty enclosure including its cover and fasteners. Electronics are excluded. The proposed service life is five years, but operating temperature, humidity, handling conditions, and the verification method are unresolved. Nothing is approved. Wei must record the proposed mass limit accurately and avoid turning the life target into a demonstrated durability claim.
Language workshop: Lightweight compared with what?
Communication goal: Convert an ambiguous customer request into a measurable proposal while keeping unresolved conditions and approval visible.
Language guidance: Mass is not the same as the mass of the fully populated product. The proposal includes the enclosure, cover, and fasteners but excludes electronics. A five-year target needs defined use conditions and a suitable evidence plan before it can support a durability claim.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): What maximum mass would count as lightweight? / Does that value include the cover and fasteners? / Electronics are outside this mass boundary. / We propose a maximum mass of 1.2 kg. / This is a proposal, not an approved requirement. / What operating temperature range must the enclosure withstand? / Five years under which use and maintenance conditions? / The handling conditions have not been specified. / The service-life target is not a demonstrated result. / Which verification method will support acceptance? / Separate the mass limit from the service-life requirement. / A packaging change should not silently alter the measured assembly. / Which customer task makes this limit necessary? / Put the unconfirmed condition in the assumption register. / Who will confirm the environmental requirements? / We have clarified the proposal, but approval is still pending.
Vocabulary: stakeholder need: The capability or outcome a stakeholder wants. / design requirement: A specified condition the design must satisfy. / design constraint: A limit on available design choices. / mass budget: The planned allocation of allowable mass. / maximum mass: The greatest permitted mass under a stated boundary. / system boundary: The line defining what is included in the system. / operating envelope: The range of conditions for intended operation. / service life: The intended period of use under specified conditions. / duty cycle: The pattern of operation and rest over time. / environmental condition: A surrounding condition affecting operation or storage. / acceptance criterion: A condition used to judge whether a result is acceptable. / verification method: The means used to check a stated requirement. / testability: The extent to which a claim can be checked by testing. / requirement allocation: Assignment of a requirement to a system element. / derived requirement: A requirement developed from higher-level needs or analysis. / design margin: Allowance between a predicted value and a defined limit. / threshold: A stated boundary for an acceptable result. / objective value: A desired value beyond a minimum acceptable level. / assumption register: A record of unverified premises used in planning or design. / traceability: Recorded connections between needs, requirements, and evidence. / baseline: An approved reference placed under change control. / ambiguity: Wording that permits more than one interpretation. / rationale: The reason supporting a requirement or decision. / requirement owner: The role responsible for maintaining a requirement.
Speaking task: Read the requirements dialogue. Stress the enclosure, cover, and fasteners included in 1.2 kg, with electronics excluded. Switch roles. Keep five years as proposed service life while operating conditions, verification, and approval remain open. Complete and check the transfer. State the 0.8 kg boundary without adding batteries or an approved humidity condition.
Partner: Julia: Customer representative / Wei: Design engineer
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A proposal limits an empty housing with lid and screws to 0.8 kg. Batteries are excluded. The operating humidity and acceptance test are not yet agreed. Approval remains pending.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Julia: I will send the customer the proposed boundary and the missing questions. Please do not label the specification approved until their review is complete.
Wei: We will establish the baseline only through the agreed approval process. Today's useful result is a precise proposal and an explicit list of remaining decisions.
Julia: Good. I can explain that progress without claiming the enclosure has already met the five-year target. Who should keep the environmental questions moving?
Wei: Name a requirement owner for that follow-up. The record should say who confirms the conditions and when, so the open questions do not disappear between teams.

Lesson 02: Design Reviews and Technical Pushback
Original fictional case: At a design review, mechanical lead Ahmed and reviewer Clara examine an enclosure interface. The CAD model shows 2 mm nominal clearance. Requirement R-14 specifies at least 1 mm clearance across the approved dimensional tolerances. The tolerance stack-up has not been documented, and the supplier drawing revision used in the model is unconfirmed. The chair is preparing to recommend release. Clara must distinguish nominal fit from verified minimum clearance and record a specific closure action without claiming the design has already failed.
Language workshop: The drawing fits; the evidence is missing
