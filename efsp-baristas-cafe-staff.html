01Taking a precise drink orderUnpack the word 'regular' · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Clarify a familiar word that can describe several order details.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A customer says 'a regular latte.' This cafe uses regular for its middle size, but the customer might mean caffeinated coffee or the usual milk. No drink has been made. Clarify size, hot or iced, and coffee choice separately.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

espresso
A concentrated coffee drink prepared by passing hot water through finely ground coffee under pressure.
shot
A cafe's specified serving of espresso; exact size and recipe vary.
decaf
Coffee from which most caffeine has been removed; it is not necessarily caffeine-free.
dine in
An order intended to be consumed in the cafe rather than taken away.

Useful word combinations

  • confirm the drink size: check the selected menu size
  • make it decaf: request the cafe's decaffeinated coffee option
  • leave room: request space below the top of a filled cup
  • repeat the full drink: check type, size, temperature style, and changes together

03 · Notice the language

Unpack the word 'regular'

Ask one specification at a time. Decaf describes coffee, not milk or sweetness, and doesn't mean guaranteed zero caffeine.

  • By regular, do you mean ___?
  • Would you like that hot or ___?
  • That's a ___ latte with ___, for ___.

Improve this: A regular, got it.

See a clearer version

Do you mean our regular size, and would you like caffeinated or decaf coffee?

The check separates size from coffee choice.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Customer: I mean your middle size, not the coffee type.
Barista: So 'regular' refers to the ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. size

  1. A. milk The customer hasn't chosen milk here.
  2. B. sweetness No sweetness choice is stated.
  3. C. size Correct. The customer uses 'middle' to identify the size.
  4. D. coffee type The customer explicitly separates size from the coffee type.
2. Customer: Does decaf guarantee no caffeine at all?
Barista: No. It means ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. coffee with most caffeine removed

  1. A. coffee with every trace of caffeine removed This overstates decaf as necessarily containing zero caffeine.
  2. B. a smaller serving of regular coffee A smaller serving doesn't make regular coffee decaffeinated.
  3. C. regular coffee diluted with milk Adding milk changes the drink, not the coffee's decaffeination status.
  4. D. coffee with most caffeine removed Correct. Decaffeination removes most caffeine without guaranteeing complete absence.

05 · Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Regular needs three checks

The ambiguous latte order from the case.

  1. Customer: Could I have a regular latte, please, and make that one to go?
  2. Barista: By regular, do you mean our middle size or the caffeinated coffee option?
  3. Customer: The middle size. I'd actually like decaf because I've already had coffee today.
  4. Barista: Certainly. Would you like that latte hot or iced on this order?
  5. Customer: Hot, please. I didn't realize regular could refer to more than the size.
  6. Barista: It can. Which milk would you like? We have whole milk and the listed alternatives.
  7. Customer: Whole milk is fine. I don't want any syrup added to the drink.
  8. Barista: That's one middle-size hot decaf latte, whole milk, no syrup, to take away.
  9. Customer: Yes, that's everything. Please use the name Ellis when you call the order.
  10. Barista: Ellis, thank you. I'll put that name with those specifications on your ticket.
2. A shot is not a bigger cup

A customer asks what an extra-shot option changes.

  1. Customer: Does choosing an extra shot make the drink come in a bigger cup?
  2. Barista: No, it adds espresso under our menu option; the cup size stays the same.
  3. Customer: I want a larger drink, not extra coffee in the size I already chose.
  4. Barista: Then let's change the size rather than automatically add an extra shot to it.
  5. Customer: Thank you. What's the price difference between the small and large sizes here?
  6. Barista: The large is one dollar more. An extra shot has a separate listed charge.
  7. Customer: I'll take the large standard version, then, without choosing that extra-shot option today.
  8. Barista: Understood. Large latte, standard coffee recipe, with no separately added extra shot.
  9. Customer: And still hot with whole milk, as I said when I first ordered.
  10. Barista: Yes, those choices stay unchanged. I'll repeat the final ticket before taking payment.
3. Leaving room is an amount request

A customer clarifies space in a filter-coffee cup.

  1. Barista: You asked me to leave room. How much space would you like for your milk?
  2. Customer: Just a little at the top, not a cup that's only half full.
  3. Barista: Thanks. Would you like the milk added here, or served separately for you?
  4. Customer: Separately, please. I'd like to decide how much to add at the table.
  5. Barista: I'll note room for milk and a separate serving, with no syrup added.
  6. Customer: Yes. This is for drinking here, so I don't need a takeaway lid.
  7. Barista: A dine-in cup, then. Is the coffee choice still the house filter?
  8. Customer: It is. I haven't changed the coffee itself, only how it's served today.
  9. Barista: Understood. House filter with room and milk separately, for here rather than takeaway.
  10. Customer: Exactly. Thank you for checking what I meant before reducing the amount too much.

06 · Say it

Two scenarios to practice

Scenario 1 · Taking a precise drink order

A customer says 'a regular latte.' This cafe uses regular for its middle size, but the customer might mean caffeinated coffee or the usual milk. No drink has been made. Clarify size, hot or iced, and coffee choice separately.

Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.

Partner's role and follow-up

Customer: you mean middle size; choose hot, decaf, and whole milk when asked.

Try a harder second round

The customer says 'the normal one' about milk. Ask for the actual milk choice.

Scenario 2 · Hot order, iced label

A customer orders a small hot decaf latte. Before payment, the screen shows small iced decaf latte. Milk choice is correct.

Role A: Barista: read the screen and correct temperature style.

Role B: Customer: confirm hot, not iced; keep the other choices.

Possible opening: “I have iced on the screen, but I heard hot. Which did you want?”

Success checks
  • Obtain hot or iced explicitly.
  • Keep decaf and size unchanged.
  • Read back the corrected order.
Add a complication

The customer says 'same as before,' but the previous order isn't visible.

Studying alone? Speak both roles aloud. For each scenario, prepare for two minutes, speak for one minute, then answer a follow-up. Switch roles and repeat using fewer notes. Use only the supplied facts; identify missing information instead of inventing it.

Compare scenario 1 with a model response
When you say regular, do you mean our middle size or caffeinated coffee? I'll check those separately so I enter the right drink. Would you like the latte hot or iced, and which milk would you prefer? Once those details are clear, I'll repeat the full order before sending it to the bar.

This is one possible spoken response, not the only acceptable wording. Preserve the case facts when you try your own version.

In “Taking a precise drink order”, choose one confirmed detail from the situation and one item that still needs clarification. Say a two-sentence response using “By regular, do you mean ___?”. Keep the known detail accurate and ask about the missing one.

Check your reasoning

Compare with the case above and the model response. Can you point to the words that support your factual statement? Is your question about something the case leaves open? If you introduce a possible outcome, clearly label it as a possibility.

Review, then try again

  • Keeps the case facts accurate; clearly separates confirmed and unknown information.
  • Uses understandable sentences and explains specialist terms when the listener needs it.
  • Responds to the other person's question, checks understanding, and uses an appropriate tone.
  • Makes the requested action or unresolved question clear without inventing authority or facts.

Revise one sentence and repeat the response. A useful response can be clear even with a few grammar errors; judge meaning and task completion, not accent.

Vocabulary for this lesson

order ticket
A printed or electronic record connecting a cafe order to its specifications and identifier.
pickup name
The name used to identify a completed order at the collection point.
mobile order
An order placed through a phone app or similar online ordering service.
order queue
The sequence of orders waiting for preparation or completion.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A customer buys a gift bag and asks for 'normal ground.' They don't know the recipient's brewer. This cafe sells the same coffee whole or ground. Grinding cannot be undone. The package's 'hazelnut' note describes flavor, not an ingredient statement.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

whole bean
Coffee sold as intact roasted beans rather than already ground.
grind size
The relative coarseness or fineness of ground coffee.
roast date
The date shown for when coffee was roasted, not when the package was purchased.
tasting notes
Descriptors suggesting a coffee's perceived flavors, not necessarily added ingredients.

Useful word combinations

  • ask about the brewer: identify the customer's coffee-making equipment
  • leave the beans whole: sell the coffee without grinding it
  • read the tasting notes: explain the package's flavor descriptions
  • confirm before grinding: check the chosen product and intended brewer before an irreversible change

03 · Notice the language

Clarify the brewer before changing the beans

A tasting note doesn't prove an ingredient is present or absent. Ask about the brewer; don't prescribe an unsupported grind when the equipment is unknown.

  • What kind of brewer do they use?
  • Would you prefer to leave the beans ___?
  • That phrase describes flavor, not ___.

Improve this: Normal ground will work for anything.

The question avoids an irreversible change based on an undefined request.

  2. B. finely ground A fine grind assumes a requirement that hasn't been established.
  3. C. ground for espresso An espresso grind assumes a brewer the customer hasn't identified.

Your goal

State included items and additional charges before the customer commits.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A fictional cafe lists a latte at $4, an oat-drink upcharge of $0.50, and a pastry at $3. The $6 combo includes a standard latte and pastry; the oat upcharge still applies. Explain $6.50 for that combo, versus $7.50 separately, before payment.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

upcharge
An additional price for a specified change or extra item.
combo
A stated combination of items offered together at a listed price.
loyalty reward
A benefit available under a cafe's stated customer-reward conditions.
sold out
No longer available for purchase because the available stock has been used or sold.

Useful word combinations

  • quote the full drink price: state the price including selected changes
  • apply an eligible reward: use a benefit only when its stated conditions are met
  • offer the remaining choice: explain an available alternative to a sold-out item
  • separate item and offer prices: distinguish an individual price from a combination deal

03 · Notice the language

Explain the complete offer

An offer name doesn't specify every condition. Explain this fictional cafe's prices without implying all cafes charge the same way.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. six fifty

  1. A. seven dollars That adds one dollar rather than fifty cents.
  2. B. seven fifty That is the separate-item total, not the combo total.
  3. C. six dollars This leaves out the stated oat-drink upcharge.
  4. D. six fifty Correct. Six dollars plus fifty cents is six fifty.
2. Customer: This is my first order today. If cinnamon has sold out, what can replace it?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. instead

  1. A. instead Correct. 'Instead' offers the plain pastry as a substitute.
  2. B. as well 'As well' adds an item rather than replacing the unavailable one.
  3. C. again 'Again' implies a repeat order, unlike this first order.
  4. D. too 'Too' also signals addition rather than the requested replacement.

Your goal

Hand over orders, supplies, and a till question without merging them.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

At 2:00, ticket 108's coffee is complete but its tea is pending. Large takeaway lids are unavailable, though small lids remain. The outgoing worker counted the till, but the lead's reconciliation is pending. Assign the tea and stock follow-up without calling the till signed off.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

till
The cafe's sales register or the cash drawer associated with it.
cash float
The starting cash kept in a till to provide change.
reconciliation
A comparison of recorded transactions with counted money or other relevant records to identify differences.
stockout
A situation in which a needed product or supply is unavailable.

Useful word combinations

  • hand over the order queue: explain unfinished orders to the incoming barista
  • count the float: check the starting change cash without assuming sales totals
  • flag a till discrepancy: report a difference between the count and the record
  • report a stockout: name the exact unavailable product or supply

03 · Notice the language

Transfer three different statuses

Counting cash is not the same as reconciling it with records. Explain the specific stockout so the next worker doesn't assume every size is unavailable.

  • ___ is complete; ___ remains on the queue.
  • We're out of ___, not ___.
  • I've counted ___; ___ is still pending.

Improve this: Everything's done except a few things.

See a clearer version

One tea remains on 108; large lids are out; till reconciliation is pending.

The incoming worker can identify separate actions.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Incoming barista: Have all lid sizes run out?
Outgoing barista: No, the stockout affects ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. large lids only

  1. A. all packaging The shortage doesn't cover all packaging supplies.
  2. B. every cup size The case preserves stock of small lids.
  3. C. small lids only Small lids remain available in this case.
  4. D. large lids only Correct. Only large takeaway lids are reported unavailable.
2. Lead: Is the till reconciliation complete?
Worker: The cash is counted, but the comparison with records is ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. still pending

  1. A. still pending Correct. Counting alone doesn't complete the required comparison.
  2. B. already completed The case says that comparison hasn't been completed.
  3. C. fully signed off No completed review or sign-off has been given.
  4. D. no longer needed Counting doesn't remove the need to compare the records.

Dialogue 08: The closing worker inherits a customer query
Original fictional setting: A departing barista transfers an unfinished receipt query to the incoming counter worker.
Professional roles: Outgoing barista / Incoming worker
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Outgoing barista: Before I leave, one customer is waiting for an itemized receipt from an earlier mobile order.
Incoming worker: Do you have the order number, or should I ask them to explain everything again?
Outgoing barista: It's one-forty-six, under Avery. They received their drinks and paid; only the receipt query remains.
Incoming worker: Thanks. Have you already printed the itemized version, or is that still the action needed?
Outgoing barista: I found the completed transaction but haven't printed the itemized receipt. The lead is checking that option.
Incoming worker: I'll take the follow-up with the lead. I won't treat this as a new unpaid drink order.
Outgoing barista: Exactly. The customer doesn't want another payment or changes to the original drink specifications.
Incoming worker: I'll tell them I've taken over and ask the lead whether the requested receipt is available.
Outgoing barista: Please also explain if only a payment receipt is available; don't promise item detail we can't provide.
Incoming worker: Understood. Completed order, completed payment, itemized receipt still unresolved. Is there another issue attached to this customer?
Outgoing barista: No other issue. The till review is separate and remains with the lead, not this customer.
Incoming worker: I'll keep the two matters distinct and return to Avery with the lead's answer about the receipt.
Vocabulary in this exchange: mobile order: An order placed through a phone app or similar online ordering service. / till: The cafe's sales register or the cash drawer associated with it.
