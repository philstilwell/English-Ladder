Course: Dental Assisting English
Professional audience: Dental assistants and dental support staff
Scope: Fictional English practice, not dental training. Permitted duties, supervision, imaging authorization, and infection-prevention procedures depend on local requirements and qualifications. Refer diagnosis, treatment decisions, informed-consent questions, and aftercare advice to the authorized dental professional.
Lesson 01: Clarifying the appointment purpose
Original fictional case: A fictional patient arrives expecting a filling today. The schedule shows a thirty-minute consultation, and no procedure is confirmed. The dentist can clarify the purpose before the patient decides whether to continue.
Language workshop: Separate a booked discussion from a promised treatment
Communication goal: Check the appointment mismatch and explain what is confirmed.
Language guidance: 'Consultation' and 'procedure' are not interchangeable. Describe the booking you can see, acknowledge the patient's understanding, and ask the authorized person to clarify without blaming the patient or promising treatment.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): The schedule shows ___; you were expecting ___. / I haven't confirmed ___ for today's visit. / Let me check ___ before we say ___.
Vocabulary: consultation: A visit for discussing or assessing a concern with the appropriate dental professional. / procedure: A particular dental action or treatment, not simply any visit to the office. / appointment slot: A reserved period in the appointment schedule. / treatment plan: The clinician's proposed course of dental care, which requires appropriate explanation and agreement.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Patient: you took time off expecting a filling and remember discussing it by phone. You do not have a written procedure confirmation.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: Reception finds a note saying 'discuss filling options.' Explain why that note does not by itself confirm that a filling will be performed.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: I see a thirty-minute consultation on today's schedule, but you were expecting a filling. I have not confirmed a procedure for this visit. Let me check with the dentist and reception before we describe what will happen today. Please tell us what you understood from the earlier booking conversation.
END REFERENCE

Lesson 02: Room readiness and safe clarification
Original fictional case: A fictional instrument pack is present, but its required readiness documentation cannot be confirmed. The dentist is about to start. You are not authorized to waive the practice's checks. A verified replacement may be available through the lead assistant.
Language workshop: Speak up before a readiness assumption becomes an action
Communication goal: Name the unresolved check and ask for an authorized pause or alternative.
Language guidance: A room can look tidy without every required readiness check being complete. This lesson practices escalation language, not sterilization methods. Describe the missing confirmation accurately; do not label equipment contaminated without evidence or bypass a required check.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): Before we begin, I cannot confirm ___. / Could we hold ___ while ___ is checked? / The status is unverified, not confirmed as ___.
Vocabulary: operatory: The dental treatment room where patient care is provided. / instrument tray: The arranged set of instruments for a particular clinical task. / sterilization record: The required documentation used by the practice to verify an instrument-processing cycle. / turnover: The preparation of a room between patients under the practice's approved procedures.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Dentist: you are ready to begin and need a concise statement of the unresolved issue and a workable authorized next step.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: A colleague says the pack looks identical to a verified one. Explain why visual similarity does not answer the missing-record question.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Before we start, I need to flag a readiness discrepancy. I cannot confirm the required record for this pack, so I am not treating it as ready for use. Could we hold this setup while the lead assistant follows the approved verification process or provides a verified replacement? I will report the status as soon as it is confirmed.
