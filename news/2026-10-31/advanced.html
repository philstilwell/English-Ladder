📅 October 31, 2026 - Celebrity Traitors Finale Delivers Victory Amid Mixed Reactions

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Some of Britain's biggest stars spent four weeks engaged in a battle of wits on The Celebrity Traitors. By Friday's final, the original field of 21 celebrities had been whittled down to seven. Comedian Romesh Ranganathan and Heartstopper actor Sebastian “Bash” Croft ultimately emerged as the two winners. The pair shared an £83,000 charity prize after the contestants banished the last two traitors, Maya Jama and King Kenny. Their winnings will be divided between two refugee charities, the Refugee Council and Choose Love. Kenny had reached the endgame, the final stage, by turning on his fellow traitor Maya at the last round table. Singer James Blunt joined Kenny, Romesh and Bash in the endgame, where the group had to decide whether any traitors were left. When a vote left Kenny and James tied, Bash convinced Romesh to banish Kenny, thereby securing victory for the faithful contestants. James could also have won if he had chosen to end the game, but his decision to hold another vote led to his own eviction. The series also featured two unorthodox confessions, with traitors revealing their own identities before being swiftly removed by the others. Richard E Grant blurted out his secret role in the second episode because he was so excited to have been selected. James Acaster, described in the report as the programme's most reluctant traitor, gave himself up under pressure during Thursday's penultimate episode. An apparently misplaced reaction shot fuelled speculation about how heavily the round-table scene had been edited. The report suggested that Acaster's confession revived the programme after what some viewers regarded as a mid-series lull. More than 15 million people had been watching the series. A Hollywood Reporter article contrasted record-breaking ratings with a perceived consensus on social media that the series had been rather dull. Some viewers pointed to a shortage of double-crossing and explosive arguments, describing the celebrities as more friendly and strategically aware than earlier groups. The drama intensified in the final, which the report suggested was likely to have been the year's most watched television programme.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. battle of wits (noun phrase): A contest in which success depends on outthinking opponents through reasoning, perception or strategy.
2. whittled down (phrasal verb, past participle): Gradually made smaller through successive removals, emphasising a process rather than a single reduction.
3. turning on (phrasal verb, present participle): Acting against someone previously treated as an ally, implying a breakdown of loyalty.
4. unorthodox (adjective): Departing from an established or expected approach, without necessarily being forbidden.
5. blurted out (phrasal verb, past simple): Said something suddenly and without sufficient thought, often disclosing information unintentionally.
6. gave himself up (reflexive phrasal verb, past simple): Stopped concealing his identity and voluntarily admitted his role, allowing others to act against him.
7. fuelled (verb, past simple): Made an existing reaction or belief stronger, without necessarily providing proof that it was justified.
8. lull (countable noun): A temporary period of reduced activity or excitement within a continuing event.
9. consensus (noun): A broadly shared judgement within a group, which need not involve complete agreement.
10. double-crossing (uncountable noun): Deceiving someone who expects your cooperation, especially by secretly acting against their interests.

Advanced Grammar: Past unreal conditionals with a possible outcome
Use the past perfect in an “if” clause to imagine a different past decision, and “could have + past participle” for its possible result. Here, “could also have won” expresses an unrealised possibility; “would certainly have won” would instead present the outcome as certain.
Example from the text: "James could also have won if he had chosen to end the game, but his decision to hold another vote led to his own eviction."
