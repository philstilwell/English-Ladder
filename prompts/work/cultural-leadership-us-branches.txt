Lesson 01: Check meaning before assuming agreement
Original fictional case: Aiko recorded agreement to a Monday product launch after hearing that the plan was "fine." Jordan then sent a staffing concern. The branch has six trained people; the launch plan needs eight. Two more people may finish training on Friday, but completion is not yet confirmed. Aiko can authorize the branch launch after the staffing check.
Language workshop: When "fine" is not a green light
Communication goal: Clarify scope, distinguish acknowledgment from approval, and confirm conditions.
Language guidance: Acknowledgment confirms that a message was received or understood. Endorsement expresses support. Authorization permits action. Ask which of these the speaker is giving; do not record one as another.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): When you say "fine," are you referring to the date or the overall approach? / I heard support for the approach, not approval of the date. / My support is conditional on having eight trained people. / Can I record that as a commitment, or is it still a possibility? / That remains provisional until we complete the staffing check. / Could you confirm whether the additional training is complete? / Let me read that back so we have the same decision record. / I will send the revised record and ask you to confirm it. / I may be reading more into that than you intended. / We agree in principle; the staffing condition is still open. / By "ready," do you mean trained, scheduled, or available for the first shift? / Please acknowledge receipt separately from confirming approval. / What evidence would let us change the status from pending to confirmed? / Monday is our working date, subject to the Friday staffing check. / This note supersedes the earlier statement that the date was final. / Is there any condition missing from that summary?
Vocabulary: acknowledgment: A signal that a message has been received or understood. / endorsement: An expression of support for a proposal or position. / authorization: Permission from someone entitled to approve an action. / reservation: A concern that limits or qualifies support. / provisional: Not final; subject to a stated check or decision. / dependency: Something that must happen for another action to proceed. / read-back: A spoken restatement used to check the shared record. / sign-off: A formal or agreed approval step. / scope: The limits of what a statement or agreement covers. / conditional agreement: Support that applies only if a requirement is met. / go/no-go decision: A decision to proceed or stop at a defined checkpoint. / green light: Informal permission to proceed, not merely positive feedback. / clarification: Additional detail that resolves an unclear meaning. / alignment: A shared understanding or compatible direction; not necessarily formal approval. / pending: Waiting for an event, result, or decision. / prerequisite: A requirement that must be met before another action. / assumption: Something treated as true before it has been established. / explicit: Stated directly rather than left for the listener to infer. / implicit: Suggested or understood without being stated directly. / qualification: A condition or limitation attached to a statement. / tentative: Not firmly settled and still open to change. / supersede: Replace an earlier record, instruction, or decision. / discrepancy: A difference between accounts or records that should match. / in principle: At the level of the general idea, with details still unresolved.
Speaking task: Read turns 1-10 with a partner; insert your selected answers without adding facts. Switch roles for turns 11-20. Pause after each clarification question until the partner finishes the printed reply. Read the four-line vendor conversation twice, switching roles. Use the answer key only after both readers have selected their choices.
Partner: Aiko: International branch manager / Jordan: US-based operations lead
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: Aiko and Jordan now discuss a software demonstration. The team likes the interface. Security review is pending, and the purchasing director alone can approve a contract.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Aiko: I will amend the minutes now and label Monday provisional. We will make the go/no-go decision after your Friday update, not before it.
Jordan: Please send me the revised paragraph. I can confirm its accuracy today; that confirmation should not be treated as authorization to launch.
Aiko: I will make that distinction explicit. Thank you for raising it before the team acted on the old record.
Jordan: Thanks for checking. Next time I will say, "The sequence works; the date is still provisional." That leaves less room for a different interpretation.

Lesson 02: Challenge ideas respectfully
Original fictional case: Mei proposes a six-week pilot with two account managers. Chris says, "This proposal makes no sense." The plan assumes two hours of follow-up per account each week; the last trial used four. The staffing allocation is fixed for this pilot. Mei can revise the account count, but cannot add staff without separate approval.
Language workshop: A sharp objection at the budget review
Communication goal: Probe evidence, make a concession without surrendering the decision, and set a conduct boundary.
