Lesson 01: Turning a reference photo into a clear request
Original fictional case: Client Alex shows stylist Ren a photograph of a chin-length bob with a straight fringe. Alex likes the outline but wants enough length for a low ponytail. No cut has started. Through clarification, they agree to discuss a collarbone-length outline rather than copying the chin-length endpoint. Alex confirms that no fringe is requested. Ren must keep the visual reference, practical length requirement, and agreed plan distinct. The conversation does not guarantee that every strand will tie back or authorize other changes not specifically agreed.
Language workshop: Keep the ponytail length
Communication goal: Separate a reference image into chosen and unchosen features, then confirm the agreed length before cutting.
Language guidance: The photo has a chin-length endpoint and a straight fringe. Alex chooses neither automatically. The agreed outline discussion is collarbone-length, with no fringe requested and retained ponytail length as the practical requirement.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): Which features of this photograph do you like? / Do you want the outline, the length, or both? / The photograph shows a chin-length bob. / Do you need to be able to tie it back? / Keeping enough length for a low ponytail matters to you. / Shall we discuss a collarbone-length outline instead? / The photo has a straight fringe; is that part of your request? / No fringe is requested. / Let us agree on the length before any cut begins. / The photo is a reference, not permission for every feature. / How much front length do you want retained? / Is this the shape you mean? / I cannot promise every strand will tie back from the photo alone. / Please stop me if I have misunderstood the priority. / Collarbone-length outline, retained ponytail length, no fringe. / That is the agreed direction, with no cut started yet.
Vocabulary: consultation: Discussion to establish preferences, suitability, and the proposed service. / reference photo: Image used to communicate an idea, not automatically a complete instruction. / bob: Short-to-medium haircut family with a defined outline, varying in length and shape. / outline: Outer visible shape or boundary of the haircut. / perimeter: Outer edge defining the haircut's overall length and shape. / chin length: Length described relative to the chin. / collarbone length: Length described relative to the collarbone. / retained length: Hair length kept rather than removed. / low ponytail: Hair gathered and secured low at the back of the head. / fringe: Front hair shaped to fall over or around the forehead; also called bangs. / bangs: Common US term for a fringe. / straight fringe: Fringe with a visually straight outline across the front. / face-framing: Hair shaped around the face as a distinct design feature. / front length: Length retained in the hair toward the face. / side length: Length of hair at the sides of the head. / back length: Length of hair at the back of the head. / parting: Line or division where hair is separated; also called a part. / blunt outline: Perimeter with a visually solid, even edge. / layers: Hair lengths arranged at different levels within a haircut. / trim: Removal of a limited amount, which still needs an agreed length. / length removal: Amount of hair to be cut off. / shrinkage: Shorter visible length when curls recoil from a stretched or wet state. / agreed plan: Service details explicitly confirmed with the client. / consent: Permission for the specified action, not every possible variation.
Speaking task: Read turns 1-10 in pairs. Stress chin-length, collarbone-length, and no fringe without changing the selected outline. Switch roles for turns 11-20. Read the question about trim and distinguish length retained from length removed. Complete the shoulder-length transfer, check the key, then read it with the shorter reference and longer chosen endpoint clearly contrasted.
Partner: Alex: Client / Ren: Stylist
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A client shows an ear-length reference but agrees to a shoulder-length outline. The client wants front length retained and explicitly declines bangs. No cut has started.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Alex: Then let's agree on that before you start. I'd rather check the length in the mirror first.
Ren: Certainly. We can confirm the length removal before any cutting. Nothing has been cut yet.
Alex: Great. Longer than the picture, enough length to discuss tying it back, and definitely no fringe.
Ren: That's the agreed direction. I'll check the remaining details with you before we begin.

Client: Thank you. That's a fair description, and I can explain the front issue myself tomorrow.
