Your goal

Clarify similar laboratory identifiers without merging cases.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

The receiving log lists accession A314; a clinic caller asks about A341. No identity match has been established. Technician Noor can check the accession log. The caller must identify the requested case through the laboratory's privacy and verification arrangements.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

accession number
A laboratory-assigned identifier linking a received specimen or case to its testing records.
requisition
The clinician's paper or electronic request identifying the laboratory tests ordered.
specimen label
The identifying information attached to a specimen container under the laboratory's requirements.
accessioning
The laboratory's process of registering a specimen or case and associating it with its order.

Useful word combinations

  • confirm the accession number: establish which laboratory case is being discussed
  • match the requisition to the label: compare the order and specimen identifiers
  • read back an identifier: repeat a complete code to check accurate communication
  • report an accessioning discrepancy: identify an unresolved registration mismatch

03 · Notice the language

Resolve the reference before answering the status question

Repeat the complete identifier and name its source. A similar name, location, or code is not a verified match. These exchanges do not replace the laboratory's identity requirements.

  • Your enquiry says ___; the receiving log lists ___.
  • Could you read the complete ___ back?
  • We have not yet established that ___ refers to ___.

Improve this: The digits are nearly the same, so it must be your sample.

See a clearer version

A314 and A341 differ. Let's establish the intended accession before discussing status.

The revised wording prevents a reference guess from becoming an identity claim.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Clinic: Which reference did the laboratory assign to this received case?
Technician: Its ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. accession number

  1. A. accession number Correct. The accession number links the laboratory case and records.
  2. B. invoice total An invoice total is a payment amount.
  3. C. reagent code A reagent code identifies a testing material, not the patient case.
  4. D. appointment date An appointment date identifies timing, not the registered case.
2. Caller: A341 is the same as A314, isn't it?
Technician: No. The digits are ____, so we must clarify the reference.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. transposed

  1. A. transposed Correct. The last two digits have exchanged positions.
  2. B. omitted Omitted would mean missing, not exchanged.
  3. C. identical Identical would mean exactly the same sequence.
  4. D. consecutive Consecutive means following one another in order.

05 · Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. A314 or A341

Fictional laboratory call using the accession case.

  1. Clinic caller: I'm asking about A341. Has it reached the laboratory, or is it still with the courier?
  2. Technician: Our log lists accession number A314. Let's clarify it before discussing specimen status.
  3. Clinic caller: Both came from our clinic, so I assumed the two references described the same case.
  4. Technician: The clinic location doesn't establish a match. Could you check the reference on your request?
  5. Clinic caller: The requisition says A314. I transposed the last two digits in my note.
  6. Technician: Thank you. Please repeat the full identifier so we can confirm the enquiry accurately.
  7. Clinic caller: A314. I'm asking whether that specimen's receipt is recorded, not whether a result is complete.
  8. Technician: Following our verification checks, I can confirm receipt for A314. Testing status is a separate question.
  9. Clinic caller: I'll correct my enquiry note to A314 and keep the question limited to receipt.
  10. Technician: That resolves the reference mismatch. Please use A314 for any further enquiry about this case.
2. An order without a registered specimen

Fictional electronic order exists, but laboratory receipt has not been confirmed.

  1. Clinic caller: The order appears in our system, so why can't I see an accession receipt?
  2. Technician: An electronic order and a received specimen are different things. I can see the request, not confirmed receipt.
  3. Clinic caller: Does that mean the specimen is lost somewhere between the clinic and the laboratory?
  4. Technician: We haven't established that. The order alone doesn't tell us whether collection or dispatch occurred.
  5. Clinic caller: I'll check the clinic's collection and courier records instead of assuming the laboratory has it.
  6. Technician: Please use the order reference when you check. That helps us compare the same enquiry.
  7. Clinic caller: Can you mark received while we look, just so the order stays visible to staff?
  8. Technician: Received would be inaccurate. The order can remain visible with specimen receipt unconfirmed.
  9. Clinic caller: Understood. I'll report that distinction to the clinician rather than say testing has begun.
  10. Technician: I'll keep the receipt enquiry open under the order reference while your clinic checks its records.
3. One request, an additional container

Fictional receiving enquiry about a second container; intended relationship is unresolved.

  1. Technician: A second container arrived with paperwork mentioning the same request. Was it intended as an additional specimen?
  2. Clinic coordinator: I need to check with the collection team. The courier sheet doesn't explain the relationship.
  3. Technician: Please don't infer that it replaces the first specimen simply because it arrived later.
  4. Clinic coordinator: I won't. Do you need the intended relationship or confirmation of a particular test result?
  5. Technician: The relationship is the question. No result status is being discussed in this call.
  6. Clinic coordinator: I'll ask the collection team which request the second container was intended to accompany.
  7. Technician: Our receiving lead will review the specimen label and paperwork before resolving this accessioning discrepancy.
  8. Clinic coordinator: Should I describe both as duplicates in the clinic note while we check?
  9. Technician: Not yet. Duplicate would be a conclusion about their relationship, which remains unresolved.
  10. Clinic coordinator: I'll describe a second container with an unclear relationship and get the collection team's account.

Two scenarios to practice

Scenario 1 · Accessioning and unambiguous identifiers

Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.

Partner's role and follow-up

Clinic caller: your note says A341; check the actual request when asked.

Try a harder second round

Say both cases came from the same clinic and ask whether that is enough to match them.

Scenario 2 · A shared surname on two requests

Two fictional requests have the same surname but different accession references. A caller uses the surname only. The technician can request the full required verification; neither case status has been disclosed.

Role A: Technician: explain why the surname does not identify one case.

Role B: Caller: you have the request reference but initially omit it.

Possible opening: “I'm checking the Morgan sample from this morning.”

Success checks
  • Ask for the required reference and identity verification.
  • Avoid choosing a case by arrival order.
  • Read back the established reference before discussing its status.
Add a complication

The caller assumes the earlier arrival must be the intended case.

Studying alone? Speak both roles aloud. For each scenario, prepare for two minutes, speak for one minute, then answer a follow-up. Switch roles and repeat using fewer notes. Use only the supplied facts; identify missing information instead of inventing it.

Compare scenario 1 with a model response
Before we discuss a specimen status, could we clarify the accession number? Our receiving log lists A314, while your enquiry says A341. Those are different identifiers. Please check the reference on your request through our verification arrangements. I will keep the enquiries separate until we establish which case you mean; no match has been confirmed yet.

This is one possible spoken response, not the only acceptable wording. Preserve the case facts when you try your own version.

Notice: Clarify similar laboratory identifiers without merging cases. Identify the wording that does this, then name one detail from the case that the response preserves.

Apply it to this case

In “Accessioning and unambiguous identifiers”, choose one confirmed detail from the situation and one item that still needs clarification. Say a two-sentence response using “Your enquiry says ___; the receiving log lists ___.”. Keep the known detail accurate and ask about the missing one.

Check your reasoning

Compare with the case above and the model response. Can you point to the words that support your factual statement? Is your question about something the case leaves open? If you introduce a possible outcome, clearly label it as a possibility.

Review, then try again

  • Keeps the case facts accurate; clearly separates confirmed and unknown information.
  • Uses understandable sentences and explains specialist terms when the listener needs it.
  • Responds to the other person's question, checks understanding, and uses an appropriate tone.
  • Makes the requested action or unresolved question clear without inventing authority or facts.

Revise one sentence and repeat the response. A useful response can be clear even with a few grammar errors; judge meaning and task completion, not accent.

1. Clinic: The run finished. Are results released?
Technician: Not yet; the quality-control review remains ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. unresolved

  1. A. archived Archived means stored, not awaiting a decision.
  2. B. completed Completed would say the review has finished.
  3. C. unresolved Correct. Unresolved describes the outstanding review.
  4. D. waived Waived means the requirement was set aside.
2. Lead: What checks the test system rather than representing this patient's specimen?
Technician: The ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. control material

  1. A. patient aliquot A patient aliquot is part of a patient sample.
  2. B. control material Correct. Control material monitors test-system performance.
  3. C. recollection specimen A recollection specimen is another patient sample.
  4. D. referral specimen A referral specimen is sent for external testing.

Vocabulary for this lesson

turnaround time
The interval between defined starting and ending events in a laboratory service, such as receipt and reporting.
STAT priority
A designation for urgent testing under the laboratory's established priority and response arrangements.
backlog
Laboratory work waiting to be completed beyond the work currently being processed.
add-on request
A request to perform additional testing on an existing specimen, subject to order and suitability requirements.

  • define the turnaround clock: state which events start and end the quoted interval
  • update the expected reporting time: revise a timing estimate using current information
  • clarify the stated priority: check the requested urgency against the recorded order

Name the starting point before discussing the estimate

Turnaround intervals need defined start and end events. Correcting the starting point does not eliminate delays. STAT is a priority designation, not a promise that a particular result already exists.

Vocabulary for this lesson

preliminary report
A report issued before all relevant testing or review is complete, with its stated limitations.
final report
A report issued at the completed reporting stage for the specified request, subject to any later correction.
amended report
A report revised after issue, with the change recorded through the laboratory's correction process.
reference interval
A comparison range supplied with some test results, derived for a defined reference population and method.

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

critical result
A result meeting the laboratory's established criteria for prompt notification through its designated clinical pathway.
read-back
Repeating communicated details so the sender can check that they were received accurately.
notification log
The laboratory record of result-notification attempts, recipients, acknowledgments, and times.
escalation pathway
The designated sequence of contacts and actions used when the required response or recipient is not reached.

Useful word combinations

  • verify the receiving clinician: establish that the intended qualified recipient is on the line
  • obtain a read-back: request repetition of the critical communicated details
  • record acknowledgment: document that the intended recipient confirmed receipt
  • escalate an unanswered notification: use the designated pathway when the required contact is not achieved

03 · Notice the language

Complete the read-back and correct what was misheard

The sender states the information, the receiver repeats it, and the sender confirms or corrects it. Analyte means the substance measured. Millimoles per liter (mmol/L) and milligrams per deciliter (mg/dL) are different units; do not interchange or convert them in these exercises. All critical designations are fictional lab-specific facts, not universal thresholds.

  • For verified case ___: [analyte], [value], [units].
  • You repeated ___; the reported value is ___. Please read it back.
  • That read-back matches. Please confirm you accept this notification.

Improve this: You heard a number, so I'll mark it done.

See a clearer version

You repeated six point one; the report says six point four millimoles per liter. Please repeat the complete result.

A corrected number needs a fresh complete read-back, followed by the sender's confirmation.

A documented record of possession and transfer used when required for a specimen's particular purpose.

03 · Notice the language

Transfer the item-level question, not just the parcel status

A manifest lists intended contents; parcel delivery does not itself confirm item acceptance. Chain-of-custody requirements apply only in their proper context, not automatically to every routine specimen.

  • The manifest lists ___; the receiving laboratory has confirmed ___.
  • The outstanding question is ___ for accession ___.
  • Please confirm you accept both ___ and ___.

Improve this: The courier delivered it, so everything is finished.

See a clearer version

The parcel arrived; X8's acceptance and the clinic update remain outstanding.

This identifies the item-level gap and the communication still owed.
