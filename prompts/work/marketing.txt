Lesson 01: Marketing Strategy: Audience, Insight, Problem, Outcome
Original fictional case: A scheduling-software brief names the audience as "everyone who works in an office." Six exploratory interviews with operations coordinators at small agencies describe repeated calendar checking. No research with purchasing managers has been completed. The proposed campaign invites coordinators to book a workflow demonstration. A draft slide calls those six interviews proof of market-wide demand. Strategist Mira and product marketer Owen must revise the brief without inventing buyer research or promising that a demonstration request will become a sale.
Language workshop: The scheduling tool with an audience of everyone
Communication goal: Narrow an audience brief while distinguishing the daily user, purchasing role, and evidence of need.
Language guidance: An audience is not necessarily the buyer, and the buyer is not necessarily the person using the product daily. "Coordinators report repeated calendar checking" preserves the actual sample. "Office workers demand our product" changes both the population and the strength of the finding.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): For this campaign, which role are we trying to reach first? / The interviews involved six coordinators, not the entire office market. / The daily user may not control the purchasing budget. / We want qualified coordinators to book a workflow demonstration. / Repeated calendar checking is the reported friction. / That is an exploratory signal, not proof of market-wide demand. / We still need to understand the purchasing manager's criteria. / Let us revise the audience, evidence statement, and conversion action. / We should label the buyer assumption as a hypothesis. / Which part of the current workflow would the demonstration address? / A focused first campaign does not rule out other segments later. / We need an agreed meaning of qualified before reporting those requests. / Does the slide distinguish what people said from our interpretation? / What prevents the user from trying the workflow? / A demonstration request is not the same as a completed sale. / We have a useful starting point, with buyer research still open.
Vocabulary: target audience: The people a particular communication is intended to reach. / market segment: A group distinguished by relevant shared needs or characteristics. / ideal customer profile: A description of organizations or customers considered a strong fit. / buyer persona: A research-informed representation of a purchasing role. / end user: The person who uses the product in practice. / economic buyer: The role controlling the relevant purchasing budget. / buying committee: The group influencing or deciding an organizational purchase. / pain point: A specific problem the audience experiences. / customer insight: An interpretation that explains a meaningful customer pattern. / jobs to be done: A framework focused on the progress people seek in a situation. / use case: A concrete situation in which a product serves a need. / value proposition: The relevant benefit and reason to choose an offering. / demand signal: Evidence suggesting interest or need, with limits. / qualitative research: Research examining experiences and meaning rather than only numerical estimates. / sample bias: Distortion arising from who is included in a sample. / purchase trigger: An event or condition prompting buying consideration. / decision criterion: A factor used to compare possible purchases. / barrier to adoption: A condition that makes initial or continued use harder. / addressable market: The market that could be served under a specified definition. / penetration: Adoption or participation relative to a defined market base. / campaign objective: The particular outcome a campaign is intended to achieve. / conversion action: The defined behavior counted as a conversion. / leading indicator: A measure that may precede a later outcome. / research hypothesis: A testable proposition that still requires evidence.

Stress the proposed demo-booking action and the buyer research still missing; keep a demonstration request separate from a sale. Read the corrected transfer twice. Keep employees as users and the learning director as purchase approver.
Partner: Mira: Marketing strategist / Owen: Product marketer
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.

Mira: I will narrow the brief and change the call to action to a demonstration request. Send me the unanswered purchasing questions for the research brief.
