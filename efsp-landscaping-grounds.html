01Confirming the booked grounds workName what the visit includes · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Clarify an extra task without dismissing the customer's request.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Oak Court's work order includes front-lawn mowing and path leaf clearance. Resident asks for rear-hedge shaping. Hedges are excluded from today's booking; office can review a separate request, with no price or date confirmed.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

work order
The job record stating the grounds work requested or agreed for a visit.
service area
The specified part of a property included in a grounds job.
exclusion
A task or location expressly left outside the agreed work.
recurring visit
A grounds-service appointment that repeats on an agreed schedule.

Useful word combinations

  • confirm the work order: compare the customer's request with the job record
  • define the service area: name the included part of the property
  • flag an extra request: separate new work from the existing booking
  • check the recurring schedule: verify which tasks happen on which visits

C Notice the language

Name what the visit includes

A recurring service does not automatically include every grounds task on every visit.

  • Today's work order includes ___.
  • ___ is outside this visit's listed work.
  • Would you like the office to review ___ separately?

Improve this: "We do the garden."

See a clearer version

Today's booking covers the front lawn and path; hedge shaping needs a separate review.

Specific tasks prevent a broad description from becoming an unintended promise.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Resident: Is the hedge part of today's booking?
Worker: No; it is an ____ request.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. additional

  1. A. included The work order excludes it.
  2. B. additional Correct. The hedge adds to the listed work.
  3. C. identical It differs from mowing and leaf clearance.
  4. D. completed It has not been completed.
2. Office: Is every recurring visit a hedge visit?
Worker: No, the tasks vary ____ the agreed schedule.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. according to

  1. A. except 'Except' does not express dependence on a schedule.
  2. B. despite 'Despite' suggests contradiction rather than following the schedule.
  3. C. according to Correct. 'According to' links tasks to the schedule.
  4. D. instead of 'Instead of' would replace the schedule.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. The rear hedge is a new request

At Oak Court, a worker checks the resident's expectation against today's work order.

  1. Resident: Could you shape the rear hedge during today's grounds visit?
  2. Worker: Today's work order covers the front lawn and leaf clearance from the path, not hedge shaping.
  3. Resident: I thought our recurring service meant the whole garden was included on every visit.
  4. Worker: The recurring schedule lists particular tasks. I can ask the office to review your hedge request separately.
  5. Resident: Would asking them now guarantee that your crew can finish it during this visit?
  6. Worker: No. The scope, availability, and any price need confirmation before we describe it as booked.
  7. Resident: Please query the rear hedge, not the front shrubs.
  8. Worker: I'll name that location clearly and keep it separate from the work already listed today.
  9. Resident: Thank you. Can the office contact me with the actual options rather than a general estimate?
  10. Worker: I'll request office contact without promising extra work or timing.
2. The annual task is not every visit

A grounds worker clarifies a customer's seasonal-service expectation.

  1. Customer: Why isn't seasonal bed review listed on this week's work order?
  2. Worker: The agreement covers several visits, but this work order lists lawn care and front-path clearance only.
  3. Customer: Does that mean the seasonal review has been removed from my service agreement altogether?
  4. Worker: I don't have evidence of that. A task absent from today's list isn't necessarily canceled from the agreement.
  5. Customer: Can you check which visit it belongs to so I don't keep asking every crew?
  6. Worker: I can ask the office to confirm its scheduled visit and let you know the current status.
  7. Customer: Please specify front beds; another company handles our rear beds.
  8. Worker: I'll include that limit. We shouldn't combine two different service areas under a vague garden review.
  9. Customer: Then today's crew completes its listed work, while the office confirms the seasonal appointment separately.
  10. Worker: Today's work remains as listed; the office will clarify the seasonal task.
3. A request from someone who is not the contact

A worker checks a tenant's extra-work request at an office property.

  1. Tenant: Could you add the side courtyard? Our visitors arrive later.
  2. Worker: The work order names the main entrance only. Who coordinates grounds requests for your building?
  3. Tenant: Mara in facilities is the site contact, but I'm the tenant using the courtyard today.
  4. Worker: Thanks. I can relay your request to Mara rather than treat it as a change already approved.
  5. Tenant: Please explain the visitor timing, because otherwise she might think I'm asking about next month.
  6. Worker: I will. What time are visitors expected, and which courtyard entrance are they using?
  7. Tenant: Two o'clock at the east door; the rear parking area is excluded.
  8. Worker: I'll state the side courtyard and east door, with the two-o'clock event as your constraint.
  9. Tenant: That describes it accurately. I understand you can't promise the additional work on your own.
  10. Worker: The main-entrance booking stays unchanged while Mara reviews your courtyard request.

Two scenarios to practice

Partner's role and follow-up

Try a harder second round

Scenario 2 · Two tasks under the word tidy

Work order: clear leaves from front steps. Customer says 'tidy the front' and also means reshape shrubs. Shrub work is not booked.

Role A: Worker: separate cleanup from plant work.

Role B: Customer: ask about both, but accept a separate scope review.

Possible opening: “When you say tidy, do you mean the leaves or the shrub shape?”

Success checks
  • Name the included leaf clearance.
  • Describe the shrub request separately.
  • Avoid quoting an unconfirmed price.
Add a complication

Customer assumes both tasks have the same price.

Studying alone? Speak both roles aloud. For each scenario, prepare for two minutes, speak for one minute, then answer a follow-up. Switch roles and repeat using fewer notes. Use only the supplied facts; identify missing information instead of inventing it.

Compare scenario 1 with a model response
Today's work order covers the front lawn and leaf clearance from the path. Rear-hedge shaping isn't included in this visit. Is that an additional request you would like the office to review? I'll keep the new hedge request separate from the booked work so we don't imply that its price or timing has already been agreed.

This is one possible spoken response, not the only acceptable wording. Preserve the case facts when you try your own version.

Notice: Clarify an extra task without dismissing the customer's request. Identify the wording that does this, then name one detail from the case that the response preserves.

Apply it to this case

In “Confirming the booked grounds work”, choose one confirmed detail from the situation and one item that still needs clarification. Say a two-sentence response using “Today's work order includes ___.”. Keep the known detail accurate and ask about the missing one.

Check your reasoning

Compare with the case above and the model response. Can you point to the words that support your factual statement? Is your question about something the case leaves open? If you introduce a possible outcome, clearly label it as a possibility.

Review, then try again

  • Keeps the case facts accurate; clearly separates confirmed and unknown information.
  • Uses understandable sentences and explains specialist terms when the listener needs it.
  • Responds to the other person's question, checks understanding, and uses an appropriate tone.
  • Makes the requested action or unresolved question clear without inventing authority or facts.

Revise one sentence and repeat the response. A useful response can be clear even with a few grammar errors; judge meaning and task completion, not accent.

02Clarifying access and property limitsKeep uncertain boundaries explicit · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Decline an unconfirmed extension while preserving the agreed job.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Customer asks for clearing beyond a rear fence, saying the strip is probably theirs. Work plan ends at the fence; ownership is unconfirmed. Worker can flag the request to the office but cannot decide the property boundary.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

access arrangement
The agreed way and time for workers to enter a property or service area.
property boundary
The limit separating properties; its legal position requires appropriate confirmation, not a worker's guess.
no-work area
A specifically identified area excluded from work during the job.
site contact
The named person who answers access or job-location questions at a property.

Useful word combinations

  • confirm access with the site contact: verify entry arrangements
  • identify the no-work area: name the excluded location
  • query the boundary: ask for confirmation rather than infer ownership
  • preserve the agreed limit: keep work within the confirmed scope

C Notice the language

Keep uncertain boundaries explicit

A fence, hedge, or informal statement may not establish the legal property limit. Practice referral, not boundary advice.

  • Our confirmed work area ends at ___.
  • The status of ___ hasn't been established.
  • We can refer ___ while continuing with ___.

Improve this: "It's close enough to yours."

See a clearer version

The plan ends at the fence; the extra strip needs confirmation before inclusion.

The response does not make an ownership judgment.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Customer: Does the fence prove the legal boundary?
Worker: No; it shows our current work ____, not a legal conclusion.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. limit

  1. A. limit Correct. The fence marks the plan's agreed work limit.
  2. B. estimate An estimate does not identify this location boundary.
  3. C. schedule A schedule concerns timing rather than this area limit.
  4. D. finish standard A finish standard describes the result, not property ownership.
2. Office: Has the strip beyond the fence been confirmed as included?
Worker: No; it is ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. awaiting inclusion confirmation

  1. A. awaiting inclusion confirmation Correct. Inclusion of the additional area is still unconfirmed.
  2. B. already inside the work order Today's work order ends at the fence.
  3. C. excluded from every future visit Today's limit does not decide every future booking.
  4. D. part of every recurring visit No agreement makes the strip part of recurring work.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Beyond the rear fence

A grounds worker responds to an uncertain property-area request.

  1. Customer: Could you clear beyond the fence? I think that strip is ours.
  2. Worker: The confirmed work plan ends at the fence. The extra strip isn't included in today's area.
  3. Customer: But the previous owner used it. Doesn't that make it reasonable to include while you're here?
  4. Worker: I can't establish the property boundary from that history. The office needs to review the request.
  5. Customer: Then you're not saying it belongs to someone else; you're saying it hasn't been confirmed?
  6. Worker: Exactly. I'm describing the limit of this job, not making a legal ownership decision.
  7. Customer: Can your team continue within the confirmed area while I contact the office?
  8. Worker: Yes, subject to the lead's site decisions. I'll keep the extra-area request separate in the handoff.
  9. Customer: Please call it the strip beyond the rear fence, not the whole back garden.
  10. Worker: I'll identify the strip without changing today's confirmed work boundary.
2. A locked gate is an access issue

A worker calls the named site contact about a closed service entrance.

  1. Worker: The agreed courtyard service gate is locked; we've arrived for the visit.
  2. Site contact: I thought reception had arranged access. Have you spoken with anyone who can confirm today's entry arrangement?
  3. Worker: Reception said they weren't given the booking. I haven't treated another entrance as approved access.
  4. Site contact: I can coordinate with the building team. Please keep the issue described as access unconfirmed, not customer cancellation.
  5. Worker: Understood. The scheduled work still exists; we can't yet reach the agreed service area.
  6. Site contact: The building contact will call you in ten minutes with a confirmed arrangement or a status update.
  7. Worker: Ten minutes means an update, not guaranteed access, correct?
  8. Site contact: Yes. I don't want you planning around an opening time the building team hasn't confirmed.
  9. Worker: I'll tell the lead the access check is underway and keep the rest of the visit status accurate.
  10. Site contact: I'll give reception the courtyard work-order reference when checking access.
3. A marked no-work area

A worker confirms an excluded flower bed with a facilities contact.

  1. Worker: The bed beside the west bench is marked as a no-work area today.
  2. Facilities contact: That's correct. Another team is reviewing it, and this grounds visit should exclude that particular bed.
  3. Worker: A resident asked us to include it because the rest of the courtyard is being serviced.
  4. Facilities contact: Please explain that the bed has a specific exclusion, not that the entire courtyard visit is canceled.
  5. Worker: Should I refer their question to you rather than try to explain the other team's technical work?
  6. Facilities contact: Yes. You can identify me as the site contact for that request without guessing what their review involves.

Vocabulary for this lesson

hedge line
The visible outline or agreed shape of a row of closely grown shrubs.
flower bed
A defined planting area intended for flowers or other ornamental plants.

An individual plant treated as a distinctive feature of the landscape.

A customer's proposed change to plant growth or shape that requires suitability and scope review.

Useful word combinations

  • describe the desired appearance: explain the visual result being requested
  • identify the specimen plant: distinguish the individual plant concerned
  • confirm which bed: use a location rather than a vague direction
  • refer a pruning request: ask the qualified lead to review suitability

Replace vague appearance words with a clear request

Clarifying a preference is not diagnosing plant health or prescribing pruning.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. clarifying

  2. B. clarified The past form does not follow 'for' this way.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. preference

  1. A. method No pruning method has been selected or agreed.

Your goal

Give a useful weather update without making unsupported safety or scheduling judgments.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Lead has paused Cedar Court's planned outdoor work because of site conditions after rain. Office suggests Thursday, but crew availability is unconfirmed. Customer has an event Friday. Worker can relay the event and request a scheduling update by Wednesday noon.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

weather delay
A change in planned grounds work caused by weather conditions affecting the job.
rescheduled visit
A replacement appointment confirmed after the original visit changes.
service interval
The time between successive grounds-service visits.
provisional date
A suggested date that has not yet become a confirmed appointment.

Useful word combinations

  • report a weather delay: explain the changed visit status
  • offer a provisional date: identify a possible time without confirming it
  • review the service interval: check the gap created by a delay
  • confirm the revised visit: state the actual agreed replacement booking

C Notice the language

Explain what is paused and what is provisional

Relay the lead's decision and the actual booking status. Avoid declaring a site suitable yourself.

  • The lead has paused ___ because ___.
  • ___ is provisional until ___ confirms it.
  • Your event is ___; I'll request an update by ___.

Improve this: "We'll definitely be back Thursday."

See a clearer version

Thursday is proposed; the office still needs to confirm crew availability.

The revision prevents a suggestion from becoming a guarantee.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Customer: Is Thursday definitely booked?
Worker: No, it is still ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. provisional

  1. A. canceled Thursday has not been described as canceled.
  2. B. overdue No confirmed Thursday appointment is overdue.
  3. C. completed The visit has not occurred.
  4. D. provisional Correct. It is proposed but unconfirmed.
2. Office: Is Wednesday noon the finish time?
Worker: No, it is the requested ____ time.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. update

  1. A. inspection No inspection appointment was booked for Wednesday noon.
  2. B. arrival No crew arrival was promised for Wednesday noon.
  3. C. completion No work completion was promised for Wednesday noon.
  4. D. update Correct. Wednesday noon is the requested communication deadline.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Thursday remains provisional

A worker updates Cedar Court's customer about a paused visit.

  1. Worker: The lead paused today's outdoor work because of post-rain site conditions.
  2. Customer: The office mentioned Thursday. My event is Friday, so can I count on everything being finished beforehand?
  3. Worker: Thursday is a provisional date; crew availability and completion before your event remain unconfirmed.
  4. Customer: Please tell the office the front entrance matters most to us for Friday's visitors.
  5. Worker: I'll relay that priority as your request, not as a change already approved by the crew.
  6. Customer: Could someone give me an update by Wednesday noon even if the date remains uncertain?
  7. Worker: I'll request Wednesday noon's update, explaining your Friday event constraint.
  8. Customer: Then Wednesday noon is the update request, and Thursday is still only a suggested visit?
  9. Worker: Exactly. The office needs to confirm an actual replacement arrangement before we treat it as booked.
  10. Customer: Thanks. I'll keep Thursday provisional when discussing arrangements with our event team.
2. A visit moved, not removed

A worker clarifies a weather message with a customer.

  1. Customer: Does postponed mean this month's service has been canceled?
  2. Worker: No. It means the scheduled visit has been delayed; the replacement date is still being arranged.
  3. Customer: I was concerned we'd skip straight to next month and lose the work that was due.
  4. Worker: I can pass that concern to scheduling and ask how the delay affects the service interval.
  5. Customer: Please don't promise an extra visit unless the office has actually agreed one for our property.
  6. Worker: I won't. I'll ask for the revised arrangement rather than infer it from the word postponed.
  7. Customer: Is Tuesday confirmed, or merely a provisional replacement date?
  8. Worker: The note labels Tuesday provisional. The office still needs to confirm the crew and timing.

Your goal

Acknowledge a visible shortfall without accepting unrelated scope changes.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Customer says path cleanup was incomplete after today's front-lawn visit. Worker can see clippings beside the front steps, within the listed cleanup area. Rear patio is excluded. Lead can organize the missed front cleanup; no wider task is agreed.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

finish standard
The agreed description of how the completed grounds work should look or be left.
clippings
Cut pieces of grass or other vegetation produced during grounds maintenance.
debris removal
The collection and removal of specified loose waste material from the job area.
walk-through
A joint review of the worked area to check completion against the agreed tasks.

Useful word combinations

  • acknowledge a missed area: recognize a specific unfinished part
  • compare with the finish standard: check the result against the agreement
  • arrange a walk-through: review the actual area together
  • clarify debris removal: distinguish included cleanup from additional waste

C Notice the language

Own the specific omission

An apology can name the actual missed work. It need not imply that every requested extra was included.

  • You're right that ___ remains within the listed area.
  • I'm sorry ___ was missed.
  • I'll confirm how ___ will be completed.

Improve this: "We cleaned everything."

See a clearer version

The front-step area still has clippings; that part of the listed cleanup was missed.

The response acknowledges the customer's supported observation.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Customer: Are the front steps an extra?
Worker: No; that cleanup was already ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. included

  1. A. excluded The front steps were included, not excluded from cleanup.
  2. B. included Correct. It was part of the agreed task.
  3. C. unrelated It directly concerns the booked cleanup.
  4. D. provisional It was listed, not merely proposed.
2. Lead: Does this include the rear patio now?
Worker: No, that remains a ____ request.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. separate

  1. A. duplicate No duplicate request is established.
  2. B. withdrawn The customer has not withdrawn it.
  3. C. separate Correct. It is distinct from correcting the missed front area.
  4. D. completed It has not been done.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

At Pine Court, front-path leaves are cleared. Rear-bed review was deferred by the lead; no planting or diagnosis was done. Customer requests a callback about that bed before Friday. Office contact Ava owns scheduling; a visit date is not booked.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

completion note
A job record distinguishing completed grounds tasks from any stated exceptions.
follow-up visit
A later appointment to review or complete specifically identified work.
seasonal task
A grounds task scheduled for a particular season or period, subject to the job plan.
outstanding item
A task or decision still unresolved at the end of the current visit.

Useful word combinations

  • separate completed from deferred: name each task's real status
  • hand over the customer request: preserve its exact scope
  • confirm the follow-up owner: identify who arranges the next response
  • close out the visit: report the actual completed work and exceptions

C Notice the language

Close a visit without closing unresolved work

  • ___ is complete; ___ remains deferred.
  • The customer requests ___ before ___.
  • ___ owns the callback; the visit date is ___.

Improve this: "Pine Court is finished."

See a clearer version

The front path is complete; the rear-bed review and callback remain outstanding.

The distinction prevents unfinished work being lost.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Office: Did deferred mean the customer canceled it?
Worker: No, it means the review was ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. postponed

  1. A. diagnosed The review was postponed; no diagnosis was made.
  2. B. replaced The work was not replaced.
  3. C. completed The review has not finished.
  4. D. postponed Correct. Deferred means postponed, not canceled or completed.
2. Customer: Is the callback a return appointment?
Worker: No; a callback arranges ____, not automatically a site visit.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. contact

  1. A. contact Correct. The callback arranges communication, not a site visit.
  2. B. completion A callback does not establish completed site work.
  3. C. approval A callback alone does not approve a grounds task.
  4. D. assessment A site assessment has not been booked here.
