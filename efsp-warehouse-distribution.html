01Receiving against the expected shipmentReport the extra quantity precisely · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Separate receipt facts from a purchasing decision.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

Purchase order 740 expects twenty cartons. Receiving counts twenty-two with matching item labels. The supplier has not explained the additional two; the clerk can flag an overage but cannot approve an order change.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

advance shipping notice
A supplier's message describing goods expected to arrive, often shortened to ASN.
receiving discrepancy
A difference between an arriving shipment and its expected quantity, identity, or condition.
purchase order
A buyer's order identifying requested goods and quantities, often shortened to PO.
overage
A quantity received above the quantity expected for that shipment.

Useful word combinations

  • compare the expected quantity: check arrival against the notice
  • report an overage: identify extra units with their reference
  • match the purchase order: link goods to the buyer's order
  • keep the discrepancy open: avoid calling an unresolved difference settled

03 · Notice the language

Report the extra quantity precisely

A matching label does not by itself approve an order increase.

  • We expected ___; we counted ___.
  • The labels match, but ___ remains unclear.
  • Please refer the quantity difference to ___.

Improve this: The order is now twenty-two.

See a clearer version

Twenty-two arrived against an expected twenty; the extra two need review.

Arrival changes the observed count, not automatically the order.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Receiver: Twenty were expected and twenty-two arrived.
Clerk: That is an ____ of two cartons.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. overage

  1. A. balance Balance alone does not identify the extra received quantity.
  2. B. allocation Allocation concerns assigned stock, not an excess receipt.
  3. C. overage Correct. Twenty-two against twenty creates a two-carton overage.
  4. D. shortage A shortage would mean fewer than twenty arrived.
2. Buyer: Did matching labels approve the extra cartons?
Clerk: No. They help confirm ____, not an increase to the order.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. the received item's identity

  1. A. the amended purchase-order total The purchase order has not been amended by this check.
  2. B. the supplier's instruction to send extras Matching labels do not reveal the supplier's shipping instruction.
  3. C. the buyer's acceptance of extras Item identity does not establish the buyer's approval.
  4. D. the received item's identity Correct. Labels support identification, not approval of extra quantity.

05 · Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Two cartons above the order

Receiving clerk and buyer discuss purchase order 740.

  1. Receiver: Purchase order seven forty expects twenty cartons, but our receiving count is twenty-two today.
  2. Buyer: Do the extra cartons carry the same item code as the twenty we ordered?
  3. Receiver: Yes, the labels match. That confirms identity, but not why the quantity increased.
  4. Buyer: I haven't approved an increase. Please keep the expected quantity at twenty during my supplier check.
  5. Receiver: I'll report this receiving discrepancy as a two-carton overage, without changing the purchase order.
  6. Buyer: Thank you. Are you also reporting a contents difference inside any of those cartons?
  7. Receiver: No. This report concerns outer-carton count only; individual contents have not been checked.
  8. Buyer: Then I'll ask the supplier about the additional two cartons using that exact distinction.
  9. Receiver: Who should receive the supplier's explanation when it arrives so the discrepancy can be reviewed?
  10. Buyer: Send it to receiving against seven forty. I'll remain the contact for the purchasing decision.
2. The shipping notice names another site

A receiver queries an arrival notice with a carrier driver.

  1. Receiver: The advance shipping notice names our west warehouse, but this is the east distribution site.
  2. Driver: My delivery document lists the east address. Can we compare the shipment reference before deciding?
  3. Receiver: Both show N82, twelve cartons of display stands. The site names are the conflicting detail.
  4. Driver: Then it isn't enough to say the item and quantity match; the destination needs clarification.
  5. Receiver: I'll contact our receiving desk with both document versions and keep that destination question explicit.
  6. Driver: Dispatch can check my delivery booking too. I won't claim either address is corrected yet.
  7. Receiver: Is there a later document on your side that could explain a destination change?
  8. Driver: This one was issued yesterday afternoon. I don't know when your advance notice was created.
  9. Receiver: Ours dates from Monday. I'll report both dates without assuming the newer address is correct.
  10. Driver: Agreed. The desks can confirm the intended site before this is recorded as a completed receipt.
3. One arrival divided between purchase orders

A receiver checks an apparent overage with a purchasing clerk.

  1. Receiver: Nine cartons arrived together, but purchase order four ten only lists five. Are four extra?
  2. Purchasing clerk: Check the references on the carton labels. We also ordered four cartons under four eleven.
  3. Receiver: Five labels show four ten; the other four show four eleven with the same supplier.
  4. Purchasing clerk: Then these are two expected receipts arriving together, not an overage against the first order.
  5. Receiver: I'll keep the quantities separate rather than receive all nine against four ten.
  6. Purchasing clerk: Please do. The departments need distinct receipt records even though the supplier used one vehicle.
  7. Receiver: Does either order include a change to the item description that receiving should query?
  8. Purchasing clerk: No changes are confirmed. Compare each order with its own listed items as usual.
  9. Receiver: So five against four ten and four against four eleven, with no quantity difference currently identified.
  10. Purchasing clerk: Correct. That resolves the apparent extra quantity without altering either purchase order.

Two scenarios to practice

Scenario 1 · Receiving against the expected shipment

Partner's role and follow-up

Try a harder second round

A colleague suggests replacing the expected quantity with the count. Explain why both figures matter.

Scenario 2 · The delivery has one fewer pallet

Notice A09 expects four pallets; three arrive. Each pallet's carton contents remain unchecked. Supplier has not confirmed a separate shipment.

Role A: Receiver: state the missing handling unit accurately.

Role B: Receiving clerk: ask whether the count concerns pallets or cartons.

Possible opening: “We received three of four pallets, not three of four cartons.”

Success checks
  • State one pallet short.
  • Do not invent contents quantity.
  • Ask for the matching packing detail.
Add a complication

Someone wants to call the missing pallet twenty missing cartons without a packing breakdown.

Studying alone? Speak both roles aloud. For each scenario, prepare for two minutes, speak for one minute, then answer a follow-up. Switch roles and repeat using fewer notes. Use only the supplied facts; identify missing information instead of inventing it.

Compare scenario 1 with a model response
Purchase order 740 expects twenty cartons, and our receiving count is twenty-two. The item labels match, but we haven't confirmed why there are two extra cartons. I'll report an overage against this receipt. Please keep the expected quantity unchanged until the purchasing contact clarifies whether the additional goods belong to this order.

This is one possible spoken response, not the only acceptable wording. Preserve the case facts when you try your own version.

Check your reasoning

Compare with the case above and the model response. Can you point to the words that support your factual statement? Is your question about something the case leaves open? If you introduce a possible outcome, clearly label it as a possibility.

Review, then try again

  • Keeps the case facts accurate; clearly separates confirmed and unknown information.
  • Uses understandable sentences and explains specialist terms when the listener needs it.
  • Responds to the other person's question, checks understanding, and uses an appropriate tone.
  • Makes the requested action or unresolved question clear without inventing authority or facts.

Revise one sentence and repeat the response. A useful response can be clear even with a few grammar errors; judge meaning and task completion, not accent.

Your goal

Clarify an item-location conflict before relying on the location sign.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A picker needs item BK-14-B, blue folders. Bin A12 contains BK-14-G, green folders. The bin sign says BK-14-B. Inventory clerk can check the putaway record; no move or substitution has been approved.

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

stock keeping unit
An identifier for a distinct stocked product or variant, commonly shortened to SKU.
bin location
The coded storage position assigned to stock within a warehouse.
lot number
An identifier linking units produced or supplied as one particular batch.
putaway
The placement and recording of received goods in their assigned storage locations.

Useful word combinations

  • read the full item code: include the variant suffix

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

staging lane
A designated area where outbound goods wait for their next transport movement.
load manifest
The list identifying shipments or handling units assigned to a particular load.
carrier cutoff
The latest agreed time for goods or bookings to meet a specified carrier departure.
dispatch confirmation
A record confirming that goods have actually been dispatched, not merely prepared.

Useful word combinations

  • check the staging lane: verify the assigned outbound location
  • compare against the manifest: match staged goods to the load list
  • flag the carrier cutoff: state the departure-related deadline
  • distinguish staged from dispatched: name the actual transport stage

03 · Notice the language

Name the stage accurately

A staging location, manifest entry, and departure confirmation provide different evidence.

  • ___ cartons are staged in ___.
  • The manifest includes ___, leaving ___ unresolved.
  • Departure is ___, not ___.

Improve this: L66 has shipped.

See a clearer version

L66 is staged; its three-carton count does not match the two-carton manifest.

The update preserves the unresolved loading record.

Two short language checks

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. staged

  1. A. staged Correct. Staged goods await their next transport movement.
  2. B. dispatched Dispatch would overstate the current waiting status.
  3. C. returned There is no return movement in this example.
  4. D. loaded Loading is not confirmed; the cartons remain in the lane.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. of

Your goal

State a return's actual status without inferring saleability.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

Returned kettle K25 matches its return reference. Packaging appears unopened, but condition review is pending. Intake worker cannot mark it ready for sale; returns checker Mira owns the review.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

returns intake
The receipt and identification of goods coming back into the warehouse.
inspection hold
A status preventing ordinary release while goods await a required condition check.
disposition
The approved decision about what happens next to goods, such as restocking or supplier return.
restock
To return eligible goods to available inventory after required checks and approval.

Useful word combinations

  • match the return authorization: identify the approved return
  • place on inspection hold: record that release awaits review
  • request a disposition decision: ask the responsible team for the next status
  • confirm eligibility to restock: verify release criteria have been met

03 · Notice the language

Separate received from released

A return can be identified and received while its condition remains unverified.

  • The reference matches; condition is ___.
  • The item is received but not ___.
  • ___ owns the disposition review.

Improve this: It looks new; it's available.

See a clearer version

It is received on inspection hold; restocking has not been approved.

The change names the unresolved review.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Picker: The return has arrived. Can it fill this order?
Intake worker: Not yet; it is ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. received on inspection hold

  1. A. received and released Receipt is confirmed, but release has not been approved.
  2. B. approved for restocking Restocking approval is still awaiting the required review.
  3. C. received on inspection hold Correct. The goods are received but remain unreleased pending inspection.
  4. D. confirmed as defective Lack of inspection does not establish a defect.
2. Checker: Has a next-use decision been made for K25?
Worker: No, its ____ is pending.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. disposition

  1. A. arrival Arrival is already confirmed by returns intake.
  2. B. reference The return reference has already been matched.
  3. C. receipt Receipt is confirmed; the release decision is not.
  4. D. disposition Correct. Disposition is the decision about the next stock status.

Your goal

Hand over count evidence, remaining work, and ownership.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

Cycle count for item T9 in B11 found eighteen; the system shows twenty. No cause is confirmed. Evening clerk Sam can review transaction history. Recount is complete and agrees at eighteen; no stock adjustment is approved.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

cycle count
A scheduled count of selected stock rather than a full warehouse inventory.
inventory variance
A difference between the physical stock count and the recorded quantity.
transaction history
The record of stock movements and adjustments affecting an item.
shift handoff
The transfer of work status and outstanding issues to the next shift.

Useful word combinations

  • report a count variance: give physical and recorded quantities
  • check transaction history: look for relevant recorded movements
  • name the unresolved item: specify the count or order needing follow-up
  • acknowledge the handoff: confirm receipt and ownership of the next task

03 · Notice the language

Transfer the discrepancy, not a guessed cause

A reliable recount can establish a difference without explaining it.

  • Both counts show ___; the system shows ___.
  • The variance is confirmed, but the cause is ___.
  • Please take ___ and acknowledge ___.

Improve this: Someone lost two.

See a clearer version

Two fewer units were counted than recorded; the cause remains unknown.

The correction removes an unsupported accusation.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Sam: Did the recount eliminate the difference?
Worker: No; it ____ the same physical count.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. confirmed

  1. A. replaced The recount agreed with, rather than replaced, the earlier result.
  2. B. reversed Reversed would imply a changed rather than matching result.
  3. C. invalidated The matching recount did not invalidate the original count.
  4. D. confirmed Correct. Both counts agreed at eighteen physical units.
2. Worker: Have we approved an inventory adjustment?
Sam: No, the adjustment remains ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. unapproved

  1. A. fully posted Fully posted would imply a completed system adjustment.
  2. B. approved by the recount A recount supplies evidence but is not adjustment approval.
  3. C. automatically accepted A confirmed variance does not automatically approve an adjustment.
  4. D. unapproved Correct. No adjustment approval has been recorded for this variance.

Three conversations at work

1. Two counts agree, the system differs
