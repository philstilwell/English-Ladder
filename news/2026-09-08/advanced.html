Critics Praise Nigella Lawson as Bake Off’s New Judge

Advanced · C1+

News archive →
📅 September 08, 2026 - Critics Praise Nigella Lawson as Bake Off’s New Judge

Read the story

TV chef Nigella Lawson has been praised by critics for her new role as a judge on The Great British Bake Off, which returns to Channel 4 later this month. Lawson is taking over from Dame Prue Leith, who expressed her departure from the series as a desire to 'work less and play more'. The 17th series officially premieres on 22 September, though its opening episode was screened to critics earlier this week. The Times lauded Lawson's engaging presence, stating that 'lashings of Nigella Lawson help everyone rise to the occasion,' while the Telegraph found her 'more delectable than anything the contestants could whip up in a showstopper challenge'. The roster of contestants for the new series has also been officially confirmed, comprising a paediatric doctor, a competitive sprinter, a hairdresser, and a drag artist. Ben Dowell of the Times specifically highlighted some of Lawson's evocative lines from the debut episode, such as her wish for a bake to be 'really sumptuous,' delivered in her 'highly seductive voice'. Dowell further noted the production team's evident delight in having her, commenting, 'at times... it feels as if the programme is entertaining royalty,' and concluding that 'Bake Off has found a new star'. Anita Singh of The Telegraph similarly concurred, observing that 'Nigella is heaven for social media. The woman is a walking meme factory,' underscoring her inherent appeal for generating online content. Singh further quoted Lawson's distinctive utterances, including 'The flavours are giving me a lot of pleasure,' 'I like a bit of goo,' and 'I'm hoping to find that wonderful frisson.' Among the latest crop of aspiring star bakers is Clara, a marketing graduate from Portugal who only started baking two years ago. Connie, an NHS hospital discharge lead, is affectionately known as 'the cake lady' and utilises baking as a therapeutic means to unwind after demanding days. Gabe, a 32-year-old artist and drag king, developed an early fascination with baking after watching the initial series of Bake Off at thirteen. Moyin, a political economy student and accomplished competitive sprinter, creatively infuses his bakes with flavours inspired by his rich Nigerian heritage. Yannis, a paediatric doctor working in a busy NICU, aspires to dismantle the misconception that Bangladeshi men do not belong in culinary spaces, often brightening his ward with his baking creations.

Words and grammar

1. expressed (verb): To convey a thought, feeling, or intention, often in words or actions.
2. premieres (verb): To have the first public showing or performance of a film, play, or television programme.
3. lauded (verb): Highly praised or celebrated, often publicly and enthusiastically.
4. engaging (adjective): Charming and attractive in a way that captivates attention and interest.
5. roster (noun): A list of people or things, especially one showing who is available or assigned to tasks.
6. comprising (verb, present participle): Consisting of or including various parts or members.
7. evocative (adjective): Bringing strong images, memories, or feelings vividly to mind.
8. underscoring (verb, present participle): Emphasizing the importance or truth of something.
9. inherent (adjective): Existing in something as a permanent, essential, or characteristic attribute.
10. dismantle (verb): To take apart a system, belief, or practice; to effectively destroy or abolish it.

Advanced Grammar: Participial Phrases
Participial phrases, formed with present participles (-ing) or past participles (-ed/-en), function as adjectives or adverbs, providing additional information about a noun or verb. They often reduce clauses, making sentences more concise and sophisticated. In the example, 'delivered in her 'highly seductive voice'' is a past participial phrase modifying 'wish,' clarifying how it was expressed.
Example from the text: "Ben Dowell of the Times specifically highlighted some of Lawson's evocative lines from the debut episode, such as her wish for a bake to be 'really sumptuous,' delivered in her 'highly seductive voice'."
