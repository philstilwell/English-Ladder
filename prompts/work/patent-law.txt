Lesson 01: Interviewing inventors without leading their answers
Original fictional case: Engineer Amira describes a prototype battery enclosure with a curved coolant channel. Technician Leo built it from drawings and suggested a detachable connector. One internal test reports a shorter cooling time, but the test conditions have not been compared. Patent professional Daniel is gathering facts, not deciding inventorship or patentability.
Language workshop: The improvement is a channel, not simply faster cooling
Communication goal: Elicit the mechanism, alternatives, and individual contributions behind an invention disclosure.
Language guidance: Describe each contribution before assessing its legal significance. Building a prototype and conceiving a claimed feature are not interchangeable descriptions.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): What does the curved channel change in the flow path? / Would the same effect occur with a straight channel? / Who first proposed that feature, and what records support the account? / The test reports a shorter time; it does not yet establish why. / Did Leo follow the drawings or propose a different arrangement? / We have not compared the test conditions yet. / Please use the approved confidential channel for the drawings. / We have a prototype, a reported result, and contribution details to verify. / Are you asking about the finding or the next step? / Please correct me if I have misunderstood that detail. / That is what the current record says; it has not been independently confirmed. / That is a possibility, not an agreed outcome. / That decision sits outside my authority. / Who is responsible for that specific next step? / Let me restate the distinction more clearly. / We agree on the action, the owner, and the point for review.
Vocabulary: invention disclosure: Internal account of a proposed invention and its development. / technical problem: Practical technical difficulty the proposed invention addresses. / technical effect: Technical result attributed to a feature or process. / conception: Formation of the inventive idea under the applicable legal test. / reduction to practice: Implementation or legally relevant realization of an invention. / inventorship: Legal identification of the inventor or inventors. / contribution record: Account of who contributed what and when. / prototype: Preliminary implementation used to explore or test a design. / embodiment: Particular implementation described for an invention. / alternative configuration: Different arrangement of components or steps. / coolant channel: Passage through which cooling fluid moves. / detachable connector: Connection component designed to be separated. / causal mechanism: Process explaining how a feature produces a result. / comparative test: Test comparing implementations under stated conditions. / baseline configuration: Reference arrangement used for comparison. / laboratory notebook: Contemporaneous record of experimental work. / confidential disclosure: Communication within the relevant confidentiality arrangements. / public disclosure: Making information available outside the relevant confidential setting. / clarification request: Question that resolves an unclear meaning or instruction. / source attribution: Identification of where information came from. / working assumption: Provisional premise used while information is incomplete. / scope boundary: Limit of the work or authority being discussed. / action owner: Named person responsible for a specified next step. / check-back: Restatement used to verify that a message was understood.
Speaking task: Read the corrected interview and stress designed, built, suggested, and tested where their meanings differ. Switch roles and deliver the final document list using only the supplied development facts. Complete the four-line follow-up with the supplied choices. Check the explanations, then read the corrected exchange twice, changing roles.
Partner: Amira: Engineer / Daniel: Patent professional
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A second enclosure appears only in a dated sketch. It has not been built or tested. The team wants to include it in the confidential disclosure.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Amira: Marketing wants to show the prototype at a public event next month. I have not approved that request.
Daniel: Flag the proposed public disclosure for prompt legal review before anyone assumes the timing is harmless.
Amira: I will supply the drawings, contribution details, test conditions, and event request as separate items for your review.
Daniel: That gives the invention disclosure a factual foundation without promising protection or treating an initial result as a verified product claim.

Lesson 02: Discussing prior art without overstating the search
Original fictional case: Analyst Noor finds Reference R describing a housing and curved channel. The draft claim also requires a detachable connector, which Noor has not located in R. Reference S mentions a detachable connector in a different device. Dates and legal relevance need review. Counsel Ethan must distinguish the references and search limitations.
Language workshop: One reference matches two features, not the entire claim
Communication goal: Explain a search result using feature mapping, dates, and the limits of the search scope.

Mei: Once the design is reviewed, I will flag any further connector or channel change before relying on the same conclusion.
Arun: Make that a review trigger. A scoped assessment stays useful only when the team knows when its underlying facts have changed.

Sublicensing requires a separately negotiated provision. No agreement has been executed.
Professional roles: Ben / Nia
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Ben: The heading says exclusive license. Our sales team reads that as permission to use the technology in any product we develop.
Nia: The proposed field of use is industrial cooling. The heading must be read with that limitation and the rest of the grant.
Ben: We also plan to sell in market B. I assumed the same exclusivity would follow the product wherever it travels.
Nia: The draft licensed territory is A. Any request to include B needs explicit negotiation rather than an assumption from the word exclusive.
Ben: Understood. We have discussed the percentage, but our finance team cannot calculate the expected payment from the current wording.
Nia: We still need to settle the royalty base, including which sales and adjustments the definition actually covers.
Ben: Returns matter for our business. We need the calculation to distinguish invoiced sales from products later returned by customers.
Nia: That belongs in the net sales definition. We should agree the treatment and timing rather than rely on an undefined accounting shorthand.
Ben: A regional partner would distribute the product and may want rights to manufacture a version for local customers.
Nia: Distribution and a sublicense are not interchangeable. Any onward manufacturing rights need the appropriate negotiated provision.
Ben: Could we discuss a lower initial payment if we accept a payment when the first approved product enters commercial production?
Nia: We can discuss a milestone payment, but the event, evidence, and payment obligation would need precise agreement.
Ben: Our finance team also wants to know what records we would need to maintain for royalty verification.
Nia: The proposed audit right should address the relevant records and conditions, with appropriate confidentiality and practical arrangements.
Ben: We expect to develop improved connectors. I do not want either side to assume ownership or access without discussing it.
Nia: Then include improvement rights as a separate open item, alongside the licensed patents and any other defined subject matter.
Ben: I will send a marked-up version showing our requests on territory, returns, sublicensing, and later improvements.
Nia: Please keep the term sheet status clear. A discussion of those points does not mean the license has been completed.
Ben: Our closing summary should say that the commercial proposal is progressing, with several specified provisions still unresolved.
Nia: Agreed. Do not describe execution as complete. We need the final terms, proper review, and authorized completion before reporting an executed license.
Vocabulary in this exchange: exclusive license: License granting exclusivity as defined by the actual agreement. / field of use: Defined application area within a license. / licensed territory: Geographic scope of licensed rights. / sublicense: Permission granted onward under rights allowed by the primary license. / royalty base: Amount or measure to which the royalty rate applies. / net sales: Contract-defined sales measure after specified adjustments.
