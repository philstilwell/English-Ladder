Gwynedd Residents Protest Major Data Centre Development

📅 October 11, 2026 - Gwynedd Residents Protest Major Data Centre Development

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Hundreds of people have taken part in a protest against plans to build a new data centre because of concerns about its effects on the area. Placard-waving opponents highlight concerns about a data centre development on the local landscape. Developers want to construct a complex on a 101-acre site between Caernarfon and Y Felinheli, in Gwynedd. They claim it could create more than 900 roles during construction and 200 permanent roles. About 300 gathered for a rally in Caernarfon to highlight their opposition. More than 22,500 people have signed a petition opposing the plan. Canolfan Ddata Menai has previously stated its proposals would "transform and revitalise the long-derelict" site and offer significant employment. However, local opponent Ernest Dyson said the project represented "basically the destruction of the most beautiful part of the Menai Strait". He further conveyed his incredulity, stating, "It is quite incredible it should come to this." Liz Saville Roberts, the Plaid Cymru MP for Dwyfor Meirionnydd, commented that data centres must be "the right size, the right technology and in the right places." She stressed the need for "proper regulations" and ensuring communities benefit from such developments. Roberts highlighted Wales's "too long a history of extractive industries that left nothing behind and have little benefit for communities." She asserted that this pattern "mustn't happen again in the 21st Century." Canolfan Ddata Menai responded that the project would ensure "continued use" of the surrounding area, including Lôn Las Menai cycleway and footpath. They also claimed it would have "no undue impact" on the Menai Strait and an associated Site of Special Scientific Interest (SSSI).

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. roles (noun): Positions or jobs in an organization, often implying specific responsibilities and duties.
2. stated (verb): Expressed something clearly and explicitly, often in a formal manner.
3. proposals (noun): Formal plans or suggestions put forward for consideration or discussion.
4. transform (verb): To make a thorough or dramatic change in the form, appearance, or character of something.
5. revitalise (verb): To give new life, energy, or vigor to something, restoring it to a more active or healthy state.
6. long-derelict (adjective): Referring to a place or building that has been neglected and in a poor state for a significant, extended period.
7. significant (adjective): Sufficiently great or important to be worthy of attention; notably large or meaningful.
8. incredulity (noun): The state of being unwilling or unable to believe something, often accompanied by a sense of surprise.
9. extractive industries (noun phrase): Industries that involve the removal of natural resources from the earth, such as mining, quarrying, or oil extraction.
10. undue impact (noun phrase): An impact or effect that is excessive, disproportionate, or inappropriate in a given situation.

Advanced Grammar: The structure "too + adjective + a/an + noun"
This advanced grammatical structure emphasizes that a quality is excessive to the point of being problematic or undesirable. Instead of the typical adjective-noun order, 'a/an' is placed between the adjective and the noun. It often conveys a sense of regret or criticism, indicating that something has existed or lasted for an unacceptable duration or degree. For example, 'too long a history' implies a history that is regrettably extensive or problematic.
Example from the text: "Roberts highlighted Wales's "too long a history of extractive industries that left nothing behind and have little benefit for communities.""
