📅 November 10, 2026 - Ground Force Star Tommy Walsh Dies at 69

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Tommy Walsh, known for his work on Ground Force, Homes Under the Hammer and Challenge Tommy Walsh, has died aged 69. Walsh was a builder by trade before rising to prominence alongside Alan Titchmarsh and Charlie Dimmock on the BBC’s Ground Force, where he appeared from 1997 to 2005. Walsh’s television breakthrough came after he renovated the home of a BBC producer who was developing ideas for what would become Ground Force. Walsh told the BBC in 2004 that the producer had asked him to look at two pilots, or trial episodes, one of which was a forerunner to Ground Force called Over the Garden Wall. He gave a blunt assessment of that pilot, likening the experience to watching paint dry. Walsh said he declined the offer to appear on Ground Force three times. He said he relented when his eight-year-old daughter told him she had wagered £10 with a boy at school that her father would appear on television. Walsh and his co-stars found a winning formula in transforming gardens for members of the public. Walsh later fronted Cowboys and Angels as well as Clean It, Fix It. In 2021, he began appearing on Homes Under the Hammer, a popular daytime programme. His work extended beyond broadcasting, with several do-it-yourself books to his name. Earlier reports said he had undergone surgery for throat cancer in 2022 and for lung cancer in 2023. His agent told BBC News that Walsh had died peacefully after a prolonged illness, with his family around him, and asked for the family to be given privacy.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. by trade (prepositional phrase): In one’s established occupation, especially one that involves a practical skill.
2. rising to prominence (verb phrase in the -ing form): Coming to be widely known or regarded as important in a particular field.
3. forerunner (noun): An earlier version or model that helps pave the way for a later one.
4. blunt assessment (noun phrase): A judgement expressed directly, with little effort to soften any criticism.
5. likening (verb in the -ing form): Comparing one thing to another to draw attention to a quality they share.
6. relented (past-tense verb): Became willing to agree after previously resisting or refusing.
7. wagered (past-tense verb): Risked losing money if an outcome differed from what one predicted.
8. winning formula (noun phrase): A combination of elements or a repeatable approach that proves successful.
9. fronted (past-tense verb): Presented a programme as one of its main on-screen figures.
10. to his name (prepositional phrase): Credited to him as part of his record of work or achievements.

Advanced Grammar: Future in the past with “would”
Use “would” with the base form of a verb to look ahead from a point in the past. In “would become”, the narrator looks back on the producer’s early work and identifies what that project later developed into.
Example from the text: "Walsh’s television breakthrough came after he renovated the home of a BBC producer who was developing ideas for what would become Ground Force."
