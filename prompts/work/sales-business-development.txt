Lesson 01 · Discovery and Qualification | Build vocabulary and collocations

START PROMPT
TASK: Retrieve occupational vocabulary
1. Select four terms from the reference. Explain each in plain English, give two natural collocations and quote or create a short workplace example. Label invented examples. Contrast each term with a plausible confusable term; acknowledge regional variation.
2. Present one role-labelled exchange with one missing word or phrase and four choices A-D. Exactly one choice must fit the stated facts and grammar. Stop and wait for my answer before revealing it.
3. Explain all four options, then invite me to read the completed exchange aloud. Offer a sentence frame for optional spoken practice, never an open-ended writing assignment.
4. Repeat for the other three terms, one question at a time. Vary the correct letter and the situation; preserve the reference facts. If a choice is ambiguous, replace the item.
5. Finish with two new four-choice collocation questions for later review, with a clearly separate answer key. Do not claim to schedule a reminder.

Lesson 01: Discovery and Qualification
Original fictional case: Prospect Nina requests a demonstration of an approval-workflow product. Her four branch teams handle about 600 purchase requests per month through spreadsheets and email. She says managers spend time chasing status, but waiting time and the cost of the problem have not been measured. Nina coordinates operations and has not identified the budget holder, approved budget, or purchasing process. Sales representative Caleb must agree a focused demonstration objective and useful discovery steps. Product fit, savings, purchase authority, and a close date are not yet established.
Language workshop: Make the demonstration relevant before opening the slides
Communication goal: Clarify the workflow, problem, and buying steps without turning a demonstration request into a purchase commitment.
Language guidance: About 600 requests per month describes volume, not delay or savings. Managers chasing status identifies a problem worth examining, but neither the waiting-time baseline nor the cost is supplied. Do not turn those unknowns into a quantified benefit or a guaranteed product result.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): We can show the product; first, may I clarify what would make the demonstration useful? / How does a purchase request move through approval today? / Four branches handle about six hundred requests a month through spreadsheets and email. / Where do managers lose visibility or need to chase an update? / Is the delay in approval, information gathering, or another step? / Do you have a measured waiting-time baseline? / The status-chasing problem is reported; its cost is not yet quantified. / Let us focus on routing requests and seeing their current status. / We still need to test whether the workflow fits your requirements. / Which parts of the evaluation do you coordinate? / Who controls the relevant budget and approves a purchase? / We have not confirmed the budget position yet. / Is there a customer deadline driving the evaluation? / A demonstration does not establish a purchase date. / After the demonstration, we can review the fit and remaining questions together. / I will summarize the objective, participants, known facts, and open questions.
Vocabulary: discovery call: A conversation to understand a prospect's situation and needs. / qualification: Assessment of whether a sales opportunity has relevant fit and buying conditions. / prospect: A potential customer being considered or engaged. / lead: A potential customer contact or indication of interest under the team's definitions. / use case: A specific situation in which a capability would be used. / current-state workflow: The sequence of work as it operates now. / pain point: A practical problem or difficulty relevant to the customer. / business impact: The effect of a problem or change on the organization. / discovery gap: Information still missing from understanding the opportunity. / ICP: Ideal customer profile; characteristics used to describe a suitable target organization. / BANT: Budget, authority, need, and timing; a commonly used qualification checklist. / budget holder: The person or function controlling the relevant funding. / buying process: The customer's steps for evaluating and authorizing a purchase. / decision-maker: A person with authority over a relevant purchase decision. / end user: A person who will use the product or service. / technical evaluator: A person assessing relevant technical suitability. / demonstration: A showing of specified product capabilities. / demo objective: The question or capability the demonstration should address. / discovery agenda: The planned topics for the initial needs conversation. / baseline measure: A defined current value used to assess a proposed change. / compelling event: A customer-relevant event creating a genuine reason for timing. / next step: A specific agreed action moving the conversation forward. / mutual action plan: A shared sequence of customer and seller actions with owners and dates. / solution fit: How well a proposed offering meets the relevant need and constraints.
Speaking task: Read Nina and Caleb aloud, then swap roles. Ask for context without making the demonstration conditional on immediate budget answers. Check all ten gaps. Reread the measured-baseline and buying-role exchanges without inventing missing information. Repeat the agreed demonstration agenda and follow-up, keeping product interest distinct from a purchase commitment.
Partner: Nina: Prospect operations coordinator / Caleb: Sales representative
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: An operations contact asks to see a scheduling product. Three sites currently coordinate by email. Missed updates are reported, but their frequency, budget, approver, and purchase date are unconfirmed.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Nina: After the demonstration, I would like the branch leads to help identify what fits and what would need further examination.
Caleb: That is how we can evaluate solution fit. We should capture both useful capabilities and unresolved requirements, including anything that the demonstration does not establish about your environment.
Nina: Please send a short agenda with the workflow focus and the questions we should bring to that follow-up.
Caleb: I will summarize the agreed next step, participants, known facts, and open questions. That will make the demonstration useful without turning your interest into a commitment you have not made.

START PROMPT
TASK: Interactive workplace role-play
1. Use the supplied case or dialogue as the starting situation. Give a two-sentence briefing and offer two relevant professional roles for me to choose from. For a supplied dialogue, use its actual roles. Ask which role I want, then stop and wait. Do not write my replies.
2. After I choose, identify who you will play and the immediate communication goal. Play the other professional; where a meeting requires a third person, label each of your speakers clearly. Start with one natural workplace turn and wait for my reply. Keep most turns to one to three sentences and ask no more than one question at a time.
3. Let the exchange develop across six to ten learner turns, or end earlier if I type "feedback". Respond to what I actually say. Include a plausible clarification, disagreement or tradeoff without silently changing the starting facts. Mark any added constraint as a fictional second-round variation. Do not resolve approvals, evidence or commitments that remain uncertain.
4. Stay in role during the exchange. If my meaning is unclear, ask for clarification naturally. Give a hint only if I ask or cannot proceed. Do not deliver a model conversation in advance.
5. At the debrief, quote two of my phrases: one successful choice and one worth improving. Check factual accuracy, language, register and whether the next step was clear. Give up to three focused suggestions. Ask me to retry the weakest turn; wait. Only then offer an alternative wording and a harder replay.
Use voice replies when available. If I prefer selections, offer four plausible spoken replies and explain my choice before continuing. Do not require written compositions.
