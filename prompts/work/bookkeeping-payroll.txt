Lesson 01: Matching an invoice to its supporting records
Original fictional case: Fictional supplier Alder bills twelve desk lamps on invoice A184. Purchase order P61 lists twelve, but receiving record R9 lists ten. The remaining two may be on another delivery; no second record is available.
Language workshop: Locate the exact mismatch
Communication goal: Clarify which document supports a billed quantity.
Language guidance: Name the invoice, order, and receiving record separately. Agreement between two documents does not settle a third document's different quantity. Ask about the missing evidence without assuming either a supplier error or an unrecorded delivery.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): The invoice shows ___, whereas the receiving record shows ___. / Could you confirm whether ___ arrived separately? / Which reference supports the remaining ___?
Vocabulary: invoice: A supplier's or seller's bill listing goods or services and the amount requested. / purchase order: A buyer's purchasing document specifying the goods or services ordered. / receiving record: A record of the goods or quantities actually received by an organization. / credit note: A seller's document reducing an amount previously billed, with a reference to the relevant charge.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Buyer: you ordered twelve lamps and can see only R9. The warehouse can check whether a second delivery occurred.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: The supplier sends the purchase order again. Explain why it confirms the order quantity but not receipt.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Invoice A184 and purchase order P61 both show twelve lamps, while receiving record R9 shows ten. Could you confirm whether the other two arrived separately or remain outstanding? I need the related delivery reference before I can describe the invoice as fully matched. At present, the two-lamp difference is unresolved.

Lesson 02: Explaining receipts and outstanding customer balances
Original fictional case: Fictional customer Pine has invoices 410 for $600 and 411 for $400. A $600 receipt is identified as Pine's but has no invoice reference. Both invoices remain open in the ledger pending allocation.
Language workshop: Separate receipt from allocation
Communication goal: Explain why received cash and open invoice balances can coexist.
Language guidance: A receipt records money received. Allocation connects it to a particular balance. Do not equate an unallocated receipt with nonpayment, or assume the customer's intended allocation from the amount alone.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): We have received ___, but it has not been allocated to ___. / The open invoices total ___; the separate receipt is ___. / Which invoice does your remittance identify?
Vocabulary: accounts receivable: Amounts owed to an organization by customers for billed goods or services. / remittance advice: Information from a payer identifying the invoices or charges a payment is intended to cover. / unapplied cash: A recorded receipt that has not yet been allocated to a specific customer balance or invoice. / aging report: A report grouping unpaid balances by their age or time past due, using a stated date basis.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Customer: the receipt is yours, but you must check your payment listing to confirm the invoice reference. Ask why the aging report still includes both invoices.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: The amount equals invoice 410. Explain why that is a useful clue, not confirmation of the intended allocation.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: We have received Pine's six hundred dollars, but the invoice allocation is still unconfirmed. The open invoices total one thousand dollars; the unapplied receipt must be shown alongside them. A remittance advice identifies what the customer intended to pay. Could you confirm the invoice reference so we can explain the remaining balance accurately?
END REFERENCE

Dialogue 08: A date correction changes the report, not the payment
Original fictional setting: A fictional bookkeeper explains a correction to a manager who worries money moved twice.
Professional roles: Manager / Bookkeeper
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Manager: The revised cash report moves a payment from Thursday to Friday. Did the date correction send it again?
Bookkeeper: No. It changed the report's date label. We have no evidence of a second payment.
Manager: Why was the original date wrong? Please distinguish the report edit from the actual bank activity.
Bookkeeper: The import used preparation date in the bank-date column. The bank record shows Friday.
Manager: So Thursday's report included it too early under that column's meaning. Is that the confirmed issue?
Bookkeeper: Yes. The corrected version uses bank date and links the original report, preserving the explanation.
Manager: Has the weekly total changed, or does the amount move between days within the week?
Bookkeeper: The weekly total is unchanged. Daily totals shift by this item's two hundred twenty dollars.
Manager: I'll correct my earlier suggestion of a second payment. The evidence supports a reporting-date correction.
Bookkeeper: Thank you. Please mention both the unchanged weekly total and changed daily allocation when explaining it.
Manager: Which version should the department use now, and how will they identify the old one?
Bookkeeper: Version five is current; four is marked superseded. The history retains both and explains this date correction.
