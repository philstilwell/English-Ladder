Read the story

The United States has articulated a clear expectation for Iran to pledge formally that it will desist from any further hostile actions against commercial and naval vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz. This imperative arises from escalating concerns regarding regional maritime security, a critical issue for global trade and stability. Vice-President JD Vance is slated to be a key participant among American dignitaries in the upcoming diplomatic overtures. These crucial negotiations are poised to resume on Saturday in Oman, indicating a persistent effort to de-escalate tensions. The Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway connecting the Persian Gulf to the open sea, remains an exceptionally strategic chokepoint for global oil shipments. Incidents involving Iranian forces and international shipping have intermittently heightened anxieties, underscoring the urgency of these discussions. Washington seeks not merely a temporary lull but a binding assurance that future maritime transit will proceed unimpeded and without threat. The diplomatic efforts in Oman represent a concerted attempt to establish a more stable operational environment in this volatile region. Achieving a durable resolution necessitates a significant shift in Iran's posture towards international shipping lanes. Therefore, the focus of these high-stakes talks is unequivocally on securing an explicit and verifiable commitment from Tehran regarding its conduct.

Words and grammar

1. pledge formally (verb phrase): To make a solemn and binding promise or commitment, usually in an official or public capacity, to do or refrain from doing something.
2. maritime security (noun phrase): The protection of ships, ports, and sea lanes from threats such as piracy, terrorism, and other unlawful acts, ensuring safe passage and trade.
3. diplomatic overtures (noun phrase): Initial approaches or proposals made by one government or state to another, typically with the aim of initiating negotiations, establishing relations, or resolving disputes.
4. strategic chokepoint (noun phrase): A narrow passage, such as a strait or canal, that is critically important for transportation and trade, where control can confer significant military or economic advantage.
5. binding assurance (noun phrase): A firm and legally enforceable guarantee or promise that something will be done or will happen, creating an obligation.

Advanced Grammar: Reporting Verb Structures with Infinitives (e.g., 'is slated to be')
Advanced English often uses passive reporting verbs followed by an infinitive to convey expectations, plans, or predictions in a formal and concise manner. This construction allows for the presentation of information as generally understood or officially planned without explicitly stating the agent of the expectation. It adds a layer of formality and objectivity, typical of news reporting.
Example from the text: "Vice-President JD Vance is slated to be a key participant among American dignitaries in the upcoming diplomatic overtures."
