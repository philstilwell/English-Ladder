Read the story

A powerful flash flood recently struck a remote region along the Nepal-Tibet border, causing significant destruction. The disaster primarily affected Rasuwa, a district situated to the north of Nepal's capital city, Kathmandu. Authorities have confirmed that at least 17 people have been killed by the fast-moving waters. There are serious fears that numerous villages in the affected area may have been completely swept away. Local officials are currently assessing the extent of the damage, which appears widespread. The challenging mountainous terrain makes search and rescue operations particularly difficult for emergency teams. Many residents are currently displaced, and the exact number of missing individuals remains unknown. Flash floods are a recurring danger in this Himalayan region, often intensified during the monsoon season. Such sudden natural events frequently result in tragic loss of life and severe damage to infrastructure. International organizations are closely observing the situation, ready to provide humanitarian assistance if needed.

Words and grammar

1. flash flood (noun): A sudden and rapid flood, typically caused by heavy rain or melting snow, often occurring in a short period of time.
2. swept away (verb phrase): To be carried rapidly by a strong force, usually water or wind, often leading to loss or destruction.
3. assessing (verb): Evaluating or estimating the nature, ability, or quality of something, usually to understand its importance or value.
4. terrain (noun): A stretch of land, especially with regard to its physical features, like whether it is rocky, mountainous, or flat.
5. humanitarian (adjective): Concerned with or seeking to promote human welfare, especially by providing aid and support to people in need.

Grammar Focus: The Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action itself and the object of the action, rather than the subject performing the action. It's frequently used in news reporting when the agent (who or what performed the action) is unknown, unimportant, or obvious, or when we want to emphasize the outcome. It is formed with a form of 'to be' followed by the past participle of the main verb.
Example from the text: "Authorities have confirmed that at least 17 people have been killed by the fast-moving waters."
