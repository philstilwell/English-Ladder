Lesson 01: Enrollment, Admissions, and Placement
Original fictional case: Admissions officer Leena speaks with guardian Mr. Ortiz about a fictional program with 20 places and 24 eligible applicants. The published process uses a lottery among eligible applicants. His child met the entry criteria but was not selected and is fourth on the waiting list. The local procedure allows a factual-error review requested within five school days of the decision notice. A review is not a second lottery, and no vacancy is confirmed. Leena may explain the family's own record and published process, not other applicants' information.
Language workshop: Eligible, but no place available yet
Communication goal: Explain an allocation decision without disclosing other applicants or promising a future offer.
Language guidance: Eligibility means the applicant meets the conditions for consideration. It does not guarantee selection in an oversubscribed program. Selection, acceptance of an offer, enrollment, and class placement should not be treated as interchangeable.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): "I understand why this outcome is disappointing." / "Your child met the published entry criteria." / "Eligibility did not guarantee a place in this intake." / "There were 20 places for 24 eligible applicants." / "The published procedure used a lottery." / "Your child is fourth on the waiting list." / "No vacancy is confirmed at present." / "I cannot discuss another applicant's record." / "I can explain your child's record and the published process." / "The review examines a claimed factual error." / "A review is not a second lottery." / "The local window is five school days from the notice." / "Please identify the disputed fact and supporting document." / "We will acknowledge your review request." / "Enrollment begins only after the relevant offer steps." / "I will send the procedure and confirm the current waiting-list position."
Vocabulary: admissions: The process of considering applicants for entry. / applicant: A person seeking entry to a program. / eligibility: Meeting the conditions for consideration. / entry criteria: The published conditions an applicant must satisfy. / selection: Choosing among applicants for available places. / capacity: The number of places available. / oversubscribed: Having more applicants than available places. / allocation: Assignment of available places under a process. / lottery: A random selection process among an eligible group. / offer: A formal invitation to take an available place. / waiting list: A list of applicants awaiting possible vacancies. / vacancy: An available, unfilled place. / decision notice: The communication of an application outcome. / factual error: An incorrect statement or record of fact. / review request: A request to examine a decision under a stated process. / review window: The period allowed for requesting review. / school day: A day counted under the institution's school-day definition. / supporting document: Material supplied to substantiate a claim. / enrollment: Formal registration after the applicable admission steps. / placement: Assignment to an appropriate class, level, or setting. / intake: A group admitted during a particular entry period. / confidential record: Information subject to restricted access or disclosure. / published procedure: An officially available description of the process. / acknowledgment: Confirmation that a message or request was received.
Speaking task: Read the completed conversation in pairs; stress eligible, lottery, and fourth. Repeat turns 9-16, distinguishing the review request from its acknowledgment. Switch roles and read the four completed transfer lines, retaining the second waiting-list position.
Partner: Leena: Admissions officer / Mr. Ortiz: Guardian
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A fictional course has 12 places and 15 eligible applicants. An applicant not selected by lottery is second on its waiting list. No vacancy is confirmed. Its local review covers factual errors only.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Mr. Ortiz: Thank you. I had also assumed the placement discussion would happen now, but perhaps that is a different stage from this application review.
Leena: Yes. Placement concerns the class or setting, rather than whether a place has been offered. We should keep those stages separate while the admissions position remains unchanged.
Mr. Ortiz: Please send the procedure, the permitted record information, and confirmation that she remains fourth. I will check for any factual error before deciding about review.
Leena: I will include the relevant decision notice details. Your daughter met the entry criteria; the lottery did not select her; and no vacancy is currently confirmed. Those are the facts we can rely on.

Lesson 02: Curriculum Planning and Learning Outcomes
Original fictional case: Curriculum coordinator Pavel and teacher Nia review a draft course before teaching begins. Its topic list includes media literacy, but the proposed quiz asks only for definitions. The agreed intended outcome is to compare two supplied sources and justify which is more reliable using authorship and supporting evidence. The draft lessons currently practice only recalling terms. The team can revise the lessons and assessment before approval. They need source-comparison practice and a task scored on both named criteria, not a claim that remembering definitions demonstrates evaluation.
Language workshop: A topic list is not a learning outcome
