Read the story

A catastrophic wildfire has ravaged a region in southern Spain, leading to a significant human toll. Initial reports confirm at least twelve fatalities and twenty-three individuals currently unaccounted for in the aftermath of the blaze. The conflagration, which erupted near Los Gallardos in Almería, has presented formidable challenges for emergency services. Local authorities are grappling with the arduous task of victim identification and locating those still missing. A local official indicated that four of the confirmed victims may be British nationals, pending further verification. This revelation underscores the potential international dimension of the tragedy, highlighting the popular tourist appeal of the area. Rescue operations have been intensified, with extensive resources deployed across the affected terrain. The sheer scale of the devastation has prompted urgent calls for both regional and national support. This incident marks a particularly grim milestone for the area, often prone to seasonal fires but rarely with such a devastating loss of life. The community is bracing for what is anticipated to be a protracted period of recovery and investigation into the fire's origins.

Words and grammar

1. catastrophic wildfire (noun phrase): An extremely destructive and extensive uncontrolled fire, causing immense damage and often leading to loss of life and property.
2. unaccounted for (adjective phrase): Not included in a tally or report; not known where they are, typically implying they are missing and their status is unknown.
3. grappling with (verb phrase): Dealing with or struggling to overcome a difficult problem or challenge, often one that requires significant effort and resilience.
4. intensified (verb (past participle)): Made or become more extreme, severe, or stronger; often referring to efforts, operations, or phenomena.
5. grim milestone (noun phrase): A significant and often unwelcome event or point in time that serves as a marker of a serious or tragic development.

Advanced Grammar: Modals of Possibility and Speculation
Modals like 'may', 'might', 'could', and 'must' are used to express varying degrees of certainty or possibility about present or future events, or to make deductions about past events. 'May' and 'might' suggest that something is possible but not certain, while 'could' indicates possibility in a weaker sense. In the given context, 'may be' expresses a degree of uncertainty regarding the nationality of the victims, indicating it is a possibility rather than a confirmed fact.
Example from the text: "A local official indicated that four of the confirmed victims may be British nationals, pending further verification."
