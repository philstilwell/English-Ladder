Lesson 01: Platform Technology and Scientific Thesis
Original fictional case: Research lead Nia reviews a fictional platform presentation with venture lead Leo. Three independent preparations of one cell line show an average reporter-signal reduction of 40% against the stated control. No orthogonal functional result or data from other disease systems are available. The slide claims proven activity across three diseases. The immediate request is funding for a bounded follow-up study, not approval for clinical use. Nia and Leo must retain the promising observation while correcting the unsupported breadth.
Language workshop: One cell line, three proposed diseases
Communication goal: Distinguish a measured platform signal from a proposed mechanism and a broader development hypothesis.
Language guidance: Three independent preparations provide information about variation within the tested biological system. They are not three diseases or three clinical trials. A reporter signal can support a research direction without establishing the full mechanism or proving human benefit.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): We observed a 40% average reduction in the reporter signal. / The data come from three independent preparations of one cell line. / Other disease systems have not yet been tested. / The reporter result does not yet establish the functional effect. / The broader platform application remains a testable hypothesis. / We need a different readout to examine the underlying question. / Could the signal reflect an effect on the assay rather than the intended biology? / The percentage is relative to the control specified in this experiment. / We have not established the full mechanism from this result alone. / The signal is promising enough to justify a focused next study. / The missing evidence is functional confirmation in a relevant additional system. / The next study should answer whether this application merits further development. / These are independent preparations, not repeated reads presented as independent samples. / Replace proven across three diseases with the result actually measured. / We are requesting the next research step, not clinical authorization. / Agree what evidence would support or stop the next stage.
Vocabulary: platform technology: A reusable technological approach intended to support multiple applications. / scientific thesis: The evidence-based argument and hypotheses underlying a research program. / modality: A type of therapeutic or technological approach. / target: The biological entity a program aims to affect. / target engagement: Evidence that an intervention interacts with its intended biological target. / mechanism of action: The biological process through which an intervention produces an effect. / reporter signal: A measurable output used as an indicator in an experimental system. / cell line: A maintained population of cells used for research. / biological replicate: An independently prepared biological sample or experimental unit. / technical replicate: A repeated measurement of the same biological material or preparation. / control condition: A comparison used to interpret an experimental result. / orthogonal assay: A different measurement approach used to examine the same underlying question. / functional readout: A measurement of a relevant biological activity or outcome. / assay interference: An effect on the measurement system that can distort the apparent result. / counter-screen: A separate screen used to identify unwanted or misleading effects. / selectivity: Preference for the intended target or effect relative to alternatives. / off-target effect: An effect involving an unintended biological target or pathway. / proof of concept: Evidence supporting feasibility within a specified scope. / proof of mechanism: Evidence supporting the proposed causal biological mechanism. / generalizability: The extent to which a finding applies beyond the tested setting. / disease model: An experimental system representing selected aspects of a disease. / translational hypothesis: A proposed link between experimental findings and a later development setting. / evidence gap: A missing piece of information relevant to a claim or decision. / decision gate: A defined point at which evidence is reviewed against criteria.
Speaking task: Read the slide-review exchange. State 40% average reporter reduction, three independent preparations, and one cell line together. Swap roles. Contrast a technical repeat with an orthogonal method; retain the untested broader applications. Read the corrected one-model transfer. Keep two proposed models untested and the wider claim a hypothesis.
Partner: Nia: Research lead / Leo: Venture lead
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A screening result is observed in one cell model. Two other proposed models have not been tested. The team requests a study of those additional systems.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Leo: For the decision itself, we want a bounded research budget, not a commitment to take every suggested indication into development. The proposal needs a stopping point.
Nia: Set a decision gate with agreed evidence criteria. We should know what question the work will answer and how that answer affects the next development choice.
Leo: I will remove the three-disease claim and request the bounded follow-up. Keep the forty-percent result beside the actual cell system so the claim cannot drift again.
Nia: Good. That preserves the platform ambition while keeping generalizability open to investigation. We can be confident about the observation without claiming that every future application already works.
END REFERENCE
