Netanyahu faces criticism over election claims

📅 October 30, 2026 - Netanyahu faces criticism over election claims

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Netanyahu's coalition, a group of parties governing together, lost its majority in Israel's election. Final results showed that neither Netanyahu's bloc, or political group, nor the main Jewish opposition won more than half the seats in parliament. The two Arab party lists won a record 19 of parliament's 120 seats following a sharp rise in turnout among Arab voters. Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats, while the bloc led by his rival Gadi Eisenkot won 48. These figures could give the Arab parties an important role in deciding who forms the next government. Likud, Netanyahu's party, accused the Arab-led parties of stealing the election. The possibility of illegal funding from Qatar and Turkey is being investigated by Israel's home security agency, Shin Bet. Qatar denied the allegations and accused Israeli politicians of spreading false information about the country. Both Arab parties rejected the claim of wrongdoing, and the Joint List said it had no basis. The agency later clarified that its investigation did not accuse an entire community whose members had voted legally. An election official said there was no basis for claims of a pattern of actions breaking rules protecting fair elections. Eisenkot said it was too early to know whether anything had happened and accused Netanyahu of trying to weaken trust in the results. Netanyahu's critics said he was trying to prevent Eisenkot from agreeing to join forces with the Arab parties.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. coalition (noun): A group of political parties that work together to govern.
2. majority (noun): More than half of the total, here referring to seats in parliament.
3. turnout (uncountable noun): The number or percentage of people who vote in an election.
4. accused (verb, past tense): Said that someone had done something wrong, without necessarily proving it.
5. funding (uncountable noun): Money provided to pay for an activity or organisation.
6. allegations (plural noun): Claims that someone has done something wrong which have not been proved.
7. investigation (noun): A process of carefully examining information to find out what happened.
8. join forces (verb phrase): Work together to achieve a shared aim.

Grammar Focus: Present continuous passive
Use “is” or “are” + “being” + a past participle to describe an action happening now when the person or thing receiving the action is the focus. In the example sentence, “is being investigated” describes an examination that is still in progress; it does not mean the claim has been proved. The phrase beginning with “by” identifies who is carrying out the investigation.
Example from the text: "The possibility of illegal funding from Qatar and Turkey is being investigated by Israel's home security agency, Shin Bet."
