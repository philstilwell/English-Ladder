James Bourne Says Brother's Kidney Donation Saved His Life

📅 October 27, 2026 - James Bourne Says Brother's Kidney Donation Saved His Life

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Busted musician James Bourne has credited his brother with saving his life by donating a kidney. He described a grave prognosis, an assessment of how an illness is likely to develop, saying doctors had given him only a few years to live. Bourne, 43, pulled out of the arena tour featuring Busted and McFly on the eve of its launch in September 2025. He attributed his departure to health problems, and his younger brother Chris stepped in as his replacement. Bourne later said he needed substantial surgery to extend his life, and he has since thanked Chris as both brothers recover from their operations. Although Bourne said both brothers were recovering well after surgery, he tempered this positive account by acknowledging that he still needed time. Chris marked the outcome with a brief message on X expressing their shared achievement. Bourne has not disclosed the precise nature of his illness. He rose to prominence in the early 2000s alongside Charlie Simpson and Matt Willis in Busted, whose hits included Year 3000. The group achieved four UK number one singles before it disbanded in 2005. Bourne and Willis subsequently joined forces with McFly to form McBusted in 2013. Simpson returned to Busted in 2015, completing the original trio's reunion. Beyond Busted, Bourne has written songs for McFly and 5 Seconds of Summer and fronted Son of Dork. He also recently released music from his stage musical Murder at the Gates.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. credited (verb, past participle): Publicly identified someone as responsible for a positive outcome; the pattern “credit someone with doing something” assigns recognition.
2. prognosis (countable noun): A medical assessment of how an illness is likely to develop, including the prospects for survival or improvement.
3. on the eve of (prepositional phrase): Immediately before an important event; here, specifically the day before the performances began.
4. attributed (verb, past simple): Identified something as the cause of an event; “attribute something to something” reports a causal explanation.
5. extend his life (verb phrase): Enable him to live longer than he otherwise might; the expression does not itself imply a complete cure.
6. tempered (verb, past simple): Made a statement or impression less absolute by adding a balancing qualification.
7. disclosed (verb, past participle): Made previously private information known; this relatively formal verb often concerns sensitive or confidential matters.
8. rose to prominence (verb phrase, past simple): Became widely known and attracted substantial public attention within a particular field.
9. disbanded (verb, past simple): Stopped operating together as an established group, without necessarily ruling out a later return.
10. fronted (verb, past simple): Served as the lead performer or principal public presence of a musical group, rather than merely writing material for it.

Advanced Grammar: Concessive clauses with “although”
“Although” introduces information that might lead readers to expect a different main-clause message: positive recovery might suggest that little further time is needed, yet Bourne qualifies that impression. The concessive relationship presents both statements as compatible while highlighting the tension between them.
Example from the text: "Although Bourne said both brothers were recovering well after surgery, he tempered this positive account by acknowledging that he still needed time."
