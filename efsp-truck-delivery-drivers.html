01Confirming the delivery destinationAsk which destination a note identifies · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Resolve conflicting location details using names and reference numbers.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Shipment 418 names Alder Books at 26 Mill Street, suite 4. The route note says rear annex. The receiver confirms that the annex belongs to suite 6; suite 4 uses the staffed front desk. Dispatch must correct the conflicting note.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

consignee
The person or business named to receive a shipment.
delivery window
The agreed period during which a delivery is expected or accepted.
stop sequence
The planned order of delivery locations on a route.
delivery instruction
A shipment-specific note about where or how receipt is arranged.

Useful word combinations

  • confirm the receiving address: check the shipment's actual destination
  • read back the suite number: repeat it for confirmation
  • check the stop sequence: identify the planned next delivery
  • clarify the delivery window: establish the agreed arrival period

C Notice the language

Ask which destination a note identifies

An address can identify a building without identifying the consignee. Ask a narrow question and repeat the answer.

  • The label names ___, while the note says ___.
  • Which entrance receives deliveries for ___?
  • So ___ refers to ___, not ___.

Improve this: "It says the back somewhere."

See a clearer version

Does the rear-annex note apply to Alder Books in suite 4?

The revised question links the direction to the named recipient.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Driver: The label says suite 4, but your annex is suite 6. Which detail should I ____ with dispatch?
Receiver: The entrance note.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. confirm

  1. A. confirmation A noun cannot follow 'should I' here.
  2. B. confirm Correct. 'Confirm' is the base verb after 'should.'
  3. C. confirmed The past form does not fit after 'should.'
  4. D. confirming The modal 'should' takes the base verb.
2. Receiver: Alder Books receives at the front desk, not the annex.
Driver: So the ____ for this consignee needs correcting.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. delivery instruction

  1. A. stop sequence The order of stops is not disputed.
  2. B. arrival time The arrival time is not disputed.
  3. C. delivery instruction Correct. The incorrect detail concerns the receiving location.
  4. D. delivery window No time window has changed.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. The annex belongs to another tenant

Parked at Mill Street, the driver calls Alder Books about shipment 418.

  1. Driver: I have shipment four eighteen for Alder Books, suite four, at twenty-six Mill Street.
  2. Receiver: That's our address, but please tell me which entrance your delivery instruction mentions.
  3. Driver: It says rear annex. Does that describe your receiving entrance or another tenant's?
  4. Receiver: The annex serves suite six. Our deliveries come to the staffed front desk.
  5. Driver: Thanks. Is the front desk identified as Alder Books rather than the building's general reception?
  6. Receiver: Yes, the Alder Books sign is beside it. I'll be there to receive.
  7. Driver: I'll ask dispatch to correct that entrance note specifically for your suite today.
  8. Receiver: Please keep suite six separate; they still need their deliveries at the annex.
  9. Driver: Understood. Suite four, Alder Books, staffed front desk; the annex instruction doesn't apply.
  10. Receiver: That's correct. Use shipment four eighteen when you speak to our front desk.
2. Two businesses called Pine

A driver checks an ambiguous consignee name with a shipping clerk before departure.

  1. Driver: My next stop says Pine, but two businesses on Station Road use that name.
  2. Shipping clerk: The consignee should be Pine Fabrics. What street number appears on your label?
  3. Driver: It shows eighty-two. The route summary only says Pine and leaves out the number.
  4. Shipping clerk: Pine Cycles is at twenty-eight; Pine Fabrics is at eighty-two, beside the library.
  5. Driver: Could you confirm the shipment reference before I connect that address to this stop?
  6. Shipping clerk: Yes, it's shipment six seventy, four cartons addressed to Pine Fabrics receiving.
  7. Driver: That matches my label. I'll use the full business name in my delivery call.
  8. Shipping clerk: Please do. Their receiving window is ten to twelve, unlike the cycle shop's.
  9. Driver: So eight two Station Road, Pine Fabrics, four cartons, between ten and twelve.
  10. Shipping clerk: Exactly. I'll expand the shortened consignee name in the route summary now.
3. The gate note is for yesterday

A parked driver checks a delivery instruction with a site receptionist.

  1. Driver: My delivery instruction says contact Lee at the west gate for today's paper order.
  2. Receptionist: Lee covered receiving yesterday. Today Rosa is the receiving contact at the east office.
  3. Driver: Has the receiving location changed, or just the person answering the delivery call?
  4. Receptionist: Both changed for this order. The west gate note was temporary for yesterday.
  5. Driver: I need dispatch to verify the revised instruction before treating that as my destination.
  6. Receptionist: Certainly. Give them order nine twelve; our booking confirmation names Rosa and the east office.
  7. Driver: Do today's delivery hours remain the same as the original ten-to-eleven window?
  8. Receptionist: Yes. That window is unchanged, and Rosa is expecting your call within it.
  9. Driver: I'll report a contact and location correction, with no change to the booked window.
  10. Receptionist: That describes it accurately. I'll have Rosa available to confirm the order details.

Try a harder second round

Scenario 2 · A building number without a unit

Label: 54 Cedar Court, Oak Tutors, three cartons. Reception serves units A and B; Oak Tutors is in B. Dispatch has no unit recorded.

Role A: Driver: ask for the full receiving identity and repeat it.

Role B: Receptionist: Oak Tutors is unit B; unit A is a different company.

Possible opening: “Does this address identify your company or just the building?”

Success checks
  • Obtain unit B explicitly.
  • Keep the three-carton shipment linked to Oak Tutors.
  • Request the missing unit correction.
Add a complication

Reception says 'the second one' instead of naming the unit.

Studying alone? Speak both roles aloud. For each scenario, prepare for two minutes, speak for one minute, then answer a follow-up. Switch roles and repeat using fewer notes. Use only the supplied facts; identify missing information instead of inventing it.

Compare scenario 1 with a model response
Shipment 418 names suite 4, but the route note points to the rear annex. Could you confirm which entrance receives Alder Books deliveries? I need the receiving location for this shipment, not just the building address. I'll ask dispatch to correct the note once we have that distinction clear.

This is one possible spoken response, not the only acceptable wording. Preserve the case facts when you try your own version.

Check your reasoning

  • Makes the requested action or unresolved question clear without inventing authority or facts.

02Checking shipment countsState what was actually counted · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Report a quantity discrepancy without inventing its cause.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

At Finch Office Supplies, the bill of lading lists twelve cartons. Driver and receiver count eleven. Contents have not been checked; dispatch has no location for the twelfth carton.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

bill of lading
A transport document identifying the shipment, parties, and goods; its legal effect depends on the shipment and applicable rules.
piece count
The number of separately counted packages or handling units, with the unit stated.
short shipment
A delivery containing fewer goods than the agreed or documented quantity.
packing list
An itemized description of the contents packed for an order.

Useful word combinations

  • reconcile the piece count: compare counted units with the document
  • count by carton: state cartons rather than individual products
  • note a shortage: record the confirmed missing quantity
  • match the shipment reference: ensure documents concern the same load

C Notice the language

State what was actually counted

A carton count and a product count answer different questions. Name the unit and evidence.

  • The document lists ___ cartons; we counted ___.
  • That confirms ___, but not ___.
  • Can we note the difference against ___?

Improve this: "There are twelve things missing."

See a clearer version

We counted eleven of the twelve listed cartons; the missing product quantity is unconfirmed.

The correction prevents a carton difference from becoming an invented product shortage.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Receiver: Did you count the pens inside?
Driver: No. Eleven is the ____ count, not the pen count.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. carton

  1. A. inner-pack The inner packs have not been counted during this handover.
  2. B. pallet The shipment count concerns cartons, not pallets.
  3. C. individual-item Individual products inside the cartons were not counted.
  4. D. carton Correct. Eleven refers to the outer cartons counted.
2. Dispatcher: Can you locate the missing carton or give a replacement date?
Driver: No. What we have confirmed is ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. a one-carton difference against the document

  1. A. a one-carton difference against the document Correct. Twelve listed versus eleven counted confirms this difference.
  2. B. the missing carton's current depot No depot location has been confirmed for the carton.
  3. C. the number of pens inside it The individual contents have not been verified or counted.
  4. D. the date its replacement will arrive No replacement delivery date has been established here.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Eleven cartons at Finch

The driver and receiver compare the physical delivery with its bill of lading.

  1. Driver: The bill of lading lists twelve cartons for Finch, but I count eleven here.
  2. Receiver: I get eleven too. Does that mean twelve boxes of pens are missing inside?
  3. Driver: Not necessarily. We've counted outer cartons, and we haven't checked their individual contents.
  4. Receiver: Then the short shipment is one carton, with the product quantity still unknown?
  5. Driver: Yes. Please compare the shipment reference as well so we're using the matching document.
  6. Receiver: The reference matches. Can you tell me where that last carton is now?
  7. Driver: Dispatch doesn't have its location yet. I'll report the confirmed difference, not guess.
  8. Receiver: I need the receipt to show that we received eleven against twelve listed.
  9. Driver: That wording matches our count. We'll keep any later contents discrepancy separate.
  10. Receiver: Agreed. I'll use eleven cartons received and one carton short for this handover.
2. Bundles counted as pieces

At a print shop, a driver and receiver clarify the counting unit.

  1. Receiver: Your document says six pieces, but I ordered twenty-four packs of printed menus.
  2. Driver: These are six bundles. Let's compare the packing list before calling the order short.
  3. Receiver: The list says four packs per bundle, with six bundles in this shipment.
  4. Driver: That explains the difference in units: six handling pieces, twenty-four listed packs overall.
  5. Receiver: We haven't opened every bundle, so twenty-four is the listed contents, not our verified count.
  6. Driver: Exactly. I can confirm six bundles here, matching the transport document's piece count.
  7. Receiver: Please use the word bundles when you describe what I am receiving today.
  8. Driver: I will. That avoids making six sound like the total number of menu packs.
  9. Receiver: I'll compare the contents with the packing list separately after this handover.
  10. Driver: Then today's receipt can distinguish the six received bundles from their listed contents.
3. Two orders on one counter

A driver checks a shop's apparent overdelivery.

  1. Driver: You mentioned an extra carton. Which order reference are you comparing with the delivery?
  2. Shop assistant: My list shows three cartons for order eight forty, but four are on the counter.
  3. Driver: Three carry eight forty. The fourth has eight forty-one and a different department.
  4. Shop assistant: That second department is ours too, but I only have the first order's list.
  5. Driver: Could we keep the references separate rather than mark the first order overdelivered?
  6. Shop assistant: Yes. Our stockroom contact handles eight forty-one and can confirm that carton separately.
  7. Driver: Then for your order, we agree three cartons listed and three cartons received?
  8. Shop assistant: Correct. I won't add the fourth carton to this order's receipt quantity.
  9. Driver: I'll contact the stockroom recipient about the separate order before completing its handover.
  10. Shop assistant: Thanks. The count now makes sense without changing either order's expected quantity.

03Updating an arrival estimateSeparate arrival, acceptance, and update times · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Give a delay message with an explicit unresolved booking.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Driver is parked and calls dispatch at 10:20. Estimated arrival is 11:40; receiver's cutoff is 11:30. No later slot is confirmed. Dispatch can contact receiving and update the driver by 10:40.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

estimated time of arrival
The currently expected arrival time, often shortened to ETA, rather than a guaranteed arrival.
receiving cutoff
The latest time a site accepts a specified delivery under its booking arrangements.
delay notice
A message explaining that planned delivery timing has changed.
revised appointment
A replacement delivery booking agreed after the original booking changes.

Useful word combinations

  • revise the arrival estimate: give the latest supported timing
  • miss the receiving cutoff: arrive after the acceptance time
  • give an update by noon: promise a communication time
  • request a revised appointment: ask for another confirmed slot

C Notice the language

Separate arrival, acceptance, and update times

A later estimate does not extend a cutoff. Agree the next message time without promising delivery.

  • Our current estimated arrival is ___.
  • That falls ___ the receiving cutoff.
  • Please confirm the booking status by ___.

Improve this: "We'll be there soon."

See a clearer version

Our estimate is 11:40; receiving closes at 11:30. A later slot is not confirmed.

The two times explain why dispatch needs to act.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Dispatcher: Is 11:40 within their window?
Driver: No, it is ten minutes ____ the cutoff.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. after

  1. A. within 'Within' would mean inside the period.
  2. B. until 'Until' expresses duration toward a time.
  3. C. before 11:40 is later, not earlier.
  4. D. after Correct. 'After' states the time relationship.
2. Receiver: Can you promise arrival at noon?
Driver: I can provide an ____, but arrival still depends on conditions.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. estimate

  1. A. estimate Correct. An estimate communicates expected, not guaranteed, timing.
  2. B. appointment confirmation An appointment confirmation establishes a booking, not predicted arrival.
  3. C. delivery receipt A delivery receipt records a handover that already happened.
  4. D. dispatch confirmation Dispatch confirmation concerns departure, not the expected arrival time.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Ten minutes beyond the cutoff

A parked driver calls dispatch at 10:20 about stop five.

  1. Driver: Stop five's estimated time of arrival is eleven forty, after their eleven thirty cutoff.
  2. Dispatcher: Thanks for calling while parked. Have they offered a later receiving appointment today?
  3. Driver: Not yet. The original delivery window no longer fits the estimate I have.
  4. Dispatcher: I can call receiving now and give you a booking update by ten forty.
  5. Driver: Please distinguish their acceptance decision from my arrival estimate when you speak to them.
  6. Dispatcher: Certainly. I won't say the later arrival automatically means they will unload us.
  7. Driver: If they only offer tomorrow, I'll need you to review the rest of this route.
  8. Dispatcher: I'll include that in the update. For now, no replacement appointment is confirmed.
  9. Driver: So ten forty is our next communication time, not a revised delivery promise.
  10. Dispatcher: Correct. I'll report the actual offer or explain that we're still waiting.
2. Early does not mean booked

A parked driver discusses an early arrival with a receiver.

  1. Driver: I'm earlier than expected, but my delivery appointment is still scheduled for two thirty.
  2. Receiver: Our receiving team is handling another booking. We can't confirm earlier acceptance today.
  3. Driver: Understood. Should I describe my status to dispatch as arrived, awaiting the booked window?
  4. Receiver: Yes, although check-in also needs confirmation from the receiving desk, not just this call.
  5. Driver: Thanks for distinguishing those. I'm at the site, but receiving hasn't checked me in.
  6. Receiver: Exactly. Your appointment has not moved simply because your journey took less time.
  7. Driver: I'll tell dispatch the original two-thirty appointment remains in place until receiving confirms otherwise.
  8. Receiver: That will prevent them from telling the next customer you've already finished here.
  9. Driver: Please let the receiving desk know I'm asking about status, not demanding immediate service.
  10. Receiver: I can pass that on. They will confirm your check-in and any available change.
3. A range instead of a sharp promise

A driver calls a customer from a parked vehicle about an uncertain arrival.

  1. Driver: The latest estimate is between one fifteen and one forty-five for your delivery.
  2. Customer: I heard one fifteen earlier and arranged for someone to arrive at exactly then.
  3. Driver: I'm sorry; I gave the lower end as though it were the whole estimate.
  4. Customer: Does that mean you're now promising one forty-five instead, or is that uncertain too?
  5. Driver: That's still an estimate, not a guaranteed latest arrival. Dispatch is tracking the delay.
  6. Customer: I need to tell my colleague what we can actually rely on today.
  7. Driver: You can tell them the current estimated range and that dispatch will update you at noon.
  8. Customer: All right. Please make sure noon refers to the update, not the delivery.
  9. Driver: It does. I'll ask dispatch to use the full range in that message.
  10. Customer: Thank you. I'll pass along the range instead of another exact arrival promise.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Receiver: Have we checked the equipment inside?
Driver: No; the contents are ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. uninspected

  1. A. undamaged No inspection supports calling the contents undamaged.
  2. B. uninspected Correct. The contents have not yet been inspected.
  3. C. damaged Outer-carton damage does not prove damage inside.
  4. D. inspected Inspected contradicts the receiver's stated lack of checking.
2. Receiver: Does the crushed corner tell us who caused it?
Driver: No. What it directly shows is ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. the visible packaging condition

  1. A. who last handled the carton It does not identify the last person who handled it.
  2. B. that the equipment cannot function The uninspected equipment's function has not been established.
  3. C. the visible packaging condition Correct. The observable fact concerns the carton packaging.
  4. D. when the damage occurred The visible condition does not establish when it happened.

1. A crushed corner, not a proven cause

At Grove Studio, a driver discusses an exception note with the receiver.

  2. Receiver: I see it. Can you confirm the notebooks inside have been damaged by rain?

  4. Receiver: Then let's say damp outer packaging rather than wet notebooks throughout the box.
  5. Driver: That is more precise. Shall we identify the affected carton as number three?
  6. Receiver: Yes, three of five. The other four don't show the same visible patch.
  7. Driver: I'll record that distinction so the note doesn't describe every carton as affected.
  8. Receiver: Please also note that we haven't checked the inside during this receipt discussion.
  9. Driver: I can include that. Further findings can be reported against the same shipment reference.
  10. Receiver: That gives our receiving team a clear starting point without claiming an inspection happened.

Your goal

Clarify the identity and quantity of goods offered for collection.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Return R62 lists two printer cartons. Customer presents them and a third carton of cables without a matching reference. Dispatch can query the additional carton; the original two are identifiable.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

return authorization
Approval identifying goods accepted for return under a business's return arrangements.
collection reference
The identifier matching a pickup booking with the goods being collected.
refused delivery
A delivery that the recipient explicitly declines to accept.
return label
A label identifying the destination and reference for goods sent back.

Useful word combinations

  • match the return reference: connect goods with their approval
  • distinguish refusal from absence: report why handover failed
  • confirm the collection quantity: agree the booked units
  • query an extra carton: check an unlisted package

C Notice the language

Keep an extra return separate

Wanting to return an item does not automatically match it to a collection booking.

  • This reference covers ___, not ___.
  • Does the extra carton have ___?
  • I'll describe it as an additional request until ___.

Improve this: "All three must be the same return."

See a clearer version

Two cartons match R62; the cable carton needs its own booking check.

The distinction preserves the confirmed collection and identifies the unresolved extra.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Customer: Are all three cartons listed?
Driver: Two match R62; the third is an ____ request.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. additional

  1. A. identical Different contents and no reference prevent that description.
  2. B. original It was not in the original quantity.
  3. C. completed Its collection has not completed.
  4. D. additional Correct. It adds to the booking.
2. Dispatcher: Did the receiver refuse the delivery?
Driver: No, nobody was there; the recipient was ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. unavailable

  1. A. unavailable Correct. Absence is described as recipient unavailable.
  2. B. unwilling Unwilling implies refusal, but nobody was present to refuse.
  3. C. uncertain Uncertain suggests indecision by a person who was present.
  4. D. unprepared Unprepared suggests someone was present but not ready.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. A third carton beside R62

A driver checks a return collection with a customer.

  1. Driver: Collection R62 lists two printer cartons. I can match these two to that reference.
  2. Customer: The third carton holds cables from the same purchase. I'd like those returned too.
  3. Driver: Do the cables have a separate return authorization or a matching collection reference?
  4. Customer: I only have the printer return message. I thought the purchase reference covered everything.
  5. Driver: A purchase reference and a return collection don't necessarily include the same items.
  6. Customer: Then please ask dispatch whether the cable carton can be added to today's collection.
  7. Driver: I can query it separately. Until confirmed, it remains an additional request, not part of R62.
  8. Customer: That makes sense. Please don't describe all three as printers in your collection record.
  9. Driver: I won't. The two printer cartons match; the cable carton still needs a booking decision.
  10. Customer: Thank you. I'll look for the cable authorization while dispatch checks that separate request.
2. Refusal because of a reported duplicate

A driver speaks with a receiver who declines a shipment.

  1. Driver: You're declining order seven sixty. Could you explain the reason for the delivery record?
  2. Receiver: Our office believes it's a duplicate. We received what looks like this order yesterday.
  3. Driver: Have you confirmed the two order references match, or is that still being checked?
  4. Receiver: It's still being checked. I can report our concern, but I can't prove duplication yet.
  5. Driver: I'll record refused delivery, with your reported reason, rather than confirmed duplicate order.
  6. Receiver: Please include that I declined it, not that nobody was here to receive it.
  7. Driver: Certainly. Customer service can compare the references and discuss the order with your buyer.
  8. Receiver: Can you promise that refusing this one will automatically cancel the invoice as well?
  9. Driver: I can't decide that. Delivery status and billing decisions need separate review by customer service.
  10. Receiver: Understood. Record today's refusal and the reason as reported, with the billing question outstanding.
3. The label points to an old return

A driver checks a collection label before matching it to the booking.

  1. Customer: Here's the return carton. I reused the box, so the label should already be there.
  2. Driver: The visible return label shows R19, but today's collection booking is for R91.
  3. Customer: Those numbers look similar. Is the label perhaps from my previous return last month?
  4. Driver: That could explain it, but we need to match this carton to today's actual authorization.
  5. Customer: My confirmation message says R91 for one lamp carton collected from this address.
  6. Driver: That matches the booking details. The old label still creates a destination-reference discrepancy.

  8. Driver: I will. You have an R91 confirmation; the carton displays a different reference.
  9. Customer: Who can tell us how the correct collection identification should be provided now?

Two short language checks

1. Dispatcher: Was stop 7 physically delivered?
Driver: Yes; only the receipt ____ is pending.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. upload

  1. A. upload Correct. The receipt's transmission, not the handover, remains pending.
  2. B. signature No missing-signature issue is established by this account.
  3. C. handover Mei already received the goods at 16:05.
  4. D. delivery The physical delivery occurred; its digital evidence remains pending.
2. Dispatcher: The customer says it may be a duplicate but hasn't checked.
Driver: I'll record that as an unverified ____, not a confirmed duplicate.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. report

  1. A. report Correct. A report preserves the unverified account without confirming duplication.
  2. B. resolution The order question remains unresolved, not settled.
  3. C. instruction The statement describes a possible issue rather than giving an instruction.
  4. D. approval The customer expressed a concern, not an approval.

1. Received goods, pending upload

- delay notice: A message explaining that planned delivery timing has changed.

- visible damage: Damage observable on the outside of a shipment during the check.
- exception note: A record of a specific irregularity noticed during delivery.
- delivery receipt: A record acknowledging delivery and any recorded qualifications about it.
- concealed damage: Damage to contents that is not apparent from the outside at receipt.
- delivery attempt: A visit made to complete delivery, whether or not goods are handed over.
- recipient unavailable: A status indicating that an appropriate person could not be reached to receive a shipment.
- redelivery: Another delivery visit arranged after an earlier visit did not complete delivery.
- collection point: A designated location where an eligible recipient can collect a shipment.
- curbside delivery: A delivery service with handover at the agreed curbside location, as defined by the booking.
- inside delivery: A booked service including movement to a specified interior location within stated conditions.
- access restriction: A site condition or rule limiting where or when delivery can take place.
- service level: The delivery features and limits included in a particular booking.
- return authorization: Approval identifying goods accepted for return under a business's return arrangements.
- collection reference: The identifier matching a pickup booking with the goods being collected.
- refused delivery: A delivery that the recipient explicitly declines to accept.
- return label: A label identifying the destination and reference for goods sent back.
- proof of delivery: A record supporting a delivery event, such as named receipt or other evidence required for that service.
- outstanding stop: A scheduled delivery whose outcome still needs completion or clarification.
- dispatch log: The dispatch team's record of route events and messages.
- follow-up owner: The named person responsible for the next action on an unresolved delivery issue.

Driver: Today's stop sequence includes E21 as well. Can you check whether that reflects a confirmed appointment change?
Dispatcher: I'm checking the booking record now. No change is recorded; E21 still has tomorrow's appointment at the annex.
Driver: So E20 stays on today's plan, while E21 needs a route-list correction rather than an order cancellation?
Dispatcher: Correct. I'll remove tomorrow's booking from today's stop list while preserving the shipment and its actual appointment.
Driver: Please expand both location names for the next driver.
Dispatcher: I've expanded both location names. E20 is today's main-office delivery; E21 remains tomorrow's training-annex delivery.
Driver: Both orders remain active; only today's route entry needs correction.
Dispatcher: Exactly. The correction concerns today's route list, and both customer orders remain recorded under their separate references.

Dialogue 08: The evening desk challenges a vague status
Original fictional setting: A driver and evening dispatcher resolve an ambiguous end-of-day route entry.
Professional roles: Dispatcher / Driver
Published dialogue (reference script, not my own performance):
Dispatcher: Your final stop says done, but a contents query remains open.
Driver: The physical handover is complete. The customer received six cartons, while the order query concerns contents they expected separately.
Dispatcher: Did you verify the contents yourself, or are you passing on the customer's report?
Driver: I'm passing on their report. We confirmed six cartons, but I didn't verify the expected items inside them.
Dispatcher: Then done is too broad for our handoff. We need to distinguish completed delivery from an unresolved order question.
Driver: Agreed. I'll state six cartons received by Omar at five ten, with a separate reported contents query outstanding.
Dispatcher: I can send that query to customer service. What reference should they use to connect it to the receipt?
Driver: Use shipment Q14. The receipt is already available, so this isn't a missing-upload issue like yesterday's stop.
Dispatcher: I have Q14 and its proof of delivery. I'll link the query to that shipment.
Driver: Please don't tell the customer their whole order is complete solely because the delivery receipt is available.
Dispatcher: Understood. I'll take the customer-service follow-up and keep the contents query open until they respond.
Driver: That accurately separates what happened at the door from what customer service still needs to establish.
Vocabulary in this exchange: delivery receipt: A record acknowledging delivery and any recorded qualifications about it. / proof of delivery: A record supporting a delivery event, such as named receipt or other evidence required for that service.
END REFERENCE
