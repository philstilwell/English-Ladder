Read the story

Alison Hammond feared she was having a heart attack on live TV. She went to the hospital for a heart check-up. But it was a panic attack, not a heart attack. She said, "I couldn't catch my breath and I genuinely thought I was having a heart attack." Panic attacks can happen to anyone. A panic attack can feel like a heart attack because symptoms are similar. You can try to calm yourself by breathing slowly.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. feared (verb): worried or believed something bad would happen.
2. heart attack (noun): a serious medical problem when blood flow to the heart stops.
3. live TV (noun): television broadcast happening at the same time it is filmed.
4. check-up (noun): a medical examination to see if you are healthy.
5. panic attack (noun): a sudden feeling of strong fear, stress, or anxiety.
6. catch my breath (phrase): to breathe normally again after effort or stress.
7. similar (adjective): almost the same.
8. calm (verb): to make yourself feel quiet and relaxed.

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense
We use the simple past tense to talk about actions that finished in the past. We often add "-ed" to regular verbs, but some verbs are irregular and change their form, like "go" to "went".
Example from the text: "She went to the hospital for a heart check-up."
