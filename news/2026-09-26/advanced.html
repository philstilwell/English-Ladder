Ed Sheeran Concerts Called Off Amid Storm and Tour Controversy

📅 September 26, 2026 - Ed Sheeran Concerts Called Off Amid Storm and Tour Controversy

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Ed Sheeran's upcoming US concerts were called off because of imminent storm activity. Performances initially planned for Friday and Saturday in Massachusetts were called off following extensive weather alerts. The singer was to have given two concerts at the Gillette Stadium in Foxborough, near Boston, as part of his ongoing US tour. Beyond the weather, the tour recently garnered significant attention regarding a dispute over removing Macklemore as a support act. This action stemmed from Macklemore's pro-Palestinian comments made during previous stage appearances. A statement indicated that, after consulting with local authorities, the tour organisers chose to suspend the events at Gillette Stadium. This critical determination made the well-being of attendees, staff, and everyone involved in the productions paramount. Those with tickets are able to secure reimbursement at their original point of acquisition. High wind advisories remain in force across the region until early Sunday, prompting officials to recommend people minimise travel. This was not the only difficult situation Sheeran navigated; he was at the eye of a storm last week concerning Macklemore's departure. Robert Kraft, a stadium owner, lobbied for Macklemore's removal after Kraft accused the rapper of hate speech for his comments and showing images of Gaza. Sheeran clarified that the choice to drop Macklemore was made by his tour management, not by him, with reports suggesting owners would not allow shows if Macklemore was on the bill. Consequently, Macklemore's exclusion from the tour prompted other backing artists to pull out in solidarity, which sparked a heated debate on artistic freedom and political expression in music. Upon returning to the stage, Sheeran expressed regret, acknowledging he had to make challenging choices and was making mistakes.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. imminent (adjective): likely to happen very soon; impending.
2. extensive (adjective): covering a large area or range; widespread.
3. garnered (verb): gathered or acquired (something, especially attention or support).
4. dispute (noun): a disagreement or argument, especially a formal one.
5. consulting (verb): seeking information or advice from (someone with expertise or authority).
6. paramount (adjective): more important than anything else; supreme.
7. reimbursement (noun): the action of repaying a person who has spent or lost money.
8. in force (adjective phrase): (of a law, rule, or advisory) operative; in effect.
9. minimise (verb): reduce (something undesirable) to the smallest possible amount or degree.
10. eye of a storm (idiom/noun phrase): the central point of a controversy, difficult situation, or crisis.
11. lobbied (verb): sought to influence (a politician or public official) on an issue; pressured for a particular outcome.
12. on the bill (idiom/adjective phrase): scheduled or listed as a performer for an event.
13. expressed regret (verb phrase): showed or stated that one felt sadness, repentance, or disappointment about something.
14. acknowledging (verb): accepting or admitting the existence or truth of something.

Advanced Grammar: Varied Reporting Verbs
In advanced English, using a range of reporting verbs beyond just 'said' adds precision and nuance when attributing speech or ideas. Verbs like 'expressed regret' and 'acknowledging' in the example convey not only what was communicated but also the speaker's tone, emotional state, or the nature of their message, allowing for a more sophisticated understanding of the information.
Example from the text: "Upon returning to the stage, Sheeran expressed regret, acknowledging he had to make challenging choices and was making mistakes."
