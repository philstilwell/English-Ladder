📅 November 12, 2026 - Young People Could Receive Less Support Under Welfare Proposals
A proposed UK welfare reform could reduce financial support for some young people while expanding help with finding work.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

A review ordered by the government could lead to some young people receiving less financial support. According to the Times, Alan Milburn's report will recommend moving people under 25 off the main sickness and disability benefits. Milburn has been asked to help reduce the number of young adults who are not in education, employment or training. Under the proposals, young people would receive an allowance at the same standard rate as universal credit. Some could qualify for an additional allowance to cover everyday costs linked to their health conditions. People with more severe disabilities who cannot work would receive a new benefit at a higher rate. The report is also expected to recommend billions in spending on employment support, including work placements and personal advisers. Milburn said the changes would affect both existing claimants and people making new claims. Young people could lose some financial support if they did not take part in the employment support offered. If the recommendations were approved, it could take almost ten years for the government to save money. Milburn said the new system would require significant investment and would need to be built again from the beginning. A government spokesperson promised to keep support available for people who most need protection and those unable to work.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. financial support (noun phrase): Money given to help someone pay for things they need.
2. proposals (plural noun): Plans or suggestions that people will consider before deciding what to do.
3. allowance (noun): An amount of money given regularly to help someone meet their needs.
4. qualify for (verb phrase): Meet the conditions needed to receive something.
5. severe (adjective): Very serious and causing major difficulties.
6. lead to (phrasal verb): Cause something to happen or produce a particular result.
7. claimants (plural noun): People who apply for or receive money through a government support system.
8. investment (noun): Money spent now to improve something for the future.

Grammar Focus: Second conditional with “could”
Use “if” with the past simple and “could” with a base verb to describe an imagined situation and its possible result. Here, “were approved” is a past simple passive form: it presents approval as a possibility, not a completed decision.
Example from the text: "If the recommendations were approved, it could take almost ten years for the government to save money."

📅 November 11, 2026 - Robot Boats Help Navies Search for Sea Mines
Navies are using robot systems to find sea mines and protect people, but uncertainty about the danger remains.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The conflict in the Gulf gives the US an opportunity to test robot systems designed to find sea mines. Modern sea mines rest on the seabed and explode when they detect sound or magnetic signals from a passing ship. Even the threat of these weapons can stop a group of tankers from sailing. Traditionally, specialist mine-hunting ships have carried crews of around 80 people. To keep people away from danger, autonomous systems that can operate on their own are monitored from shore. Some systems may need only 20 people, who work in shifts on land. A Thales robot boat carries sensors that detect mines using sonar, a technology that uses sound to find objects. It pulls equipment that produces a detailed three-dimensional picture of the seabed, using artificial intelligence to improve the image. Compared with conventional mine-hunting ships, autonomous systems are cheaper and can be transported by air with their control centres. The report says US robot minehunters can either blow up a mine or mark its location as a place to avoid. The report also explains that robots can reduce the risk but cannot prove that no mines are present. Neil Roberts, who speaks for marine insurers, says uncertainty about how many mines may be present affects how people feel.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. conflict (noun): Fighting between countries or armed groups.
2. threat (noun): The possibility that something dangerous or harmful could happen.
3. specialist (adjective): Designed or trained for a particular kind of work.
4. autonomous (adjective): Able to operate without a person controlling every action.
5. monitored (verb, past participle): Watched or checked over time to see what is happening.
6. in shifts (adverbial phrase): In separate working periods, with different groups taking turns.
7. conventional (adjective): Following the usual or traditional way of doing something.
8. uncertainty (noun): A situation in which something is not known or certain.

Grammar Focus: Present simple passive
Use the present simple passive to describe a regular process when the action matters more than who does it. Form it with “am”, “is” or “are” plus a past participle. Here, “are monitored” focuses on what happens to the systems.
Example from the text: "To keep people away from danger, autonomous systems that can operate on their own are monitored from shore."

The Leonid meteor shower is expected to be visible across the UK during November. The shower continues until 30 November and is expected to reach its peak on the night of 17 November. Viewers could spot as many as 10 to 15 meteors in an hour. As the comet Tempel-Tuttle travels around the Sun, it leaves behind a trail of dust particles. These particles enter the layer of air around Earth at speeds of up to 71 kilometres per second, producing streaks of light. Experts recommend finding a safe, dark place away from light pollution, which is unwanted light from sources such as street lights. You can see the meteors with the naked eye, so you do not need binoculars or a telescope. Allow about 20 to 30 minutes for your eyes to adjust to the dark so that you have a better chance of spotting the meteors. An open area gives you a wide view of the night sky. Although the forecast calls for dry weather much of the time, clouds may still block the view in many areas. Fog could also form in places where the sky stays clear for a while, making the meteors harder to see. Anyone who misses the meteors this weekend may have better viewing conditions near the peak in mid-November. Near the peak, the Moon will be below the horizon around midnight, so its light is less likely to spoil the view.

Definitions in English.

1. peak (noun): The time when the meteor shower is most active.
2. spot (verb): To notice or see something, especially when it is hard to see.
3. trail (noun): A line of material left behind as something moves.
4. streaks (plural noun): Long, narrow lines, here made of light.

6. adjust to (verb phrase): To get used to a change, such as going from a bright place to a dark one.

8. block the view (verb phrase): To get in the way so that someone cannot see what they are trying to look at.

Read the story

A rare beetle has been discovered in Pembrokeshire after being missing from Wales for 60 years. In the past, habitat loss had brought the Short-Necked Oil Beetle close to extinction, meaning it could have disappeared completely. Last year, a survey at Castlemartin found only dead adult beetles, raising concerns about the species' future there. This year, after three years of searching, experts discovered larvae, or young beetles at an early stage of development, in a small area. Anne Hobbs from the Bumblebee Conservation Trust said the evidence suggested that the species was breeding in Wales again. The beetles are dependent on bees to complete their life cycle and need a particular habitat, or type of natural environment. They need thick plants for shelter and plenty of flowers in their habitat. They are usually seen from March to June in coastal grassland and sand dunes, especially on warm, sunny days. Because they need such specific conditions, only a handful of sites in the UK are suitable for them. Castlemartin Range is currently the only known site for the species in Wales, and wildlife protection workers regularly survey it. Hobbs said protection work needed to consider the needs of several species to be effective. She thanked the project's partners and volunteers for continuing their support, despite two years without any sightings of larvae.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. habitat loss (noun phrase): The damage or disappearance of natural places where animals and plants live.
2. raising concerns (verb phrase): Making people worry about a possible problem.
3. evidence (uncountable noun): Facts or signs that help people judge whether something is true.
4. breeding (verb, present participle): Producing young animals.
5. dependent on (adjective phrase): Needing someone or something in order to live or develop.
6. shelter (uncountable noun): Protection provided by a place where an animal can stay safely.
7. a handful of (quantity phrase): A small number of people or things.
8. suitable (adjective): Having the right features for a particular need or purpose.
