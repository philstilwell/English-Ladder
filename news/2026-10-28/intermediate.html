The Search for Quieter Delivery Drones

Intermediate · B1–B2

📅 October 28, 2026 - The Search for Quieter Delivery Drones

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

As drone deliveries become more common, manufacturers are looking for ways to reduce the sound their aircraft make. Ashley Schmidt now spends little time in her garden in North Garland, near Dallas. She cannot stand the sound of delivery drones flying overhead with her neighbours’ online orders. Steady traffic sounds may blend into the background, while the sudden sound of a drone can stand out. Researcher Antonio Torija Martinez says drone sounds can be particularly noticeable even when overall sound levels are relatively low. Drones operate at low altitudes, or heights, and may fly directly over residential areas, where people live. People who benefit from drone delivery services may be less bothered by their sound, according to Torija Martinez. London-based company Greenjets claims its system for powering drones can reduce their noise by at least two thirds. It places spinning blades inside a pod, a case that helps soften the sound and direct it away from the ground. It also slows the rotating blades and uses materials that reduce the sound caused by vibration, or repeated shaking. One propeller design has twisted, loop-shaped blades with no pointed tips. Amazon says its latest propellers produce almost half the perceived sound of its previous design, with a milder tone. Wing, which supplies drones for Walmart deliveries, varies its flight paths to avoid repeatedly flying over the same homes. Schmidt wants deliveries in her area to stop until the service no longer causes a nuisance, or an annoying problem.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. manufacturers (plural noun): Companies that make products.
2. background (noun): The sounds around you that you do not pay much attention to.
3. noticeable (adjective): Easy to hear, see or become aware of.
4. residential areas (plural noun phrase): Parts of a town or city where people live.
5. benefit from (verb phrase): Receive something helpful or useful from something.
6. vibration (noun): Repeated small, fast movements that can produce sound.
7. flight paths (plural noun phrase): The routes that aircraft follow through the air.
8. nuisance (noun): Something that annoys people or causes problems for them.

Grammar Focus: Relative clauses that add extra information
“Wing, which supplies drones for Walmart deliveries, varies its flight paths to avoid repeatedly flying over the same homes.” The clause “which supplies drones for Walmart deliveries” adds extra information about Wing. Here, “which” refers to a company; it can also refer to a thing. Commas separate this extra information from the main sentence, which remains complete without it.
Example from the text: "Wing, which supplies drones for Walmart deliveries, varies its flight paths to avoid repeatedly flying over the same homes."
