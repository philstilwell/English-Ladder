AI Graduate Jobs Raise Questions About Workplace Learning

Intermediate · B1–B2

News archive →
📅 November 04, 2026 - AI Graduate Jobs Raise Questions About Workplace Learning

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Some companies are creating graduate roles focused on artificial intelligence, or AI, even as the technology is linked to graduate job cuts. Recent graduate Lotte Underwood works in public relations, a field concerned with organisations' public image, but mainly uses AI. She manages AI agents, checks their outputs and passes the results to colleagues who work directly with clients and the media. However, she also wants to pick up traditional public relations skills that her current role does not focus on. Dillan Patel began developing an internal chatbot to collect the knowledge of a senior employee who was preparing to retire. He now develops further AI agents and identifies manual or repetitive jobs that the technology could do. Research by PwC suggests that AI allows employers to expect more senior abilities from people in entry-level jobs. Cian Clinton uses around ten AI agents each day and says AI makes him feel more like an equal team member. He believes this experience will help his career progress faster. Simon Pratt says AI knowledge matters, but his company's two-year graduate programme focuses on learning the trade. Graduates on this programme rotate across four areas, spending six months in each and learning alongside experienced managers. Claire Reed warns that graduate roles focused too heavily on AI may not support long-term career progress. She argues that AI skills need to be combined with sector knowledge and an understanding of what clients actually want.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. outputs (plural noun): The information or results produced by an AI tool.
2. pick up (phrasal verb): To learn something through experience or practice.
3. internal (adjective): Used or happening within an organisation.
4. repetitive (adjective): Involving the same actions again and again.
5. entry-level (adjective): Describing jobs for people near the start of their careers.
6. learning the trade (verb phrase): Developing the practical knowledge and abilities needed for a particular kind of work.
7. rotate (verb): To move between different areas of work according to a planned schedule.
8. sector knowledge (noun phrase): Understanding of a particular type of business and how it works.

Grammar Focus: Defining relative clauses with “who”
Use a defining relative clause to identify the person you mean. Put “who” after a noun referring to a person, then add information about them. Here, “who was preparing to retire” identifies the senior employee involved in the chatbot project.
Example from the text: "Dillan Patel began developing an internal chatbot to collect the knowledge of a senior employee who was preparing to retire."
