Course: Cooks and Kitchen Staff English

Lesson 01: Clarifying a prep list
Original fictional case: The prep list says 'soup: 24' without a unit. Yesterday's sheet used portions; the storeroom label uses containers. The lead intended 24 portions from the current recipe, not 24 containers. No batch has started.
Language workshop: Ask what the number counts
Communication goal: Clarify quantities before interpreting a kitchen shorthand.
Language guidance: Numbers need units. A recipe's yield and a container count describe different things; familiarity with yesterday's sheet isn't confirmation for today.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): Does ___ mean portions or ___? / Which recipe version applies to ___? / So the required yield is ___, not ___.
Vocabulary: mise en place: Ingredients and equipment organized for a kitchen's planned work. / prep list: A list of ingredients or components to prepare before or during service. / yield: The usable amount a recipe or ingredient is expected to produce. / batch: A quantity of food prepared as one planned production unit.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Lead cook: confirm 24 portions using Monday's recipe; container numbers are storage counts.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: The cook finds a second sheet dated Friday. Identify which sheet governs today's task.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: The soup line says twenty-four, but it doesn't specify portions or containers. Before I start a batch, could you confirm the unit and the recipe version? Yesterday's sheet used portions, while the storage label counts containers. I'll repeat the required yield once we know which quantity today's prep list means.
END REFERENCE

Lesson 02: Reporting deliveries and stock discrepancies
Original fictional case: The delivery note lists six cases of tomatoes; four are physically present. The driver says two may be on another trolley but has not checked. The receiving lead must resolve the record. Report the observed count without accusing the supplier or recording six as received.
Language workshop: Report what was actually counted
Communication goal: Distinguish a physical count from an expected quantity.
Language guidance: Use 'lists' for the document and 'counted' for the observation. A possible explanation is not a verified delivery.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): The note lists ___; I counted ___. / We haven't established whether ___. / Could you reconcile ___ before ___?
Vocabulary: delivery note: A supplier's document listing items and quantities sent with a delivery. / short delivery: A delivery containing less than the quantity expected. / traceability: The ability to connect a food item to its source and relevant identifying records. / lot code: A label identifier linking a product to a production batch or lot.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Driver: six are on the note; you haven't checked the second trolley yet.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: The driver finds one more case. Update the observed count to five without declaring the delivery complete.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: I can count four tomato cases here, while the delivery note lists six. Two may be on the other trolley, but that hasn't been checked. Please keep the received quantity unconfirmed until the receiving lead reconciles the count. I'll show them the four cases and the delivery note together.
END REFERENCE
