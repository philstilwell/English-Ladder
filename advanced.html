📅 October 23, 2026 - Trump Claims Musk Reinstated Starlink Access for Ukraine
Amid political pressure and tactical concerns, Donald Trump claims Elon Musk has reinstated crucial satellite network access for Ukraine's defense.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

US President Donald Trump claims that Elon Musk has reactivated full access to Ukraine’s satellite internet network, following reports that deliberate limitations had severely hampered Kyiv's tactical maneuvers. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky previously noted that connectivity to the highly secure Starshield network, provided by Musk's infrastructure, had yet to be fully re-established. The connectivity limitations coincided with Trump pressuring Zelensky to halt Ukrainian aerial strikes on Russian oil refineries, which the US leader argued were causing global prices to surge. Facing crucial midterm elections next month, Trump also waived specific sanctions on Moscow and approved a controversial deal to import Russian diesel. According to unnamed officials, Trump contacted Musk to express frustration that the network's data speeds remained severely capped. These artificial speed limits rendered Ukraine's frontline interceptors practically useless against incoming Russian strikes. Although Trump refused to confirm whether he directly asked Musk to lift the limitations, he confidently asserted that the service had been successfully reinstated. For his part, Musk has consistently denied deliberately throttling the network, which is vital for targeting remote positions and protecting the country. Furthermore, the tech entrepreneur suggested that the US government might be responsible for the limitations, as the Pentagon fundamentally controls access to the secure platform. Nevertheless, the White House has categorically denied issuing any formal orders to curtail the crucial satellite service.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. hampered (verb): hindered, impeded, or created difficulties for the progress or movement of something.
2. connectivity (noun): the ability of a device or network to successfully link to the internet or other communication platforms.
3. waived (verb): officially chose not to enforce or apply a rule, penalty, or requirement.
4. sanctions (noun): official orders or economic penalties taken against a country to force it to obey international law.
5. capped (verb): had an upper limit strictly enforced, preventing speed or performance from increasing beyond a certain level.
6. interceptors (noun): fast aircraft or defensive missiles specifically designed to stop incoming enemy strikes before they reach their targets.
7. asserted (verb): stated a fact or belief confidently and forcefully.
8. throttling (verb): intentionally slowing down the speed or bandwidth of an internet connection or digital service.
9. fundamentally (adverb): at the most basic, essential, and foundational level; in a core or underlying way.
10. categorically (adverb): in a completely clear, direct, and explicit manner, leaving absolutely no room for doubt or confusion.

Advanced Grammar: Present participle clauses
A present participle clause starting with an '-ing' verb can establish the political context, cause, or background for the main action. Here, it concisely explains why the subject made their decision without requiring a separate sentence.
Example from the text: "Facing crucial midterm elections next month, Trump also waived specific sanctions on Moscow and approved a controversial deal to import Russian diesel."

📅 October 22, 2026 - Britain's Missed Opportunity for a Trillion-Pound Wealth Fund
New Treasury calculations reveal that systematically investing historical North Sea oil proceeds could have endowed the UK with a sovereign wealth portfolio worth a trillion pounds today.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

For the first time, Treasury officials have modelled the staggering potential value of a British sovereign wealth portfolio built from historical North Sea proceeds. According to these calculations, had the capital been systematically retained without withdrawals, the nation would possess an endowment worth approximately one trillion pounds today. Instead, critics of 1980s economic policies argue that peak oil receipts were frittered away on pre-election tax cuts rather than preserved. Defenders counter that subsequent administrations also failed to act, pointing out that Norway only began investing its wealth in earnest in 1996. In contrast, Norway’s methodical approach has yielded a formidable war chest of $2.4 trillion from the same North Sea reserves. To address this historical gap, Chancellor John Healey recently announced the creation of a domestic Energy Wealth Fund. However, the new British entity will operate on a markedly different premise, as it intends to finance domestic environmental projects. This contrasts sharply with Norway, which is mandated to invest its vast capital abroad and has generated immense returns from global technology stocks. Analysts caution that this constrained domestic focus may cause the British entity to struggle to match Norway's exceptional financial performance. Furthermore, as North Sea production slows, the anticipated influx of new fossil fuel receipts into the British portfolio is projected to decline steadily. Ultimately, rather than expecting the initial £2 billion allocation to match such exponential growth, officials are left asking what might have been had a similar, far-sighted idea been adopted in decades past.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. staggering (adjective): Deeply shocking or astonishing, often due to an overwhelming size or amount.
2. proceeds (noun): The monetary revenue or profit obtained from a specific event, activity, or enterprise.
3. endowment (noun): A large reserve of capital invested to provide long-term financial support for an institution or nation.
4. frittered away (phrasal verb): Wasted money, time, or resources gradually on trivial or unimportant things.
5. in earnest (phrase): Occurring to a greater extent or more intensely and seriously than before.
6. war chest (noun): A substantial reserve of funds built up to be used for a challenging situation or long-term strategic goal.
7. premise (noun): A fundamental principle or foundational idea upon which an argument, theory, or operation is based.
8. mandated (verb): Officially required or commanded by authority to take a specific action.
9. constrained (adjective): Severely restricted in scope, extent, or activity.
10. influx (noun): The arrival or entry of a large amount of money, resources, or people into a place.

Advanced Grammar: Inverted mixed conditional
In formal English, conditional clauses can omit the word "if" by inverting the auxiliary verb and the subject. Here, "had the capital been systematically retained" acts as a past hypothetical condition (third conditional). It connects to a present hypothetical result ("would possess... today"), creating a mixed conditional sentence.
Example from the text: "According to these calculations, had the capital been systematically retained without withdrawals, the nation would possess an endowment worth approximately one trillion pounds today."
