Lesson 01: Patient Access, Scheduling, and Referrals
Original fictional case: Scheduler Elena receives a referral with patient identifiers and contact details but no requested specialty or reason for the appointment. The patient expects a booking confirmation today. No appointment has been reserved and no clinical priority has been assigned. Referral-office colleague Marcus can seek clarification from the referring team. Elena can promise a status update by 3 p.m., not a confirmed appointment. Clinical urgency questions must follow the clinical review process rather than a scheduler's guess.
Language workshop: The referral arrived, but the booking is not ready
Communication goal: Request missing referral information and give a patient a reliable update without inventing clinical details.
Language guidance: Received means the referral arrived; complete means the required information is present; booked means an appointment is arranged. None of these statuses automatically establishes clinical urgency, insurance coverage, or authorization. State each confirmed fact separately.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): We received your referral and are checking the information needed to book. / The requested specialty and appointment reason are not shown. / Please confirm the intended receiving service with the referring team. / I cannot supply a clinical reason from the contact details alone. / I will contact you by 3 p.m. with the current status. / That is an update time, not a confirmed appointment time. / No appointment has been reserved yet. / Clinical priority needs review by the qualified clinical team. / I will track the clarification request rather than ask you to restart it. / What is the appropriate contact route recorded for this request? / Let us confirm any interpreter or accessibility arrangements through the usual process. / We are waiting for specific information, and I will update you even if it is still pending. / Please confirm that the referring team received the clarification request. / A provisional hold would still need its conditions explained. / I cannot promise an available slot before the receiving service is confirmed. / Record the response, next action, owner, and patient update.
Vocabulary: patient access: Administrative processes helping patients reach appropriate services. / referral: A request directing a patient to another service or professional. / referring clinician: The clinician requesting another service or consultation. / receiving service: The team or department expected to act on a referral. / specialty: A defined branch of clinical practice. / reason for referral: The stated clinical purpose of the requested consultation. / referral completeness: Whether the required referral information has been supplied. / clinical triage: Qualified assessment of clinical priority and appropriate next care steps. / appointment slot: A defined time available for a scheduled visit. / scheduling template: The configured pattern of appointment types and times. / booking confirmation: A message establishing that an appointment has been arranged. / provisional hold: A temporary reservation subject to stated conditions. / waitlist: A managed list of patients awaiting an available appointment. / cancellation list: A list used to offer newly available appointments. / intake queue: The worklist holding incoming requests for initial processing. / demographic verification: Checking patient identity and contact details for accuracy. / eligibility verification: Checking current insurance or service eligibility information. / network status: Whether a provider participates in the relevant insurance network. / authorization requirement: A condition requiring the relevant approval before a specified service or coverage. / interpreter request: A request for appropriate language assistance. / accessibility need: A requirement affecting a person's ability to use a service. / callback commitment: An explicit promise to contact someone again by a stated time. / closed-loop referral: A referral process with tracked receipt, action, and feedback to the relevant parties. / escalation pathway: The designated route for unresolved or urgent issues.
Speaking task: Check the cloze key, then read the referral exchange. Keep identifiers received separate from the missing specialty and appointment reason. Switch roles for the scheduling discussion. Stress update by three, no reserved slot, and clinical priority unconfirmed; do not choose a specialty for convenience. Complete and check the two-o'clock callback exchange. Make the promised status call while review remains pending, without calling it a consultation.
Partner: Elena: Scheduler / Marcus: Referral-office colleague
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A scheduler promises a status call by 2 p.m. The specialist has not reviewed the referral, and no appointment is reserved. At 2 p.m., review is still pending.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Elena: I will update the patient by three. Please confirm when the referring office receives your request, and tell me if you need help reaching them.
Marcus: That gives us a closed-loop referral process rather than two disconnected messages. We need a visible response and next action, not just evidence that someone pressed send.
Elena: If the usual contact cannot resolve it, who takes the next step? I want a clear route while I keep the patient updated.
Marcus: Follow the escalation pathway and preserve the distinction between administrative follow-up and clinical judgment. The immediate commitment remains a clear update by three, with no invented appointment or priority.
END REFERENCE

Lesson 03: Patient Flow, Capacity, and Staffing
Original fictional case: A unit has 28 physical beds, of which 24 are staffed and operational today. All 24 operational beds are occupied at the 10 a.m. census. Two discharges are planned, but neither timing nor completion is confirmed. The other four beds are not staffed for use. Flow coordinator Hana and unit manager Paul must correct a message saying two beds are available now. Any new placement depends on confirmed departure, room readiness, appropriate staffing, and the relevant clinical allocation process.
Language workshop: Planned discharges are not available beds
