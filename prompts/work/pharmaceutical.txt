Lesson 01: Drug Development Strategy, Unmet Need, TPP, and Evidence Logic
Original fictional case: An internal slide for fictional candidate PX-17 says it improves patient mobility. The available experiment used a cell-based assay, not patients. Across three experimental runs, the team observed a change in a disease-related marker at one tested concentration. Human exposure, tolerability, and clinical benefit have not been established. Scientist Leila and development lead Owen are preparing a portfolio discussion. The proposed target product profile describes a desired future benefit, not an achieved product characteristic. No clinical study is authorized by this discussion.
Language workshop: A laboratory signal becomes a patient-benefit claim
Communication goal: Replace an overstated development claim with a precise finding and a testable next question.
Language guidance: A biomarker change is not automatically clinical benefit. Its meaning depends on the marker, context, and supporting evidence. Here, one concentration and three laboratory runs do not establish human exposure, tolerability, dose response, or improved mobility.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): The observation comes from a cell-based assay, not a patient study. / We observed a marker change under the tested conditions. / Clinical benefit has not been established. / The target product profile describes what we aim to demonstrate. / What evidence connects this marker to the intended patient outcome? / Three runs support repeatability within these conditions, not general clinical effectiveness. / We tested one concentration, so this does not characterize a dose-response relationship. / We need to test whether the finding persists under relevant follow-up conditions. / The current experiment does not establish human tolerability. / Which patients would the proposed product eventually be intended to serve? / An early signal supports investigation, not a treatment recommendation. / What result would support the next development decision? / Are we discussing target engagement, a biomarker, or a patient outcome? / The desired benefit remains a hypothesis to be evaluated. / This portfolio discussion does not authorize a clinical study. / Retain the finding, its conditions, and the unresolved evidence gap.
Vocabulary: unmet medical need: A health-related need not adequately addressed by available options. / target product profile: A planning description of desired future product characteristics. / development candidate: A selected investigational substance being evaluated for further development. / mechanism of action: The biological process through which an intervention produces an effect. / target engagement: Evidence that an intervention interacts with its intended biological target. / proof of concept: Evidence supporting the feasibility of a defined scientific or clinical idea. / cell-based assay: A laboratory test using cells to assess a specified response. / biomarker: A measured characteristic indicating a biological process or response. / surrogate endpoint: A substitute outcome used to predict a clinical outcome, with context-dependent support. / clinical benefit: A meaningful favorable effect on how patients feel, function, or survive. / translational evidence: Information connecting findings across experimental and human contexts. / biological plausibility: A reasoned basis for believing a biological explanation could be credible. / dose-response relationship: How a measured effect varies with administered dose. / exposure-response relationship: How an effect varies with drug exposure in the body. / pharmacokinetics: Study of the time course of drug exposure through absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination. / pharmacodynamics: Study of a drug's biological effects and their relation to exposure. / nonclinical study: Research conducted outside human clinical studies. / tolerability: The extent to which unwanted effects can be endured in a defined context. / therapeutic window: The exposure range associated with desired effects and acceptable toxicity in context. / repeatability: Agreement between repeated results under the same specified measurement conditions. / assay limitation: A restriction affecting what a test can establish. / go/no-go criterion: A defined condition informing whether development proceeds or stops. / evidence gap: Missing information needed to support a proposed conclusion. / development milestone: A defined point of progress or review in a development program.
Speaking task: Check the cloze key and read the PX-17 discussion. Emphasize cell-based assay, one concentration, and three runs without adding patient results. Switch roles for turns 11-20. Distinguish repeatability under the stated conditions from human tolerability and the desired mobility benefit. Complete and check the planning-profile exchange. Read once-daily use as a goal, target engagement as the observed finding, and human dosing as unestablished.
Partner: Leila: Translational scientist / Owen: Development lead
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A planning profile targets once-daily use. The candidate has no human dosing data. A laboratory test measured target engagement but did not measure patient symptoms.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Owen: The committee will ask what would count as enough progress to continue. A broad instruction to produce more data will not help them compare programs.
Leila: Propose a defined go/no-go criterion for qualified review. It should match the next decision, without pretending that an early laboratory threshold alone establishes a product suitable for patients.
Owen: I will replace the mobility headline with the assay finding. Send me the limits and proposed next question so the committee sees what this result supports.
Leila: Good. The next development milestone remains subject to the required review. This discussion does not authorize a clinical study, and the final slide should say no more than the evidence supports.

Original fictional case: A fictional trial measures week-12 symptoms; lower scores are better. The primary treatment-minus-control estimate is -2 points (95% confidence interval: -5 to +1; p=0.19). The prespecified two-sided threshold is 0.05. A secondary endpoint has nominal p=0.01, but the testing plan requires primary success before a confirmatory secondary claim. Statistician Anika and medical lead Luis must correct the headline: the trial proved efficacy.
Language workshop: A favorable secondary result takes over the headline
Communication goal: Explain a trial readout without replacing the prespecified question or overstating statistical certainty.
Language guidance: The estimate favors treatment, but the interval includes zero and the primary p-value exceeds 0.05. This is not proof of no effect. It is also not a successful primary test. The secondary finding retains its actual status under the prespecified plan.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): The primary analysis did not meet the prespecified significance threshold. / The point estimate favors treatment on this lower-is-better scale. / The confidence interval includes no difference. / The secondary p-value is nominal under this testing plan. / Primary success was required before a confirmatory secondary claim. / Failure to meet the threshold does not prove there is no effect. / Statistical significance and clinical importance are different questions. / Which treatment effect does the estimand specify? / Confirm which participants are included in this analysis. / State how treatment discontinuation affects the target question. / What assumptions support the handling of missing outcomes? / This post hoc analysis was not the prespecified primary analysis. / The difference is treatment minus control, measured in score points. / Identify the data cutoff for this readout. / A favorable secondary result does not replace the primary outcome. / Present the estimate, uncertainty, testing status, and clinical context together.
