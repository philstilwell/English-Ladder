📅 November 01, 2026 - Rediscovered Beetle Shows Signs of Reproducing in Wales

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The short-necked oil beetle has been rediscovered in Pembrokeshire after an apparent absence of 60 years from Wales. Recent surveys had prompted fears of local extinction, meaning that the beetle might no longer exist in Wales. Across the UK, the species went unreported for almost 60 years after 1948, as habitat loss brought it to the brink of extinction. Last year's survey at Castlemartin recorded only dead adults, prompting concern about the species' persistence at the site. After three years of searching, experts discovered larvae, or immature beetles, in a small area of the site, suggesting that they had located part of a larger batch of young beetles. According to Natural Resources Wales, the number and distribution of the larvae corroborated this interpretation. Anne Hobbs, from the Bumblebee Conservation Trust, welcomed indications that the species was reproducing in Wales again. The beetle relies on solitary bees for its life cycle and requires both thick vegetation for cover and abundant flowers. Natural Resources Wales said these exacting habitat requirements leave only a few suitable sites in the UK. The species is classified as nationally rare in Great Britain and is listed as vulnerable on the IUCN Red List. The beetles are typically encountered between March and June in coastal grassland rich in flowers and in sand dunes, with activity greatest on warm, sunny days. Rediscoveries elsewhere in the UK included Devon in 2006 and the Isle of Coll in Scotland in 2009. Castlemartin Range remains the species' only known Welsh location and is subject to sustained monitoring by conservationists. The work forms part of Wales's flagship species recovery programme, which also seeks to conserve other species sharing similar habitats. Hobbs stressed that effective conservation must accommodate the needs of several species.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. apparent absence (noun phrase): A period when something seems to be missing, although its actual absence has not been established.
2. local extinction (noun phrase): The disappearance of a species from a particular area, even though it may continue to exist elsewhere.
3. brink of extinction (noun phrase): A situation in which a species is close to disappearing completely.
4. persistence (noun): The continued existence of a species in a location despite conditions that threaten it.
5. corroborated (verb, past tense): Provided additional support for an interpretation, making it more credible without necessarily proving it.
6. exacting (adjective): Demanding precise conditions that can be difficult to satisfy.
7. vulnerable (adjective): Assigned a conservation category indicating a significant risk of extinction.
8. sustained monitoring (noun phrase): Repeated observation maintained over time to track a situation or detect changes.
9. flagship (noun used attributively): Used to identify a particularly prominent programme that represents an organisation's wider work or priorities.
10. accommodate (verb): Take different needs into account and make provision for them.

Advanced Grammar: Supplementary participle clauses expressing inference
A supplementary “-ing” clause can express an inference from the preceding main clause. Here, “suggesting” presents an interpretation of the discovery without treating it as established fact; it comments on the finding as a whole.
Example from the text: "After three years of searching, experts discovered larvae, or immature beetles, in a small area of the site, suggesting that they had located part of a larger batch of young beetles."
