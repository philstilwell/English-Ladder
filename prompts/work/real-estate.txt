Course: Real Estate English
Professional audience: real estate agents, brokers, transaction coordinators, property managers, leasing teams, and commercial real estate staff
Scope: Fictional English practice using mainly US real-estate terminology, not legal, lending, valuation, engineering, or safety advice. Lesson 01: Agency, Representation, Compensation, and Trust
Original fictional case: Prospective buyer Lena asks licensee Ren whether Ren represents both sides of a property transaction. Their relationship has not been established or documented, and the brokerage role for the particular listing still needs confirmation. No fee terms have been agreed. Lena is about to disclose her maximum offer. Ren can explain the questions that must be settled and obtain the applicable documents and broker guidance, but must not promise unrestricted advocacy, a standard commission, or confidentiality beyond the actual relationship and applicable duties.
Language workshop: Before the buyer shares a negotiating limit
Communication goal: Clarify representation, confidentiality, and compensation without assuming an agreement already exists.
Language guidance: Providing access or information does not, by itself, explain whom a licensee represents. Ask for the actual relationship and its duties. Labels such as dual agency or transaction broker cannot be assumed available or identical across jurisdictions.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): We should clarify whom the brokerage represents before discussing your strategy. / Please hold your maximum offer until we have clarified the relationship. / I need to confirm the brokerage's role for this listing. / Let us review the applicable agreement and disclosures. / The representation role and fee obligations are distinct questions. / Available arrangements and duties depend on the applicable rules. / We can explain the proposed terms before you decide whether to agree. / I will obtain the role information and broker guidance first. / The agreement should identify the services included. / What dates and properties does this term cover? / Let us check what the exclusivity provision actually restricts. / No compensation terms have been agreed in this conversation. / The documents need to explain who owes which payment under what conditions. / I cannot promise duties beyond the actual relationship and applicable requirements. / Which part of the proposed arrangement would you like clarified? / Does this explanation distinguish representation from payment?
Vocabulary: agency: A legal representation relationship defined by applicable law. / principal: The person or entity represented by an agent. / fiduciary duty: A duty of loyalty or care arising in a relationship under applicable law. / buyer representation: Acting for a buyer under an established relationship. / seller representation: Acting for a seller under an established relationship. / dual agency: Representation of both sides where permitted and subject to applicable conditions. / designated agency: An arrangement assigning different representatives within a brokerage where permitted. / non-agency relationship: A permitted service relationship without an agency role. / transaction broker: A professional facilitating a transaction under a jurisdiction's non-agency framework. / brokerage: The business through which real-estate services are provided. / managing broker: The broker responsible for supervision under the relevant structure. / representation agreement: A document setting out a representation relationship and terms. / listing agreement: An agreement for marketing or representing a property's seller. / exclusivity: A contractual restriction on using other representatives within a stated scope. / agreement term: The period during which an agreement operates. / service scope: The work included in the professional engagement. / compensation: Payment for professional services on agreed terms. / commission: A form of compensation often linked to a completed transaction. / fee obligation: A contractual responsibility to pay a specified fee. / informed consent: Agreement based on relevant information and understanding. / conflict of interest: A competing interest that may affect professional judgment or duties. / confidential information: Information subject to protection under applicable duties or terms. / material fact: A fact important to a transaction or decision in context. / termination provision: Terms governing how an agreement may end.
Speaking task: Check the cloze key, then read Lena and Ren's exchange. Pause before Lena's maximum-offer question; stress Ren's words that leave the relationship unresolved. Switch roles and reread turns 9-20. Keep service scope, exclusivity, compensation, and termination as separate questions; do not invent agreed terms. Complete the shorter agreement exchange and check its key. Read it with the proposed shorter term still unsigned and unaccepted.
Partner: Lena: Prospective buyer / Ren: Real-estate licensee
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A prospective seller receives a proposed service package and fee. They ask for a shorter term. The brokerage has not accepted the change, and the proposal is unsigned.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Lena: I would like time to read the terms, including when the agreement ends and what would happen if I wanted to stop working together.
Ren: We should review the termination provision as well as the duration. I can explain the proposal within my role and direct legal-interpretation questions to qualified counsel when needed.
Lena: Please send the proposed terms and confirm your office's role. I will wait to discuss my maximum offer until those questions are answered.
Ren: Correct. Any required informed consent must follow a clear explanation of the actual arrangement. Today I will obtain the missing information, not promise unrestricted advocacy before the relationship is settled.
END REFERENCE

Seller Rosa wants to call $480,000 a proven value; agent Dev must explain the comparison's limits.
Language workshop: Three sale prices do not make three equal comparables
