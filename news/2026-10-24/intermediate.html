📅 October 24, 2026 - France Prepares for Demonstrations After Severe Unrest

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

France is on high alert ahead of new nationwide demonstrations, a week after the country experienced severe unrest. The interior ministry plans to deploy 70,000 police to increase security in French cities. Opposition parties accused the government of failing to predict the scale of the recent violence. Hundreds of people have been jailed for vandalism and looting after rapid court hearings. Disillusionment is high among the public due to rising living costs and warnings about spending cuts. Several key cities have announced complete bans on public gatherings over the weekend. Despite these restrictions, huge crowds are still expected to gather in Paris and elsewhere. Meanwhile, some local businesses have boarded up their windows. This unrest has played out as France prepares for a highly unpredictable election period.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. unrest (noun): A state of angry dissatisfaction and disturbance in a group of people.
2. deploy (verb): To move police or military into a position so they are ready for action.
3. scale (noun): The size, level, or extent of something, especially when it is large.
4. vandalism (noun): The crime of intentionally damaging public or private property.
5. looting (noun): The activity of stealing things from shops or homes during a disturbance.
6. disillusionment (noun): A feeling of disappointment when discovering something is not as good as believed.
7. restrictions (noun): Rules or laws that limit what people are allowed to do.
8. unpredictable (adjective): Not able to be known or guessed before it happens.

Grammar Focus: Present Perfect Passive
The present perfect passive is formed with "have/has been" plus a past participle. It describes a completed action with a present result, often focusing on the person experiencing the action rather than who did it. In this example, the focus is on what happened to the people ("have been jailed"), rather than the specific authorities who locked them up.
Example from the text: "Hundreds of people have been jailed for vandalism and looting after rapid court hearings."
