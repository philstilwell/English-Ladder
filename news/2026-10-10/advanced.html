Read the story

Television presenter Alison Hammond recounted her alarming experience of feeling as though she was having a cardiac event while live on ITV's This Morning last week. She left the programme midway through the broadcast, visibly clutching her chest before being hospitalised for a medical assessment. She later discovered she had suffered a panic attack, an experience she described as one of the 'scariest things I've ever been through'. Returning to her weekly slot, Hammond explained she couldn't catch her breath, genuinely thought she was having a heart attack, and expressed immense gratitude for being present. She added that the physical sensation felt "very real" and exactly "like a heart attack". Hammond described the instantaneous onset of the panic attack as a sudden wave of adrenaline that overwhelmed her body. Overwhelmed by the sensation, she immediately ran off the set, something she stated she would never normally do on live television. This frightening episode has instigated a profound shift in her outlook, prompting her to re-evaluate life and prioritise her well-being. She likened the surreal feeling of the attack to entering the 'Upside Down world' from the television series Stranger Things. Despite initial apprehension, she deemed it crucial to return to work quickly, thereby avoiding associating the panic with the programme itself. She engaged in discussions with Williams regarding the demanding pressures of presenting live television. Dr Williams clarified that while panic attacks and heart attacks share some symptoms, they are distinct medical conditions. She explained that heart attack pain typically radiates to areas like the arm or jaw, and does not subside with grounding techniques. According to the MIND mental health charity, panic attacks manifest as an extreme anxiety response with rapidly developing physical and mental symptoms.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. recounted (verb): Narrated or told a story, especially a personal experience.
2. cardiac event (noun phrase): A medical incident involving the heart, such as a heart attack.
3. midway (adverb): In the middle of a process or period.
4. assessment (noun): The evaluation or estimation of the nature, quality, or ability of someone or something, often for medical purposes.
5. immense (adjective): Extremely large or great; often used to emphasize the scale of a feeling or quality.
6. gratitude (noun): The quality of being thankful; readiness to show appreciation for and to return kindness.
7. instantaneous (adjective): Occurring or done in an instant or immediately.
8. profound (adjective): (Of a state, quality, or emotion) very great or intense; (of a person or statement) having or showing great knowledge or insight.
9. outlook (noun): A person's point of view or general attitude to life.
10. likened (verb): Compared to something else, suggesting a similarity.
11. surreal (adjective): Having the bizarre or dreamlike quality of surrealism; strange, unreal, or fantastic.
12. crucial (adjective): Of great importance; critical.
13. associating (verb): Connecting or linking something with something else in one's mind.
14. demanding (adjective): Requiring much skill and effort; difficult.
15. distinct (adjective): Recognizably different in nature from something else of a similar type.

Advanced Grammar: Participle Clauses of Result
Participle clauses starting with "thereby" + present participle (-ing form) are used to show the result or consequence of the action described in the main clause. They create a concise and formal way to link cause and effect, often indicating a direct and intended outcome.
Example from the text: "Despite initial apprehension, she deemed it crucial to return to work quickly, thereby avoiding associating the panic with the programme itself."
