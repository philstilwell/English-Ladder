Read the story

Matthew Rhys, a Welsh performer, recently made Emmy history by winning two lead acting awards in one night. He received one award for a psychological thriller and another for a comedy series. Earlier in his career, Rhys was so desperate for employment that he thought about joining the British Army. However, an army officer was suspicious of him because he was a performer, and Rhys was later rejected. Years later, Rhys moved to Hollywood and lived with other Welsh performers, including Michael Sheen and Ioan Gruffudd. They would speak Welsh to each other and watch rugby games together in a British pub. Rhys is married to actress Keri Russell, and they have a son named Sam, whom Rhys hopes will understand the Welsh language. He also owns a 1939 wooden boat named Rarebit, which is also a traditional Welsh dish. Rhys's first language is Welsh, and he regularly uses it at home in New York. The passionate Welshman dedicated his second Emmy award to the great and good people of Wales. He said, "We are a small but mighty nation," expressing pride in his heritage. Rhys has also been revealed as the voice of Han Solo in a new Welsh language adaptation of Star Wars. This project, which required translating well-known phrases, brought up some unexpected feelings for Rhys.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. performer (noun): Someone who entertains an audience, for example, by acting, singing, or dancing.
2. desperate (adjective): Feeling or showing that you have little hope and are ready to do anything to change a difficult situation.
3. employment (noun): The state of having a job or working for an employer.
4. suspicious (adjective): Feeling or thinking that someone is probably guilty of something bad.
5. rejected (verb): To refuse to accept, use, or believe something or someone.
6. passionate (adjective): Showing or involving strong feelings or a strong belief.
7. dedicated (verb): To give all of your energy, time, etc. to something.
8. mighty (adjective): Very strong and powerful.
9. adaptation (noun): A film, book, play, etc., that has been made from another film, book, or play.
10. phrases (noun): A small group of words that stands together as a conceptual unit, but is not necessarily a complete sentence.

Grammar Focus: Relative Clauses with "which"
Relative clauses provide extra information about a noun. When referring to a thing or an idea, we can use the relative pronoun "which" to introduce these clauses. They help to connect ideas and make sentences more detailed without repeating information.
Example from the text: "He also owns a 1939 wooden boat named Rarebit, which is also a traditional Welsh dish."
