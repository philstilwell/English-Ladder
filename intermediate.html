📅 October 03, 2026 - Social Media and Live Shows: Are Spoilers Ruining the Experience?
Social media allows fans to share live show details instantly, which can ruin the element of surprise for others planning to attend later performances.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Important performances by famous musicians are often shared quickly on social media before many fans get to see them live. Long before attending a show, fans might already know about key moments planned by their favorite artists. However, it is hard to avoid a continuous flow of videos from live shows if you use social media. Recently, US pop star Olivia Rodrigo began her Unraveled tour, and key moments from her show were quickly discussed widely online. While some fans enjoy seeing what is coming, others go to great lengths to keep the element of surprise. For example, Aurélia Collins tries to stop her social media feed from showing content about her favorite musicians. Not all fans dislike spoilers; Georgia McAdam, for instance, actually enjoys them. In response, some artists change small parts of their shows each night to create new surprises for later audiences. Music editor Laura Snapes notes that artists also benefit from these changes. Phoebe Bridgers has even banned phones from her shows to ensure fans experience true surprise.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. performances (noun): Live presentations such as music shows, plays, or dances given in front of an audience.
2. attending (verb): Being present at an event, meeting, or institution; participating in something.
3. key (adjective): Extremely important or crucial; essential to understanding or achieving something.
4. continuous flow (noun phrase): A steady, uninterrupted stream or movement of something, like information or videos.
5. live shows (noun phrase): Public events where performers entertain an audience directly.
6. discussed widely (adverb phrase): Talked about by many people in many places, making the topic well-known.
7. go to great lengths (verb phrase): To make a great or special effort to achieve something, often involving difficulty or much work.
8. feed (noun): A stream of content, like posts or updates, that you see on a social media platform, often personalized for you.

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action or the receiver of the action, rather than the performer. It is formed with a form of the verb 'to be' (e.g., 'is,' 'are,' 'was,' 'were,' 'been,' 'being') and the past participle of the main verb. For example, in the sentence 'key moments from her show were quickly discussed widely online,' the focus is on the 'key moments' and the action 'discussed,' rather than who did the discussing.
Example from the text: "Recently, US pop star Olivia Rodrigo began her Unraveled tour, and key moments from her show were quickly discussed widely online."

📅 October 02, 2026 - Pilot's Heroism on Flydubai Flight During Co-pilot Attack
An Indian pilot was stabbed by his co-pilot during a Flydubai flight but bravely opened the cockpit door for help, saving over 170 passengers.

Read the story

Capt Smit Machchhar, an Indian pilot, was stabbed by his co-pilot during a Flydubai flight. Despite his injuries, Machchhar was determined not to let others die and opened the cockpit door for help. He realized the plane, carrying over 170 passengers, was sinking and knew he had to act. Flydubai's flight FZ1073, bound for Tel Aviv from Dubai, plunged over 17,000 feet during its journey. The plane sent an emergency signal to air traffic controllers, then switched to a code indicating hijacking. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu reported that one pilot stabbed the other, apparently trying to crash the plane. After passengers overpowered the attacker, two reserve pilots onboard safely landed the plane in Saudi Arabia. Machchhar, hailed as a "true hero" by Netanyahu, is now recovering in a stable condition in Abu Dhabi. Machchhar said he views his wound "as a journey which is slowly going on and will take time." He is confident that his recovery will happen and he will become fit and healthy again.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. determined (adjective): having made a firm decision and being resolved not to change it.
2. sinking (verb (present participle)): going down to a lower level, often quickly and unexpectedly.
3. plunged (verb (past tense)): dropped or fell very quickly and suddenly.
4. emergency signal (noun phrase): a sign or message sent to ask for help in a dangerous or critical situation.
5. indicating (verb (present participle)): showing or pointing out something; serving as a sign of something.
6. hijacking (noun): the illegal seizure of a vehicle, especially an aircraft, while it is traveling.
7. overpowered (verb (past tense)): gained control over someone or something by using greater strength or force.
8. hailed (verb (past tense)): publicly praised someone or something as being excellent or special.

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action or the receiver of the action, rather than the person or thing performing the action. It is often formed with a form of the verb 'to be' (e.g., is, was, has been) followed by the past participle of the main verb. For example, instead of 'The co-pilot stabbed Machchhar,' we can say 'Machchhar was stabbed by the co-pilot' to emphasize Machchhar.
Example from the text: "Capt Smit Machchhar, an Indian pilot, was stabbed by his co-pilot during a Flydubai flight."

Consumers are feeling the pinch from the rising cost of protein. Amarpreet Singh has been drinking protein shakes since he was 16. Amarpreet says a 4.5kg bag of the whey protein he buys cost around £60 in 2020. By last year, its price had gone up to £120. Fourteen years later, shakes are still part of his routine, but they have become considerably more expensive. Over the years, protein powders and shakes have attracted a much broader customer base. Evie Lewis began using protein powder because she felt she wasn't getting enough protein in her diet. At £32.99 for a 420g tub, Evie is feeling the high price. She admits, "I've been rationing it and using less than what's suggested." The growing appetite for protein is running up against a problem: supply has not kept pace. Manufacturers say that increasing production takes time and is costly, as producing high-protein whey requires specialist processing and drying equipment. Some companies are starting to adapt what they make by changing recipes or looking for cheaper sources of protein. For students like Evie, managing on a much tighter budget, buying protein is a different story. She says that now she is at university, protein is not a necessity right now. One protein user explains, "If you're doing exercise and not taking any, you feel like you wouldn't be recovering properly."

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. feeling the pinch (idiom): experiencing financial difficulty because you have less money than before
2. considerably (adverb): much or a lot; to a large degree
3. routine (noun): a regular way of doing things in a particular order
4. customer base (noun phrase): all the people who buy or use a particular product or service
5. rationing (verb): controlling the amount of something that people can have or use, often because there is not enough of it
6. appetite (noun): a strong desire or need for something, in this context, demand for protein products
7. kept pace (verb phrase): moved or developed at the same speed as something else; to keep up
8. specialist (adjective): having or involving expert knowledge or training in a particular area
9. adapt (verb): to change something or yourself to suit a new situation
10. necessity (noun): something that you must have or do, or that is very important

Grammar Focus: Present Conditionals (Type 0 and 1)
We use present conditionals to talk about general truths, habits, or likely outcomes in the present or future. The 'if' clause describes the condition, usually in the present simple or present continuous. The main clause describes the result, which can be in the present simple or use a modal verb to express a feeling or possibility. For example, 'If you're doing exercise and not taking any,' describes a present, ongoing condition.
Example from the text: "One protein user explains, "If you're doing exercise and not taking any, you feel like you wouldn't be recovering properly.""
