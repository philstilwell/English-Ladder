TASK: Retrieve occupational vocabulary
1. Select four terms from the reference. Explain each in plain English, give two natural collocations and quote or create a short workplace example. Label invented examples. Contrast each term with a plausible confusable term; acknowledge regional variation.
2. Present one role-labelled exchange with one missing word or phrase and four choices A-D. Exactly one choice must fit the stated facts and grammar. Stop and wait for my answer before revealing it.
3. Explain all four options, then invite me to read the completed exchange aloud. Offer a sentence frame for optional spoken practice, never an open-ended writing assignment.
4. Repeat for the other three terms, one question at a time. Vary the correct letter and the situation; preserve the reference facts. If a choice is ambiguous, replace the item.
5. Finish with two new four-choice collocation questions for later review, with a clearly separate answer key. Do not claim to schedule a reminder.

Lesson 01: Onboarding and Implementation Expectations
Original fictional case: Customer lead Omar expects a launch on Friday, 9 October. The export owner and training slot are still unassigned. Customer success manager Lena cannot confirm readiness. The agreed launch conditions are an approved customer export, a successful test import, user acceptance of the main workflow, and completed administrator training. The customer owns data preparation; the supplier owns import mapping and training delivery. No one has yet confirmed that the required people are available. A readiness review is scheduled for Thursday at 15:00 UTC.
Language workshop: Turn a hoped-for launch into a shared readiness plan
Communication goal: Clarify owners, dependencies, and acceptance checks before confirming a customer launch date.
Language guidance: A target date is not evidence of readiness. The four stated conditions are export approval, a successful test import, workflow acceptance, and administrator training. The Thursday review checks those conditions; holding the meeting alone does not satisfy them.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): Which workflow must the team be able to complete on launch day? / Friday is the target, but readiness is not yet confirmed. / The test import depends on an approved customer export. / Your team prepares the data; our team maps the agreed fields. / Who can own the export and confirm its availability? / Has that person accepted the task and the timing? / We need users to complete the agreed workflow successfully. / Who will attend the administrator training? / A calendar invitation does not mean the training has happened. / Let us review readiness on Thursday at fifteen hundred UTC. / Once the approved export is available, we can assess the import schedule. / Use the agreed secure transfer route and only the required fields. / What support coverage is agreed for the initial operating period? / The export owner and training slot are still open. / Confirm launch only after the agreed conditions are met. / I will update the plan with each owner, dependency, and confirmed date.
Vocabulary: onboarding: The process of preparing a customer to use a service effectively. / implementation: Work to configure, connect, and introduce a solution. / kickoff: The initial meeting aligning purpose, people, and approach. / success plan: A shared record of outcomes, actions, ownership, and measures. / target date: A desired date whose commitment status must be specified. / go-live: The start of operational use under agreed conditions. / dependency: A task or condition on which another step relies. / prerequisite: Something required before a particular activity can proceed. / data migration: Moving data between systems with appropriate checks. / source export: Data extracted from the originating system. / data mapping: Specifying how source fields correspond to destination fields. / data owner: The role accountable for the relevant data and its use. / test import: A controlled trial of bringing data into the destination. / UAT: User acceptance testing; users checking the solution against agreed needs. / acceptance criterion: A defined condition used to judge whether work is acceptable. / administrator: A user with specified management permissions in the service. / training slot: A scheduled period with the required trainer and participants. / readiness review: A check of whether the conditions for the next stage are met. / cutover: The coordinated transition into a new operational arrangement. / rollback plan: An approved approach for reversing a change if required. / hypercare: A defined period of enhanced support after introduction. / handover: Transfer of responsibility with the necessary information. / RACI: Responsible, accountable, consulted, and informed; a role-assignment framework. / open dependency: A required preceding task or condition not yet satisfied.
Speaking task: Read Omar and Lena aloud, then swap roles. Explain customer data preparation and supplier mapping as separate, coordinated responsibilities. Check all ten gaps. Reread the four launch conditions and Thursday fifteen-hundred UTC review without substituting one completed task for another. Repeat the conditional announcement and ownership close, retaining the unassigned people and training slot as open work.
Partner: Omar: Customer implementation lead / Lena: Customer success manager
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A Monday launch remains a target. Customer lead Farah owns source-data approval. Supplier lead Ben owns import mapping. Training is complete, but the test import has failed. The readiness review is Friday at 10:00 UTC.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Omar: Please also make clear who supports the team after launch. They should not have to guess which contact to use.
Lena: We will confirm the handover and initial support arrangements. Coverage and contacts need to be explicit, with any enhanced support period defined rather than assumed to continue indefinitely.
Omar: I will confirm the data lead and user testers, check their availability, and send a conditional update to the team.
Lena: I will update the success plan with the owners, dependencies, and actual status. At Thursday's review, we will decide from the evidence against all four conditions, not from the date alone.
END REFERENCE

START PROMPT
TASK: Interactive workplace role-play
1. Use the supplied case or dialogue as the starting situation. Give a two-sentence briefing and offer two relevant professional roles for me to choose from. For a supplied dialogue, use its actual roles. Ask which role I want, then stop and wait. Do not write my replies.
2. After I choose, identify who you will play and the immediate communication goal. Play the other professional; where a meeting requires a third person, label each of your speakers clearly. Start with one natural workplace turn and wait for my reply. Keep most turns to one to three sentences and ask no more than one question at a time.
3. Let the exchange develop across six to ten learner turns, or end earlier if I type "feedback". Respond to what I actually say. Include a plausible clarification, disagreement or tradeoff without silently changing the starting facts. Mark any added constraint as a fictional second-round variation. Do not resolve approvals, evidence or commitments that remain uncertain.
4. Stay in role during the exchange. If my meaning is unclear, ask for clarification naturally. Give a hint only if I ask or cannot proceed. Do not deliver a model conversation in advance.
5. At the debrief, quote two of my phrases: one successful choice and one worth improving. Check factual accuracy, language, register and whether the next step was clear. Give up to three focused suggestions. Ask me to retry the weakest turn; wait. Only then offer an alternative wording and a harder replay.
Use voice replies when available. If I prefer selections, offer four plausible spoken replies and explain my choice before continuing. Do not require written compositions.

Language workshop: Offer the verified workaround without inventing a fix date
Communication goal: Communicate confirmed impact, temporary relief, unresolved questions, and the next update under pressure.
