Anthropic Blocks AI Attempts to Create Biological Weapons

📅 September 11, 2026 - Anthropic Blocks AI Attempts to Create Biological Weapons

Read the story

Anthropic has identified and disrupted attempts to use its AI model for "malicious activity" linked to biological weapons. The company's threat intelligence report includes revelations about these dangerous activities. This follows warnings from a former top researcher about the potential risks of AI to humanity. Claude, Anthropic's AI model, was also used by actors linked to a Russia-based cyber espionage campaign. Other cases of concern ranged from fake dating apps to surveillance built to identify dissidents. Anthropic also blocked scientists who used its AI in ways that could support biological weapons development. Biological misuse is considered "one of the most serious risks of frontier AI model" because it "could have catastrophic consequences" without proper safeguards. The company noted that the same information used for biological weapons can also develop vaccines or disease cures. Anthropic is incorporating these findings into its processes to better prevent and disrupt similar activities in the future.

Words and grammar

1. identified (verb): recognized or found something that exists.
2. disrupted (verb): interrupted something or caused it to stop working normally.
3. malicious (adjective): intending or intended to cause harm or trouble.
4. revelations (noun): surprising or important facts that are revealed.
5. potential (adjective): possible in the future, but not yet real.
6. dissidents (noun): people who disagree strongly with official policies, especially in a country with strict control.
7. safeguards (noun): measures taken to protect something or someone from harm or danger.
8. catastrophic (adjective): causing sudden, great damage or suffering.
9. incorporating (verb): including something as part of a larger whole.

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action itself and the object of the action, rather than the subject performing the action. We often use it when we don't know who performed the action, or when the actor is less important than the action or the receiver of the action. It is formed with a form of "to be" + the past participle. For example, in the sentence "The ball was kicked by the boy," the action "was kicked" is more important, or the receiver "the ball" is the focus, not "the boy" (the actor).
Example from the text: "Claude, Anthropic's AI model, was also used by actors linked to a Russia-based cyber espionage campaign."
