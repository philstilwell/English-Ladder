Your goal

Compare options using the same criteria and state the tradeoff.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

Two product ranges have similar sales revenue, but one sells through more slowly and requires larger markdowns.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

assortment
The selection of products offered by a retailer.
SKU
Stock keeping unit: an identifier for a distinct inventory item.
sell-through
The proportion of available stock sold during a defined period using a stated calculation.
gross margin
Revenue minus cost of goods sold, expressed as an amount or a percentage of revenue.

03 · Notice the language

Explain a fair comparison

Use the same time period, scope, and measurement basis for both options. Explain a difference with 'whereas' or 'while'. State what improves and what is given up; do not hide the weaker side of a recommendation.

  • Both options ..., but ... .
  • Option A ..., whereas option B ... .
  • The comparison depends on ... .

Improve this: This option is more better.

See a clearer version

This option is better on cost, but slower to deliver.

'Better' already has comparative meaning, so it does not need 'more'. Naming the criterion and tradeoff makes the comparison useful.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence expresses a clear contrast?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Option A is faster, whereas option B costs less.

  1. A. Option A is faster, so option B costs less. 'So' implies one fact causes the other.
  2. B. Option A is faster, whereas option B costs less. 'Whereas' marks a contrast between two options.
  3. C. Option A is faster because option B costs less. 'Because' claims a causal connection that is not given.
2. A rate increases from 6% to 7%. What is the absolute difference?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. An increase of one percentage point.

  1. A. An increase of one percentage point. Subtracting 6% from 7% gives one percentage point. The relative increase is about 16.7%.
  2. B. An increase of one percent relative to the original rate. A one-percent relative increase on 6% would be 6.06%, not 7%.
  3. C. An increase of seven percentage points. The new rate is 7%; the difference between the rates is one percentage point.

02Pricing, Promotions, and MarginMatch certainty to evidence · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Separate observations, assumptions, and conclusions.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A promotion increases units sold by 20%, while the selling price falls by 15%. A team member assumes profit must have increased.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

markdown
A reduction in a product's selling price, often to improve stock movement.
promotion
An activity intended to increase attention or sales, sometimes using a temporary price offer.
gross margin
Revenue minus cost of goods sold, expressed as an amount or a percentage of revenue.
cannibalization
Sales of one offering replacing sales of another offering from the same business.

03 · Notice the language

Match certainty to evidence

Say what the evidence shows, identify what is still unknown, and limit the conclusion to that evidence. Words such as 'may', 'suggests', and 'in this sample' are useful when the uncertainty is real. Do not soften an urgent, verified safety concern.

  • The available evidence shows ... .
  • This may indicate ..., but ... .
  • We have not yet established whether ... .

Improve this: The pilot proves this will work everywhere.

See a clearer version

The pilot worked in the tested setting; performance elsewhere remains untested.

The revision states the finding and its scope. It avoids turning limited evidence into a universal claim.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence preserves uncertainty about an unconfirmed cause?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. The change may have contributed to the delay.

  1. A. The change may have contributed to the delay. 'May have contributed' presents a possibility without claiming the cause is established.
  2. B. The change must have caused the delay. 'Must have' expresses a strong inference that the evidence may not support.
  3. C. The change definitely caused the delay. 'Definitely' presents the cause as certain.
2. A result comes from a small pilot in one location. Which phrase accurately limits the claim?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. In this pilot, the observed result was ...

  1. A. In similar locations, we can now expect the same result ... Similarity alone does not establish that the result will transfer.
  2. B. Across the service, the observed result was ... A pilot in one location does not establish a service-wide observation.
  3. C. In this pilot, the observed result was ... This locates the finding in the setting actually studied.

allocation

replenishment
Replacing stock that has been used or sold.
stockout
A situation in which an item is unavailable when it is needed.
inventory turn
How often inventory is sold or used over a stated period, based on a defined calculation.

03 · Notice the language

Give a useful status update

Lead with the current state, then the important change or open item. Use completed-action language only for completed work. Add a next update point when it is known; do not invent a date to make the message sound complete.

  • We have completed ...; ... remains open.
  • As of ..., the status is ... .
  • The next update will cover ... .

Improve this: Everything is progressing well.

See a clearer version

Three checks are complete; two remain open and are being reviewed.

The revision gives observable progress. A positive label alone does not tell the reader what is complete or still needed.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence says a review is finished?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. The team has completed the review.

  1. A. The team is completing the review. 'Is completing' presents the work as in progress.
  2. B. The team plans to complete the review. 'Plans to complete' describes an intention, not completion.
  3. C. The team has completed the review. 'Has completed' states that the action is finished.
2. Which update distinguishes a plan from a confirmed fact?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Delivery is planned for Friday; the carrier has not confirmed it.

  1. A. The delivery plan proves that arrival will be Friday. A plan cannot prove a future arrival time.
  2. B. Delivery is planned for Friday; the carrier has not confirmed it. This explicitly labels the date as planned and identifies the missing confirmation.
  3. C. Delivery is confirmed for Friday because we planned it. A plan does not establish carrier confirmation.

Vocabulary for this lesson

vendor scorecard
A set of defined measures used to review a supplier's performance.
SLA
Service-level agreement defining expected service performance or response commitments.
defect rate
The proportion of items counted as defective under a stated definition and denominator.

03 · Notice the language

Ask for an actionable next step

State the action and the information needed. Explain why it matters and specify a deadline only when one is given or agreed. 'Could you' is polite; repeated apologies can obscure a legitimate request. Urgent situations may require a direct request.

  • Could you provide ... so that ...?
  • Please confirm ... by [agreed time].
  • If that timing is not possible, please let me know ... .

Improve this: Please revert soonest with the necessary.

See a clearer version

Could you send the missing document and confirm when it will be available?

The revision names the document and requested response. It avoids expressions that may be unfamiliar or ambiguous for an international audience.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. What does 'Please send it by Thursday' normally mean?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. Send it no later than Thursday.

  1. A. Send it on Thursday, but not earlier. By Thursday permits earlier delivery; it is not restricted to that day.
  2. B. Keep sending it regularly until Thursday. This interprets a deadline as a continuing activity, which would use until.
  3. C. Send it no later than Thursday. 'By' sets the latest point, while 'after' refers to a later time.
2. Which request gives the recipient enough detail to act?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review?

  1. A. Could you confirm that you received my earlier message? This checks receipt, not which version is required for the review.
  2. B. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review? The requested action and information are explicit.
  3. C. Could you confirm that the review is important? This checks importance rather than identifying the needed document version.

Offer a workable tradeoff

Acknowledge the goal and explain the available choices. Connect an offer to its actual conditions. A useful response makes room for discussion without promising approval or resources you do not control.

  • Would you prefer ... or ...?

Improve this: If you will approve the scope, we can assess the schedule.

See a clearer version

If you approve the scope, we can assess the schedule.

For an ordinary future condition, the if-clause uses present tense. The other clause can express the future consequence or possibility.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence presents a normal future condition clearly?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. If the scope changes, we will review the schedule.

  1. A. If the scope changes, we reviewed the schedule. Reviewed places the response in the past instead of expressing the future response.
  2. B. If the scope changes, we will review the schedule. The if-clause uses present tense; the main clause states the future response.
  3. C. If the scope will change, we will review the schedule. For this ordinary future condition, use present simple in the if-clause.
2. Which response negotiates a new request without silently promising extra work?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. We can assess the addition and explain its effect on the agreed scope.

  1. A. We can assess the addition and explain its effect on the agreed scope. This offers a useful next step while keeping the approval and scope question open.
  2. B. We have included it, although its scope has not been reviewed. It treats unreviewed work as already included.
  3. C. We guarantee the original date without assessing the addition. It promises an outcome without examining the consequences.
