📅 October 31, 2026 - Romesh Ranganathan and Sebastian Croft Win Celebrity Traitors

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The second series of BBC One's The Celebrity Traitors reached its final on Friday. For four weeks, famous contestants faced suspense and suspicion as they tried to win through clever thinking. More than 15 million people have been watching this series. The group of 21 original contestants was reduced to seven before the final. Comedian Romesh Ranganathan and actor Sebastian Croft became the two winners, both belonging to the team called the faithful. The faithful team succeeded in removing the remaining traitors, Maya Jama and King Kenny, and the winners shared £83,000 for charity. During the final stage, contestants gathered around a fire pit to decide whether any traitors were still in the game. After a tied vote between James Blunt and Kenny, Croft persuaded Ranganathan to vote Kenny out, securing victory for the faithful. James then chose another round of voting and was removed himself, leaving Ranganathan and Croft as the final two. James could have shared the win if he had chosen to finish the game, as Ranganathan and Croft did. The winners will divide their money between two refugee charities, the Refugee Council and Choose Love.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. suspense (noun): An excited or worried feeling while waiting to discover what will happen.
2. suspicion (noun): A feeling that someone may be dishonest or doing something wrong.
3. contestants (plural noun): People who take part in a competition.
4. reduced (verb, past participle): Made smaller in size or number.
5. remaining (adjective): Still present after others have left or been removed.
6. persuaded (verb, past tense): Caused someone to agree to do something by giving reasons or discussing it.
7. round (noun): One stage of an activity that can happen several times, such as voting.
8. tied vote (noun phrase): A vote in which two choices receive the same number of votes.

Grammar Focus: Past simple passive
Use “was” or “were” followed by a past participle to focus on what happened to someone or something. For example: “The group of 21 original contestants was reduced to seven before the final.” Here, “was reduced” focuses on the change in the group's size without naming who caused it.
Example from the text: "The group of 21 original contestants was reduced to seven before the final."
