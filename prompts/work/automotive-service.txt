TASK: Retrieve occupational vocabulary
1. Select four terms from the reference. Explain each in plain English, give two natural collocations and quote or create a short workplace example. Label invented examples. Contrast each term with a plausible confusable term; acknowledge regional variation.
2. Present one role-labelled exchange with one missing word or phrase and four choices A-D. Exactly one choice must fit the stated facts and grammar. Stop and wait for my answer before revealing it.
3. Explain all four options, then invite me to read the completed exchange aloud. Offer a sentence frame for optional spoken practice, never an open-ended writing assignment.
4. Repeat for the other three terms, one question at a time. Vary the correct letter and the situation; preserve the reference facts. If a choice is ambiguous, replace the item.
5. Finish with two new four-choice collocation questions for later review, with a clearly separate answer key. Do not claim to schedule a reminder.

Lesson 01: Capturing the customer's concern
Original fictional case: Two household cars are listed on the account, but today Mina has booked the blue hatchback. She reports an occasional rattle from the rear after the car has been parked overnight. She does not know the cause, and no technician assessment has occurred. Advisor Ellis must confirm the vehicle, preserve the reported sound and conditions, and read back the concern. Mina is describing existing observations; she is not asked to drive or perform a test to reproduce the sound.
Language workshop: The blue hatchback and an occasional rear rattle
Communication goal: Identify the booked vehicle and capture an intermittent customer concern without inventing its frequency, cause, or technical finding.
Language guidance: The booked vehicle is the blue hatchback, not both cars on the account. The sound is occasional and reported from the rear after overnight parking. Do not change those details to a constant noise or a named failed component.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): Which of the two cars is booked today? / I have the blue hatchback for this appointment. / I will not attach this concern to the other vehicle. / How have you described the sound you noticed? / You report a rattle from the rear. / It is occasional, not constant. / You have noticed it after the car was parked overnight. / You have not identified what causes it. / No technician assessment has taken place yet. / I will record rattle without naming a failed component. / The customer reports the following concern. / This description does not establish whether the vehicle is fault-free. / A model name and a vehicle identification number are different. / Does that wording accurately describe your experience? / Blue hatchback; occasional rear rattle after overnight parking; cause unknown. / I will put that concern on the job card for assessment.
Vocabulary: service advisor: Staff member coordinating customer communication and workshop work. / customer concern: Issue described by the vehicle owner or user. / vehicle identification: Information establishing which vehicle a record concerns. / service interval: Specified time or accumulated-distance basis for scheduled maintenance, subject to the actual vehicle schedule. / hatchback: Car body style with an upward-opening rear door to the cargo area. / booking reference: Identifier for a scheduled service appointment. / tire aspect ratio: Nominal sidewall height expressed as a percentage of nominal section width in a tire-size designation. / vehicle identification number: Unique vehicle identifier commonly abbreviated VIN. / load index: Tire rating code associated with load capacity, not a literal weight in kilograms. / model: Named vehicle design within a manufacturer's range. / state of charge: Battery charge level expressed as a percentage on the applicable battery system's basis. / odometer: Instrument recording accumulated vehicle distance. / intermittent: Occurring at intervals rather than continuously. / rattle: Repeated light knocking or vibrating sound. / rear: Part or direction toward the back of the vehicle. / overnight parking: Period during which a vehicle remains parked through the night. / reported conditions: Circumstances described as accompanying the concern. / frequency: How often a symptom is reported to occur. / total toe: Combined toe value for the two wheels on an axle under the report's sign convention. / symptom description: Account of the experienced sound, behavior, or condition. / technician assessment: Relevant professional evaluation of the concern. / angular minute: One sixtieth of a degree, not one hundredth of a degree. / job card: Workshop record containing the requested work and relevant details. / concern readback: Repetition of the customer's account to confirm accuracy.
Speaking task: Read the concern in the brief, then complete the dialogue using the supplied word bank. Read Mina's turns aloud, stressing occasional, rear, and after overnight parking. Then read Ellis's matching readback. Complete the four-line transfer and check each answer against the explained key. Keep the vehicle and reported conditions unchanged.
Partner: Mina: Customer / Ellis: Service advisor
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: Complete the intake summary. Preserve the correct car, sound, location, and reported condition.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Mina: Yes, that readback matches. No fixed frequency, and no part identified.
Ellis: Here is the concern readback: customer reports an occasional rear rattle in the blue hatchback after overnight parking. Cause unknown; no technician assessment has occurred.
Mina: Thank you. That's what I wanted recorded, without making the sound seem constant.
Ellis: I will retain that customer concern for the workshop handoff. The next assessment can build on an accurate description instead of starting from an assumed cause.
END REFERENCE

Give up to three focused suggestions. Ask me to retry the weakest turn; wait. Only then offer an alternative wording and a harder replay.
Use voice replies when available. If I prefer selections, offer four plausible spoken replies and explain my choice before continuing. Do not require written compositions.
