Protein Users Squeezed as Costs Soar: The Rise of 'Proteinflation'

Advanced · C1+

📅 September 24, 2026 - Protein Users Squeezed as Costs Soar: The Rise of 'Proteinflation'

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Consumers are experiencing significant financial pressure due to the escalating cost of protein supplements. Amarpreet Singh, who has consumed protein shakes since age 16, has witnessed a substantial increase in prices over fourteen years. A 4.5kg bag of whey protein he purchased for around £60 in 2020 had soared to £120 by last year, though he secured one for less via eBay. Among his gym peers, this upward trend in protein costs has colloquially been termed "proteinflation." Over recent years, protein powders have attracted a considerably wider demographic, with food manufacturers diversifying into high-protein versions of various everyday items. Nineteen-year-old student Evie Lewis recently started using protein powder as she believed her diet lacked sufficient protein. She finds the £32.99 price for a 420g tub prohibitive, leading her to conserve the product by using less than the recommended amount. Max Longman, a long-term user, notes his monthly expenditure on whey protein has climbed from approximately £45 to £60 within the last year. The burgeoning demand for protein is exacerbated by new weight-loss drugs, whose users are often advised to maintain adequate protein intake to preserve muscle mass. This surging appetite, however, is encountering a significant obstacle: supply has not grown sufficiently to meet the heightened consumption. Consequently, wholesale prices for crucial ingredients have sharply escalated, intensifying the pressure on consumers. For instance, the European wholesale price of a common high-protein whey type was 130% higher in late August compared to the previous year, and more than four times its value three years ago. Manufacturers acknowledge that increasing production is both time-consuming and capital-intensive, particularly as high-protein whey processing demands specialized equipment. Some companies, like UK sports nutrition firm Warrior, are exploring alternative protein sources to mitigate production costs. Kieran Fisher, Warrior's founder, indicated that their whey protein powder's shelf price rose from £12.50 to £17.99, yet the company still subsidises it. While wholesale prices for high-protein whey have stabilised recently, they remain substantially elevated compared to several years prior. Despite these increases, committed users like Max view protein as increasingly vital for post-exercise recovery, making it a widespread staple. Conversely, students like Evie, operating on stringent budgets, are less inclined to continue purchasing it, deeming it a dispensable item for now. Amarpreet considers continued protein consumption a luxury and is seeking a more affordable brand, though he expects to pay around £100 per bag.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. escalating (adjective): Increasing rapidly in intensity, amount, or size; becoming more severe or serious.
2. substantial (adjective): Of considerable importance, size, or worth; significantly large.
3. soared (verb): Increased very quickly to a high level, often used for prices, temperatures, or amounts.
4. colloquially (adverb): In a way that is used in ordinary or familiar conversation; informally.
5. demographic (noun): A particular sector of a population, especially one defined by age, sex, income, etc.
6. diversifying (verb): Expanding the range of products, services, or activities, often to reduce risk or increase opportunities.
7. prohibitive (adjective): So high or expensive as to prevent or discourage people from buying or doing something.
8. conserve (verb): To protect something from harm or destruction, or to use something sparingly so as not to deplete it.
9. expenditure (noun): The act of spending or using money; an amount of money spent.
10. burgeoning (adjective): Beginning to grow or increase rapidly; flourishing.
11. exacerbated (verb (past participle)): Made a problem, bad situation, or negative feeling worse.
12. mitigate (verb): To make something less severe, serious, or painful; to lessen the gravity of an offence or mistake.
13. subsidises (verb): Supports an organisation or activity financially, often by covering part of the costs.
14. stringent (adjective): Strict, precise, and exacting; especially of regulations, requirements, or conditions.
15. dispensable (adjective): Able to be replaced or done without; not essential.

Advanced Grammar: Relative Pronoun 'Whose' for Possession
The relative pronoun 'whose' is used to indicate possession and connect a subordinate clause to a noun in the main clause. It can refer to people, animals, or things, and often introduces a non-restrictive clause, providing additional, non-essential information about the noun it modifies, as seen in the example referring to the users of weight-loss drugs.
Example from the text: "The burgeoning demand for protein is exacerbated by new weight-loss drugs, whose users are often advised to maintain adequate protein intake to preserve muscle mass."
