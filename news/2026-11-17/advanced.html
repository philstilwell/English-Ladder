Robert Pattinson Named Bond as Amazon Gains Creative Control

Advanced · C1+

📅 November 17, 2026 - Robert Pattinson Named Bond as Amazon Gains Creative Control

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Robert Pattinson, known for Twilight and The Batman, has been selected as the next James Bond. Amazon MGM Studios revealed the casting in a social media post accompanied by an image of Pattinson as 007. The 40-year-old will become the seventh actor to portray Bond, with the next film expected in 2028. Denis Villeneuve will direct and Steven Knight will write the film, although its title and exact release date remain unannounced. The casting follows years of speculation surrounding one of entertainment’s most highly scrutinised roles. Amazon MGM chose an established, bankable star who is widely regarded as a safe pair of hands. Pattinson’s fearlessness, self-deprecating wit and ability to reinvent himself were praised as qualities that suit him to Bond. Although reports suggested that his agreement committed him to three films, that detail has not been officially confirmed. Pattinson first rose to prominence as Cedric Diggory in the 2005 film Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire. His five Twilight films alongside Kristen Stewart collectively earned more than $3 billion worldwide. He has also earned respect for taking roles in smaller productions, including experimental and arthouse films. In 2025, Barbara Broccoli and Michael G Wilson agreed to sell creative control of the franchise to Amazon MGM. The Broccoli family had controlled the series since 1962, when Sean Connery first appeared as Bond. For decades, the family protected the character’s integrity while resisting spin-offs and licensing. Some industry experts expect these forms of expansion to follow Amazon’s takeover. If the next Bond film opens in 2028 as expected, about seven years will have passed since No Time To Die by the time it is released, making this the longest gap in the franchise’s history.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. highly scrutinised (adjective phrase): Examined closely and critically, with considerable attention paid to choices and performance.
2. bankable (adjective): Considered likely to attract audiences and generate revenue, especially through an established reputation.
3. safe pair of hands (idiomatic noun phrase): Someone trusted to handle an important responsibility competently and reliably.
4. self-deprecating wit (noun phrase): Clever humour that gently makes fun of the speaker’s own qualities or limitations.
5. reinvent himself (verb phrase): Develop a substantially different professional identity or approach instead of remaining associated with one established image.
6. arthouse (adjective): Describing films that emphasise artistic expression or unconventional approaches rather than broad commercial appeal.
7. creative control (noun phrase): Authority over artistic decisions, such as how characters and stories are developed.
8. franchise (noun): A connected series of films together with its recurring characters, identity and associated commercial rights.
9. integrity (noun): The preservation of a character’s essential qualities and consistency.
10. spin-offs (plural noun): New productions developed from an existing series, often focusing on a related character or setting.
11. licensing (uncountable noun): The practice of granting others permission to use protected characters, names or other material commercially.

Advanced Grammar: Future perfect with a future time clause
Use the future perfect, “will have” plus a past participle, to view a completed period from a future point. After “by the time”, use the present simple for that future point. Here, the approximate seven-year interval depends on the film opening in 2028 as expected; it is not a confirmed release date.
Example from the text: "If the next Bond film opens in 2028 as expected, about seven years will have passed since No Time To Die by the time it is released, making this the longest gap in the franchise’s history."
