📅 October 13, 2026 - Breaking Bad actor RJ Mitte dies at age 34

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

US actor RJ Mitte, who was best known for playing the defiant son of a drug kingpin in the television drama Breaking Bad, has died at the age of 34. The local police department confirmed his death, stating that no foul play is suspected and their investigation remains ongoing. Like his character on the show, Mitte lived with cerebral palsy and drew on his own personal experience for the role. His representative described the young man as a beloved philanthropist and a passionate advocate for disability rights. Mitte used his fame to challenge stereotypes and promote greater representation for people with health conditions. Bryan Cranston, who played his on-screen father, posted a tribute expressing deep despair over the tragic news. Cranston noted that the teen was quite raw when they started shooting the pilot episode in 2007, but he gradually grew into a fine performer. After achieving sudden fame, Mitte continued to fight for more varied roles and complex depictions of characters with disabilities on screen.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. defiant (adjective): Proudly refusing to obey authority or accept something you disagree with.
2. kingpin (noun): The most important or powerful person in a specific organization, especially a criminal one.
3. foul play (noun): Criminal violence or murder, especially when investigated by police.
4. ongoing (adjective): Continuing to exist, happen, or develop right now.
5. advocate (noun): Someone who publicly supports and works for a particular cause or policy.
6. stereotypes (noun): Fixed, very simple, and often wrong ideas about a particular type of person.
7. tribute (noun): A public message or action showing respect, gratitude, and admiration for someone.
8. depictions (noun): Ways that someone or something is shown or described in stories, art, or films.

Grammar Focus: Non-defining relative clauses
We use non-defining relative clauses to give extra, non-essential information about a person or thing. They are separated from the main sentence by commas. In this sentence, the clause starting with 'who' gives extra background about Bryan Cranston, but it is not strictly required to identify who he is.
Example from the text: "Bryan Cranston, who played his on-screen father, posted a tribute expressing deep despair over the tragic news."
