📅 September 22, 2026 - Rare Harper Lee First Edition Found in Oxfam Shop Goes to British Library

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

A first edition of the novel To Kill A Mockingbird has been found among items donated to Oxfam, according to the charity. The copy of Harper Lee's seminal 1960 work was discovered in a box of American books given to a bookshop in west London. It has since been acquired by the British Library and will join its national collection. Shop manager Olivia Haysman-Walker identified the copy as being a first US edition, saying it could easily have been missed. She realised quickly that they had something truly valuable. Although its dust jacket was worn around the edges, the pages inside were in beautiful condition. It is remarkable to think this book passed through the Oxfam bookshop and into the British Library's national collection. This discovery is a lovely reminder, during Second Hand September, of treasures hiding among donations in charity shops. Second Hand September is Oxfam's annual campaign encouraging people to buy second-hand items. Lee's Pulitzer Prize-winning book tells a story of racial injustice and childhood innocence, selling over 40 million copies worldwide.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. seminal (adjective): very important and influential, especially in the development of a subject or idea.
2. acquired (verb): to get or obtain something, often by buying it or receiving it.
3. national collection (noun phrase): a group of important or valuable items, such as books or art, that are owned and preserved by a country's main institution, like a library or museum.
4. identified (verb): recognized or proved who or what someone or something is.
5. dust jacket (noun): a removable paper cover on a hardback book, usually with information about the book or author.
6. worn around the edges (adjective phrase): showing signs of damage or deterioration along the outer borders due to age or frequent use.
7. passed through (phrasal verb): to move from one place to another or to be transferred from one person or stage to another.
8. encouraging (verb): giving support, confidence, or hope to someone; persuading someone to do something.
9. racial injustice (noun phrase): unfair treatment of a person or group based on their race.

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action itself and the object that receives the action, rather than on the performer of the action. It's often used in news reporting when the 'doer' is unknown, obvious, or less important. The structure is 'to be' + past participle.
Example from the text: "It has since been acquired by the British Library and will join its national collection."
