US and Lebanon Protect Syrian Officer Linked to Abduction

Advanced · C1+

News archive →
📅 October 08, 2026 - US and Lebanon Protect Syrian Officer Linked to Abduction

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

A BBC News investigation has revealed that Bassam al-Hassan, a former high-ranking Syrian officer, is being bankrolled and safeguarded by US and Lebanese authorities. Hassan, who supervised Syria's chemical weapons program and advised former President Bashar al-Assad, is being hosted in Lebanon following the Assad regime's collapse in 2024. The investigation, conducted by the BBC and NPR, found Hassan was allowed to stay due to an agreement with the FBI and other US and Lebanese entities. His willingness to provide intelligence, particularly concerning the 2012 seizure of American journalist Austin Tice, underpins this arrangement. It was previously reported that Tice's capture was a planned operation, with Hassan involved in the leadership structure. Officials from Syria's new government seek Hassan's return for trial, but US authorities prefer he remains in Lebanon, leaving Damascus with limited recourse. The Tice family’s spokesperson said that, if the findings were true, using US taxes to support Hassan was offensive. He asserted that this constitutes assistance to a wanted war criminal’s escape and called for Hassan to be returned for trial. Sources confirm Hassan receives funds from US authorities via an intermediary, making the payments harder to track despite his US sanctions. However, individuals involved in the Tice investigation express concerns that Hassan's deal may continue for an unspecified duration with little discernible progress. He is considered valuable for his alleged knowledge of Tice's fate, Syria's chemical weapons program, and its military ties with Iran. Though Hassan told investigators Tice was executed and suggested a burial site, searches found nothing, casting doubt on his account's validity. Questions remain over the thoroughness of the search, with sources suggesting US authorities halted digging prematurely. The investigation also found that other Syrian officials connected to the Tice case had apparently found refuge abroad.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. bankrolled (verb): To provide financial support for someone or something, often in a significant way.
2. supervised (verb): To observe and direct the execution of a task, activity, or group, ensuring it is done correctly.
3. entities (noun): Organizations, institutions, or groups with a distinct and independent existence.
4. seizure (noun): The action of forcibly taking something or someone, often by legal authority or by force.
5. underpins (verb): To form the basis, support, or foundation for something, strengthening its existence or validity.
6. leadership structure (noun phrase): The hierarchical arrangement of individuals in positions of authority and decision-making within an organization.
7. limited recourse (noun phrase): Having few available options or inadequate means to obtain remedy, assistance, or legal redress.
8. asserted (verb): To state a fact or belief confidently and forcefully, often in the face of contradiction.
9. constitutes (verb): To be or be considered as; to form or make up a particular part of something.
10. intermediary (noun): A person who acts as a link between two parties to facilitate an agreement, communication, or transaction.

Advanced Grammar: Participial Phrases of Consequence
An “-ing” clause can express the result of the preceding situation. Here, “leaving Damascus with limited recourse” describes the effect of the US position on Syria’s options. Replacing it with “because” would make the limited options a reason instead of a result; an “-ing” form by itself does not always express a result.
Example from the text: "Officials from Syria's new government seek Hassan's return for trial, but US authorities prefer he remains in Lebanon, leaving Damascus with limited recourse."
