Read the story

The number of maritime strikes reported in and around the Strait of Hormuz has surged to unprecedented levels, according to data from a UK agency. Twelve assaults were documented in a single week, marking the highest weekly total since the outbreak of the current conflict involving Iran. As the frequency of these engagements has escalated, the recent recovery in oil shipments navigating the strait has noticeably decelerated. Regional specialists suggest Tehran is deliberately attempting to dominate the critical waterway while the US military shepherds commercial traffic to evade Iranian interceptions. Despite these aggressive tactics, the US Central Command maintains that commercial operations proceed with co-ordinated protection from American forces. Verified footage recently captured the aftermath of one severe strike, showing the Nicaragua-flagged ship Petro Dolphin ablaze. The situation deteriorated further when the container ship GFS Pride sank in the strait following a direct hit by an unidentified projectile. Daily oil transit volumes through the strait have plunged from fifteen million barrels at the end of September to roughly twelve and a half million. Most Gulf Arab states now utilise a shuttle system, deploying smaller fleets to traverse the strait before transferring cargo to larger tankers in the Gulf of Oman. Risk intelligence analysts observe that Iran has evolved its maritime strategy, orchestrating near-daily operations in response to its loosening dominance over the strait.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. surged (verb): Increased suddenly and powerfully in degree, volume, or frequency.
2. unprecedented (adjective): Never having happened or existed in the past; completely novel in scale.
3. assaults (noun): Violent physical attacks or concerted military actions against a target.
4. escalated (verb): Become or made something become significantly greater, more intense, or more serious.
5. decelerated (verb): Reduced speed or slowed down in progress, growth, or recovery.
6. shepherds (verb): Guides or directs a group of entities safely through a particular, often dangerous, area.
7. evade (verb): To avoid or escape from something, often through cleverness or strategic maneuvering.
8. ablaze (adjective): Burning fiercely or completely consumed by fire.
9. projectile (noun): An object fired, thrown, or propelled forward, typically as a weapon like a missile.
10. traverse (verb): To travel across or through a specific geographical or physical area.
11. orchestrating (verb): Carefully planning and coordinating the elements of a situation to produce a desired effect.

Advanced Grammar: Present Participle Clauses for Method
Participle clauses beginning with an '-ing' verb can elegantly condense sentences by expressing an action that happens concurrently with the main clause. In this advanced structure, the present participle is often used to explain the specific method or procedure by which the main action is accomplished without needing an additional conjunction.
Example from the text: "Most Gulf Arab states now utilise a shuttle system, deploying smaller fleets to traverse the strait before transferring cargo to larger tankers in the Gulf of Oman."
