📅 November 07, 2026 - Katherine Ryan Reacts to Russell Brand’s Conviction
Katherine Ryan describes her mixed feelings after Russell Brand’s conviction and reflects on the people who came forward.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Comedian Katherine Ryan said she felt “grief and relief” after Russell Brand was convicted of rape and sexual assault. On Thursday, the court found Brand guilty of one charge of rape and three charges of sexual assault. He was acquitted of three other charges, including two rape charges. On her podcast, Ryan said the verdict had left her emotional and that she had great respect for the survivors. She said they came forward even though Brand was famous and powerful and had fans ready to attack them. Ryan said that whatever the outcome in court, the people who had been hurt would continue to feel the effects. In an earlier TV interview, Ryan described someone in the entertainment industry as a “predator”, a person who harms others by taking advantage of them. In 2022, Ryan said she could not name the man because she believed doing so could cause legal problems. There were widespread rumours that Ryan meant Brand, but she has not confirmed that she was talking about him. Police said they were also investigating further rape allegations from two women.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. charges (plural noun): Formal claims in a criminal case that someone broke the law.
2. was acquitted of (passive verb phrase): Was found not guilty of a particular crime in court.
3. came forward (phrasal verb, past tense): Chose to speak up and tell others about harm they had experienced themselves.
4. verdict (noun): The decision in a trial about whether someone is guilty.
5. entertainment industry (noun phrase): The businesses that make shows, films, music and other entertainment.
6. widespread (adjective): Heard or discussed by many people.
7. confirmed (verb, past participle): Stated clearly that something people believed or reported was true.
8. allegations (plural noun): Claims that someone did something wrong; the claims alone do not prove what happened.

Grammar Focus: Past simple passive
Form the past simple passive with “was” or “were” plus a past participle. In “was acquitted”, the focus is on what happened to Brand. You can leave out who took the action when that is clear from the context.
Example from the text: "He was acquitted of three other charges, including two rape charges."

📅 November 06, 2026 - Tech Stocks Slide as US Midterms Raise AI Concerns
Tech stocks dropped after the US midterms as investors worried that closer checks on new data centres could slow the growth of AI.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Tech stocks in the US and Asia dropped sharply after the US midterms raised fears that artificial intelligence (AI) development could slow. Democrats across the US campaigned to limit the growth of AI and data centres, the facilities that help power the technology. Democrats won control of both houses of Congress, the Senate and the House of Representatives. In Texas, Gina Hinojosa, who won the race for governor, had promised to put new data centre construction on hold. The Nasdaq Composite, an index that tracks a group of stocks, closed more than 5.5% lower. In Japan, the Nikkei 225 was down 6% in early trading as shares in chip companies Kioxia and Tokyo Electron lost value. In Seoul, the sell-off pushed the Kospi index down more than 8.5%, leading to a brief pause in trading to limit panic selling. Investors were already questioning whether the large sums going into AI would pay off. Investment strategist Charu Chanana said closer checks on data centre plans could delay projects and raise costs. Chanana pointed to the effects of data centres on the environment and on electricity bills as issues that could face closer review. OpenAI co-founder Greg Brockman said more work was needed to win support from local communities.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. raised fears (verb phrase): Made people worry that something bad might happen.
2. campaigned (verb): Worked to win public support for a plan during an election.
3. on hold (prepositional phrase): Delayed or paused for a period of time.
4. index (noun): A measure that shows how the prices of a group of stocks change.
5. closed (verb): Finished the trading day at the level given.
6. sell-off (noun): A period when many investors sell their stocks, causing prices to drop sharply.
7. pay off (phrasal verb): Bring enough financial benefit to make the money invested worthwhile.
8. win support (verb phrase): Persuade people to agree with a plan or back it.

Grammar Focus: Using “could” for possible effects
Use “could” followed by the base form of a verb to describe a possible effect. In the reading, “could” applies to both “delay” and “raise”. Chanana presents both effects as possibilities if data centre plans face closer checks.
Example from the text: "Investment strategist Charu Chanana said closer checks on data centre plans could delay projects and raise costs."

This year, after three years of searching, experts discovered larvae, or young beetles at an early stage of development, in a small area. Anne Hobbs from the Bumblebee Conservation Trust said the evidence suggested that the species was breeding in Wales again. The beetles are dependent on bees to complete their life cycle and need a particular habitat, or type of natural environment. They need thick plants for shelter and plenty of flowers in their habitat. They are usually seen from March to June in coastal grassland and sand dunes, especially on warm, sunny days. Because they need such specific conditions, only a handful of sites in the UK are suitable for them. Castlemartin Range is currently the only known site for the species in Wales, and wildlife protection workers regularly survey it. Hobbs said protection work needed to consider the needs of several species to be effective. She thanked the project's partners and volunteers for continuing their support, despite two years without any sightings of larvae.

4. breeding (verb, present participle): Producing young animals.
5. dependent on (adjective phrase): Needing someone or something in order to live or develop.
6. shelter (uncountable noun): Protection provided by a place where an animal can stay safely.
7. a handful of (quantity phrase): A small number of people or things.

Amazon says its latest propellers produce almost half the perceived sound of its previous design, with a milder tone. Wing, which supplies drones for Walmart deliveries, varies its flight paths to avoid repeatedly flying over the same homes. Schmidt wants deliveries in her area to stop until the service no longer causes a nuisance, or an annoying problem.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

2. background (noun): The sounds around you that you do not pay much attention to.
3. noticeable (adjective): Easy to hear, see or become aware of.
4. residential areas (plural noun phrase): Parts of a town or city where people live.
5. benefit from (verb phrase): Receive something helpful or useful from something.
6. vibration (noun): Repeated small, fast movements that can produce sound.
7. flight paths (plural noun phrase): The routes that aircraft follow through the air.
8. nuisance (noun): Something that annoys people or causes problems for them.

Hundreds of thousands of people took part in mass demonstrations across France on Saturday. While many marches were calm, violent conflict erupted between protesters and riot police in Paris. Despite the violence, officials have so far managed to avoid the extreme disorder seen last weekend. Officials stated that nearly 1,000 arrests were made following the protests. Two demonstrators explained that they feel abandoned by the government. Similar issues have triggered mass protests in several other European cities. These recent events follow weeks of escalating tension over rising fuel prices.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. demonstrations (noun): Public events where large groups of people meet or march to show support or opposition for something.
2. erupted (verb): Started suddenly and often violently.
3. makeshift (adjective): Made to be used temporarily when nothing better is available.
4. projectiles (noun): Objects that are thrown at a target, often as weapons.
5. disperse (verb): To make a crowd of people leave an area and spread out in different directions.
6. disorder (noun): A situation where people behave in a noisy, disorganized, or violent way in public.
7. triggered (verb): Caused a reaction or series of events to start happening.
8. escalating (adjective): Becoming greater, more serious, or worse over time.

Grammar Focus: Present Perfect Tense
We use the present perfect tense ("have" or "has" + past participle) to describe an action that happened at an unspecified time in the past and has a result or connection to the present. In this sentence, the issues "have triggered" protests, showing that a past event is closely connected to the current situation.
Example from the text: "Similar issues have triggered mass protests in several other European cities."
