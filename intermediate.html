📅 November 22, 2026 - Fossil Fuel Dispute Blocks COP31 Climate Agreement
Countries at COP31 remain divided over fossil fuels and financial support for dealing with climate change.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Negotiations, or formal discussions, at the COP31 climate meeting in Turkey continued more than a day beyond the planned finish. Countries had still not agreed on a global climate agreement, so the discussions were suspended, or stopped temporarily, for the night. The EU and Pacific island nations opposed a draft agreement, an unfinished version, because it did not mention reducing fossil fuel demand. Ministers from several countries and the EU wanted the final agreement to include a commitment to move away from oil, gas and coal. Fossil fuel producers, led by Saudi Arabia, had pushed to remove that commitment from the agreement. The Africa Group wanted commitments, or firm promises, to provide more money for adaptation, which means preparing for climate change's effects. The EU had previously said it would not support this request for more adaptation money. An earlier draft included a target of $120 billion for adaptation by 2035, but this amount was later removed. Some teams were moving their Sunday flights to Monday, suggesting that an agreement was still some distance away. If countries do not reach consensus, meaning agreement among all participants, the negotiations could be suspended. In that case, the meeting would end without an agreement and continue next year. However, the UK's chief negotiator remained hopeful and said her team was still trying to reach an agreement. Another risk was that too many teams might leave, leaving too few countries to approve a global agreement.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. negotiations (plural noun): Formal discussions in which people or groups try to reach an agreement.
2. suspended (verb, past participle): Stopped temporarily, with the possibility of continuing later.
3. draft agreement (noun phrase): An unfinished version of an agreement that people can still discuss and change.
4. demand (noun): The amount of something that people need or want to use.
5. move away from (phrasal verb): Gradually stop using or depending on something.
6. commitments (plural noun): Firm promises to take particular actions.
7. adaptation (noun): Changes that help people prepare for and manage the effects of climate change.
8. consensus (noun): Agreement among all the people or groups taking part in a discussion.

Grammar Focus: First conditional with “could”
Use “if” with the present simple to describe a possible future condition. Use “could” with the base form of a verb in the main clause to show a possible result. This pattern presents the result as possible rather than certain.
Example from the text: "If countries do not reach consensus, meaning agreement among all participants, the negotiations could be suspended."

📅 November 21, 2026 - New Hunger Games Stars Find Support in Each Other
Three young actors explain the challenges of joining The Hunger Games series and describe how they supported one another.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Joseph Zada, Mckenna Grace and Whitney Peak have the leading roles in the Hunger Games prequel Sunrise on the Reaping. A prequel tells an earlier part of a story, and this film is set 24 years before the original Hunger Games. Before the release, the three actors showed excitement and worry as they began a demanding series of press interviews. Zada, who plays Haymitch Abernathy, said the actors stayed close and made sure everyone felt supported. Grace is the most experienced of the three, with a career beginning in childhood that includes television series and recent Ghostbusters films. However, she said that joining the Hunger Games series was unlike anything she had experienced before. Peak said she and Zada turned to Grace when confused during film promotion, and Zada described Grace as a mentor. Zada plays a teenage version of Haymitch, the role played by Woody Harrelson in the original films. To develop his performance, Zada spent months studying Harrelson's acting in the first four Hunger Games films and taking notes. He used this research as a starting point while adding his own ideas to the role. Peak said filming could quickly become overwhelming, so the actors tried to keep the mood light between difficult scenes. Grace explained that sharing physically and emotionally difficult work helped the cast form close relationships.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. leading roles (plural noun phrase): The main acting parts in a film or play.
2. prequel (noun): A film or book about events that happen before those in an earlier film or book.
3. demanding (adjective): Needing a lot of effort, time or energy.
4. supported (adjective): Receiving help and encouragement from other people.
5. experienced (adjective): Having knowledge and skill from doing something before.
6. mentor (noun): A more experienced person who gives someone advice and help.
7. performance (noun): The way an actor plays a part in a film or show.
8. overwhelming (adjective): Causing feelings so strong that a situation becomes difficult to manage.

Grammar Focus: Relative clauses with “who” for extra information
Use “who” after a person to add information about them. Put commas around this information when the person is already identified. The main sentence remains complete if you remove the part beginning with “who”.
Example from the text: "Zada, who plays Haymitch Abernathy, said the actors stayed close and made sure everyone felt supported."

Neil Roberts, who speaks for marine insurers, says uncertainty about how many mines may be present affects how people feel.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. conflict (noun): Fighting between countries or armed groups.
2. threat (noun): The possibility that something dangerous or harmful could happen.
3. specialist (adjective): Designed or trained for a particular kind of work.
4. autonomous (adjective): Able to operate without a person controlling every action.
5. monitored (verb, past participle): Watched or checked over time to see what is happening.
6. in shifts (adverbial phrase): In separate working periods, with different groups taking turns.
7. conventional (adjective): Following the usual or traditional way of doing something.
8. uncertainty (noun): A situation in which something is not known or certain.

Grammar Focus: Present simple passive

Tommy Walsh, who went from working as a builder to appearing on popular television shows, has died at the age of 69.

Read the story

Tommy Walsh, a building and DIY expert who starred on several television shows, has died at the age of 69. His agent told BBC News that Walsh died peacefully after a long illness, with his family around him, and asked for privacy for the family. Walsh worked as a professional builder before becoming known to television audiences. He got his big break after restoring the home of a BBC producer who was developing ideas for a show that later became Ground Force. Walsh said he turned down the offer to appear on the show three times. He said he agreed after his eight-year-old daughter told him about a £10 bet she had made with a boy at school. She had bet that her father would appear on television. Walsh appeared on the BBC’s Ground Force with Alan Titchmarsh and Charlie Dimmock from 1997 to 2005. On Ground Force, Walsh’s co-stars carried out makeovers on gardens that belonged to members of the public. He went on to present Cowboys and Angels as well as Clean It, Fix It. In 2021, Walsh joined the daytime programme Homes Under the Hammer. Walsh also published several books about DIY projects that people can do themselves.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. agent (noun): Someone who represents another person in their working life and may speak to the media for them.
2. privacy (noun): The freedom to deal with personal matters without public attention.
3. big break (noun phrase): An important opportunity that helps someone become successful in their career.
4. restoring (verb, -ing form): Repairing a building to bring it back into good condition.
5. turned down (phrasal verb, past simple): Said no to an offer or invitation.
6. bet (noun): An agreement in which someone can win or lose money depending on whether a prediction comes true.
7. makeovers (plural noun): Changes that give places a new look.
8. went on to (verb phrase, past simple): Did something else later, often as the next step in a career.

Grammar Focus: Defining relative clauses with “who”
A defining relative clause with “who” tells us which person or kind of person we mean. Put it directly after the noun or noun phrase it describes, without commas. In the brief, the clause after “a BBC producer” tells us what the producer was working on.
Example from the text: "He got his big break after restoring the home of a BBC producer who was developing ideas for a show that later became Ground Force."

📅 November 09, 2026 - Hazel Dormice May Be Recovering in England and Wales
Monitoring suggests that hazel dormouse numbers may be rising after years of decline, and warmer summers may help explain the change.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Dormouse numbers may be recovering in England and Wales after years of decline. The hazel dormouse is the only dormouse species in the UK, and it now lives mainly in southern England and parts of Wales. The National Dormouse Monitoring Programme has been tracking dormouse populations at sites in England and Wales since 1998. The programme found that dormouse numbers have fallen by almost 60% since 2000. More recent results suggest that numbers rose by nearly 10% between 2020 and 2025, although researchers say the estimate is uncertain. This apparent rise came during a period when springs and summers were warmer and drier than average. In cold, wet weather or when food is scarce, dormice can enter torpor, a short period of deep sleep that saves energy. Researchers think warmer summers may mean less time in torpor and leave dormice with more energy to feed and breed. The animals may also have a better chance to build up fat reserves before winter. One ecologist who worked on the survey says it is still unclear how dormice will respond to warmer, wetter winters. People working to save wildlife say looking after the woodlands and hedgerows where dormice live is essential to their long-term survival.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. decline (noun): A fall in the number or amount of something over time.
2. tracking (verb, -ing form): Regularly checking something to see how it changes over time.
3. estimate (noun): A number based on the information available that may differ from the exact number.
4. scarce (adjective): Hard to find or available only in small amounts.
5. breed (verb): To produce young, when talking about animals.
6. build up (phrasal verb): To gradually increase the amount of something.
7. respond to (verb phrase): To react or change when something happens.
8. hedgerows (plural noun): Long rows of bushes or small trees, often along the edges of fields.
9. long-term survival (noun phrase): The ability of a group or species to keep living for many years into the future.

Grammar Focus: Present perfect continuous with “since”
Use the present perfect continuous for an activity that started in the past and is still happening. Use “has” or “have” followed by “been” and the “-ing” form of the verb. Use “since” with the point in time when the activity began.
Example from the text: "The National Dormouse Monitoring Programme has been tracking dormouse populations at sites in England and Wales since 1998."
