Muratov: Putin's Ukraine War Strategy Shifts to Destruction, Not Conquest

Nobel Peace Prize laureate Dmitry Muratov contends that after four years, Russian President Vladimir Putin's military objectives in Ukraine have irrevocably shifted from achieving outright victory to merely enacting widespread destruction, rendering true conquest impossible.

Read the story

Dmitry Muratov, the esteemed Nobel Peace Prize winner, has asserted unequivocally that Russian President Vladimir Putin is no longer in a position to claim a genuine victory in Ukraine. In a candid interview with the BBC, Muratov conveyed his firm belief that the Kremlin's strategic aims have fundamentally transformed. The ongoing conflict, now in its fifth year, underscores the protracted and devastating nature of the hostilities. Muratov posited that Putin can now only aspire to decimate Ukraine, not truly conquer it. This assessment highlights a significant recalibration of potential outcomes as perceived by international observers. The journalist's pronouncement reflects a broader sentiment among many analysts concerning the irreversible damage inflicted upon the nation. Furthermore, Muratov's perspective suggests that the initial aspirations of a swift and decisive triumph have evaporated. The sheer scale of destruction wrought by the invasion precludes any notion of a truly successful occupation in the traditional sense. His comments serve as a poignant reminder of the war's grim reality and the immense human cost. This unflinching appraisal challenges the narrative often propagated by official Russian sources regarding their military objectives.

Words and grammar

1. decimate (verb): To reduce drastically in number or strength; to destroy a large proportion of something.
2. irreversible (adjective): Impossible to reverse or undo; permanent.
3. evaporated (verb (past participle)): To vanish, disappear, or fade away gradually, as if turning into vapor.
4. sheer scale (noun phrase): The immense or extensive size, degree, or magnitude of something, emphasizing its overwhelming nature.
5. unflinching appraisal (noun phrase): A resolute, steadfast, and honest evaluation or assessment, made without hesitation, fear, or shrinking from harsh realities.

Advanced Grammar: Formal Verbs of Reporting/Attribution
In advanced English, especially in formal writing and news analysis, using a diverse range of sophisticated verbs to attribute statements or ideas adds nuance and precision. 'Posited' implies that someone put forward a theory, idea, or statement for consideration, often with a degree of intellectual formality, rather than merely stating or saying.
Example from the text: "Muratov posited that Putin can now only aspire to decimate Ukraine, not truly conquer it."
