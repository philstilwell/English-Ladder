Lesson 01: Taking a precise drink order
Original fictional case: At this fictional cafe, regular is the middle cup size. Customer Alex asks for a regular latte, meaning non-decaf coffee rather than a particular size. Barista Nia has not started the drink. When asked separately, Alex chooses a small hot latte, non-decaf, with the standard dairy milk and no syrup, to take away. The task is to resolve the ambiguous word without correcting the customer as if there were one universal cafe vocabulary. No price, exact temperature, or caffeine quantity is supplied.
Language workshop: What does regular mean?
Communication goal: Separate size, temperature, and coffee choice, then give a concise read-back before the drink is made.
Language guidance: Regular is not a reliable substitute for a complete order. In this cafe it names a size, but Alex means non-decaf. Ask which meaning is intended, then confirm the remaining choices separately.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): When you say regular, do you mean size or non-decaf? / Here, regular is our middle size. / Would you like small, regular, or large? / Would you like that hot or iced? / Non-decaf coffee, correct? / Would you like our standard dairy milk? / Any syrup, or none? / Is that for here or to take away? / One small hot latte with non-decaf coffee. / No syrup in that one. / Standard dairy milk for this order. / Have I got all of that right? / I will confirm the details before we make it. / Small, not our regular size. / Thanks; I understand which meaning you intended. / Small, hot, non-decaf, standard milk, no syrup, takeaway.
Vocabulary: barista: Worker who prepares and serves coffee drinks. / drink order: Specified beverage and selected variations. / cup size: Named or measured capacity option for a drink. / regular: Context-dependent label that may describe size or coffee choice. / small: Cafe-defined size below its larger options. / hot: Prepared as a hot drink rather than an iced version. / iced: Served with ice according to the drink recipe. / non-decaf: Coffee not sold as decaffeinated. / decaf: Coffee processed to remove most caffeine, not necessarily all. / latte: Espresso-based drink with milk; proportions depend on the recipe. / espresso: Concentrated coffee brewed by forcing hot water through ground coffee under pressure. / shot: Cafe serving of espresso, with size defined by its recipe. / standard milk: Milk used by default in a particular recipe. / dairy milk: Milk from an animal source rather than a plant-based drink. / plant-based drink: Beverage made from plant ingredients and used as a milk alternative. / syrup: Liquid flavoring or sweetener added to a drink. / modifier: Order detail that changes the standard item. / takeaway: Order intended to be taken away from the premises. / for here: Order intended for consumption on the premises. / read-back: Spoken repetition used to confirm the received order. / clarifying question: Question that resolves a specific uncertainty. / size convention: Local system for naming cup sizes. / order entry: Recording of the selected item and its details. / preparation start: Point at which making the drink begins.
Speaking task: Read Nia's questions and Alex's answers with the cloze words restored. Repeat the final order in this order: size, temperature, coffee choice, milk, syrup, destination. Complete Jo's four-line order and check that decaf did not become a size.
Partner: Nia: Barista / Alex: Customer
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: Customer Jo calls their usual order regular coffee, then explicitly clarifies that it must be decaf. After separate questions, Jo chooses a large iced latte with oat drink. Use these four confirmed details.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Alex: Thanks. Could you run through the whole order before you send it to the bar?
Nia: Here is the read-back: small hot latte, non-decaf, standard dairy milk, no syrup, takeaway.
Alex: That is exactly right. Just the one drink, please.
Nia: Great. The order entry now matches that. We have not started the drink yet; the bar will use these details.

Lesson 02: Comparing drinks without overclaiming
Original fictional case: Customer Morgan is choosing between the small latte and small cappuccino at this fictional cafe. Both recipes use the same espresso serving. The cappuccino has more foam; the latte has more liquid milk. Morgan wants less foam, not an extra espresso serving or a claim about caffeine. Barista Sora must explain the practical difference and recommend the option matching that preference. The recipes describe this cafe only. No laboratory caffeine measurement, universal cup proportion, or health recommendation is supplied.
Language workshop: Less foam, not more caffeine

Incoming worker: Do you have the order number, or should I ask them to explain everything again?
Outgoing barista: It's one-forty-six, under Avery. They received their drinks and paid; only the receipt query remains.
Incoming worker: Thanks. Have you already printed the itemized version, or is that still the action needed?
Outgoing barista: I found the completed transaction but haven't printed the itemized receipt. The lead is checking that option.
Incoming worker: I'll take the follow-up with the lead. I won't treat this as a new unpaid drink order.
Outgoing barista: Exactly. The customer doesn't want another payment or changes to the original drink specifications.
Incoming worker: I'll tell them I've taken over and ask the lead whether the requested receipt is available.
Outgoing barista: Please also explain if only a payment receipt is available; don't promise item detail we can't provide.
Incoming worker: Understood. Completed order, completed payment, itemized receipt still unresolved. Is there another issue attached to this customer?
Outgoing barista: No other issue. The till review is separate and remains with the lead, not this customer.
Incoming worker: I'll keep the two matters distinct and return to Avery with the lead's answer about the receipt.
Vocabulary in this exchange: mobile order: An order placed through a phone app or similar online ordering service. / till: The cafe's sales register or the cash drawer associated with it.
