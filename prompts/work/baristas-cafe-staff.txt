Lesson 01: Taking a precise drink order
Original fictional case: A customer says 'a regular latte.' This cafe uses regular for its middle size, but the customer might mean caffeinated coffee or the usual milk. No drink has been made. Clarify size, hot or iced, and coffee choice separately.
Language workshop: Unpack the word 'regular'
Communication goal: Clarify a familiar word that can describe several order details.
Language guidance: Ask one specification at a time. Decaf describes coffee, not milk or sweetness, and doesn't mean guaranteed zero caffeine.
Useful frames (complete the gaps with case facts): By regular, do you mean ___? / Would you like that hot or ___? / That's a ___ latte with ___, for ___.
Vocabulary: espresso: A concentrated coffee drink prepared by passing hot water through finely ground coffee under pressure. / shot: A cafe's specified serving of espresso; exact size and recipe vary. / decaf: Coffee from which most caffeine has been removed; it is not necessarily caffeine-free. / dine in: An order intended to be consumed in the cafe rather than taken away.
Speaking task: Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.
Partner: Customer: you mean middle size; choose hot, decaf, and whole milk when asked.
Guided response task: Complete a workplace dialogue by selecting one of four supplied options. Compare the feedback and say the completed exchange aloud. Do not write an open-ended message.
Optional second-round challenge: The customer says 'the normal one' about milk. Ask for the actual milk choice.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: When you say regular, do you mean our middle size or caffeinated coffee? I'll check those separately so I enter the right drink. Would you like the latte hot or iced, and which milk would you prefer? Once those details are clear, I'll repeat the full order before sending it to the bar.
