📅 November 14, 2026 - Women Speak Up About Problems in Television
Recent television news shows changes in how people report bad behaviour.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Recent news stories raise questions about how powerful people in television treat others. These stories follow changes that began years ago. Reporters started looking into these stories years ago. Their work can take a long time. Kirsty Wark says more women speak up because they see other women doing this. Freeman says women now feel that people will listen to them. A workers' group says some television workers are still afraid to report problems. Lucy Beaumont says some well-known people stay quiet because they fear losing work. A meeting with television leaders is planned for next week to discuss keeping people safe. People who run television companies also need to help stop people from hurting others.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. Reporters (plural noun): People who find information and write or tell news stories.
2. speak up (verb phrase): Tell other people about a problem instead of staying quiet.
3. listen (verb): Pay attention to what someone says.
4. afraid (adjective): Worried that something bad may happen.
5. losing work (verb phrase): No longer having a job or paid work.
6. safe (adjective): Not in danger of being hurt.

Grammar Focus: Giving a reason with “because”
Use “because” to explain why something happens or why someone does something. Put the reason after “because”. Both parts have a subject and a verb, as in “She speaks because she needs help”.
Example from the text: "Kirsty Wark says more women speak up because they see other women doing this."

A report describes a change at Nvidia and questions about Huang's private life.

The company makes computer chips, small parts that help computers work. Colette Kress is the company's leader for now. She called an emergency meeting to talk about the company's future. Nvidia is looking for a new leader.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. leader (noun): A person who tells a group or company what to do.
2. questions (plural noun): Things you ask when you want answers.
3. check (verb): To look at something carefully to learn more about it.
4. for now (adverb phrase): At this time, but this may change later.
5. emergency meeting (noun phrase): A meeting people need to have very soon because of a serious problem.
6. looking for (verb phrase, present participle): Trying to find someone or something.

Grammar Focus: Simple present for facts
Use the simple present for facts. With one company, usually add "-s" to the verb, as in "makes". With "they" or a plural noun, use the verb without "-s". Use "help" after "chips" because "chips" is plural.
Example from the text: "The company makes computer chips, small parts that help computers work."

📅 November 12, 2026 - Some Young People Could Get Less Government Support
New plans could change money and job support for young people.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Some people under 25 could get less money from the government under new plans. Benefits are government payments to help people who need support. Alan Milburn's report is expected to suggest changes to benefits for illness or disability. Under the plans, young people would get a basic payment instead. Some could get extra money for daily costs linked to their health problems. People with more serious disabilities who cannot work would get a higher payment. The report is also expected to suggest more help for young people looking for work. This could include work experience and help from personal advisers. Milburn said the changes would affect people already getting benefits and new people asking for them.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. benefits (plural noun): Money the government gives people who need help.
2. payments (plural noun): Amounts of money given to someone.
3. basic (adjective): At a simple level, without anything extra.
4. extra (adjective): More than the usual amount.
5. daily (adjective): Happening or needed every day.
6. looking for work (verb phrase): Trying to find a job.

Grammar Focus: Could for possibility

Read the story

A rare beetle, a type of insect, was found in Pembrokeshire, Wales, after 60 years. It is called the Short-Necked Oil Beetle. Last year, people found only dead adult beetles at a place called Castlemartin. This year, experts found very young beetles there after three years of searching. Anne Hobbs said the beetles may be having young in Wales again. These beetles need certain bees to survive. They also need many flowers and plants to hide in. They can live in grassy places near the sea. They move around most on warm, sunny days. Castlemartin is the only place in Wales where people can find these beetles now.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. rare (adjective): Not often seen or found.
2. found (verb (past form)): Saw or discovered something after looking for it.
3. dead (adjective): No longer alive.
4. survive (verb): To stay alive.
5. flowers (plural noun): The parts of plants that often have bright colours.
6. warm (adjective): A little hot, but not very hot.

Grammar Focus: Can + verb
Use “can” to say that something is possible. Put the basic form of a verb after “can”, such as “live” or “survive”. The verb stays the same with one beetle or many beetles, and we do not add “to”.
