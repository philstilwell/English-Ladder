How AI Is Changing Farming and Supporting Human Judgement

📅 October 26, 2026 - How AI Is Changing Farming and Supporting Human Judgement

Read the story

George McMaster began his farming career milking eight cows and relying on visual judgement to detect health problems. He now watches a herd of 300 through an AI camera in the milking parlour at his farm near Islandmagee in County Antrim. The camera uses an algorithm, a set of computational rules, to identify individual cows, analyse daily footage and flag changes that may not yet be visible to a person. A farmer reports that the camera can identify lameness, or difficulty walking normally, earlier than ordinary daily checks had revealed it. A farmer also links healthier cattle with greater milk production and better animal welfare. In the robotic milking arrangement described, automation allows cows to enter and leave the machine when they choose, rather than being brought in at fixed times. For one farmer, obtaining high-speed broadband was a turning point that enabled him to open a farm shop accepting electronic payments. One farmer describes a lighter administrative burden, with information entered as work is done rather than written down both in the cattle house and in the office. A compliance section allows reports on medicines, feed and other farm information to be selected in preparation for inspections. Another stated priority is for AI to help reduce inputs, the resources farming uses, while increasing outputs, what it produces. Although one speaker is enthusiastic about the technology, he does not believe it will replace humans. One speaker explicitly rejects the prospect of AI taking over farmers' decision-making. One contributor argues that combining datasets could advance sustainability across its economic, environmental and social dimensions. Digitalisation is also presented as a way to help farmers meet their obligations while supplying good-quality, sustainable food.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. algorithm (countable noun): A set of rules a computer follows to process information, here to identify individual animals and detect changes.
2. automation (uncountable noun): The use of technology to perform tasks with less direct human involvement, without necessarily removing human responsibility.
3. administrative burden (noun phrase): The time and effort required to organise information and complete routine paperwork, viewed as a demand on someone's resources.
4. compliance (uncountable noun): The fulfilment of official rules or requirements; organised documentation can help demonstrate it.
5. inputs (plural noun): Resources used in production, considered in relation to the goods or quantities they help produce.
6. outputs (plural noun): The goods or quantities produced by an activity, considered in relation to the resources used.
7. decision-making (uncountable noun): The process of assessing information and choosing what to do, distinct from simply receiving information.
8. sustainability (uncountable noun): The capacity to maintain farming over time while addressing economic, environmental and social needs.
9. digitalisation (uncountable noun): The adoption of digital systems that changes how information is managed and work is carried out.
10. obligations (plural noun): Duties or requirements that someone is expected or required to fulfil, rather than optional ambitions.

Advanced Grammar: Concessive clauses with “although”
A concessive clause introduced by “although” acknowledges a fact that might suggest a different conclusion. Here, “is enthusiastic about the technology” might create an expectation of support for replacing humans, but the main clause rejects that expectation without denying the enthusiasm.
Example from the text: "Although one speaker is enthusiastic about the technology, he does not believe it will replace humans."
