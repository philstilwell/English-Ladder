01Guest Arrival and Front-Desk EscalationRepair a misunderstanding · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Acknowledge a communication problem and give a corrected message.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A guest arrives before the room is ready after receiving an unclear confirmation message. The desk has a luggage-storage option but no verified room-ready time.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

check-in
The process of registering an arrival; also a short meeting to review progress.
room inventory
The rooms available for sale or assignment under the property's current status definitions.
upgrade
A move to a higher service category, room type, or product capability.
service recovery
Actions taken to address a service failure and restore the customer's experience.

03 · Notice the language

Repair a misunderstanding

Name what was unclear or wrong, acknowledge its effect, and correct it. A brief apology can help, followed by a practical next step. Avoid explaining away the other person's reaction or promising an outcome you cannot control.

  • My earlier message did not make ... clear.
  • I'm sorry for ... . The correct information is ... .
  • Let me check that the revised explanation addresses ... .

Improve this: I'm sorry if you failed to understand.

See a clearer version

I'm sorry my message did not explain the timing clearly.

The revision takes responsibility for the message without blaming the listener. It identifies what needs to be corrected.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which apology takes responsibility for unclear wording?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. I'm sorry my earlier wording was unclear.

  1. A. I'm sorry you did not read it correctly. This shifts blame to the reader.
  2. B. I'm sorry if you chose to misunderstand it. This attributes an unverified motive to the listener.
  3. C. I'm sorry my earlier wording was unclear. This acknowledges the speaker's contribution to the problem.
2. What should follow an apology for an incorrect date?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. The corrected date and the current confirmation status.

  1. A. A new guaranteed date even if it is unverified. An unsupported new promise can create a second misunderstanding.
  2. B. The corrected date and the current confirmation status. A repair needs usable corrected information, including any uncertainty.
  3. C. A longer explanation that never states the correct date. The reader still lacks the information they need.

05 · Say it

Practice with a partner

Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.

Partner's role and follow-up

You are frustrated because an earlier message created an expectation. Explain that expectation. Ask your partner to distinguish the correction from anything still uncertain.

Try a harder second round

The listener remains disappointed after the correction. Acknowledge the impact and restate the available next step calmly.

Studying alone? Give both sides of the conversation aloud. Pause before answering the follow-up, then repeat with fewer notes.

Vocabulary for this lesson

A guest's expression of dissatisfaction with a hospitality service.
online review
Public feedback posted on a digital platform about a product or experience.
brand voice
The recognizable style and tone a brand uses in communication.
compensation
Payment or another remedy; in employment, the pay and benefits provided for work.

03 · Notice the language

Give feedback people can use

Use a specific example instead of a personality label. Explain its effect, invite the person's perspective, and agree on a practical improvement. Focus on communication that can be changed, not accent or national identity.

  • In [specific situation], I noticed ... .
  • The effect was ... . What was happening from your perspective?
  • Could we agree to ... and review it ...?

Improve this: You are always careless.

See a clearer version

The last two reports omitted the agreed summary section.

The revision identifies observable work. 'Always' and a personality judgment make the feedback harder to verify or act on.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which feedback begins with observable behavior?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. The last two updates omitted the agreed completion date.

  1. A. You are the kind of person who ignores details. This labels a person rather than an action.
  2. B. The last two updates omitted the agreed completion date. The recipient can check the specific examples and discuss how to improve them.
  3. C. You obviously do not care about our work. This assumes motivation rather than describing behavior.
2. Which follow-up invites useful information before agreeing on support?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. What is making this part of the task difficult?

  1. A. What is making this part of the task difficult? An open question may reveal a barrier the speaker has not considered.
  2. B. There cannot be any reasonable explanation. This closes off relevant information.
  3. C. You should already know what I am thinking. This relies on unstated expectations.

03Reservations, Overbooking, and WalksOffer a workable tradeoff · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Negotiate scope or timing while making conditions explicit.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A booking issue means the reserved room type is unavailable. Staff have two alternatives within their approved service process.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

overbooking
Accepting more reservations than the available capacity based on expected cancellations or no-shows.
walk
In hospitality, relocating a guest to another property when the booked accommodation cannot be supplied.
occupancy
The proportion of available rooms occupied or sold during a stated period.
rate parity
An arrangement concerning consistency of hotel rates across specified distribution channels.

03 · Notice the language

Offer a workable tradeoff

Acknowledge the goal and explain the available choices. Connect an offer to its actual conditions. A useful response makes room for discussion without promising approval or resources you do not control.

  • We can ..., provided that ... .
  • Would you prefer ... or ...?
  • I can take that proposal for review; I cannot yet confirm ... .

Improve this: If you will approve the scope, we can assess the schedule.

See a clearer version

If you approve the scope, we can assess the schedule.

For an ordinary future condition, the if-clause uses present tense. The other clause can express the future consequence or possibility.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence presents a normal future condition clearly?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. If the scope changes, we will review the schedule.

  1. A. If the scope changes, we will review the schedule. The if-clause uses present tense; the main clause states the future response.
  2. B. If the scope will change, we review yesterday's schedule. The tense pattern and time reference do not express the intended future condition.
  3. C. If the scope changed tomorrow, we reviewed the schedule yesterday. The conflicting time references make the condition unclear.
2. Which response negotiates a new request without silently promising extra work?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. We can assess the addition and explain its effect on the agreed scope.

  1. A. We have included it, although its scope has not been reviewed. It treats unreviewed work as already included.
  2. B. We guarantee the original date without assessing the addition. It promises an outcome without examining the consequences.
  3. C. We can assess the addition and explain its effect on the agreed scope. This offers a useful next step while keeping the approval and scope question open.

Try a harder second round

The preferred option is unavailable. Offer an alternative and clearly state any approval still needed.

04Revenue Management and PricingMake a complex point clear · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Explain a useful distinction in plain English.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A hotel reports a higher average daily rate but lower occupancy. A manager assumes every revenue measure must have improved.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

ADR
Average daily rate: room revenue divided by rooms sold for the stated period.
RevPAR
Revenue per available room: room revenue divided by available rooms for the stated period.
yield management
Adjusting price and availability to manage revenue from limited, time-sensitive capacity.
compression night
A hotel-market period of strong demand and limited room availability.

03 · Notice the language

Make a complex point clear

Start with the reader's question. Explain one unfamiliar term with familiar words, then give a relevant example or contrast. Check understanding without asking only 'Do you understand?'.

  • In this context, ... means ... .
  • The difference is that ... .
  • How would you explain that distinction to a colleague?

Improve this: We need to operationalize the implementation.

See a clearer version

We need to put the plan into practice.

The revised sentence names the action in familiar words. Specialist vocabulary is useful when it adds precision, but repeating abstract nouns can hide the meaning.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence explains 'backlog' rather than repeating it?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. The backlog is the work still waiting to be completed.

  1. A. The backlog is an important operational backlog. Repeating the same term does not define it.
  2. B. The backlog must be reviewed at the backlog meeting. This describes a meeting but leaves the term unexplained.
  3. C. The backlog is the work still waiting to be completed. This gives the meaning in familiar words.
2. Which follow-up best checks whether your explanation was clear?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. How would you describe the next step in your own words?

  1. A. You understand everything now, don't you? This leading question makes it easy to agree without showing understanding.
  2. B. How would you describe the next step in your own words? An open request lets the listener show their understanding.
  3. C. I have explained the next step, so shall we finish? Finishing the conversation gives no evidence of understanding.

Try a harder second round

05Housekeeping, Maintenance, and TurnoverTransfer information and responsibility · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Give a handoff that the receiving person can confirm.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

Housekeeping marks a room cleaned, but maintenance has an open repair request. Reception asks whether it can be assigned.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

room status
A room's recorded availability or service condition, such as occupied or out of order.
out of order
A room or item unavailable for normal use because of a problem or required work.
turnover
Preparing a room for the next guest; in staffing, the rate of people leaving an organization.
preventive maintenance
Planned work intended to reduce failures and maintain equipment condition.

03 · Notice the language

Transfer information and responsibility

State the current situation, relevant background, open task, and receiving role. Ask for acknowledgment or repeat-back of the critical item. Sending a message and transferring responsibility are not always the same thing.

  • The current status is ...; the outstanding item is ... .
  • The record shows ... .
  • Please confirm who has accepted ... .

Improve this: Someone should follow up on this.

See a clearer version

Please confirm which team has accepted the follow-up task.

The revised sentence asks for an identifiable receiving team instead of leaving responsibility with an unspecified 'someone'.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence checks that responsibility has been accepted?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Please confirm who will complete the outstanding check.

  1. A. The check appears somewhere in the notes. A written record alone does not identify who will act.
  2. B. Please confirm who will complete the outstanding check. It asks for an identifiable person or team responsible for the task.
  3. C. I mentioned the check in my earlier email. Mentioning a task does not show that anyone accepted it.
2. Which response best checks a critical handoff detail?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. So the result is pending and your team will follow up; is that correct?

  1. A. So the result is pending and your team will follow up; is that correct? This repeats the status and responsibility and invites correction.
  2. B. I assume all results are final, so we can finish. It changes pending information into a final result.
  3. C. I saw the message, so no clarification is needed. Receiving a message is not the same as confirming its meaning.

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

Banquet event order: a document specifying arrangements and responsibilities for an event.

A timed sequence of an event's activities and responsibilities.

An agreed reduction in booked event rooms or attendance, sometimes subject to charges.

An audio or visual equipment need for an event, such as microphones or projection.

03 · Notice the language

Ask a precise question

Name the exact word, fact, or requirement you need clarified. Ask one focused question at a time. Repeat your understanding and give the other person a chance to correct it.

  • When you say ..., do you mean ...?
  • Could you clarify which ...?
  • So, my understanding is ... . Is that right?

Improve this: Could you explain what does this mean?

See a clearer version

Could you explain what this means?

After an introductory phrase such as 'Could you explain', the embedded question uses statement word order: what this means.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which indirect question has the usual word order?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. Could you confirm when the review starts?

  1. A. Could you confirm when the review starts? The embedded question uses subject then verb: the review starts.
  2. B. Could you confirm when does the review start? Remove 'does' and use statement word order after 'confirm'.
  3. C. Could you confirm when starts the review? Place the subject 'the review' before 'starts'.
2. Someone says the report is 'ready.' Which question best clarifies its approval status?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. Has the report been approved, or is it ready for review?

  1. A. Can you send the report to me this afternoon? This asks about delivery timing, not whether approval has happened.
  2. B. Who will present the report at the meeting? This asks about the presenter, not the report's approval status.
  3. C. Has the report been approved, or is it ready for review? This question distinguishes two meanings of 'ready' that affect the next action.

07Tour Operations and Traveler SafetyMatch certainty to evidence · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Separate observations, assumptions, and conclusions.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

Heavy weather may affect an excursion. The operator has not confirmed whether it will proceed. Guests ask if the original itinerary is certain.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

itinerary
A planned sequence of travel activities, locations, and times.
force majeure
A contractual provision addressing specified events beyond a party's control; effects depend on the wording and law.
waiver
A documented relinquishment or exception concerning a right or requirement, subject to applicable terms and law.
local operator
A provider responsible for delivering travel or tour services in a destination.

03 · Notice the language

Match certainty to evidence

Say what the evidence shows, identify what is still unknown, and limit the conclusion to that evidence. Words such as 'may', 'suggests', and 'in this sample' are useful when the uncertainty is real. Do not soften an urgent, verified safety concern.

  • The available evidence shows ... .
  • This may indicate ..., but ... .
  • We have not yet established whether ... .

Improve this: The pilot proves this will work everywhere.

See a clearer version

The pilot worked in the tested setting; performance elsewhere remains untested.

The revision states the finding and its scope. It avoids turning limited evidence into a universal claim.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence preserves uncertainty about an unconfirmed cause?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. The change may have contributed to the delay.

  1. A. The change must have caused the delay. 'Must have' expresses a strong inference that the evidence may not support.
  2. B. The change definitely caused the delay. 'Definitely' presents the cause as certain.
  3. C. The change may have contributed to the delay. 'May have contributed' presents a possibility without claiming the cause is established.
2. A result comes from a small pilot in one location. Which phrase accurately limits the claim?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. In this pilot, the observed result was ...

  1. A. Without any uncertainty, the result is ... This removes uncertainty rather than explaining its limits.
  2. B. In this pilot, the observed result was ... This locates the finding in the setting actually studied.
  3. C. In every location, the result will be ... The pilot does not establish future results everywhere.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which indirect question has the usual word order?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Could you confirm when the review starts?

  1. A. Could you confirm when starts the review? Place the subject 'the review' before 'starts'.
  2. B. Could you confirm when the review starts? The embedded question uses subject then verb: the review starts.
  3. C. Could you confirm when does the review start? Remove 'does' and use statement word order after 'confirm'.
2. Someone says the report is 'ready.' Which question best clarifies its approval status?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. Has the report been approved, or is it ready for review?

  1. A. Has the report been approved, or is it ready for review? This question distinguishes two meanings of 'ready' that affect the next action.
  2. B. Can you send the report to me this afternoon? This asks about delivery timing, not whether approval has happened.
  3. C. Who will present the report at the meeting? This asks about the presenter, not the report's approval status.
