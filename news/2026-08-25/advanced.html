Read the story

Iran has unequivocally stated its readiness to counter intensified economic sanctions recently imposed by the United States. Washington describes its latest punitive measures as an "economic D-Day," signaling a severe escalation in financial pressure. However, Tehran contends it possesses a comprehensive strategy to navigate these significant challenges. The Islamic Republic asserts that it has developed various mechanisms to mitigate the impact of these unilateral restrictions effectively. This declaration comes amidst persistent tensions between the two nations regarding Iran's nuclear ambitions and regional influence. Iranian officials have consistently maintained that such coercive tactics will not compel them to alter their sovereign policies. Instead, they view these actions as a direct assault on their national sovereignty and economic resilience. The government claims to have meticulously studied past sanctions regimes to formulate a robust defense against current and future pressures. Furthermore, authorities indicate that they are prepared for a prolonged period of economic confrontation with the West. This proactive stance underscores Iran's determination to resist external economic pressure and maintain its independent trajectory.

Words and grammar

1. counter (verb): To oppose, react against, or take action to neutralize or reduce the effect of something, especially a threat or attack.
2. contends (verb): To assert something as a position in an argument or to struggle to surmount a difficulty or opposition.
3. comprehensive strategy (noun phrase): A detailed and far-reaching plan designed to address all aspects of a particular situation or achieve a specific long-term goal.
4. mitigate (verb): To make something less severe, serious, or painful; to alleviate or soften the impact of an adverse condition.
5. resilience (noun): The capacity to recover quickly from difficulties; toughness, or the ability of an economy or system to withstand or recover from shocks.

Advanced Grammar: Present Participle Clauses for Consequence/Result
A present participle clause (starting with -ing form of a verb) can be used to describe an action or event that happens as a result or consequence of the main clause's action. It typically follows the main clause and adds explanatory detail, indicating what the result of the preceding statement is, creating a more concise and sophisticated sentence structure.
Example from the text: "Washington describes its latest punitive measures as an "economic D-Day," signaling a severe escalation in financial pressure."
