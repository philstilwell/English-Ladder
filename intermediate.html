📅 September 24, 2026 - Protein Prices Soar: Consumers Feel the Squeeze
Rising wholesale prices for key ingredients are causing protein products to become considerably more expensive for consumers, impacting their buying habits.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Consumers are feeling the pinch from the rising cost of protein. Amarpreet Singh has been drinking protein shakes since he was 16. Amarpreet says a 4.5kg bag of the whey protein he buys cost around £60 in 2020. By last year, its price had gone up to £120. Fourteen years later, shakes are still part of his routine, but they have become considerably more expensive. Over the years, protein powders and shakes have attracted a much broader customer base. Evie Lewis began using protein powder because she felt she wasn't getting enough protein in her diet. At £32.99 for a 420g tub, Evie is feeling the high price. She admits, "I've been rationing it and using less than what's suggested." The growing appetite for protein is running up against a problem: supply has not kept pace. Manufacturers say that increasing production takes time and is costly, as producing high-protein whey requires specialist processing and drying equipment. Some companies are starting to adapt what they make by changing recipes or looking for cheaper sources of protein. For students like Evie, managing on a much tighter budget, buying protein is a different story. She says that now she is at university, protein is not a necessity right now. One protein user explains, "If you're doing exercise and not taking any, you feel like you wouldn't be recovering properly."

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. feeling the pinch (idiom): experiencing financial difficulty because you have less money than before
2. considerably (adverb): much or a lot; to a large degree
3. routine (noun): a regular way of doing things in a particular order
4. customer base (noun phrase): all the people who buy or use a particular product or service
5. rationing (verb): controlling the amount of something that people can have or use, often because there is not enough of it
6. appetite (noun): a strong desire or need for something, in this context, demand for protein products
7. kept pace (verb phrase): moved or developed at the same speed as something else; to keep up
8. specialist (adjective): having or involving expert knowledge or training in a particular area
9. adapt (verb): to change something or yourself to suit a new situation
10. necessity (noun): something that you must have or do, or that is very important

Grammar Focus: Present Conditionals (Type 0 and 1)
We use present conditionals to talk about general truths, habits, or likely outcomes in the present or future. The 'if' clause describes the condition, usually in the present simple or present continuous. The main clause describes the result, which can be in the present simple or use a modal verb to express a feeling or possibility. For example, 'If you're doing exercise and not taking any,' describes a present, ongoing condition.
Example from the text: "One protein user explains, "If you're doing exercise and not taking any, you feel like you wouldn't be recovering properly.""

📅 September 23, 2026 - Pornhub's Age Checks Face Scrutiny from UK Regulator
Ofcom has launched an inquiry into how Pornhub is verifying the age of its users, particularly its reliance on Apple's third-party checks.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Ofcom has launched an investigation into Aylo, Pornhub's owner, to see if the site's new age checks effectively prevent children from seeing adult material. The government body stated that Pornhub began using a new age verification process for some users, relying on Apple as a third party. Ofcom expressed concerns that Aylo might not have completed enough due diligence and testing before putting this new process into action, questioning its effectiveness. In a detailed statement, Aylo strongly defended its age verification method and promised full cooperation with the inquiry. Under the UK's Online Safety Act, websites hosting adult material must use highly effective age assurance to confirm users are over 18. As part of its investigation, Ofcom will examine how Aylo applies its age verification processes and if it carried out the necessary due diligence before implementation. However, Aylo claims that after trying various age-check methods in different markets, Apple's approach offers much stronger protection than any other currently available in the UK. The Online Safety Act gives Ofcom the authority to impose penalties on companies that don't comply, potentially up to £18 million or 10% of their annual global income.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. launched (verb): started something, often a large or important activity or project.
2. inquiry (noun): an official process to find out the truth about something.
3. prevent (verb): to stop something from happening or to stop someone from doing something.
4. effectively (adverb): in a way that produces a desired or intended result.
5. due diligence (noun phrase): the careful research or actions someone must do before entering an agreement or making an important decision.
6. defended (verb): protected someone or something from attack, or explained why something was right when others disagreed.
7. cooperation (noun): the act of working together with someone or doing what they ask to achieve a shared goal.
8. assurance (noun): a statement or promise that something will certainly happen or is true; confidence in something.
9. third party (noun phrase): a person or group involved in a situation in addition to the two main ones.
10. impose (verb): to force a rule, tax, punishment, or burden on someone or something.

Grammar Focus: Present Perfect Simple
The Present Perfect Simple is used to talk about actions that happened at an unspecified time in the past but have a connection to the present, or for actions that started in the past and continue now. It is formed with "have" or "has" followed by the past participle of the main verb. For example, in "Ofcom has launched an investigation," the launching happened in the past, but the investigation is ongoing or its results are relevant now.
Example from the text: "Ofcom has launched an investigation into Aylo, Pornhub's owner, to see if the site's new age checks effectively prevent children from seeing adult material."

Definitions in English.

8. substantially (adverb): by a large amount or to a great extent
9. benefit (verb): to receive an advantage or something good from a situation

Grammar Focus: The Passive Voice (Present Perfect)
The passive voice helps us describe actions where the focus is on the action itself or the receiver of the action, rather than who performed it. It's often used in news to report events without needing to name the specific person or group responsible. Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. identified (verb): recognized or found something that exists.
2. disrupted (verb): interrupted something or caused it to stop working normally.
3. malicious (adjective): intending or intended to cause harm or trouble.
4. revelations (noun): surprising or important facts that are revealed.
5. potential (adjective): possible in the future, but not yet real.
6. dissidents (noun): people who disagree strongly with official policies, especially in a country with strict control.
7. safeguards (noun): measures taken to protect something or someone from harm or danger.
8. catastrophic (adjective): causing sudden, great damage or suffering.
9. incorporating (verb): including something as part of a larger whole.

Grammar Focus: Passive Voice
The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action itself and the object of the action, rather than the subject performing the action. We often use it when we don't know who performed the action, or when the actor is less important than the action or the receiver of the action. It is formed with a form of "to be" + the past participle. For example, in the sentence "The ball was kicked by the boy," the action "was kicked" is more important, or the receiver "the ball" is the focus, not "the boy" (the actor).
