Households React Differently to the Budget

Intermediate · B1–B2

📅 October 29, 2026 - Households React Differently to the Budget

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

People with different incomes told the BBC how the government's Budget, its plan for taxes and spending, could affect their finances. Dave Herbert, a professional driver, says fuel prices have been extremely high this year. He says his monthly petrol costs are around £350, between 40% and 50% higher than at the start of the year. Dave was disappointed because the Budget did not include the cut in fuel duty, a tax on fuel, that he wanted. Emily Nelson has been unable to find a job since leaving college last year. She wants more funding for businesses so they can employ more young people, as well as more money for training. Robert Portlock wants to leave his parents' home, but he does not think he can afford to live independently. He says a small flat in his area would cost about two-thirds of his monthly wage in rent. Chloe has cut back on luxuries, including cinema visits, and tries to keep her weekly food shopping within £40. However, Chloe feels hopeful about the Budget because she believes small changes can help her. She says cuts to standing charges, the fixed costs on gas bills, and a £100 energy payment will help her. A higher earner wanted higher taxes for people with large incomes like his own, but says the Budget offered nothing on this. He argues that better roads, hospitals and schools require more tax income.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. finances (plural noun): The money someone has and how they manage it.
2. fuel duty (noun phrase): A government tax included in the price of fuel.
3. funding (uncountable noun): Money provided to support an activity or purpose.
4. afford (verb): To have enough money to pay for something.
5. wage (noun): Money someone receives for doing their job.
6. cut back on (phrasal verb, past participle form): To reduce how much you use, do or buy something.
7. luxuries (plural noun): Things or activities that are enjoyable but not necessary.
8. standing charges (plural noun phrase): Fixed amounts charged for an energy supply, separate from the cost of the energy used.

Grammar Focus: Present perfect with “since”
Use the present perfect for a situation that began in the past and continues now. Form it with “have” or “has” plus a past participle. In the reading, “has been unable” connects Emily's past difficulty with her situation now, while “since” introduces when that situation began.
Example from the text: "Emily Nelson has been unable to find a job since leaving college last year."
