01Finding the item the shopper actually meansAsk one question that narrows the search · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Confirm product variation before searching for stock.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A shopper asks for the blue shirt in an advertisement. The ad shows two blue shirts: linen L214 and cotton C218. The shopper wants a medium cotton shirt; local availability has not been checked. Confirm item and size before discussing stock.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

stock-keeping unit
An identifier for a particular product variation, often abbreviated SKU, such as a specific shirt size and color.
barcode
A machine-readable pattern used to identify an item when scanned.
size range
The set of sizes in which a product is offered.
fitting room
A designated shop space where customers can try on clothing.

Useful word combinations

  • check the item code: compare the exact product identifier
  • find the right size: locate the variation the shopper requests
  • point out the fitting room: show where clothing can be tried on
  • narrow down the choice: reduce options by clarifying the shopper's needs

C Notice the language

Ask one question that narrows the search

Color alone may describe several items. Use material, code, and size to distinguish them without claiming availability prematurely.

  • Do you mean the ___ or the ___?
  • The item code is ___; is that the one?
  • I'll check this store's stock in ___.

Improve this: "The blue one is over there."

See a clearer version

Do you mean the cotton C218 or the linen L214?

The question identifies the ambiguity instead of giving uncertain directions.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Shopper: The advertisement has two blue shirts. Mine is cotton, not linen.
Associate: You mean ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. C218

  1. A. C218 Correct. C218 is the cotton item specified in the case.
  2. B. L214 L214 is the linen item, which the shopper excluded.
  3. C. both advertised shirts The shopper has identified only the cotton shirt.
  4. D. the linen variation The shopper explicitly said cotton rather than linen.
2. Cashier: This label identifies one color-and-size variation.
Associate: That is its ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. stock-keeping unit

  1. A. size range A size range lists available sizes rather than identifying one variation.
  2. B. product family A product family can include multiple variations.
  3. C. department name A department name identifies a broader store area.
  4. D. stock-keeping unit Correct. The stock-keeping unit identifies the particular variation.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. Two blue shirts in one advertisement

An associate clarifies a shopper's request on the sales floor.

  1. Associate: There are two blue shirts in that advertisement. Do you mean the cotton or linen version?
  2. Shopper: The cotton one, please. I want a medium, but I can't find it here.
  3. Associate: The cotton code is C218. Let me confirm that against the item pictured on your phone.
  4. Shopper: Yes, that's the one. The linen shirt beside it looks almost the same color.
  5. Associate: I'll check the stock-keeping unit for medium C218, so we get that exact variation.
  6. Shopper: Does being in the advertisement mean you definitely have that size in this branch today?
  7. Associate: No. The advertisement identifies the product; local stock needs a separate check for your variation.
  8. Shopper: Thanks. Could I see a large for comparison if the medium isn't available here?
  9. Associate: We can discuss that after checking, but I won't substitute a different size without asking.
  10. Shopper: That's fine. Medium cotton C218 is my first choice; the other size is only a possibility.
2. The code is clearer than the display location

A shopper brings a loose item from a mixed display.

  1. Associate: You found this near the jackets, but its label identifies it as a lightweight overshirt.
  2. Shopper: I thought everything on that rail belonged to the same range and fitted similarly.
  3. Associate: Items can be moved. Let's check the product code and size label on this one.
  4. Shopper: The code is O316 and the label says small. Does it come in a medium?
  5. Associate: The listed size range includes medium; that doesn't yet confirm one is in this shop.
  6. Shopper: Could you check while I compare how the small feels in the fitting room?
  7. Associate: Certainly. The fitting rooms are beside the mirror, and I'll search this exact code.
  8. Shopper: Please don't bring a similar jacket instead. I need the same material and style.
  9. Associate: I'll look for medium O316, and tell you clearly if that variation isn't available.
  10. Shopper: Great. The label gives us a more precise starting point than where I found it.
3. A gift request without a guessed size

An associate helps a shopper buying a gift.

  1. Associate: What do you know about the item they liked: its color, material, or exact code?
  2. Shopper: Only that it was a green sweater in your window. I don't know their size.
  3. Associate: There are two green styles there. Could you point to the one they mentioned?
  4. Shopper: The one with the textured front. I don't want you to guess their size from a photograph.
  5. Associate: I won't. We can identify the style now and leave the size choice open.
  6. Shopper: Could I see the size range and the stated gift exchange terms before deciding?
  7. Associate: Yes. I'll show both, without treating an available range as proof every size is stocked.
  8. Shopper: That would help. I may ask them for a size before buying anything today.
  9. Associate: This is the textured style, code K420. You can use that code when you return.
  10. Shopper: I'll do that. Identifying the item first saves us choosing the wrong green sweater.

Scenario 2 · A barcode belongs to the packaging, not the loose item

A shopper presents a mug inside a box marked for a larger model. The mug base shows M12; the box shows M18. No sale has been entered.

Role A: Associate: compare the two identifiers and find the matching label.

Role B: Shopper: want the smaller M12 mug and its correct price.

Possible opening: “Can you scan the box? I put the mug in it myself.”

Success checks
  • Identify the mug actually selected.
  • Keep the mismatched box separate.
  • Confirm the exact item before quoting a price.
Add a complication

The shopper assumes the larger model's displayed price applies.

Studying alone? Speak both roles aloud. For each scenario, prepare for two minutes, speak for one minute, then answer a follow-up. Switch roles and repeat using fewer notes. Use only the supplied facts; identify missing information instead of inventing it.

Compare scenario 1 with a model response
The advertisement shows two blue shirts, so let me check which one you mean. Is it the cotton shirt, code C218, in medium? Once we've confirmed that variation, I can check this store's stock. The picture alone doesn't tell us whether your size is available here today.

This is one possible spoken response, not the only acceptable wording. Preserve the case facts when you try your own version.

Check your reasoning

Compare with the case above and the model response. Can you point to the words that support your factual statement? Is your question about something the case leaves open? If you introduce a possible outcome, clearly label it as a possibility.

Review, then try again

  • Keeps the case facts accurate; clearly separates confirmed and unknown information.
  • Uses understandable sentences and explains specialist terms when the listener needs it.
  • Responds to the other person's question, checks understanding, and uses an appropriate tone.
  • Makes the requested action or unresolved question clear without inventing authority or facts.

Revise one sentence and repeat the response. A useful response can be clear even with a few grammar errors; judge meaning and task completion, not accent.

02Comparing fit, materials, and label informationCompare the same practical requirement · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Use label evidence instead of broad claims about quality.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A shopper needs a machine-washable jacket. Jacket A is unlined and its label permits machine washing; B is lined and its label states dry-clean only. Both cost $60. Compare the stated care requirements without inventing comfort or durability claims.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

care label
The attached label stating the manufacturer's garment-care information.
lining
An inner layer of fabric inside a garment or bag.
seam
The join where pieces of fabric are stitched together.
fit
How a garment's shape and size sit on the wearer.

Useful word combinations

  • check the care label: read the supplied care information
  • compare the fit: discuss how two garments sit on the wearer
  • allow room through the shoulders: describe the space available in the upper garment
  • check the seam position: identify where a stitched join sits

C Notice the language

Compare the same practical requirement

Price, lining, fit, and care are different features. Identify the deciding requirement and avoid extending the label's meaning beyond what it states.

  • Both cost ___, but the labels say ___.
  • This matches your requirement for ___.
  • The label doesn't establish ___.

Improve this: "The lined one is better."

See a clearer version

The lined one requires dry cleaning; the other matches your machine-wash requirement.

The comparison uses the shopper's criterion rather than an undefined quality claim.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Shopper: Which one matches my machine-wash requirement?
Associate: ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: D. Jacket A

  1. A. Jacket B Jacket B is labelled dry-clean only.
  2. B. Both jackets equally The care requirements differ between the jackets.
  3. C. Neither jacket Jacket A does meet the stated care requirement.
  4. D. Jacket A Correct. Jacket A's label permits machine washing.
2. Shopper: What is the extra fabric inside this jacket called?
Associate: The ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. lining

  1. A. outer shell The outer shell is on the outside rather than inside.
  2. B. care label A care label carries information, not the main inner layer.
  3. C. lining Correct. A lining is the inner fabric layer.
  4. D. seam A seam is a stitched join, not a fabric layer.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. The care requirement decides the first comparison

An associate compares two jackets with a shopper.

  1. Associate: Both jackets cost sixty dollars. Is care, warmth, or fit your first priority today?
  2. Shopper: Care. I need something labelled for machine washing because I travel frequently for work.
  3. Associate: Jacket A's label permits that. Jacket B is lined and marked dry-clean only.
  4. Shopper: Then the similar price doesn't mean they have the same care requirements at all.
  5. Associate: Correct. The labels give different instructions, even though both jackets share the same displayed price.
  6. Shopper: I'll consider A first. Does being unlined automatically mean it will fit more loosely?
  7. Associate: No. Lining and fit are separate features; trying it on will help you compare.
  8. Shopper: May I try the medium and large rather than choose from the label alone?
  9. Associate: Certainly. I'll bring both available sizes so you can compare the shoulder and body fit.
  10. Shopper: That's useful. The care label narrows the choice, but I still need the right fit.
2. Sleeve length and body fit are different

An associate checks a shopper's concern outside the fitting room.

  1. Associate: Which part doesn't feel right: the body, the shoulders, or the length of the sleeves?
  2. Shopper: The body feels fine, but the sleeves cover more of my hands than I expected.
  3. Associate: So going up a size may not address the issue you're describing with sleeve length.
  4. Shopper: Exactly. I don't want more room everywhere just because the sleeve shape isn't suitable.
  5. Associate: We have another cut in the same listed size. Would you like to compare that?
  6. Shopper: Yes, as long as it's clear we're comparing a different cut, not identical sizing across styles.
  7. Associate: Certainly. Size labels don't guarantee the same fit in every design or product range.
  8. Shopper: Could you show me where the shoulder seam sits on each one for comparison?
  9. Associate: Yes, here is the stitched join on each garment. Tell me which feels more comfortable.
  10. Shopper: The second style suits me better. I'll check its care label before making my decision.
3. Reading a label without inventing a guarantee

A shopper asks about an unfamiliar fabric-care symbol.

  1. Shopper: Does this label mean the sweater will never shrink, whatever washing method I use?
  2. Associate: No. The label gives care information; it doesn't make an unlimited guarantee about every method.
  3. Shopper: Could you help me find the manufacturer's written explanation rather than guess at the symbol?

Your goal

Distinguish an average bundle price from a single-item price.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

A shopper has one juice bottle priced at $3. The shelf offer says two qualifying bottles for $5; single bottles remain $3. The shopper expects one bottle for $2.50. Explain the quantity condition without treating half the bundle price as the single-item offer.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

shelf-edge label
A label beside displayed goods stating the item description and price.
unit price
A price expressed per standard amount, such as per liter, for comparison.
multibuy offer
A promotion requiring the purchase of a stated number of qualifying items.
price check
A request to verify an item's applicable price when information is unclear.

Useful word combinations

  • match the label to the item: compare product description and code
  • read the offer conditions: identify qualifying quantity and items
  • compare the unit prices: use the same standard quantity for both products
  • request a price check: ask a colleague to verify a disputed price

C Notice the language

Explain the condition that changes the price

Use the offer's quantity condition and the same price basis. Don't infer a universal pricing rule from this fictional promotion.

  • The offer applies when you buy ___.
  • One item remains ___ under these terms.
  • That is the average, not ___.

Improve this: "They're two-fifty each."

See a clearer version

Two cost five under the offer; one alone costs three.

The quantity condition stays attached to the discounted price.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Shopper: I have only one bottle. Is it two-fifty?
Cashier: Under this offer, one bottle costs ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. three dollars

  1. A. five dollars Five dollars is the two-bottle total.
  2. B. one dollar fifty That price is not stated anywhere in the offer.
  3. C. three dollars Correct. The single-bottle price remains three dollars.
  4. D. two dollars fifty That is the average only when two qualifying bottles are bought.
2. Shopper: These packs have different amounts. How can I compare on the same quantity basis?
Associate: Compare their ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. unit prices

  1. A. multibuy quantities alone Required purchase quantities alone do not give price per standard amount.
  2. B. unit prices Correct. Unit prices use a common standard quantity.
  3. C. package totals alone Package totals alone can hide different pack sizes.
  4. D. advertised percentage discounts alone Discount percentages can start from different prices and do not establish cost per standard quantity.

Your goal

Explain the correction and verify the revised quantity and total.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Two $8 mugs were scanned, but the shopper has only one. There are no additional charges in this exercise. Payment has not begun. Acknowledge the duplicate, correct the recorded quantity through the store's checkout process, and confirm $8 before payment.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

point-of-sale system
The store equipment and software used to record purchases and process checkout, often abbreviated POS.
subtotal
The total for entered items before any separately applied tax, fee, or discount adjustments.
tender
A method or form of payment offered for a purchase, such as cash or a card.
receipt
A record of a completed transaction showing relevant purchase and payment details.

Useful word combinations

  • review the basket: check items and quantities before payment
  • remove a duplicate scan: correct an item entered twice
  • confirm the amount due: state the total still requiring payment
  • check transaction status: establish whether payment has completed

C Notice the language

Correct the basket before taking payment

Name the exact duplicate instead of saying the register is wrong generally. Keep a basket correction separate from a refund after payment.

  • The basket shows ___, but you have ___.
  • Payment hasn't started, so this is ___.
  • The revised quantity is ___ and the total is ___.

Improve this: "I'll refund that extra one."

See a clearer version

Payment hasn't started; I'll correct the duplicate entry before you pay.

A refund would imply money had already been paid.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Shopper: Have I already paid for the extra mug?
Cashier: No, this is a basket correction, not a ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. refund

  1. A. subtotal review Reviewing a subtotal also occurs before payment.
  2. B. refund Correct. A refund returns money after payment; payment hasn't begun.
  3. C. quantity check Checking quantity is part of this correction.
  4. D. price display The screen can display the corrected price before payment.
2. Shopper: You've removed the duplicate mug, but I haven't paid yet. Is the purchase finished?
Cashier: The quantity is corrected; the payment ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. has not yet been taken

  1. A. has not yet been taken Correct. Correcting the basket does not complete the still-unpaid transaction.
  2. B. has already been refunded A refund would return a payment, but none has been taken.
  3. C. has now been completed The shopper explicitly says payment has not happened yet.
  4. D. was taken before the correction This places payment before the correction, contradicting the stated sequence.

E Conversations

Three conversations at work

Original fictional training conversations. Each line is one speaking turn.

1. One mug entered twice

A cashier corrects the item count before payment. In this fictional shop, one mug costs eight dollars and no other charges apply.

  1. Cashier: The point-of-sale system shows two mugs at eight dollars each. Did you choose two?
  2. Shopper: No, I have only one mug. I think the barcode was scanned twice just now.
  3. Cashier: You're right. I'm sorry about the duplicate entry; payment hasn't begun for this basket.
  4. Shopper: Then I don't need a refund, because I haven't actually paid sixteen dollars yet?
  5. Cashier: Correct. I'll correct the basket quantity through the checkout system before taking any payment.
  6. Shopper: Could you show the updated line so I can check that only one remains?
  7. Cashier: Here it is: one mug at eight dollars. Please check the quantity on this line.
  8. Shopper: That's correct. I was worried the correction might remove the whole purchase by mistake.
  9. Cashier: The mug is still in your basket; only the duplicate entry has been removed.
  10. Shopper: I can see that now. Let's continue with the eight-dollar total for one mug.
2. A notification is not the register's final status

A cashier responds when a card-payment screen pauses.

  1. Cashier: The checkout hasn't shown a final payment result yet. Please wait while I check its status.
  2. Shopper: My phone has a banking notification. Doesn't that prove the sale is complete on your side?
  3. Cashier: It may relate to the payment attempt, but it doesn't confirm our checkout's final status.
  4. Shopper: I don't want to pay twice because two screens are giving different impressions.
  5. Cashier: I understand. I won't describe it as completed or ask for another attempt without checking.
  6. Shopper: Could you ask the supervisor to look at the transaction rather than guess from my phone?
  7. Cashier: Yes. I'll keep this sale reference with the query and explain the screen hasn't finalized.
  8. Shopper: Thank you. Please don't ask me to share account passwords to help with the check.
  9. Cashier: We don't need your password. The supervisor will review the store-side transaction information.
  10. Shopper: Then I'll wait for that result before choosing any next payment step with you.
3. A receipt choice before leaving

After a cash purchase, a cashier clarifies a shopper's preferred receipt format.

  1. Cashier: Your purchase is complete. Would you prefer the available paper receipt or a digital receipt?
  2. Shopper: Paper, please. I don't want to supply an email address just to record this purchase.
  3. Cashier: That's fine. I can print the receipt without adding you to a mailing list.
  4. Shopper: Thank you. Does choosing paper change the store's stated return terms for this item?
  5. Cashier: No. The receipt format doesn't alter the terms displayed for this purchase here.
  6. Shopper: Could you point out which line identifies the item, in case I need it later?
  7. Cashier: Certainly. This line shows the product description, quantity, and amount for your selected item.
  8. Shopper: I see cash listed as the tender below. That isn't an extra item, is it?
  9. Cashier: Exactly. It's payment information, not an extra product charge added to your basket.
  10. Shopper: That's clear. I'll keep the paper receipt with the product packaging when I get home.

A Read the situation

A moment at work

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

queue
A line of shoppers waiting for service; also called a line in US English.
accessible checkout
A checkout designed or arranged to support access needs, such as suitable space or counter height.
assistance call
A request to a colleague for help with a customer or store task.
checkout lane
A designated route and counter where shoppers complete purchases.

Useful word combinations

  • open another checkout: make another staffed payment point available
  • keep the aisle clear: preserve space for people to pass
  • offer assistance without assuming: ask what help the shopper wants
  • direct the next customer: guide the next person from the agreed queue order

C Notice the language

Ask what help is wanted

Address the shopper directly and offer the available route. Ask before handling personal belongings or mobility equipment.

  • Would you prefer ___ or ___?
  • Lane ___ has ___.
  • May I help with ___?

Improve this: "I'll move all your things for you."

See a clearer version

Would you like help with your basket, or would you prefer to keep it?

The question gives the shopper control over the help offered.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Shopper: I need a wider checkout, but I'll carry my own bag.
Associate: So your request concerns ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. checkout access

  1. A. home delivery No delivery arrangement has been requested by the shopper.
  2. B. product availability The shopper has not asked whether an item is stocked.
  3. C. checkout access Correct. The shopper needs space at the payment point.
  4. D. bagging assistance The shopper explicitly wants to keep and carry the bag.
2. Shopper: I would like some assistance getting to lane four.
Associate: Could you tell me ____?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. how you would like me to help

  1. A. how you like me would to help Place 'would' before 'like' in this indirect question.
  2. B. how you would like me to help Correct. The indirect question uses subject-verb order and 'me' after 'like'.
  3. C. how would you like me to help Use statement order after 'tell me': 'how you would like'.
  4. D. how you would like I to help Use the object form 'me' before the infinitive, not 'I'.

B Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

till float
The cash drawer starting balance provided for giving change, separate from the day's sales; often called the starting cash balance in US English.
cash discrepancy
A difference between counted cash and the amount expected from the store's records.
void
A cancelled transaction or item entry recorded by the point-of-sale system.
shift log
A store record of relevant events and open tasks passed between work shifts.

Useful word combinations

  • count against the record: compare counted amounts with expected figures
  • report a discrepancy: state the difference without inventing its cause
  • keep the transaction reference: preserve the identifier needed for a check
  • accept the follow-up: explicitly agree to handle an unfinished task

C Notice the language

Hand over the observation, not an accusation

A discrepancy is a mismatch, not an established cause. Distinguish a cash check from a separate customer's price query.

  • The count is ___ against an expected ___.
  • The cause remains ___.
  • Can you take the follow-up on ___?

Improve this: "Five dollars was stolen."

See a clearer version

The first count is five dollars below the expected amount; the cause hasn't been established.

The revised report states the evidence without accusing anyone.

D Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Supervisor: Expected cash is one-eighty, first count one-seventy-five.
Cashier: That's a ____ difference.

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. five-dollar

  1. A. one-hundred-eighty-dollar That is the expected amount, not the difference.
  2. B. five-dollar Correct. The difference between the two figures is five dollars.
  3. C. ten-dollar Ten does not match the subtraction of these figures.
  4. D. one-hundred-seventy-five-dollar That is the counted amount, not the difference.
2. Cashier: Does a void entry show an item was sold twice?
Supervisor: No, it records an entry that was ____.

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. cancelled

  1. A. cancelled Correct. A void cancels a transaction or item entry.
  2. B. reserved Reservation holds an item rather than cancelling a sale entry.
  3. C. delivered Delivery concerns physical movement, not a void entry.
  4. D. restocked Restocking concerns inventory and does not define a checkout void.

1. A five-dollar difference needs checking
