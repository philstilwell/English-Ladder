01Production Flow and Daily ManagementGive a useful status update · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Distinguish completed work from pending work and explain its impact.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A production line made 840 units against a 1,000-unit target. The log records 40 minutes of downtime; its cause is still being checked.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

throughput
Amount of work, patients, goods, cases, or transactions completed in a period of time.
cycle time
The time needed to complete one defined production or work cycle.
constraint
A limit that restricts the available choices or amount of work possible.
downtime
Time during which equipment or a service is unavailable for its intended work.

03 · Notice the language

Give a useful status update

Lead with the current state, then the important change or open item. Use completed-action language only for completed work. Add a next update point when it is known; do not invent a date to make the message sound complete.

  • We have completed ...; ... remains open.
  • As of ..., the status is ... .
  • The next update will cover ... .

Improve this: Everything is progressing well.

See a clearer version

Three checks are complete; two remain open and are being reviewed.

The revision gives observable progress. A positive label alone does not tell the reader what is complete or still needed.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence says a review is finished?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. The team has completed the review.

  1. A. The team plans to complete the review. 'Plans to complete' describes an intention, not completion.
  2. B. The team has completed the review. 'Has completed' states that the action is finished.
  3. C. The team is completing the review. 'Is completing' presents the work as in progress.
2. Which update distinguishes a plan from a confirmed fact?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. Delivery is planned for Friday; the carrier has not confirmed it.

  1. A. Delivery is planned for Friday; the carrier has not confirmed it. This explicitly labels the date as planned and identifies the missing confirmation.
  2. B. Delivery is confirmed for Friday because we planned it. A plan does not establish carrier confirmation.
  3. C. The delivery plan proves that arrival will be Friday. A plan cannot prove a future arrival time.

05 · Say it

Practice with a partner

Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.

Partner's role and follow-up

You need to brief someone who has only one minute. Ask what is complete, what is open, and which open item affects the next action.

Try a harder second round

The listener asks you to reduce the update to two sentences. Preserve the most important completed and pending items.

Studying alone? Give both sides of the conversation aloud. Pause before answering the follow-up, then repeat with fewer notes.

02Lean, Waste, and Continuous ImprovementGive feedback people can use · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Describe observed behavior, its effect, and a useful next step.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A team observes repeated waiting for shared tools during a kaizen event. A supervisor blames slow operators.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

kaizen
Continuous improvement through recurring examination and adjustment of work processes.
standard work
The documented current method for performing a task consistently.
waste
Activity or material that consumes resources without adding the intended value.
value stream
The sequence of activities involved in delivering a product or service to a customer.

03 · Notice the language

Give feedback people can use

Use a specific example instead of a personality label. Explain its effect, invite the person's perspective, and agree on a practical improvement. Focus on communication that can be changed, not accent or national identity.

  • In [specific situation], I noticed ... .
  • The effect was ... . What was happening from your perspective?
  • Could we agree to ... and review it ...?

Improve this: You are always careless.

See a clearer version

The last two reports omitted the agreed summary section.

The revision identifies observable work. 'Always' and a personality judgment make the feedback harder to verify or act on.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which feedback begins with observable behavior?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. The last two updates omitted the agreed completion date.

  1. A. The last two updates omitted the agreed completion date. The recipient can check the specific examples and discuss how to improve them.
  2. B. The last two updates seemed less professional than usual. Less professional is an evaluation; it does not identify a specific observable omission.
  3. C. I think the team needs to be more committed to the updates. Commitment is an interpretation, not a description of what the updates contained.
2. Which follow-up invites useful information before agreeing on support?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. What is making this part of the task difficult?

  1. A. Can you promise this will never happen again? A promise does not reveal the obstacle or what support would help.
  2. B. Would another reminder solve this problem? This proposes a solution before finding out what is making the task difficult.
  3. C. What is making this part of the task difficult? An open question may reveal a barrier the speaker has not considered.

03Defects, Scrap, and ReworkMake a complex point clear · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Explain a useful distinction in plain English.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A quality report combines scrapped units with units awaiting rework. The manager needs to understand the different consequences.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

defect
Failure, flaw, nonconformance, or error that prevents an output from meeting requirements.
scrap
Material or product that cannot be used as intended and is removed from the relevant production output.
rework
Additional processing intended to make nonconforming work meet requirements.
containment
Action intended to limit the spread or effect of a problem while it is addressed.

03 · Notice the language

Make a complex point clear

Start with the reader's question. Explain one unfamiliar term with familiar words, then give a relevant example or contrast. Check understanding without asking only 'Do you understand?'.

  • In this context, ... means ... .
  • The difference is that ... .
  • How would you explain that distinction to a colleague?

Improve this: We need to operationalize the implementation.

See a clearer version

We need to put the plan into practice.

The revised sentence names the action in familiar words. Specialist vocabulary is useful when it adds precision, but repeating abstract nouns can hide the meaning.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence explains 'backlog' rather than repeating it?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. The backlog is the work still waiting to be completed.

  1. A. The backlog is the work completed during the last period. Completed work is different from work still waiting.
  2. B. The backlog is the maximum work the team can finish in a period. This describes capacity, not the work waiting to be completed.
  3. C. The backlog is the work still waiting to be completed. This gives the meaning in familiar words.
2. Which follow-up best checks whether your explanation was clear?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. How would you describe the next step in your own words?

  1. A. Was the explanation detailed enough for you? This invites an opinion on detail, not a demonstration of understanding.
  2. B. How would you describe the next step in your own words? An open request lets the listener show their understanding.
  3. C. Would you like me to repeat the explanation? Offering repetition may help, but it does not show how the listener understood the next step.

04Root Cause and Corrective ActionMatch certainty to evidence · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Separate observations, assumptions, and conclusions.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A defect appears after a supplier change, but several process settings also changed. A team member names the supplier as the root cause.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

root cause
Underlying reason a problem occurred, not merely the visible symptom.
5 Whys
Root-cause method that repeatedly asks why a problem occurred until the team reaches a controllable underlying cause.
fishbone
A cause-and-effect diagram used to organize possible contributors to a problem.
8D
Eight-discipline problem-solving report used to contain a problem, identify root cause, implement corrective action, and prevent recurrence.

03 · Notice the language

Match certainty to evidence

Say what the evidence shows, identify what is still unknown, and limit the conclusion to that evidence. Words such as 'may', 'suggests', and 'in this sample' are useful when the uncertainty is real. Do not soften an urgent, verified safety concern.

  • The available evidence shows ... .
  • This may indicate ..., but ... .
  • We have not yet established whether ... .

Improve this: The pilot proves this will work everywhere.

See a clearer version

The pilot worked in the tested setting; performance elsewhere remains untested.

The revision states the finding and its scope. It avoids turning limited evidence into a universal claim.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence preserves uncertainty about an unconfirmed cause?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. The change may have contributed to the delay.

  1. A. The change definitely caused the delay. 'Definitely' presents the cause as certain.
  2. B. The change may have contributed to the delay. 'May have contributed' presents a possibility without claiming the cause is established.
  3. C. The change must have caused the delay. 'Must have' expresses a strong inference that the evidence may not support.
2. A result comes from a small pilot in one location. Which phrase accurately limits the claim?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. In this pilot, the observed result was ...

  1. A. In this pilot, the observed result was ... This locates the finding in the setting actually studied.
  2. B. In similar locations, we can now expect the same result ... Similarity alone does not establish that the result will transfer.
  3. C. Across the service, the observed result was ... A pilot in one location does not establish a service-wide observation.

05 · Say it

Practice with a partner

Partner's role and follow-up

Try a harder second round

Someone asks for a yes-or-no answer when the evidence supports only a qualified answer. Be concise while preserving the uncertainty.

05Maintenance, Reliability, and ChangeoverOffer a workable tradeoff · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Negotiate scope or timing while making conditions explicit.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A planned maintenance window conflicts with a rush order. The maintenance team needs to explain the available options to the decision maker.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

preventive maintenance
Planned work intended to reduce failures and maintain equipment condition.
MTBF
Mean time between failures: average operating time between failures for a defined repairable system.
changeover
The work and time needed to switch equipment or a line to another product or setup.
spare parts
Replacement components kept available for maintenance or repair.

03 · Notice the language

Offer a workable tradeoff

Acknowledge the goal and explain the available choices. Connect an offer to its actual conditions. A useful response makes room for discussion without promising approval or resources you do not control.

  • We can ..., provided that ... .
  • Would you prefer ... or ...?
  • I can take that proposal for review; I cannot yet confirm ... .

Improve this: If you will approve the scope, we can assess the schedule.

See a clearer version

If you approve the scope, we can assess the schedule.

For an ordinary future condition, the if-clause uses present tense. The other clause can express the future consequence or possibility.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence presents a normal future condition clearly?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. If the scope changes, we will review the schedule.

  1. A. If the scope changes, we will review the schedule. The if-clause uses present tense; the main clause states the future response.
  2. B. If the scope will change, we will review the schedule. For this ordinary future condition, use present simple in the if-clause.
  3. C. If the scope changes, we reviewed the schedule. Reviewed places the response in the past instead of expressing the future response.
2. Which response negotiates a new request without silently promising extra work?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. We can assess the addition and explain its effect on the agreed scope.

  1. A. We have included it, although its scope has not been reviewed. It treats unreviewed work as already included.
  2. B. We guarantee the original date without assessing the addition. It promises an outcome without examining the consequences.
  3. C. We can assess the addition and explain its effect on the agreed scope. This offers a useful next step while keeping the approval and scope question open.

06EHS and Safety CommunicationAsk for an actionable next step · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Make a request with a clear action, purpose, and realistic timing.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

During a safety exercise, a learner notices an unclear equipment-isolation instruction. The local approved procedure must govern the work.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

EHS
Environment, health, and safety: organizational functions addressing environmental and workplace risks.
lockout/tagout
Procedures for controlling hazardous energy during specified work; actual steps must follow approved safety requirements.
near miss
An event that could have caused harm but did not result in the specified harmful outcome.
stop work
A call or authorized action to pause work when a safety concern requires attention under local procedures.

03 · Notice the language

Ask for an actionable next step

State the action and the information needed. Explain why it matters and specify a deadline only when one is given or agreed. 'Could you' is polite; repeated apologies can obscure a legitimate request. Urgent situations may require a direct request.

  • Could you provide ... so that ...?
  • Please confirm ... by [agreed time].
  • If that timing is not possible, please let me know ... .

Improve this: Please revert soonest with the necessary.

See a clearer version

Could you send the missing document and confirm when it will be available?

The revision names the document and requested response. It avoids expressions that may be unfamiliar or ambiguous for an international audience.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. What does 'Please send it by Thursday' normally mean?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. Send it no later than Thursday.

  1. A. Send it on Thursday, but not earlier. By Thursday permits earlier delivery; it is not restricted to that day.
  2. B. Keep sending it regularly until Thursday. This interprets a deadline as a continuing activity, which would use until.
  3. C. Send it no later than Thursday. 'By' sets the latest point, while 'after' refers to a later time.
2. Which request gives the recipient enough detail to act?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review?

  1. A. Could you confirm that you received my earlier message? This checks receipt, not which version is required for the review.
  2. B. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review? The requested action and information are explicit.
  3. C. Could you confirm that the review is important? This checks importance rather than identifying the needed document version.

07Supplier Quality and Incoming MaterialsAsk a precise question · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Clarify missing information before responding.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

An incoming inspection report cites a dimension without identifying the drawing revision. The supplier used a different revision.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

supplier quality
How well supplied goods and supplier processes meet specified requirements.
incoming inspection
A check of received materials or components against specified requirements.
specification
A detailed statement of required characteristics, performance, or materials.
deviation
A departure from an approved process, requirement, or expected condition.

03 · Notice the language

Ask a precise question

Name the exact word, fact, or requirement you need clarified. Ask one focused question at a time. Repeat your understanding and give the other person a chance to correct it.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which indirect question has the usual word order?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Could you confirm when the review starts?

  1. A. Could you confirm when starts the review? Place the subject 'the review' before 'starts'.
  2. B. Could you confirm when the review starts? The embedded question uses subject then verb: the review starts.
  3. C. Could you confirm when does the review start? Remove 'does' and use statement word order after 'confirm'.
2. Someone says the report is 'ready.' Which question best clarifies its approval status?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. Has the report been approved, or is it ready for review?

  1. A. Has the report been approved, or is it ready for review? This question distinguishes two meanings of 'ready' that affect the next action.
  2. B. Can you send the report to me this afternoon? This asks about delivery timing, not whether approval has happened.
  3. C. Who will present the report at the meeting? This asks about the presenter, not the report's approval status.

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence checks that responsibility has been accepted?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. Please confirm who will complete the outstanding check.

  1. A. Please confirm who will complete the outstanding check. It asks for an identifiable person or team responsible for the task.
  2. B. I mentioned the check in my earlier email. Mentioning a task does not show that anyone accepted it.
  3. C. The check appears somewhere in the notes. A written record alone does not identify who will act.
2. Which response best checks a critical handoff detail?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. So the result is pending and your team will follow up; is that correct?

  1. A. I assume all results are final, so we can finish. It changes pending information into a final result.
  2. B. I saw the message, so no clarification is needed. Receiving a message is not the same as confirming its meaning.
  3. C. So the result is pending and your team will follow up; is that correct? This repeats the status and responsibility and invites correction.
