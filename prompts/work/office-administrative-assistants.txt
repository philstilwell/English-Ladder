Lesson 01: Scheduling across calendars and time zones
Original fictional case: Colleague Maya asks assistant Arun to arrange a 3:00 meeting with another office. For the fictional meeting date, 3:00 in Maya's office equals noon at the other office. Both calendars are open at that equivalent time, but the request does not identify whose clock Maya means. Arun must confirm the intended office reference before sending invitations. No meeting duration, location, or recurrence has been specified.
Language workshop: Three o'clock in whose office?
Communication goal: Clarify a missing time reference and read back two equivalent local times before issuing a meeting invitation.
Language guidance: The supplied equivalence is 3:00 in Maya's office and noon in the other office on the fictional date. Do not put 3:00 on both local displays or assume the same difference applies on every date.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): Do you mean 3:00 in your office or the other office? / On this date, your 3:00 is their noon. / I want to make sure both offices receive the same meeting time. / Both calendars are open at that equivalent time. / The calendars are open, but I still need your intended time. / The two offices should not both be labeled 3:00. / You mean 3:00 in your office. / That will be noon in the other office. / This conversion is for the meeting date we are discussing. / I will not assume the same offset for every date. / An open slot is not an accepted invitation. / I will confirm the reference before sending. / How long should the meeting last? / We still need the meeting location. / We have not agreed a repeating schedule. / I have the intended office time and its equivalent clearly recorded.
Vocabulary: local time: Clock time at the specified location. / time zone: Region using a defined civil-time reference. / time reference: Location or standard that gives a clock time meaning. / equivalent time: Different clock display representing the same moment elsewhere. / calendar availability: Time shown as open or occupied in a calendar. / meeting request: Proposal to arrange a meeting with specified details. / invitation: Message asking identified people to attend an event. / organizer: Person responsible for arranging the meeting. / attendee: Person invited to or participating in a meeting. / noon: Twelve o'clock in the middle of the day. / date-specific: Valid for the particular date being discussed. / daylight saving time: Seasonal clock adjustment used in some locations; abbreviated DST. / Coordinated Universal Time: International time reference commonly abbreviated UTC. / offset: Difference between one time reference and another. / calendar entry: Event recorded in a scheduling system. / time display: Clock representation shown to a particular user. / scheduling conflict: Overlap between incompatible calendar commitments. / free slot: Unoccupied period shown in a calendar. / tentative hold: Provisional reservation not yet a final agreement. / acceptance: Response agreeing to the stated invitation. / duration: Length of time an event is intended to last. / recurrence: Pattern under which an event repeats. / location field: Part of an invitation identifying where the meeting occurs. / schedule readback: Restatement of the agreed scheduling details.
Speaking task: Complete the scheduling dialogue using the word bank. Read the two office times aloud as one meeting: 3:00 here, noon there, on the supplied date. Then read the unresolved duration and location. Complete the transfer, check the explained key, and repeat the corrected scheduling readback.
Partner: Maya: Colleague / Arun: Administrative assistant
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: Complete the scheduling clarification without adding a duration or treating availability as acceptance.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Maya: Let me check: three in my office, noon in theirs, on the date we're discussing. Nothing sent yet.
Arun: That's the schedule readback. I'll keep it while we obtain the duration and location rather than assume either.
Maya: I'll send those next. And this is only one meeting for now, not a weekly series.
Arun: Understood. Recurrence hasn't been authorized. I'll wait for the remaining details before sending the single meeting request.
END REFERENCE

Lesson 02: Taking useful calls and passing accurate messages
Original fictional case: Caller Lena Park needs the facilities team about delivery D18, not the finance team about an invoice. Facilities extension is 214. Lena requests a callback before 4:00, but team availability is unknown. Assistant Dev must confirm the delivery reference and preferred contact route, read back the message, and preserve the requested deadline as a request. The conversation supplies no payment problem or confirmed callback commitment.
Language workshop: Delivery D18 belongs with facilities
Communication goal: Route a caller by purpose, capture the reference and preferred reply route, and relay a requested deadline without guaranteeing it.
