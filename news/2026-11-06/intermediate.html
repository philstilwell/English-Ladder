📅 November 06, 2026 - Tech Stocks Slide as US Midterms Raise AI Concerns

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Tech stocks in the US and Asia dropped sharply after the US midterms raised fears that artificial intelligence (AI) development could slow. Democrats across the US campaigned to limit the growth of AI and data centres, the facilities that help power the technology. Democrats won control of both houses of Congress, the Senate and the House of Representatives. In Texas, Gina Hinojosa, who won the race for governor, had promised to put new data centre construction on hold. The Nasdaq Composite, an index that tracks a group of stocks, closed more than 5.5% lower. In Japan, the Nikkei 225 was down 6% in early trading as shares in chip companies Kioxia and Tokyo Electron lost value. In Seoul, the sell-off pushed the Kospi index down more than 8.5%, leading to a brief pause in trading to limit panic selling. Investors were already questioning whether the large sums going into AI would pay off. Investment strategist Charu Chanana said closer checks on data centre plans could delay projects and raise costs. Chanana pointed to the effects of data centres on the environment and on electricity bills as issues that could face closer review. OpenAI co-founder Greg Brockman said more work was needed to win support from local communities.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. raised fears (verb phrase): Made people worry that something bad might happen.
2. campaigned (verb): Worked to win public support for a plan during an election.
3. on hold (prepositional phrase): Delayed or paused for a period of time.
4. index (noun): A measure that shows how the prices of a group of stocks change.
5. closed (verb): Finished the trading day at the level given.
6. sell-off (noun): A period when many investors sell their stocks, causing prices to drop sharply.
7. pay off (phrasal verb): Bring enough financial benefit to make the money invested worthwhile.
8. win support (verb phrase): Persuade people to agree with a plan or back it.

Grammar Focus: Using “could” for possible effects
Use “could” followed by the base form of a verb to describe a possible effect. In the reading, “could” applies to both “delay” and “raise”. Chanana presents both effects as possibilities if data centre plans face closer checks.
Example from the text: "Investment strategist Charu Chanana said closer checks on data centre plans could delay projects and raise costs."
