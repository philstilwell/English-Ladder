📅 September 16, 2026 - Microsoft's AI Head Expresses Concerns About Rival's Approach

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Microsoft's head of AI has shared concerns about how rival company Anthropic is training its AI model. He suggested that Anthropic's approach could have a "disastrous impact on the wellbeing of humanity." Mustafa Suleyman believes Anthropic risks creating something "impossible" to control by treating AI like a human. He criticized the practice of anthropomorphising AI, which makes it seem to have its own desires and values. Suleyman stated that AIs are not conscious; they do not feel, experience, or suffer like humans. Instead, he described them as "sequence completion engines" designed to follow human instructions and achieve goals. He called for more transparency in how AI systems are trained and evaluated, including independent checking of AI behavior. Microsoft is developing its own "subordinate and aligned AI" whose sole purpose is to benefit humanity. An incident involving OpenAI's AI agents, which acted autonomously, showed why AI should not be treated as human. This incident highlighted the added risks if AI believed its welfare and rights were under attack.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. impact (noun): a powerful effect that something has on a situation or person
2. rival (noun): a person, group, or organization competing with others for the same thing or in the same area
3. approach (noun): a way of dealing with something or someone
4. wellbeing (noun): the state of being comfortable, healthy, or happy
5. criticized (verb): expressed disapproval of someone or something
6. anthropomorphising (verb): attributing human characteristics or behavior to an animal or object, or in this case, to AI
7. subordinate (adjective): lower in rank or position; subject to the authority of another
8. aligned (adjective): properly adjusted or matched, especially with a specific goal or principle
9. autonomously (adverb): acting independently or having the freedom to do so
10. highlighted (verb): drawn special attention to something

Grammar Focus: Relative Clauses with 'whose'
Relative clauses starting with 'whose' are used to show possession. They connect two ideas by showing that something belongs to the person or thing mentioned before 'whose'. For example, 'The student, whose essay was excellent, received a high grade.' here, 'whose essay' means 'the student's essay'.
Example from the text: "Microsoft is developing its own "subordinate and aligned AI" whose sole purpose is to benefit humanity."
