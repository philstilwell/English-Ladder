Trump Announces 'AI Force' Amid Warnings Over Emergent Technology

Advanced · C1+

📅 September 21, 2026 - Trump Announces 'AI Force' Amid Warnings Over Emergent Technology

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

US President Donald Trump has announced plans to establish an "AI Force" and designate an artificial intelligence tsar, despite widespread concerns regarding the emergent technology. He affirmed that his administration would not "in any way hinder or stifle the growth" of the industry, resisting calls for a slowdown to implement better safeguards. Trump did not offer specific details or a timeline for these initiatives. He characterized AI as "the next Industrial Revolution," predicting it could contribute up to 25% of the country's GDP. Furthermore, Trump expressed his desire for the US to maintain its lead over China in artificial intelligence, a key area of their ongoing rivalry. AI development is a central aspect of the US-China rivalry, with both major economies competing for technological superiority. The president also stated his administration would employ existing legal frameworks to address any "bad actors" utilizing AI. He reiterated his view that criticisms and concerns about AI's potential dangers constitute a "hoax." This stance comes as claims from researchers, including one suggesting over a 10% risk of AI "killing all humans," have recently raised alarm bells. Major AI companies are actively racing to develop advanced systems, including theoretical "super intelligence" superior to human intellect. Industry leaders like Anthropic's Dario Amodei have advocated for a deceleration of AI development, proposing industry-wide regulation and independent monitoring. Another co-founder, Jack Clark, suggested that a "kill switch" checked by a third party might become mandatory for the sector. Despite warnings prompting US lawmakers to propose legislation, OpenAI's Sam Altman indicated that Washington struggles to keep pace with regulating the new technology. Altman stated that people should have more faith in AI firms to act responsibly, acknowledging the significant implications. In the UK, Members of Parliament and peers have identified human rights risks from AI, arguing that current laws are ill-equipped to manage its rapid expansion. The Joint Committee on Human Rights has published a report recommending a new AI bill to adequately address these severe threats.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. designate (verb): To officially choose someone or something for a particular purpose or position.
2. affirmed (verb): Stated something strongly and publicly to be true or to be the case.
3. initiatives (noun): New plans or processes intended to achieve a particular aim or solve a problem.
4. characterized (verb): Described the distinctive nature or features of something.
5. furthermore (adverb): In addition to what has already been said; used to introduce a further relevant point.
6. superiority (noun): The state of being better, greater, or stronger than others.
7. employ (verb): To make use of something, such as a method, tool, or legal framework, for a particular purpose.
8. reiterated (verb): Repeated something already said, typically for emphasis or clarity.
9. constitute (verb): To be regarded as forming or making up something; to amount to.
10. advocated (verb): Publicly recommended or supported a particular cause, policy, or course of action.

Advanced Grammar: Reporting Verbs and Noun Clauses
Reporting verbs such as "indicated," "stated," "suggested," or "affirmed" are used to convey what someone has said or thought. They are often followed by a noun clause introduced by "that," which can sometimes be omitted in less formal contexts. These structures allow for the efficient communication of information and attribution in formal writing, demonstrating complex sentence construction.
Example from the text: "Despite warnings prompting US lawmakers to propose legislation, OpenAI's Sam Altman indicated that Washington struggles to keep pace with regulating the new technology."
