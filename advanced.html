📅 November 21, 2026 - New Hunger Games Cast Confront Pressure Through Mutual Support
The young cast of Sunrise on the Reaping describe the pressures of joining an established franchise and the mutual support that has helped them handle publicity and emotionally intense filming.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Sunrise on the Reaping places Joseph Zada, Mckenna Grace and Whitney Peak at the centre of the latest Hunger Games instalment. Set 24 years before the original film, it adapts Suzanne Collins’ 2025 novel of the same name. The sixth film joins a series whose worldwide earnings have exceeded $3.3 billion since 2012. Beginning a gruelling press run, the trio showed a mixture of anticipation, anxiety and excitement surrounding the release. Grace brings substantial screen experience, including appearances in The Handmaid’s Tale and two recent Ghostbusters films. Nevertheless, she described joining the franchise as deeply unsettling and unlike anything her previous work had prepared her for. Peak joked that she and Zada repeatedly sought out Grace when they felt stuck or confused while promoting the film, while Zada described her as a mentor. Zada plays the teenage Haymitch, a District 12 participant portrayed by Woody Harrelson in the original films. Reviews have been generally positive, with several outlets praising Zada while acknowledging the difficulty of stepping into Harrelson's shoes. Having spent months studying Harrelson’s earlier portrayals, Zada used his research as a foundation for his own interpretation. When they met halfway through filming, Harrelson recommended Cool Hand Luke, which Zada said helped ground his portrayal, and Harold and Maud, which he described as an enjoyable watch. Jesse Plemons regarded Zada’s role as a substantial undertaking and expressed admiration for his stamina and composure. An ensemble cast including Ralph Fiennes, Maya Hawke, Kieran Culkin and Elle Fanning returns audiences to a brutal dystopian world during the fiftieth Hunger Games. Within that fictional society, the ruling Capitol organises annual games involving Panem’s twelve districts as punishment for rebellion. Peak described the work as intensely stressful at times, making lighter moments between difficult scenes particularly valuable. Grace linked the cast’s close bonds to the days they spent sharing physically strenuous and emotionally intense work.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. gruelling (adjective): Requiring prolonged, exhausting physical or mental effort.
2. anticipation (noun): An expectant feeling about an approaching event, often involving excitement or uncertainty.
3. sought out (phrasal verb, past simple): Deliberately approached a particular person to obtain help, advice or reassurance.
4. stepping into Harrelson's shoes (idiomatic verb phrase, -ing form): Taking over a role associated with Harrelson, with the expectations that come from succeeding an established actor.
5. interpretation (noun): An actor’s particular understanding and presentation of a role through creative choices.
6. ground (verb, base form): To give an artistic portrayal a convincing basis that makes it feel believable.
7. undertaking (noun): A substantial task or responsibility requiring considerable effort and commitment.
8. stamina (noun): The capacity to sustain physical or mental effort over an extended period.
9. ensemble cast (noun phrase): A group of actors whose collective participation is a significant feature of a production.
10. dystopian (adjective): Depicting an imagined society characterised by oppression, suffering or severely restricted freedom.

Advanced Grammar: Perfect participle clauses with a shared subject
Use “having” followed by a past participle to present an action as completed before the main action. The understood subject of the participle clause must match the main-clause subject; here, Zada both studied the portrayals and used the research.
Example from the text: "Having spent months studying Harrelson’s earlier portrayals, Zada used his research as a foundation for his own interpretation."

📅 November 20, 2026 - Anthropic's Market Surge Tests Confidence in AI
Anthropic's stock-market arrival reveals considerable enthusiasm for AI while leaving difficult questions about its commercial prospects, regulation and safety unresolved.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq stock exchange on Thursday against a backdrop of enormous spending on artificial intelligence (AI). The company reached a market value of roughly $1.76 trillion, placing it among the world's most valuable publicly traded businesses. As the first purely AI company to be publicly listed, Anthropic created a test for the wider industry. Its shares initially climbed to $111.53 before retreating to close at $97.17. The shares moved above their $83 allocation price, the price set for their initial distribution to investors, signalling considerable enthusiasm for AI. The broader Nasdaq Composite Index, however, closed nearly 2.3% lower. Nevertheless, Anthropic, the company behind Claude, faces intense scrutiny over whether AI will justify the resources devoted to it. A handful of major technology companies collectively spent more than $1 trillion on the AI buildout in the previous year alone. The pace of this spending has fuelled fears that prices may be inflated by excessive optimism. Economist Richard Whittle cautioned that bubbles burst when confidence in a positive future weakens, rather than necessarily on the day of a major deal. Ben Barringer, who leads technology research at Quilter Cheviot, expects substantial fluctuations in Anthropic's share price. Barringer warned that the initial exuberance, or unusually strong enthusiasm, would wear off. Investor strategist Neil Wilson said that Anthropic lacks an obvious peer against which its worth can be assessed. New laws governing AI tools are also a possibility. Some US politicians have indicated that they want AI tools to be safe and subject to guardrails, meaning limits intended to prevent harmful use. Donald Trump reiterated his opposition to robust regulation of AI, indicating resistance to substantial rules governing the technology. A bipartisan group of US lawmakers nevertheless formed a caucus, a group organised around a shared policy interest, to address AI regulation and safety. Anthropic executives, including co-founder Dario Amodei, have been outspoken in expressing fears that future AI could outsmart humans and potentially threaten humanity's survival. Despite having described its rapidly developing technology as potentially dangerous, Anthropic has made an exceptionally swift transition from start-up to public listing.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. reiterated (verb (past tense)): Stated a view or position again, often for emphasis; used especially in formal reporting.
2. intense scrutiny (noun phrase): Close, sustained examination that subjects claims or decisions to demanding questions.
3. exuberance (uncountable noun): Strong, energetic enthusiasm that, in financial reporting, can suggest optimism exceeding what the evidence warrants.
4. wear off (phrasal verb): To gradually lose strength or intensity, especially when an initial feeling or effect fades.
5. peer (countable noun): A sufficiently similar company that can provide a useful basis for assessing another company's performance or worth.
6. fluctuations (plural noun): Changes in a price, amount or level as it moves up and down rather than steadily in one direction.
7. guardrails (plural noun): Rules or limits intended to prevent harmful behaviour while allowing an activity to continue.
8. robust regulation (noun phrase): Strong, substantial rules intended to exercise meaningful control over an activity or industry.
9. bipartisan (adjective): Involving members of two political parties, indicating cooperation across a party divide.
10. outspoken (adjective): Expressing views publicly and directly, including views that may attract disagreement.

Advanced Grammar: Concession with despite and a perfect participle clause
Use “despite” with an “-ing” clause to acknowledge a point that makes the main claim seem unexpected. The perfect form “having described” places that action before the main event; its understood subject is Anthropic, also the subject of the main clause.
Example from the text: "Despite having described its rapidly developing technology as potentially dangerous, Anthropic has made an exceptionally swift transition from start-up to public listing."

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

5. contingent on (adjective phrase): Dependent on a particular condition being fulfilled, rather than certain to happen.
6. corrective action (noun phrase): Measures taken to remedy an identified problem or failure.
7. deficiencies (plural noun): Weaknesses or shortcomings that prevent something from meeting the required standard.
8. precipitated (verb, past simple): Brought about or triggered an event or decision, often with a suggestion of suddenness.
9. in keeping with (prepositional phrase): Consistent with a stated principle, policy or expected standard of behaviour.

📅 November 16, 2026 - Four Planets Form November’s Pre-dawn Parade
November’s four-planet display combines striking visual arrangements with practical observational challenges, particularly Mercury’s faint appearance near the horizon.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

November’s pre-dawn sky offers a diagonal configuration of four planets. Mercury, Venus, Mars and Jupiter will be observable without optical equipment in the eastern to south-eastern sky. Of these four planets, Mercury will be the hardest to discern. As the smallest planet and the one nearest the Sun, Mercury is persistently caught in solar glare. Its visibility is confined to twilight, and it remains faint and low on the horizon. An unobstructed south-eastern view across a flat horizon offers the recommended setting for spotting Mercury. What makes Venus especially conspicuous is that only the Moon appears brighter in the night sky. Among the major planets, Venus has the greatest reflectivity, the capacity to send incoming sunlight back into space. Observers can distinguish Venus from nearby Spica by looking for a steady glow rather than the star’s twinkling. Mars and Jupiter will be in particularly striking apparent proximity on 15 and 16 November. According to astronomer Gideon Bendavid-Walker at the Royal Observatory Greenwich, their angular separation, the angle between their positions in the sky, will be just 1.2 degrees. He compares that interval with the width of a finger held at arm’s length. Near Regulus, the two planets may resemble a group of three stars. While Uranus will be setting in the west before sunrise, Saturn and Neptune will be visible for a time in the evening. Observers should choose a location away from artificial skyglow with a good view of the eastern horizon, allowing around twenty minutes for their eyes to acclimatise to darkness. The recommended viewing period for the planetary parade is approximately 45–60 minutes before sunrise.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. configuration (noun): The arrangement of several objects in relation to one another, considered as a whole.
2. discern (verb): To manage to see or recognise something that is difficult to distinguish from its surroundings.
3. solar glare (noun phrase): Intense light from the Sun that makes nearby objects difficult to see.
4. confined to (adjective phrase): Restricted to a particular situation, place or period; here, a limited lighting condition.
5. unobstructed (adjective): Having nothing in the way that blocks the view.
6. conspicuous (adjective): Readily noticeable because something stands out strongly from its surroundings.
7. reflectivity (noun): The capacity of a surface to send incoming light back rather than absorb it.
8. apparent proximity (noun phrase): Closeness as it appears to an observer, without establishing the actual distance between the objects.
9. angular separation (noun phrase): The angle between the directions in which two objects are seen, rather than the distance between them in space.
10. acclimatise (verb): To become accustomed to different surrounding conditions; here, for the eyes to become accustomed to darkness.

Advanced Grammar: Pseudo-cleft sentences for explanatory emphasis
A pseudo-cleft uses a “what” clause followed by “be” to emphasise the information that follows. Here, the opening clause establishes what needs explaining, while the clause after “is” foregrounds the reason Venus stands out.
Example from the text: "What makes Venus especially conspicuous is that only the Moon appears brighter in the night sky."

SpaceX recovered Starship’s upper stage for the first time after a flight lasting twenty-two and a half hours. The returning vehicle hovered beside its launch tower before enormous mechanical arms, nicknamed chopsticks, gripped it. The booster triggered an automated abort, a cancellation initiated by its own systems, before diverting to the ocean, hovering above the water and exploding. During its journey into space, the upper stage released 26 satellites belonging to the Starlink constellation, a satellite system providing high-speed internet. The Ship completed fourteen circuits of Earth before returning to the launch tower. Before launch, SpaceX said an effort to catch the Ship would be contingent on a careful assessment of the vehicle in space. Had problems emerged, the Ship would have been brought down in the ocean instead. Developing a fully reusable rocket remains a long-held ambition for SpaceX. The company needs dependable recovery of both Starship sections to enable repeated flights and sharply lower costs. Falcon 9 already illustrates the financial value of partial reuse: its rockets fly more than 100 times annually, and reusing the lower section has substantially reduced launch costs. SpaceX had already caught the Super Heavy booster on three earlier test flights. Prof Malcolm Macdonald, president of the Royal Aeronautical Society, said landing an entire Starship would represent a major advance in spaceflight. In his assessment, the next milestone is to combine the two recovery achievements in one flight and demonstrate that the process works dependably, repeatedly and economically. Starship’s progress has direct implications for Nasa’s ambition to land humans on the Moon in 2028, a highly ambitious timetable. SpaceX’s Nasa contract requires it to configure the Ship as a lander that will eventually carry astronauts to the lunar surface. Starship’s development has proceeded more slowly than SpaceX intended, and considerable progress in the coming months is necessary to meet its commitments.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. automated abort (noun phrase): A cancellation of a planned operation triggered by a control system rather than a direct human decision.
2. diverting (verb, present participle): Changing course towards an alternative destination instead of continuing along the intended route.
3. constellation (noun): A group of satellites organised to operate together as a system.
4. contingent on (adjective phrase): Dependent on a particular condition being satisfied before something can proceed.
5. long-held ambition (noun phrase): An important goal maintained over an extended period, without implying that it has been achieved.
6. milestone (noun): A significant achievement that marks progress towards a larger objective.
7. economically (adverb): In a way that keeps costs low enough for an activity to be financially practical.
8. implications (plural noun): Possible consequences or wider effects that a development has for another undertaking.
9. configure (verb): Arrange or adapt a vehicle’s design and equipment so that it can perform a particular function.
10. commitments (plural noun): Responsibilities or promised undertakings that an organisation is expected to fulfil.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. commissioned (verb, past participle): Formally requested by an organisation or authority to investigate an issue or produce a report.
2. radical overhaul (noun phrase): A thorough redesign that changes how a system operates, rather than adjusting individual details.
3. entitlements (plural noun): Forms of support that people have a right to receive under the applicable rules.
4. subsidies (plural noun): Public funds used to cover part of a cost, here to encourage employment opportunities.
5. frames (verb, third-person singular present): Presents an issue in a particular way that directs attention towards certain choices or considerations.
6. incentives (plural noun): Opportunities or advantages intended to encourage a particular action.
7. sanctions (plural noun): Penalties for failing to meet requirements, here the possible loss of some welfare support.
8. from scratch (adverbial phrase): Starting again with the fundamental design rather than simply modifying existing arrangements.
9. monetary savings (plural noun phrase): Reductions in the amount of money spent, distinguished from other possible improvements.
10. safety net (noun phrase): Support intended to prevent vulnerable people from being left without essential help.

Read the story

Hazel dormouse numbers show tentative signs of recovery after decades of decline. The National Dormouse Monitoring Programme, led by the People’s Trust for Endangered Species, has monitored sites in England and Wales since 1998. Its data indicate that dormouse numbers have fallen by almost 60 per cent since 2000, equivalent to an average annual decline of 3.5 per cent. Although the monitoring data point to a possible increase of nearly 10 per cent between 2020 and 2025, researchers stress that there is considerable uncertainty around that estimate. The apparent upturn coincided with springs and summers that were warmer and drier than average. Hazel dormice typically hibernate from around October to May, spending the winter in nests on the ground. In spring and summer, cold, wet weather or a shortage of food can prompt dormice to enter torpor, a short spell of deep sleep that conserves energy. Warmer summers may reduce the need for torpor and leave dormice with more energy for feeding and reproduction, researchers suggest. Ecologist Charlotte Armitage cautioned that it remains unclear how warmer, wetter winters might affect dormice. The researchers say the potential longer-term effects of climate change on the species warrant further investigation. Once widespread, the hazel dormouse is now largely confined to southern England and parts of Wales. Hazel dormice have become much rarer amid widespread woodland loss, and native populations have disappeared from around 20 English counties. Conservationists stress that safeguarding the woodlands and hedgerows where dormice live remains essential to their long-term survival.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. tentative (adjective): Based on evidence that suggests a possibility but still leaves room for doubt.
2. equivalent to (adjective followed by a preposition): Corresponding in value when expressed on another scale or in another form.
3. point to (prepositional verb): Suggest a conclusion on the basis of available evidence, leaving room for uncertainty.
4. upturn (noun): A shift towards improvement, especially a rise after a period of falling numbers or weaker results.
5. coincided with (prepositional verb in the past tense): Occurred during the same period as something else, with any causal relationship left open.
6. torpor (uncountable noun): A temporary state like deep sleep in which an animal becomes much less active and uses less energy.
7. conserves (verb, third-person singular present simple): Keeps more of a resource available by reducing how much is used.
8. warrant (verb): Provide sufficient reason to take an action or examine something more closely.
9. confined to (past participle followed by the preposition ‘to’): Restricted in range to a particular area or set of places.
10. safeguarding (verb in the “-ing” form): Taking steps to prevent damage or loss.

📅 November 08, 2026 - Leonid Meteor Shower to Peak on 17 November
Cloud and possible fog could hamper early sightings of the Leonids across the UK, while the Moon’s position may improve viewing prospects around the mid-November peak.

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The Leonid meteor shower is set to begin this weekend and continue through the end of November. The shower is expected to reach its peak on the night of 17 November. The report estimates that observers might see as many as 10 to 15 meteors per hour. The shower originates in dust that Comet 55P/Tempel-Tuttle leaves in its wake as it orbits the Sun. Those particles can enter Earth’s atmosphere at up to 71 kilometres per second, a speed the report says makes the Leonids the fastest meteors in the sky. The Leonids’ radiant, the point from which they appear to emerge, lies near the head of the constellation Leo. That part of the sky offers a point of reference, although the meteors can appear anywhere in the sky. Experts advise seeking a safe, dark site well removed from sources of light pollution such as street lighting. The meteors can be seen without optical aids such as binoculars or a telescope. Allowing 20 to 30 minutes for your eyes to acclimatise to the dark can improve your chances of seeing the meteors. Dry though the weather is expected to be much of the time, extensive cloud is forecast in many areas. For Friday and Saturday nights, the forecast favours southern and western parts of England and Wales, where clear skies look more likely. More of the UK is likely to see clear skies by Sunday night. Fog could still form where skies stay clear for some time, obscuring the view. The report anticipates favourable viewing conditions around the peak, when the Moon will be below the horizon around midnight and its reflected light is less likely to interfere.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. as many as (quantifier phrase): Emphasises a high number; with “might see”, it highlights the upper end of an estimate.
2. originates in (verb phrase): Has its source in the material, place or process named next.
3. in its wake (prepositional phrase): Behind something as it moves away, along the path it has taken.
4. radiant (noun): The point in the sky from which the meteors in a shower appear to spread out.
5. point of reference (noun phrase): A known position that helps you work out where to look or locate something else.
6. optical aids (plural noun phrase): Instruments such as binoculars or telescopes that help people see objects that are difficult to make out unaided.
7. acclimatise to (verb phrase): Gradually become able to function under different conditions, here as the eyes become more sensitive to low light.
8. favours (verb): Indicates that one place or option has better prospects than others.
9. obscuring (verb, present participle): Making something harder or impossible to see by coming between it and the observer.
10. interfere (verb): Get in the way of an activity, as moonlight can do by making the meteors harder to see.

Advanced Grammar: Adjective fronting in concessive clauses with “though”
Put an adjective before “though” to foreground a point you acknowledge, then use the main clause to challenge what readers might expect from it. Putting “Dry” first acknowledges the dry forecast before drawing attention to extensive cloud.
Example from the text: "Dry though the weather is expected to be much of the time, extensive cloud is forecast in many areas."
