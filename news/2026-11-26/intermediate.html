UK to Release EU Treaties With Possible Exit Costs

Intermediate · B1–B2

📅 November 26, 2026 - UK to Release EU Treaties With Possible Exit Costs

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The UK government and the EU plan to release two treaties, or formal agreements between governments, on Thursday afternoon. The treaties cover food trade and emissions trading, a system for buying and selling permission to release gases that warm the planet. The BBC understands that the treaties contain a clause, or rule, about ending the deal within ten years. If either side ended the deal during that period, it would have to pay to rebuild border facilities. These border facilities would be significantly scaled back as part of the deal. Food exporters are expected to benefit from fewer border checks and less red tape, meaning difficult official procedures. An internal analysis forecasts a 16% increase in agricultural exports as a result of the changes. The government and industry hope that savings for food businesses will be passed on to customers. However, shoppers might not notice a clear difference in prices at the checkout. The deal has already sparked debate about its benefits, costs and the future relationship between the UK and the EU. Opposition leader Kemi Badenoch and Reform UK's Nigel Farage have suggested they would refuse to pay the costs of ending the deal.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. treaties (plural noun): Formal written agreements between governments.
2. clause (countable noun): A part of a legal document that gives a particular rule or condition.
3. scaled back (phrasal verb, past participle): Reduced in size or amount.
4. exporters (plural noun): People or businesses that sell goods to other countries.
5. red tape (uncountable noun phrase): Official rules and procedures that make an activity slower or more difficult.
6. forecasts (verb, present simple): Predicts what is likely to happen using available information.
7. passed on (phrasal verb, past participle): Shared with someone else, here by giving customers the benefit of business savings.
8. sparked debate (verb phrase, past participle): Started discussion in which people express different opinions.

Grammar Focus: Second conditional
Use the second conditional to imagine a possible future situation and its result. Put the past simple after “if” and “would” before the base form of the result verb. The past form here describes an imagined situation, not a past event.
Example from the text: "If either side ended the deal during that period, it would have to pay to rebuild border facilities."
