01Account Opening and KYCAsk for an actionable next step · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Make a request with a clear action, purpose, and realistic timing.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

An account application lacks requested ownership information. The applicant asks staff to open the account before the review is complete.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

KYC
Know-your-customer process used to verify a customer's identity, ownership, and risk profile.
CIP
Customer Identification Program: a bank's program for identifying customers under applicable US requirements.
beneficial owner
Natural person who ultimately owns or controls an entity or receives its economic benefit.
risk rating
A category or score assigned using defined criteria to describe a risk.

03 · Notice the language

Ask for an actionable next step

State the action and the information needed. Explain why it matters and specify a deadline only when one is given or agreed. 'Could you' is polite; repeated apologies can obscure a legitimate request. Urgent situations may require a direct request.

  • Could you provide ... so that ...?
  • Please confirm ... by [agreed time].
  • If that timing is not possible, please let me know ... .

Improve this: Please revert soonest with the necessary.

See a clearer version

Could you send the missing document and confirm when it will be available?

The revision names the document and requested response. It avoids expressions that may be unfamiliar or ambiguous for an international audience.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. What does 'Please send it by Thursday' normally mean?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. Send it no later than Thursday.

  1. A. Send it on Thursday, but not earlier. By Thursday permits earlier delivery; it is not restricted to that day.
  2. B. Keep sending it regularly until Thursday. This interprets a deadline as a continuing activity, which would use until.
  3. C. Send it no later than Thursday. 'By' sets the latest point, while 'after' refers to a later time.
2. Which request gives the recipient enough detail to act?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review?

  1. A. Could you confirm that you received my earlier message? This checks receipt, not which version is required for the review.
  2. B. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review? The requested action and information are explicit.
  3. C. Could you confirm that the review is important? This checks importance rather than identifying the needed document version.

05 · Say it

Practice with a partner

Prepare for two minutes. Speak for 45-60 seconds using the case facts, then respond to your partner's question. Switch roles and repeat without reading the model response.

Partner's role and follow-up

You can help but need a clear request and its purpose. Ask what is required and whether the timing is fixed or negotiable.

Try a harder second round

02AML Monitoring and Suspicious ActivityMatch certainty to evidence · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Separate observations, assumptions, and conclusions.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A monitoring alert identifies unusual transactions. The analyst has not determined their explanation. The internal note must remain neutral.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

AML
Anti-money laundering controls used to detect, investigate, and report suspicious financial activity.
structuring
Dividing transactions to evade specified reporting requirements; intent and legal assessment require appropriate investigation.
SAR
Suspicious Activity Report: a confidential regulatory report filed under applicable financial-crime reporting requirements.
tipping off
Improperly disclosing protected information about a suspicious-activity report or investigation under applicable rules.

03 · Notice the language

Match certainty to evidence

Say what the evidence shows, identify what is still unknown, and limit the conclusion to that evidence. Words such as 'may', 'suggests', and 'in this sample' are useful when the uncertainty is real. Do not soften an urgent, verified safety concern.

  • The available evidence shows ... .
  • This may indicate ..., but ... .
  • We have not yet established whether ... .

Improve this: The pilot proves this will work everywhere.

See a clearer version

The pilot worked in the tested setting; performance elsewhere remains untested.

The revision states the finding and its scope. It avoids turning limited evidence into a universal claim.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence preserves uncertainty about an unconfirmed cause?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. The change may have contributed to the delay.

  1. A. The change definitely caused the delay. 'Definitely' presents the cause as certain.
  2. B. The change may have contributed to the delay. 'May have contributed' presents a possibility without claiming the cause is established.
  3. C. The change must have caused the delay. 'Must have' expresses a strong inference that the evidence may not support.
2. A result comes from a small pilot in one location. Which phrase accurately limits the claim?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. In this pilot, the observed result was ...

  1. A. In this pilot, the observed result was ... This locates the finding in the setting actually studied.
  2. B. In similar locations, we can now expect the same result ... Similarity alone does not establish that the result will transfer.
  3. C. Across the service, the observed result was ... A pilot in one location does not establish a service-wide observation.

03 · Notice the language

Ask a precise question

Name the exact word, fact, or requirement you need clarified. Ask one focused question at a time. Repeat your understanding and give the other person a chance to correct it.

  • When you say ..., do you mean ...?
  • Could you clarify which ...?
  • So, my understanding is ... . Is that right?

Improve this: Could you explain what does this mean?

See a clearer version

Could you explain what this means?

After an introductory phrase such as 'Could you explain', the embedded question uses statement word order: what this means.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which indirect question has the usual word order?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. Could you confirm when the review starts?

  1. A. Could you confirm when the review starts? The embedded question uses subject then verb: the review starts.
  2. B. Could you confirm when does the review start? Remove 'does' and use statement word order after 'confirm'.
  3. C. Could you confirm when starts the review? Place the subject 'the review' before 'starts'.
2. Someone says the report is 'ready.' Which question best clarifies its approval status?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. Has the report been approved, or is it ready for review?

  1. A. Can you send the report to me this afternoon? This asks about delivery timing, not whether approval has happened.
  2. B. Who will present the report at the meeting? This asks about the presenter, not the report's approval status.
  3. C. Has the report been approved, or is it ready for review? This question distinguishes two meanings of 'ready' that affect the next action.

04Payment Operations and ExceptionsGive a useful status update · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Distinguish completed work from pending work and explain its impact.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A payments queue has 45 exceptions, with 30 reviewed and 15 pending. A manager asks whether all items have been resolved.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

wire transfer
Electronic movement of funds between financial institutions, usually with irreversible settlement characteristics once released.
ACH
US electronic bank-transfer network used for direct deposits, bill payments, and account-to-account transfers.
sanctions screening
Checking relevant parties or transactions against applicable sanctions requirements through defined systems and review.
exception queue
A list of items requiring review because normal processing could not complete them.

03 · Notice the language

Give a useful status update

Lead with the current state, then the important change or open item. Use completed-action language only for completed work. Add a next update point when it is known; do not invent a date to make the message sound complete.

  • We have completed ...; ... remains open.
  • As of ..., the status is ... .
  • The next update will cover ... .

Improve this: Everything is progressing well.

See a clearer version

Three checks are complete; two remain open and are being reviewed.

The revision gives observable progress. A positive label alone does not tell the reader what is complete or still needed.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence says a review is finished?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. The team has completed the review.

  1. A. The team is completing the review. 'Is completing' presents the work as in progress.
  2. B. The team plans to complete the review. 'Plans to complete' describes an intention, not completion.
  3. C. The team has completed the review. 'Has completed' states that the action is finished.
2. Which update distinguishes a plan from a confirmed fact?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Delivery is planned for Friday; the carrier has not confirmed it.

  1. A. The delivery plan proves that arrival will be Friday. A plan cannot prove a future arrival time.
  2. B. Delivery is planned for Friday; the carrier has not confirmed it. This explicitly labels the date as planned and identifies the missing confirmation.
  3. C. Delivery is confirmed for Friday because we planned it. A plan does not establish carrier confirmation.

05Fraud Operations and Customer EscalationRepair a misunderstanding · 45-60 minutes+

Your goal

Acknowledge a communication problem and give a corrected message.

01 · Read the situation

A moment at work

A customer reporting a disputed transaction receives conflicting explanations of the process. The case outcome has not been determined.

Before you look at the model: what is confirmed, what is missing, and who needs a response?

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

fraud claim
A report alleging that a transaction or loss involved fraudulent activity.
provisional credit
A temporary credit applied while a transaction dispute is being reviewed under applicable rules.
chargeback
A reversal of a card transaction through the payment network's dispute process.
dispute
A disagreement over facts, obligations, charges, or a decision.

03 · Notice the language

Repair a misunderstanding

Name what was unclear or wrong, acknowledge its effect, and correct it. A brief apology can help, followed by a practical next step. Avoid explaining away the other person's reaction or promising an outcome you cannot control.

  • My earlier message did not make ... clear.
  • I'm sorry for ... . The correct information is ... .
  • Let me check that the revised explanation addresses ... .

Improve this: I'm sorry if you failed to understand.

See a clearer version

I'm sorry my message did not explain the timing clearly.

The revision takes responsibility for the message without blaming the listener. It identifies what needs to be corrected.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which apology takes responsibility for unclear wording?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. I'm sorry my earlier wording was unclear.

  1. A. I'm sorry we could not meet the original date. This acknowledges a missed date, not the communication problem in the question.
  2. B. I'm sorry my earlier wording was unclear. This acknowledges the speaker's contribution to the problem.
  3. C. I'm sorry the process took longer than expected. This acknowledges duration, but does not take responsibility for the unclear wording.
2. What should follow an apology for an incorrect date?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. The corrected date and the current confirmation status.

  1. A. The corrected date and the current confirmation status. A repair needs usable corrected information, including any uncertainty.
  2. B. A longer explanation that never states the correct date. The reader still lacks the information they need.
  3. C. A new guaranteed date even if it is unverified. An unsupported new promise can create a second misunderstanding.

02 · Find the words

Vocabulary for this lesson

operational risk
The possibility of loss or disruption arising from processes, people, systems, or external events.
control
A process, approval, check, or technical safeguard designed to reduce risk.
reconciliation
Comparing records or balances and investigating differences between them.
remediation
Work to correct a problem or reduce its effects; in environmental work, addressing contamination.

03 · Notice the language

Make a complex point clear

Start with the reader's question. Explain one unfamiliar term with familiar words, then give a relevant example or contrast. Check understanding without asking only 'Do you understand?'.

  • In this context, ... means ... .
  • The difference is that ... .
  • How would you explain that distinction to a colleague?

Improve this: We need to operationalize the implementation.

See a clearer version

We need to put the plan into practice.

The revised sentence names the action in familiar words. Specialist vocabulary is useful when it adds precision, but repeating abstract nouns can hide the meaning.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which sentence explains 'backlog' rather than repeating it?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. The backlog is the work still waiting to be completed.

  1. A. The backlog is the work still waiting to be completed. This gives the meaning in familiar words.
  2. B. The backlog is the work completed during the last period. Completed work is different from work still waiting.
  3. C. The backlog is the maximum work the team can finish in a period. This describes capacity, not the work waiting to be completed.
2. Which follow-up best checks whether your explanation was clear?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. How would you describe the next step in your own words?

  1. A. Would you like me to repeat the explanation? Offering repetition may help, but it does not show how the listener understood the next step.
  2. B. Was the explanation detailed enough for you? This invites an opinion on detail, not a demonstration of understanding.
  3. C. How would you describe the next step in your own words? An open request lets the listener show their understanding.

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. What does 'Please send it by Thursday' normally mean?

Answer and explanations

Answer: C. Send it no later than Thursday.

  1. A. Send it on Thursday, but not earlier. By Thursday permits earlier delivery; it is not restricted to that day.
  2. B. Keep sending it regularly until Thursday. This interprets a deadline as a continuing activity, which would use until.
  3. C. Send it no later than Thursday. 'By' sets the latest point, while 'after' refers to a later time.
2. Which request gives the recipient enough detail to act?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review?

  1. A. Could you confirm that you received my earlier message? This checks receipt, not which version is required for the review.
  2. B. Could you confirm the document version needed for the review? The requested action and information are explicit.
  3. C. Could you confirm that the review is important? This checks importance rather than identifying the needed document version.

03 · Notice the language

Repair a misunderstanding

Name what was unclear or wrong, acknowledge its effect, and correct it. A brief apology can help, followed by a practical next step. Avoid explaining away the other person's reaction or promising an outcome you cannot control.

  • My earlier message did not make ... clear.
  • I'm sorry for ... . The correct information is ... .
  • Let me check that the revised explanation addresses ... .

Improve this: I'm sorry if you failed to understand.

See a clearer version

I'm sorry my message did not explain the timing clearly.

The revision takes responsibility for the message without blaming the listener. It identifies what needs to be corrected.

04 · Check your understanding

Two short language checks

These language patterns recur across courses so you can retrieve and reuse them.

1. Which apology takes responsibility for unclear wording?

Answer and explanations

Answer: B. I'm sorry my earlier wording was unclear.

  1. A. I'm sorry we could not meet the original date. This acknowledges a missed date, not the communication problem in the question.
  2. B. I'm sorry my earlier wording was unclear. This acknowledges the speaker's contribution to the problem.
  3. C. I'm sorry the process took longer than expected. This acknowledges duration, but does not take responsibility for the unclear wording.
2. What should follow an apology for an incorrect date?

Answer and explanations

Answer: A. The corrected date and the current confirmation status.

  1. A. The corrected date and the current confirmation status. A repair needs usable corrected information, including any uncertainty.
  2. B. A longer explanation that never states the correct date. The reader still lacks the information they need.
  3. C. A new guaranteed date even if it is unverified. An unsupported new promise can create a second misunderstanding.
