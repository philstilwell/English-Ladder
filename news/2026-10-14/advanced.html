How AI is Disrupting Graduate Job Applications

Advanced · C1+

News archive →
📅 October 14, 2026 - How AI is Disrupting Graduate Job Applications

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Research suggests that university graduates are utilising artificial intelligence to distort their qualifications in job applications, though many argue they have little alternative. This software enables jobseekers to generate multiple simultaneous submissions—a tactic dubbed "spray and pray"—which intensifies competition in an already saturated recruitment sector. Applicants frequently utilise these tools to optimise their cover letters and identify the crucial buzzwords necessary to pass early-stage automated screening. This creates a delicate tightrope for applicants, who must sound human while still satisfying the rigid parameters of algorithmic filters. According to a recent survey, major UK employers are growing more tolerant of applicants using AI assistance during certain preliminary recruitment phases. Nevertheless, 92% of these employers suspected unethical practices, frequently observing individuals relying on real-time generative assistants during online video interviews. The overwhelming influx of applications recently prompted the energy corporation EDF to prematurely close its graduate scheme after receiving 12,000 entries. Recruiters caution that this discrepancy undermines a level playing field, advantaging those who exploit automation throughout the entire selection process. To counter this trend, EDF is striving to make its hiring procedures robust by delaying document reviews and prioritising behaviour-based assessments. Navigating this moral dilemma remains challenging for employers, who must grapple with automation while maintaining their standards for suitable new recruits.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. distort (verb): To alter facts or information in a way that creates a misleading or inaccurate impression.
2. simultaneous (adjective): Happening, existing, or done at exactly the same time.
3. saturated (adjective): So full or crowded with people or things that no more can be added or accommodated.
4. optimise (verb): To make something as effective, perfect, or functional as possible.
5. buzzwords (noun): Specific technical terms or fashionable phrases that are popular in a particular context or required by algorithms.
6. tightrope (noun): A precarious situation requiring careful balancing between two contrasting choices or dangers.
7. rigid (adjective): Strict, unbending, and unable to be changed or adapted to different circumstances.
8. preliminary (adjective): Occurring before or serving as preparation for the main or final part of a process.
9. prematurely (adverb): Before the natural, expected, or scheduled time; too early.
10. discrepancy (noun): An illogical or unfair difference or inconsistency between two or more things.

Advanced Grammar: Contrastive "while" with a participle clause
The conjunction "while" can be followed directly by an active present participle ("-ing" form) to contrast two concurrent actions or requirements. This advanced structure gracefully omits the subject and auxiliary verb, keeping the sentence concise and polished.
Example from the text: "This creates a delicate tightrope for applicants, who must sound human while still satisfying the rigid parameters of algorithmic filters."
