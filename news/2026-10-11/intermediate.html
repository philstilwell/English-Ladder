📅 October 11, 2026 - Local Residents Oppose New Data Centre Project

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Hundreds of people have shown strong opposition to a proposed data centre. Opponents are concerned about its potential effects on the local area. A company wants to construct a complex on a 101-acre site in Gwynedd. The proposed site is located between Caernarfon and Y Felinheli. Canolfan Ddata Menai, the company, states the project could create more than 900 temporary construction roles and 200 permanent positions. Approximately 300 individuals gathered for a rally in Caernarfon to express their disagreement. Additionally, over 22,500 people have signed a document opposing the development. Local resident Ernest Dyson believes the project would cause "the destruction of the most beautiful part of the Menai Strait." He also stated that "The people of the area are custodians of it." Plaid Cymru MP Liz Saville Roberts highlighted the need for proper regulations and community benefits from large-scale developments. She also noted Wales has a history of extractive industries that often left little benefit for communities. Canolfan Ddata Menai claims the project will have "no undue impact" on the Menai Strait.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. opposition (noun): strong disagreement with something.
2. proposed (adjective): suggested for consideration or discussion.
3. complex (noun): a group of related buildings or facilities.
4. site (noun): a place where something is located or where an event takes place.
5. temporary (adjective): lasting for only a limited period of time; not permanent.
6. permanent (adjective): lasting for a long time or forever; not temporary.
7. gathered (verb): came together in one place.
8. rally (noun): a large public meeting, especially one held to show support for or opposition to something.
9. custodians (noun): people who are responsible for taking care of or protecting something.
10. regulations (noun): official rules or laws that control how something is done.

Grammar Focus: Relative Clauses
Relative clauses add more information about a noun. They connect to the noun using words like "that", "who", "which", or "where", helping to describe it more fully. They make sentences more detailed and specific.
Example from the text: "She also noted Wales has a history of extractive industries that often left little benefit for communities."
