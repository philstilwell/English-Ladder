Lesson 01: Prescription intake without assumptions
Original fictional case: Patient Ellis asks technician Nia to complete a prescription order whose strength field is unclear. Ellis remembers a blue label and says the prescriber usually orders the same thing. Nia cannot select the strength. Pharmacist Maya can take the clarification, but no completion time is known. Nia must distinguish remembered information from verified current instructions, explain the unresolved field, and route the question. No medicine name, dose, or numerical strength is provided in this teaching case, and the exercise does not ask the learner to infer one.
Language workshop: A familiar label does not resolve an unclear strength
Communication goal: Identify an ambiguous order field and arrange pharmacist clarification without selecting a strength from memory or appearance.
Language guidance: Strength and dose are not synonyms. A product may contain a particular amount per unit, while the prescribed administration uses a specified amount and schedule. This unit supplies neither and does not authorize calculation or selection from a remembered label.
Useful expressions (preserve the supplied facts): The strength field on this order is unclear. / I understand that you usually receive the same medicine. / The label color is useful context, but it does not confirm the current strength. / I cannot select a strength from an unclear entry. / Pharmacist Maya can take the clarification. / We need the order clarified; I am not asking you to guess it. / I will record that you remember a blue label. / Strength describes the product; dose concerns the prescribed amount used. / We need the current instructions confirmed, not just an earlier pattern. / The pharmacist can review the relevant previous record. / I do not yet have a confirmed completion time. / The clarification is pending, not completed. / Can Maya confirm receipt of this question? / Any question about how to take it should go to the pharmacist. / Please tell the pharmacist about any concern that needs prompt attention. / We will use the verified instruction rather than fill the gap with a guess.
Vocabulary: prescription: An authorized order for a medicine under the applicable rules. / intake: Receiving and organizing information needed to process an order. / prescriber: A professional authorized to prescribe the relevant medicine. / pharmacist: A qualified medicines professional with the relevant practice authority. / strength: The amount of active ingredient in a stated unit or quantity of the product. / dose: The amount prescribed for a particular administration. / dosage form: The physical form of the product, such as a tablet or liquid. / active ingredient: The substance intended to produce the medicine's therapeutic effect. / concentration: The amount of a substance within a specified quantity of a preparation. / route: The specified way a medicine is taken or applied. / frequency: How often a prescribed action or administration occurs. / directions for use: The authorized instructions for taking or using the medicine. / quantity: The amount of product specified for supply. / days supply: Days represented by quantity and verified directions under the relevant calculation rules; not proof of actual use. / brand name: A trade name for a particular product. / generic name: The recognized nonproprietary name of the medicine substance. / product identifier: A code or reference identifying a specified product. / NDC: National Drug Code; a United States drug product identification system. / order field: A defined part of the order record. / ambiguous entry: Information that permits more than one interpretation. / clarification request: A request to resolve an unclear or missing instruction. / current order: The order presently being processed, distinct from past records. / historical record: Information about a previous order or event. / verified instruction: A direction confirmed through the appropriate process.
Speaking task: Read the strength question aloud without guessing a number or naming a medicine. Complete the transfer with dosage form, the reported box description, the accepting pharmacist, and the actual timing status. Check the four answers, then repeat the handoff while keeping memory separate from a verified current instruction.
Partner: Ellis: Patient / Nia: Pharmacy technician
Guided response task: Select supplied answers for the dialogue gaps. Check the explanations, then read the completed exchange aloud; no original writing is required.
Optional second-round challenge: A current order has an unclear dosage-form field. The patient remembers a white box. Pharmacist Ren accepts the clarification. No product form or completion time has been confirmed.
Model for comparison AFTER my attempt, not a response to give me first: Ellis: If I have a question about how to take the medicine once the order is clarified, should I ask Maya that as well?
Nia: Yes. Questions about the directions for use need the pharmacist's appropriate explanation. Resolving the strength field does not mean every question about your medicine has already been answered.
Ellis: Thank you. Please tell Maya that I remember a blue label and a usual pattern, but that I have not supplied a confirmed current strength.
Nia: I will preserve that in intake. The strength remains unclear, Maya is the clarification route, and no completion time is confirmed. We will not fill the gap with an assumption.

Stock lead: Please do. We need everyone using the same status while I review the package information.
Technician: I'll leave the use decision with you and identify the photograph as the source of the uncertainty.
Stock lead: That is enough for this handoff: delivery E9, unclear date, line association unconfirmed, and my review pending.
