📅 September 01, 2026 [0 days, 0 hours old] - People Forced to Return to Afghanistan: A Difficult New Start
Many people are forced to leave Pakistan and Iran and return to Afghanistan, facing challenges to start their lives again.

Beginner ESL: Forced returns to Afghanistan under the Taliban

I. The News Brief

Many people are leaving Pakistan and Iran. They are forced to return to Afghanistan. These people are called "deportees." Six million people have been forced to go back. This year, one million more people returned. They must start over in Afghanistan. The Taliban group controls Afghanistan now. Some deportees say, "I've never been to Afghanistan." They feel like strangers in their own country. This situation is very difficult for them.

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. forced (verb): To make someone do something they do not want to do.
2. deportees (noun): People who are made to leave a country by the government.
3. return (verb): To go back to a place where you were before.
4. start over (verb phrase): To begin something again from the beginning, usually after a problem or change.
5. controls (verb): To have power over a place, a group of people, or a situation.

Grammar Focus: Simple Present Tense
We use the simple present tense to talk about things that are always true, facts, or habits. We also use it to talk about situations happening now or regularly.
Example from the text: "The Taliban group controls Afghanistan now."

I. The News Brief

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

4. rejected (verb): To refuse to accept something; to say 'no' to an idea or a proposal.
5. restart (verb): To begin something again after it has stopped, like starting a game over.

Grammar Focus: The Simple Past Tense
We use the simple past tense to talk about actions that finished in the past. For regular verbs, we often add '-ed' to the base form. For irregular verbs, the form changes completely. It tells us something happened at a specific time before now.
Example from the text: "About 52.8% of voters rejected the idea."

📅 August 29, 2026 [3 days, 14 hours old] - Tibet Floods: Hidden News
Floods happened in Tibet, but people in China cannot see all the news.

Beginner ESL: Natural Disasters and Information Control

I. The News Brief

Tibet recently had big floods. These floods caused much damage. Many people were victims of the floods. There is video footage about these events. This footage shows very bad scenes. But this video is hidden in China. People in China cannot see it. It is not for public view. So, we know little about the victims. Getting information about the floods is difficult.

II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

1. floods (noun): Too much water that covers land that is usually dry.
2. victims (noun): People who are hurt or suffer because of an accident, crime, or disaster.
3. footage (noun): Part of a film or video recording that shows an event.
4. hidden (adjective): Kept secret or put in a place where it cannot be seen or found.
5. public view (noun phrase): When something is shown to many people, not kept secret or private.

Grammar Focus: "There is" and "There are"
We use "There is" for one thing, and "There are" for many things. It tells us that something exists or is in a place. For example, "There is a book on the table." "There are two cats in the garden."
Example from the text: "There is video footage about these events."

4. scientists say (verb phrase): Researchers or experts share their findings or give their professional opinions.
5. melting Himalayan ice (noun phrase): Frozen water in the high mountains of the Himalayas that is turning into liquid water.

Beginner ESL: Natural Disaster News: Flash Flood in Nepal

I. The News Brief

Nepal had a very sudden flash flood. The flood happened in an area north of Kathmandu. This area is close to the Tibet border. At least 17 people were killed by the flood. Many people feel sad about this terrible news. The big flood might have swept away villages. Some homes are now missing in the affected places. The flood happened quickly after very heavy rain. II. Vocabulary & Grammar Focus

2. killed (verb (past tense)): Caused someone or something to die.
3. border (noun): A line that separates two countries, states, or areas.
4. swept away (verb phrase (past tense)): Carried away by water or wind with great force.
5. feared (verb (simple past)): Thought that something bad or dangerous might happen.

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense for Finished Actions
We use the simple past tense to talk about actions that happened and finished completely in the past. For regular verbs, we usually add '-ed'. For irregular verbs, the form changes.
Example from the text: "At least 17 people were killed by the flood."
