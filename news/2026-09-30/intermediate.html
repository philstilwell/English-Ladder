Regulating AI: Prioritizing Rigorous Testing and Safeguards

📅 September 30, 2026 - Regulating AI: Prioritizing Rigorous Testing and Safeguards

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

The Governor of the Bank of England has said regulating AI "is not the right place to start." Instead, he called first for rigorous testing to find vulnerabilities and safeguards to contain risk. Andrew Bailey said the risks around AI were real and increasingly significant. Bailey believes AI development should not be halted, as the benefits are immense. However, there must be a system for intervention and to establish boundaries for AI operation. Recently, the debate about AI's potential risks has intensified. Leading AI firm bosses have called for development to slow down and for a co-ordinated approach. President Donald Trump rejected the idea of a slowdown, stating the US leads the AI race. Later, Trump announced executives signed a "morally binding" document for protection from AI's potential risks. Under this agreement, companies are responsible for ensuring the safety of their own technology. In the UK, important work is underway in testing AI, which should help form part of a set of standards. The pace of progress must accelerate, but with a degree of humility, the bank governor added.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. rigorous (adjective): very careful, thorough, and exact.
2. vulnerabilities (noun): weaknesses or flaws that could be exploited or harmed.
3. safeguards (noun): measures or rules designed to protect someone or something from harm or danger.
4. increasingly significant (adverb + adjective): becoming more and more important or noticeable over time.
5. halted (verb): stopped completely or paused.
6. intervention (noun): the act of becoming involved in a difficult situation to improve it or prevent it from getting worse.
7. boundaries (noun): limits or borders that define where something can operate or what is allowed.
8. intensified (verb): became stronger, more serious, or more extreme.

Grammar Focus: Reported Speech
Reported speech is used to tell someone what another person said, but without using their exact words. When reporting past statements, verbs in the reported clause often shift to a past tense (e.g., "is" becomes "was"). Reporting verbs like "said" or "added" introduce the reported statement.
Example from the text: "Andrew Bailey said the risks around AI were real and increasingly significant."
