# Student feedback rules

- Write all student-facing assessments, feedback, summaries, headings, and practice instructions in short, clear English suitable for A1–A2 readers. Use common words and one idea per sentence. Explain necessary grammar terms with a simple example. This is a reading-register preference, not a claim about a student's assessed level. Keep the tone respectful and adult. Harder vocabulary being taught may remain, with an easy explanation.
- Put words and phrases being discussed inside quotation marks or italics, including in headings and practice instructions. For example: “Phil showed two ways to use ‘access’.” Clearly distinguish actual lesson quotations from new practice examples; never turn a paraphrase or new example into a claimed transcript quote.
- Use only a student's first name in prose. An explicitly chosen nickname may be used as a profile label. Never display or publish full names on student-facing pages. The owner explicitly authorized full names, Zoom names, contact details, and course records only in the authenticated administrator directory at phil.englishladder.com; keep its data separate from every student API and from AI authoring inputs.
- Every student's session feedback must focus exclusively on English as a second language: usage, vocabulary, grammar, comprehension, conversational skills, and language practice. Do not summarize or evaluate personal lives, work performance, beliefs, health, or unrelated conversation topics.
- Find every matching session in the requested period, including known display-name variants. Read the complete available transcript for each session, not just Zoom's generated summary. Record its coverage and identify missing transcripts honestly.
- Ground feedback in timestamped learner utterances and teacher exchanges. Distinguish explicit in-session teaching from additional practice. Treat automatic transcription errors cautiously; do not infer pronunciation quality or formal proficiency scores from text. The owner has requested a separate administrator-only provisional CEFR conversation estimate; follow the evidence and scope rules below.
- Explain language accurately even when a conversational explanation in the source was oversimplified. Label new model sentences and exercises as practice rather than transcript quotations. Keep quoted examples brief and remove unnecessary personal details.
- In “A few minutes of practice”, use fresh situations to apply the same language taught above. Check both the task and its example answer against every focus model and vocabulary example. Do not repeat those sentences, turn them into gaps, merely swap one noun, or ask students to copy the model. Give a short, self-contained task and a clear answer. When repairing existing exercises, change only practice, preserve all source evidence and quiz history, and verify every affected page.
- Store actual student feedback only in the private per-student server storage. Keep publishing inputs in the ignored private directory with restrictive file permissions. Never commit student records, raw transcripts, credentials, or provisioning inputs; never place them in public assets or browser storage.
- The owner authorizes private server caching of anonymized transcripts for language assessment only. Assign trusted Student/Instructor roles before removing names; keep other speakers distinct and never infer a role from speaking style. Remove proper names, direct identifiers, and identifying personal details before persistence, retaining timestamps, turns, and all non-redacted wording without correcting language. Require a separate privacy review; a failure must not save a transcript or publish from it. Raw responses and name mappings remain transient. Reuse reviewed copies for unchanged pending lessons. Delete all cached versions older than three calendar months by Eastern lesson date during nightly cleanup, including paused accounts and when publishing is disabled or Zoom is unavailable. Preserve assessments, feedback, quizzes, and original first choices. Original Zoom records are outside this retention policy. Keep full cached transcripts inaccessible through public/student/administrator browser APIs.
- Test account isolation, authentication, teacher-only writes, logout cleanup, and mobile access whenever changing record handling. Use fictional records in tests.
- Overall assessments follow the same ESL-only and privacy rules. Support recurring patterns with references across the full transcripts, balance them with successful uses, and label practice targets as goals rather than current scores. Link to private feedback pages, never Zoom meeting notes. Display dates only in assessment references, including accessible labels; omit elapsed transcript times and coverage ranges from student pages while retaining them in private records for verification.
- Assessment study plans are optional suggestions for independent study, never promises about a future session or tasks assigned to Phil. Use the heading “Study on your own”. Explain in simple English that the topics were chosen because the lessons showed which parts of the student's English need more practice. Give each topic a short copyable AI prompt that introduces the specific language, explains it in simple English, gives examples, and asks one question at a time with feedback. Keep prompts free of student names, personal details, transcript quotations, timestamps, and links.
- Beside each study prompt, show one “Copy AI prompt” button followed by clickable ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Microsoft Copilot logos. Keep names available to screen readers and on hover, with simple copy/choose/paste instructions. Use regular AI sites, including regular Gemini rather than AI Studio. Logo links open in new tabs without an opener or referrer and never include prompts or student records in their URLs. Do not copy or submit anything when a logo is clicked. Host the logos locally and preserve manual copying.
- Each study topic's AI prompt, copying controls, and AI-site logos belong inside a collapsible panel that is closed by default. Opening one AI prompt closes any other open AI prompt, so at most one is open at a time; students can also close the current prompt. Keep “Try this:” and its suggestion in the same paragraph, with natural wrapping on small screens.
- Quizzes should practice patterns from the student's assessment with simple instructions and feedback after each choice. Label quiz items as new practice, use quotation marks or italics for the English being taught, and do not present quiz results as a proficiency level. Keep authored quizzes in private student storage and clear in-memory attempts on sign-out or expiry.
- Save student quiz choices privately after each answer. Preserve the original first choice, source lesson references, and question wording for each attempt. Teacher previews must never count as student attempts. Show first-try mistakes and dated progress only to the authenticated teacher; do not treat practice scores as a proficiency level. Show only plain source lesson dates in the quiz introduction, with no links. Keep short answer instructions beside the questions.
- Show the Quiz menu item only when a reviewed quiz is available. If there is not enough lesson evidence to author one, omit the quiz and its menu entry instead of inventing questions or showing an empty tab.
- When automatic quizzes have been requested, follow QUIZ_AUTOMATION.md. Read each new full transcript and the saved original first choices from earlier attempts. Publish one seven-question quiz per eligible completed session, with simple instructions and choice-by-choice feedback. For the authorized nightly page updates, refresh the assessment with new evidence and preserve its earlier version privately. Always preserve saved attempts. Keep scheduling checkpoints private; do not invent mistakes when no results exist.

- Student registration and Zoom mappings are managed in the private StudentDirectory service. Preserve established student IDs, course records, learning history, and first-try results. Imported spreadsheet rows remain drafts by default. When Phil explicitly requests transcript matching and activation, activate clear matches supported by actual transcript speaker identities; ask about ambiguous names. Do not infer an alias merely because another student mentions a name. Preserve an explicit owner instruction that a student is expired or paused, even if the spreadsheet still lists a later end date.
- Phil can assign, reveal, and copy student passwords on private account pages, as explicitly requested. Keep passwords out of the directory list, ordinary save responses, student APIs, automation inputs, public assets, logs, and Git. Only the dedicated login-details endpoint on the administrator host may return a student password, after checking the host-bound teacher session; never return verification records or the universal teacher password. Store recoverable custom passwords encrypted with the separate Worker encryption secret, bound to their account and current verifier. Blank password fields preserve both records. Changing a password revokes that student's sessions and does not affect teacher access or other students. Restoring an encrypted copy of a verified existing password must preserve its verifier and sessions. Clear revealed/copied details on navigation, logout, and session expiry; discard late responses. Never set a student's password to the universal administrator password.

- When importing the New-Students spreadsheet, include its checkbox column C. Exclude any row with a checked box and any course whose end date is before today in America/New_York. A course ending today remains eligible through today. Never import a header-only selection without the checkbox column, because that would bypass the owner’s exclusion rule.

- For an explicitly requested historical backfill, inventory the whole date range, deduplicate identical Zoom transcripts, and resolve actual meeting dates from meeting records. Preserve existing reviewed pages and chosen nicknames. Fill empty nickname fields only with respectful suggestions supported by transcript evidence; keep the supporting reasons in private authoring records. Do not invent assets or nicknames for students with no usable evidence. Publish reviewed historical sets through the teacher-only atomic import; keep the current quiz tied to the latest session and preserve all first-try results. Historical imports do not change the nightly cutoff or spending limits.

- Paused students belong in a default-closed section at the bottom of the administrator list. Search and the explicit paused filter may expand it. Keep account links and quiz status available inside the section.
- Account deletion is available only through the authenticated administrator host, with exact-origin checks, the current record revision, and typed confirmation of the saved student ID (or full name for drafts without an ID). Close access and nightly updates before removing the account, password, and all per-student learning records. Keep a minimal deletion receipt, retired address, and import fingerprints so retries are safe and imports or delayed writes cannot restore deleted accounts. Incomplete cleanup stays visibly paused and retryable. Never reuse a deleted student address. Preserve original Zoom transcripts and spreadsheet records. Test deletion only with fictional records; never delete real accounts merely to verify the interface.

- Student-page and administrator main menus use an edge-to-edge dark bar with white lettering, the logo’s blue underline with a thinner white line directly above it for the current item, and remain visible while scrolling. Keep Sign out inside both fixed menu bars, aligned to the right and reachable on mobile. Do not include Quiz results in the student menu, including in teacher view; preserve the private teacher results route and its link from the administrator account page. Keep sticky side panels and section jumps below the menu, including when mobile menu items wrap.
- In the student menu, nest Assessments, Quiz, Session summaries, and Feedback in a vertical dropdown under Overview. Highlight its current submenu item with the logo-blue background and white text, without an underline; retain the top-level menu styling. Open on hover and provide a separate tap/keyboard toggle; keep Overview itself linked to the overview page. Close the dropdown on selection, outside interaction, Escape, navigation, and sign-out. Keep Account at the top level aligned right beside Sign out, and preserve the conditional Quiz visibility rule.
- Put Activities beside Overview and show only the activities saved as checked by the administrator on that student’s Edit account page. Offer the same checkboxes in registration. Students must not have an activity picker or permission to change these selections through their account API. Preserve existing selections, including none, until the administrator changes them. Give its current submenu item the same logo-blue background and white text without an underline as Overview. Keep both dropdowns usable on hover, touch, and keyboard, with only one open at a time. An empty selection tells the student to ask their teacher; a failed load must not show unselected defaults. Keep English Road Practice as a real activity and retain the clearly labeled Activity 1–3 placeholders until the owner replaces them. Refresh choices when the student opens the dropdown, ignore older read responses, and clear saved choices from the page on logout or expiry.
- On Assessments and Feedback, use dark header bands with white text for main content sections, including a visible “Your English now” heading above the opening assessment summary. Use a lighter dark grey background with white text for practice topic and study topic headers so subsections remain distinct from main sections. Use that same grey for the “Your progress” heading. Keep grey header bands thinner, with 8px top and bottom padding. Preserve readable spacing on mobile.
- Keep vertical spacing compact across student and administrator pages: reduce padding in content cards, examples, study prompts, summaries, quizzes, and forms while preserving the dark title bars, text sizes, reading line spacing, and usable controls. Keep study-card header offsets aligned with the card padding at each screen size.
- Keep the administrator’s Technical notes page (`#technical-notes`) current when changing scheduling, CEFR eligibility, retention, privacy, account behavior, or cost safeguards. Use plain explanations and a revision date, never student records or credentials. Keep its menu item and Sign out usable on mobile. It is a reference page, not a settings editor.
- The student Account page allows only nickname and own-password changes. Activity selection belongs to the administrator’s account editor and must use its teacher-only, exact-origin, validated revision-checked save. Require the current student password before changing it, preserve encrypted administrator recovery, revoke existing student sessions, and never allow teacher previews to submit these controls. Keep host/account isolation, exact-origin checks, rate limits, and transactional revision checks. Preserve activity choices when an administrator edit omits that field; an explicit empty list clears the menu. The owner authorized English Road Practice; retain and clearly label the other placeholders. Preserve existing saved checkbox selections when adding an activity. Clear account forms on navigation, logout, expiry, and page hide; never persist them in browser storage.

- Administrator CEFR estimates are provisional descriptions of conversational English visible in transcripts, never formal test results or a complete reading/writing/listening/speaking assessment. Keep them in separate private `cefr:*` storage and administrator APIs; never put them in student feedback, assessments, quizzes, or browser storage. Generate the initial estimate only after two distinct usable anonymized transcripts have been saved, using the two latest. Thereafter review no sooner than one calendar month after the prior review, using the latest four available retained sessions (at least two; use two or three when fewer than four remain). Do not repeat a review on unchanged evidence. Use each selected full transcript and validate exact learner quotations from every selected session; old quotes, assessment prose, and quiz scores cannot substitute for a source. Use conservative identical/adjacent A1–C2 bands, an explanation, four observable areas, and brief learner quotations. Account for teacher help and transcription uncertainty. Require at least three evidenced lessons before moderate confidence; never use high confidence or a numeric score. Quiz results and the page reading register are not CEFR evidence. Failed or insufficient monthly reviews preserve the earlier estimate, while useful lesson feedback continues independently. Monthly reviews run from the private cache even without a new lesson or Zoom connection; paused accounts are excluded. Preserve history, three-month transcript retention, source isolation, and clearing on navigation, logout, or expiry. Keep the existing ordinary lesson limits and a separate CEFR allowance of one attempt per UTC day and two per month, reserving before paid calls. Do not run historical backfill or paid inference merely to validate deployment.

- English Road Practice is a separate account-backed activity using a versioned copy of the reviewed English Road bank. Preserve original answers, question wording, explanations, server dates, and interrupted attempts. Score on the server, withhold explanations for unanswered questions, and make duplicate saves safe. Students resume their current set; the administrator reviews all history on the administrator host, including paused accounts. Teacher previews never write attempts. Never mix these practice results into transcript CEFR evidence or lesson-quiz authoring. Clear loaded practice data on navigation, logout, expiry, and page hide; never store answers in browser storage or send them to English Road. Deletion removes practice history. Import bank updates deliberately without altering earlier attempts or incurring AI calls.

- Grammar practice is self-study with saved Finished and Questions choices, separate from Business terms, English Road, and lesson quizzes. Use the imported Grammar tab’s 900 ranked question/choice/answer rows only; exclude student comments, tracking columns, and AI responses. Preserve the source order and show ten items at a time with a group picker and checkbox answer reveals. Checking replaces the blank item with the completed sentence in the logo’s blue; unchecking restores the exact original item and normal color. Support multiple blanks and the source’s full-sentence items. Do not score or save answers, reveal state, or position, and never send interactions to Google or AI. Finish changes to Finished and toggles a grey item background. Use compact action buttons with font size and dimensions 30% smaller than the standard secondary button. The Questions button changes to Saved to Questions when selected and is yellow, with a stronger yellow when selected, and toggles inclusion in its own Questions list, which links to the exact item and is readable on the administrator account page, including for paused students. Save these two choices privately by student and grammar item with revisions and server update times. Students can undo either choice independently; teacher views stay read-only. Keep grammar and Business terms storage separate; account deletion removes both. Preserve administrator-selected activity visibility. Serve the question bank after sign-in, allow teacher previews, and clear in-memory content on navigation, logout, expiry, and page hide. Source changes require a deliberate import, not live spreadsheet access during practice.

- Business terms is self-check practice with saved Finished and Questions choices. Keep each New BTs exercise’s three sentences together, with six alphabetical choices and one checkbox to fill all three sentences. Color only the six inserted answer terms in logo blue; keep surrounding words, punctuation, and sentence numbers in their normal color. Unchecking restores the blanks. Preserve duplicate options, answer capitalization, source order, and original item numbers. Blanks are numbered 1–6 and may be distributed unevenly among the sentences. Import only exercises with six complete matching answers; retain a source-only list of excluded item numbers and reasons without inventing missing content. Exclude student notes, questions, checkbox states, and unrelated tabs. Explain that later questions are harder. Finish changes to Finished and toggles a grey item background. Use compact action buttons with font size and dimensions 30% smaller than the standard secondary button. The Questions button changes to Saved to Questions when selected and is yellow, with a stronger yellow when selected, and toggles inclusion in the student’s Questions list. Save only these two choices with server timestamps and per-item revisions in private per-student storage. Students can undo either choice; teacher previews can read but never change them. Show the same Questions list with full exercises and answer reveals on the administrator account page, including for paused accounts. Keep administrator-selected menu visibility, authenticated content delivery, in-memory navigation/reveals, no scoring, and logout/expiry/page-hide cleanup. Reject cross-account and cross-origin access, validate item IDs, protect concurrent saves, and remove choices on account deletion. Do not save reveal states, answers, or position; do not import sheet tracking states. Never feed this activity into CEFR or lesson-quiz records.
