Brazil Achieves Record Drop in Amazon Deforestation

Advanced · C1+

News archive →
📅 October 17, 2026 - Brazil Achieves Record Drop in Amazon Deforestation

Tap an underlined word in the story to see its meaning.

Read the story

Ahead of a fiercely contested presidential run-off in which the ecosystem's future is a key issue, the Brazilian government reports that deforestation rates have fallen to record lows. Satellite data reveals that the deforestation rate in the Amazon plummeted by 41.8 percent over a twelve-month period, marking the most substantial drop since systematic records began in 1988. Given that sixty percent of this vast ecosystem lies within Brazilian borders, the region functions as an indispensable buffer against the worsening impacts of global climate change. In a healthy state, these tropical expanses assimilate massive volumes of carbon dioxide, mitigating planetary heating while sheltering millions of distinct species. Nevertheless, the biome faces relentless pressure from decades of deliberate land clearance driven by expanding agriculture and logging operations. This ecological degradation intensified under former president Jair Bolsonaro, when the Amazon endured a loss of forest lands that exceeded the previous four-year period by nearly sixty percent. This extensive destruction unfolded as the previous administration promoted mineral extraction in indigenous territories and simultaneously cut resources designed to safeguard the forest. President Lula definitively reversed his predecessor's policies, instituting rigorous environmental oversight and tightening consequences to suppress illicit logging. One climate advocate observed that this subsequent turnaround demonstrates how, given sufficient political will, it is entirely possible to curb deforestation alongside greenhouse gas emissions. However, with polls indicating a neck-and-neck race, Lula will soon confront Bolsonaro's son Flavio, who is widely anticipated to continue his father's policies should he secure victory, although he has explicitly pledged to combat organized crime in the Amazon.

Words and grammar

Definitions in English.

1. plummeted (verb): Decreased rapidly, steeply, and typically suddenly in value or amount.
2. indispensable (adjective): Absolutely necessary, essential, or impossible to manage without.
3. assimilate (verb): To take in and incorporate something, such as absorbing a gas or integrating new information.
4. mitigating (verb): Making something less severe, harmful, or painful.
5. relentless (adjective): Continuing in a severe, extreme, or uninterrupted way without softening or stopping.
6. degradation (noun): The process by which something is damaged, ruined, or reduced to a lower quality, especially an environment.
7. unfolded (verb): Developed or became clear over time, often used to describe how events happen.
8. rigorous (adjective): Extremely thorough, exhaustive, or strictly applied.
9. illicit (adjective): Forbidden by law, rules, or custom; illegal.
10. explicitly (adverb): In a clear and detailed manner, leaving absolutely no room for confusion or doubt.
11. neck-and-neck (adjective): Extremely close or tied in a competition, race, or comparison.

Advanced Grammar: Participle clauses for result or simultaneous action
Writers often use a present participle (an "-ing" verb) after a comma to describe the result of the main action or something happening at the same time. In this sentence, "mitigating planetary heating" is a direct result of the tropical expanses absorbing carbon dioxide, and "sheltering" introduces an additional simultaneous function.
Example from the text: "In a healthy state, these tropical expanses assimilate massive volumes of carbon dioxide, mitigating planetary heating while sheltering millions of distinct species."
